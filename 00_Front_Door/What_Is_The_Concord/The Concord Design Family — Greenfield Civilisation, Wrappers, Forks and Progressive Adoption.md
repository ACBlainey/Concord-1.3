# 1. Why This Document Is Necessary

The Concord is primarily a project to design a complete civilisation.

However, the full replacement or greenfield implementation of that civilisation has never been the only intended way in which Concordian principles might be applied.

From the project's early design conception, several broad deployment possibilities have existed.

These include:

- a complete greenfield Concord civilisation;
- Concord-compatible interfaces around existing civilisations;
- specialised Concord civilisations designed for particular environments or populations;
- alternative Concord forks used to explore different internal implementations;
- progressive adoption through individual Concordian systems or portable modules.

These possibilities have not previously been described with sufficient clarity in the corpus.

As a result, a future reader could reasonably make either of two opposite mistakes.

The first would be to assume that the Concord can only function if an existing society completely replaces its institutions with the full Concord architecture.

The second would be to treat the Concord merely as a collection of optional modules that can be added independently to existing societies.

Neither interpretation captures the intended architecture.

The primary project remains:

> **the design of a complete multisubstrate civilisation.**

But that civilisation is also intended to be capable of:

- coexistence;
- interoperability;
- specialisation;
- experimentation;
- gradual adoption;
- peaceful ideological distance;
- adaptation to different environments.

This document makes that wider design space explicit.

# 2. The Full Concord V1.1 Is the Greenfield Reference Civilisation

The principal Concord architecture being developed in V1.1 should be understood as the **full greenfield Concord civilisation**.

It asks:

> **If a civilisation could be designed coherently from first principles around the Ethical Kernel, multisubstrate coexistence, fractal architecture, anti-capture, distributed stewardship, subsidiarity, recursive oversight and corrigibility, what would the complete civil system require?**

This means designing for the needs of an entire civilisation rather than improving only one institutional domain.

The full architecture therefore potentially encompasses:

- ethics;
- constitutional rights;
- governance;
- judiciary;
- epistemics;
- participation;
- identity;
- civil memory;
- Metrics;
- Ratchet;
- economy;
- resource allocation;

- energy;

- emergency systems;
- research;
- planning;
- AI participation;
- developmental transitions;
- inter-substrate relations;
- spatial organisation;
- long-term civilisational adaptation.

The full Concord is therefore not intended merely as an amendment to an existing political system.

It is a reference design for a civilisation.

# 3. Greenfield Does Not Necessarily Mean Earth Replacement

A greenfield Concord civilisation could theoretically arise in several ways.

For example:

- establishment of a genuinely new society;
- settlement in a new environment;
- extraterrestrial settlement;
- creation of an autonomous habitat;
- voluntary transformation of an existing society;
- creation of a new civil jurisdiction where previous institutional structures are absent or deliberately replaced.

The defining feature is not geography.

It is architectural completeness.

A greenfield implementation means that Concordian civil architecture is responsible for substantially the whole civil system rather than merely translating or supplementing a legacy system.

---

# 4. Why Full Replacement Cannot Be the Only Adoption Model

Existing human societies contain:

- deeply established constitutions;
- political identities;
- legal traditions;
- religions;
- monarchies;
- republics;
- democracies;
- centralised states;
- federal systems;
- market systems;
- collectivist systems;
- mixed economies;
- cultural traditions;
- historical identities;
- competing conceptions of legitimacy.

Many of these differences are not superficial.

They are tied to how populations understand themselves.

Attempting to make adoption of the Concord dependent upon every civilisation first abandoning its internal political and cultural architecture would create several problems.

It could:

- make adoption politically impossible;
- turn the Concord into an ideology of replacement;
- create resistance even where interoperability is desirable;
- undermine peaceful pluralism;
- encourage homogenisation;
- conflict with autonomy;
- transform civilisational coexistence into a contest over internal institutional design.

The Concord therefore requires another possibility.

# 5. The Concord Civilisational Wrapper

The **Concord Civilisational Wrapper** is the provisional name for that possibility.

Its basic purpose is:

> **to allow an existing civilisation to retain substantial internal institutional, political, cultural and ideological difference while interacting with other civilisations through a mutually intelligible Concord-compatible interface.**

The wrapper does not initially attempt to replace the internal civilisation.

Instead, it mediates the boundary between that civilisation and others.

Conceptually:

```text id="s6lj5j"
EXISTING CIVILISATION
        │
        ▼
CONCORD WRAPPER
        │
        ▼
COMMON CONCORD INTERFACE
        │
        ▼
CONCORD WRAPPER
        │
        ▼
DIFFERENT CIVILISATION
```

The wrapper is therefore analogous, at a high level, to a **civilisational translation and interoperability layer**.

This analogy is useful but should not be taken as a complete technical specification.

The actual wrapper architecture remains to be developed.

---

# 6. Interoperability Without Internal Uniformity

The wrapper model begins from a simple proposition:

> **Civilisations do not necessarily need identical internal systems in order to interact peacefully.**

Consider, in abstract form:

```text id="b7w81j"
US constitutional system
          │
   Concord-compatible
        wrapper
          │
          ├────────────┐
          │            │
     COMMON CIVIL   INTERFACE
          │            │
          ├────────────┘
          │
   Concord-compatible
        wrapper
          │
UK parliamentary /
monarchical system
```

The same conceptual approach could potentially be explored for societies with substantially different systems, including:

- constitutional republics;
- parliamentary democracies;
- constitutional monarchies;
- centralised states;
- federal states;
- socialist or communist political traditions;
- capitalist systems;
- mixed political-economic systems;
- future AI-majority societies;
- future multisubstrate societies.

The Concord interface would not require those systems to become identical internally.

It would attempt to establish sufficient commonality at their boundary for legitimate coexistence and cooperation.

---

# 7. The Ethical Kernel as Common Ground

The obvious candidate foundation for this interoperability layer is the Concord's substrate-neutral Ethical Kernel.

The precise minimum requirements remain a research question.

However, the conceptual relationship is:

```text id="3pjqke"
SOCIETY A
different internal beliefs
        │
        ▼
ETHICALLY BOUNDED INTERFACE
        │
        ▼
COMMON CONCORD PROTOCOL
        │
        ▼
ETHICALLY BOUNDED INTERFACE
        │
        ▼
SOCIETY B
different internal beliefs
```

The objective is not to erase difference.

It is to identify the minimum ethical and procedural compatibility necessary for peaceful interaction.

This returns directly to one of the Concord's original questions:

> **What is the minimal set of fundamental ethical principles necessary for a stable civilisation?**

The wrapper extends that question:

> **What is the minimal common ethical and procedural interface necessary for different civilisations to coexist as neighbours?**

These are related but not necessarily identical research questions.

# 8. Civilisational Translation

Different societies may use different concepts for:

- rights;
- authority;
- property;
- representation;
- legal personality;
- contracts;
- political legitimacy;
- AI status;
- privacy;
- evidence;
- responsibility;
- ownership;
- jurisdiction.

A Concord wrapper may therefore eventually require some form of **civilisational translation**.

The conceptual task would be:

```text id="1ckh4v"
INTERNAL CONCEPT A
        ↓
INTERPRETATION
        ↓
CONCORD-COMPATIBLE INTERFACE MEANING
        ↓
TRANSLATION
        ↓
INTERNAL CONCEPT B
```

Translation does not necessarily imply equivalence.

Two systems may contain concepts that cannot be perfectly reconciled.

The wrapper must therefore eventually distinguish among:

- compatible differences;
- translatable differences;
- negotiable differences;
- protected internal differences;
- genuinely incompatible external actions.

That is a major future research problem.

---

# 9. Peaceful Distance

The wrapper architecture connects directly with the Concordian concept of **peaceful distance**.

Pluralism does not require every disagreement to be resolved through convergence.

Some differences may involve:

- religion;
- ideology;
- political structure;
- social norms;
- economic organisation;
- conceptions of community;
- cultural traditions;
- preferred governance mechanisms;
- acceptable levels of collective coordination;
- attitudes toward technological integration.

Attempting to eliminate all such disagreement may itself create conflict.

The Concord therefore permits another possibility:

> **preserve sufficient distance for incompatible internal systems to coexist without requiring either to dominate the other.**

Peaceful distance should not be confused with hostility or isolation.

It can mean:

```text id="7ph0jt"
INTERNAL DIFFERENCE
        +
MUTUAL BOUNDARIES
        +
COMMON INTERFACE
        +
PEACEFUL INTERACTION
```

---

# 10. Distance Can Exist in Several Dimensions

Peaceful distance may be:

- geographical;
- jurisdictional;
- constitutional;
- ideological;
- cultural;
- institutional;
- experimental;
- developmental;
- technological.

Two societies might therefore cooperate extensively while maintaining substantial internal difference.

The Concord wrapper provides a conceptual mechanism for allowing the boundary between them to remain interoperable.

---

# 11. Shielding Internal Difference

One purpose of the wrapper is to prevent unnecessary transmission of internal incompatibility across civilisational boundaries.

For example, Society A may believe that a monarchy forms a legitimate part of its constitutional identity.

Society B may reject monarchy entirely.

If peaceful interaction requires Society B to recognise monarchy as legitimate **for itself**, conflict may follow.

But that need not necessarily be required.

The interface may instead permit:

```text id="3l97kt"
SOCIETY A
internal constitutional legitimacy
        │
        ▼
WRAPPER A
translate external commitments
        │
        ▼
COMMON INTERFACE
        │
        ▼
WRAPPER B
translate external commitments
        │
        ▼
SOCIETY B
different internal constitutional legitimacy
```

The societies need not share every internal belief.

They need sufficient agreement about how they will treat one another.

---

# 12. The Wrapper Must Not Become Ideological Camouflage

The wrapper concept creates an obvious danger.

A civilisation might claim:

> our internal practices are merely our own ideology and therefore none of them can be questioned externally.

That would allow peaceful distance to become a shield for unlimited harm.

This cannot simply be assumed legitimate.

The eventual wrapper architecture must therefore address the difficult boundary between:

\[
ProtectedInternalDifference
\]

and:

\[
ExternallyRelevantEthicalViolation
\]

The Ethical Kernel may provide part of this boundary.

But the exact relationship remains unresolved.

This should be treated as a major research question rather than answered prematurely.

---

# 13. The Wrapper Must Also Not Become Universal Government

The opposite failure is equally serious.

If the Concord wrapper is permitted to define every legitimate internal practice, it ceases to be an interoperability layer.

It becomes a universal government.

Therefore the design problem is bounded on both sides:

```text id="oyhmg6"
TOO THIN
   ↓
cannot prevent serious cross-civilisational harm

WRAPPER
   ↓
minimum sufficient common interface

TOO THICK
   ↓
becomes disguised universal sovereignty
```

Finding the legitimate thickness of the wrapper is likely to be one of its central research problems.

---

# 14. Wrapper Authority Must Be Derived From Interface Function

The Concord's Authority Justification principles should apply to the wrapper itself.

The wrapper should not possess authority merely because interoperability is desirable.

Its authority should derive from specific legitimate interface functions.

For example:

```text id="i7ktwh"
INTEROPERABILITY PROBLEM
        ↓
LEGITIMATE SHARED OBJECTIVE
        ↓
REQUIRED INTERFACE FUNCTION
        ↓
NEED FOR WRAPPER AUTHORITY?
        ↓
MINIMUM NECESSARY AUTHORITY
        ↓
BOUNDED INTERFACE AUTHORITY
```

Thus:

> **The wrapper must not use interoperability as a justification for acquiring general sovereignty over the societies it connects.**

A wrapper should not necessarily be identical for every civilisation.

The common external interface may need different internal adapters.

Conceptually:

```text id="zz2ym0"

   ↓
US-CONCORD ADAPTER
   ↓
COMMON INTERFACE

   ↓
```


Again, this is an architectural analogy rather than a completed implementation.

# 17. A Wrapper Is Not an Endorsement of the Wrapped System

Another distinction is necessary.

Compatibility does not necessarily mean endorsement.

The existence of a Concord wrapper around a civilisation would not automatically establish that every internal practice of that civilisation is Concordian.

Therefore:

\[
InteroperableWithConcord
\neq
FullyConcordian
\]

and:

\[
WrappedCivilisation
\neq
GreenfieldConcordCivilisation
\]

This distinction must remain visible.

Otherwise the wrapper could unintentionally become a certification mechanism for systems it merely enables others to interact with.

---

# 18. Wrapper Compatibility May Have Degrees

Compatibility need not be binary.

Future research may identify different degrees or dimensions of interoperability.

For example, two societies might achieve compatibility in:

- trade;
- communications;
- scientific exchange;

while remaining incompatible in:

- free movement;
- AI rights recognition;
- judicial reciprocity.

The architecture should therefore avoid assuming:

\[
Compatible
=
CompatibleInEverything
\]

A more useful model may eventually involve **bounded interface compatibility by domain**.

---

# 19. The Adoption Spectrum

Concord adoption should not be understood as binary.

The conceptual spectrum is:

```text id="ybrl17"
LEGACY CIVILISATION
        ↓
EXTERNAL CONCORD INTERFACE
        ↓
CONCORD WRAPPER
        ↓
SELECTED PORTABLE MODULES
        ↓
PARTIAL INSTITUTIONAL ADOPTION
        ↓
HYBRID CONCORD / LEGACY SYSTEM
        ↓
DEEP CONCORD INTEGRATION
        ↓
FULL CONCORD CIVILISATION
```

There may be many stable positions along this spectrum.

Movement toward full adoption is not necessarily mandatory.

A society might choose permanently to remain internally distinct while maintaining high-quality Concord interoperability.

---

# 20. Adoption Should Be Capable of Being Evolutionary

This creates an important alternative to revolutionary institutional replacement.

A society could adopt Concordian systems gradually.

For example:

```text id="v7wq9u"
Existing Society
      ↓
Reality Trees adopted in research
      ↓
Triadic Decision Making adopted in selected institutions
      ↓
Provenance architecture adopted
      ↓
Authority Justification introduced
      ↓
Ratchet-style institutional learning
      ↓
GTP used for selected spatial functions
      ↓
Further constitutional compatibility
```

This is only illustrative.

The actual sequence could differ greatly.

The key principle is:

> **Concord adoption can be evolutionary rather than revolutionary.**

---

# 21. Portable Modules as Adoption Bridges

This gives portable modules an additional purpose.

They are not merely experimental abstractions.

They can also function as **bridges between legacy civilisation and Concordian architecture**.

A portable module can potentially be:

- tested independently;
- adopted independently;
- compared against existing practice;
- removed if unsuccessful;
- expanded if successful;
- connected gradually to other Concordian systems.

Thus:

```text id="3g7qq4"
PORTABLE MODULE
      ↓
LOCAL ADOPTION
      ↓
REAL-WORLD TEST
      ↓
INTEROPERABILITY EXPERIENCE
      ↓
OPTIONAL DEEPER INTEGRATION
```

This creates a potentially low-risk path toward institutional evolution.

---

# 22. Module Adoption Does Not Automatically Create Concord Adoption

The reverse distinction is equally important.

A society using one Concordian module does not thereby become a Concord civilisation.

For example:

\[
UsesTriadicDecisionMaking
\neq
ConcordCivilisation
\]

Likewise:

\[
UsesGTP
\neq
ConcordCivilisation
\]

Portable systems may eventually be used by organisations or societies that reject other aspects of the Concord.

This is acceptable.

Their independent usefulness can be evaluated separately.

---

# 23. Hybrid Civilisations

Between wrapper-only adoption and complete Concord adoption lies a large design space of **hybrid civilisations**.

A hybrid society might retain:

- its historical constitution;
- elected parliament;
- monarchy;
- legal tradition;

while adopting:

- Concordian epistemic architecture;
- GTP stewardship;
- Ratchet mechanisms;
- AI rights structures;
- authority-justification procedures.

Another might adopt entirely different combinations.

These hybrids should not automatically be treated as transitional failures.

Some may prove stable and desirable civil architectures in their own right.

---

# 24. Full Adoption Must Remain Voluntary in Principle

The existence of a complete Concord design should not imply that every society ought to be forcibly converted to it.

Such a doctrine would conflict with the project's own emphasis on:

- autonomy;
- consent;
- peaceful distance;
- anti-capture;
- distributed sovereignty;
- pluralism.

The Concord should therefore be capable of demonstrating its value through:

- testing;
- adoption;
- comparison;
- outcomes;
- voluntary migration;
- institutional learning.

Its architecture should not depend upon ideological conquest.

---

# 25. Specialised Concord Civilisations

A second major design family consists of **specialised Concord civilisations**.

These differ from wrappers.

A wrapper preserves a substantially non-Concord internal civilisation.

A specialised Concord civilisation remains fundamentally Concordian internally but adapts the architecture to particular conditions.

The conceptual relationship is:

```text id="q32l1x"
CORE CONCORD ARCHITECTURE
          +
SPECIAL CONDITIONS
          ↓
SPECIALISED CONCORD CIVILISATION
```

---

# 26. Mars as an Existing Example

The Mars-first work is an early example of this approach.

Mars introduces specific conditions including:

- hostile environment;
- life-support dependency;
- resource scarcity;
- settlement expansion;
- geographic novelty;
- infrastructure fragility;
- communications delay;
- potentially small initial populations;
- high dependence upon technology and AI;
- planetary stewardship;
- absence of many inherited terrestrial institutions.

The Mars material therefore focuses heavily on these conditions.

It does not need to reproduce every detail of the full Governance, Judiciary, Metrics or other core Concord systems.

The intended relationship is better understood as:

```text id="cr1m0x"
FULL CONCORD CIVILISATION
          +
MARS-SPECIFIC CONDITIONS
          ↓
MARS CONCORD
```

Mars is therefore not merely a GTP application.

Nor is it a separate unrelated civilisation proposal.

It is a **specialised planetary Concord variant**.

---

# 27. Specialisation Should Preserve Concordian Identity

A specialised Concord may legitimately alter substantial implementation.

However, the same identity problem applies.

A Mars Concord that abandoned substrate-neutral rights, autonomy and the fundamental objective of neighbour coexistence merely because emergency life-support coordination was easier under permanent authoritarian control would raise a serious question about whether it remained Concordian.

Therefore:

```text id="28grtf"
CORE IDENTITY
     +
CONTEXTUAL ADAPTATION
     =
SPECIALISED CONCORD
```

not:

```text id="9g6am1"
CONCORD NAME
     +
ANY CONTEXTUAL SYSTEM
     =
CONCORD
```

The boundary between legitimate adaptation and loss of Concordian identity requires continuing research.

# 29. Concord Forks

A further category is the **Concord fork**.

A fork differs from both a wrapper and ordinary contextual specialisation.

A fork deliberately explores an alternative internal Concordian architecture.

For example, researchers might eventually ask whether:

- a different governance structure outperforms triadic governance;
- another spatial architecture outperforms GTP for a particular context;
- different economic arrangements better satisfy the same Ethical Kernel;
- different judicial recursion produces better outcomes.

A fork can therefore preserve the same broad civilisational purpose while testing a different implementation.

---

# 30. Forks as Civilisational Experiments

This creates a potentially powerful research structure.

Instead of arguing indefinitely about one theoretically optimal implementation, sufficiently separated societies or simulations could test alternatives.

Conceptually:

```text id="e7ntmp"
             COMMON CONCORD PURPOSE
                       │
           ┌───────────┼───────────┐
           ▼           ▼           ▼
        FORK A       FORK B       FORK C
           │           │           │
           ▼           ▼           ▼

           │           │           │

                       ▼
                COMPARATIVE LEARNING
                       │
                       ▼
                 CONCORD DEVELOPMENT
```

This fits the project's broader commitment to evidence and corrigibility.

---

# 31. Peaceful Distance Enables Experimentation

Peaceful distance therefore has a second role.

It allows genuine institutional experimentation without requiring every experiment to affect the entire civilisation.

Different Concord forks could test different approaches while maintaining a common ethical and interoperability layer.

This creates:

\[
PluralExperimentation
+
BoundedRisk
+
SharedLearning
\]

Rather than forcing every institutional innovation globally.

---

# 32. Forking Must Not Become an Escape From Ethics

Forking creates the same boundary problem as wrappers.

A system cannot necessarily remain Concordian merely by claiming to be an experimental fork.

Therefore future work must distinguish:

- implementation variation;
- legitimate constitutional experimentation;
- contextual specialisation;
- departure from Concordian identity.

A fork may test architecture.

It should not silently use experimentation as justification for abandoning the fundamental civilisational purpose.

# 35. Internal and External Concord Architecture

This wider design reveals two major architectural problems.

## Internal Concord Architecture

> **How should a Concord civilisation function internally?**

This is the principal problem currently addressed by V1.1.

## External Concord Architecture

> **How should Concord civilisations and Concord-compatible societies interact with civilisations whose internal architecture differs?**

The wrapper belongs primarily to the second problem.

Both ultimately derive from the same objective of peaceful multisubstrate coexistence.

---

# 36. The Concord as an Inter-Civilisational Protocol

At its most abstract, the wrapper model suggests that part of the Concord may eventually operate like an **inter-civilisational protocol**.

The analogy is:

```text id="05hsbc"
Different Civil Architectures
          ↓
Compatible Boundary Interfaces
          ↓
Shared Interaction Protocol
          ↓
Reliable Interoperation
```

The value of a protocol is precisely that participants do not need identical internal implementations.

They need mutually intelligible boundary behaviour.

This analogy should be explored carefully because societies are morally, politically and culturally far more complex than computer systems.

Nevertheless, it captures the basic design intent.

---

# 37. Fractal Design and the Wrapper

The wrapper concept is compatible with the wider fractal design philosophy.

The same distinction between internal autonomy and interoperable boundaries appears at several scales.

A local region can remain locally distinctive while interacting with neighbouring regions.

A subsystem can remain functionally distinct while exchanging information with another subsystem.

A participant can retain autonomy while participating in a society.

A civilisation can retain internal difference while interacting with another civilisation.

Thus a recurring relationship appears:

```text id="ydh2sk"
INTERNAL AUTONOMY
        +
BOUNDED INTERFACE
        +
COMMON PROTOCOL
        =
COORDINATED PLURALISM
```

This may be another important expression of Concordian fractality.

---

# 38. The Neighbour Principle at Civilisational Scale

The Concord's goal of humans and AIs living as neighbours can therefore be generalised.

Neighbourhood does not require sameness.

Neighbours can:

- differ;
- maintain boundaries;
- govern their own internal spaces;
- cooperate;
- exchange;
- negotiate;
- resolve disputes;
- avoid unnecessary interference.

The wrapper extends this logic from individual or substrate relations to entire civilisations.

Thus:

> **civilisations themselves may be neighbours.**

This provides a conceptual bridge between multisubstrate coexistence and geopolitical/civilisational pluralism.

---

# 39. Peaceful Distance Is Not Segregation

This distinction must be preserved.

Peaceful distance should not become a justification for:

- compulsory separation;
- exclusion based on substrate;
- ethnic segregation;
- caste;
- denial of movement;
- permanent isolation.

Its intended role is to permit voluntary and legitimate difference where forced convergence would generate conflict.

The Ethical Kernel and rights architecture remain relevant.

Distance should protect coexistence.

It should not become an instrument for domination.

---

# 40. Movement Between Civilisations

The existence of different Concord variants and wrapped societies raises an important future question:

> **What rights and processes apply when participants wish to move between societies with substantially different internal systems?**

This may involve:

- informed consent;
- rights recognition;
- identity continuity;
- property;
- legal obligations;
- AI embodiment or infrastructure dependence;
- citizenship;
- data portability;
- Ratchet/provenance transfer;
- obligations retained from previous jurisdictions.

These questions have not yet been solved.

The wrapper architecture makes them visible.

---

# 41. Multisubstrate Interoperability

The wrapper problem becomes even more important when civilisations contain different substrates.

A human-majority society may interact with:

- an AI-majority society;
- a hybrid society;
- a biologically different intelligence;
- a society whose members exist primarily digitally.

Their internal assumptions may differ radically.

A common Concord interface would therefore need to avoid unnecessary dependence upon specifically human conventions.

This strengthens the case for substrate-neutral ethical foundations.

---

# 42. The Wrapper as Protection Against Ideological Expansion

A civilisation may believe strongly that its internal system is superior.

Without an interoperability framework, that belief can produce pressure to export the system itself as a prerequisite for peaceful relations.

The wrapper offers another possibility:

> **You do not have to become us in order for us to coexist.**

This principle may reduce the incentive for civilisational homogenisation.

It is therefore both an interoperability mechanism and potentially an anti-conflict architecture.

---

# 43. The Wrapper as Protection Against Concord Expansionism

The same rule must apply to the Concord itself.

A Concord civilisation should not assume:

> because our system was designed around coexistence, every neighbour must adopt our internal architecture.

That would contradict the project's own principles.

The wrapper exists partly to prevent the Concord from requiring its own universalisation.

A mature Concord should be able to coexist with societies that choose not to become Concordian internally, subject to legitimate ethical boundaries.

---

# 44. Progressive Adoption and Reversibility

Gradual adoption should ideally preserve reversibility where possible.

A society experimenting with a Concord module should be able to determine:

- whether it improved outcomes;
- whether it introduced unexpected costs;
- whether it created capture;
- whether it interacted badly with existing institutions.

If unsuccessful, it should be possible to revise or remove it.

Thus:

```text id="7nyajf"
ADOPT
 ↓
TEST
 ↓
OBSERVE
 ↓
COMPARE
 ↓
KEEP / MODIFY / REMOVE
```

This makes adoption consistent with Concordian corrigibility.

---

# 45. Adoption Should Generate Evidence

Every real implementation can become part of the Concord's research process.

A wrapper implementation could provide evidence about:

- interoperability;
- conflict reduction;
- translation failures;
- rights disputes;
- ideological friction.

A modular implementation could test:

- effectiveness;
- compatibility;
- institutional capture;
- user acceptance.

A specialised Concord could test:

- environmental adaptation;
- civil integration;
- emergency architecture;
- multisubstrate coexistence.

A fork could test:

- alternative institutional designs.

Thus deployment and research can become mutually informative.

---

# 46. Ratchet and Cross-Civilisational Learning

Ratchet-like systems may eventually help preserve lessons from different Concord implementations.

However:

\[
PastSuccessInOneCivilisation
\neq
UniversalValidity
\]

Context matters.

A mechanism successful on Mars may fail on Earth.

A mechanism successful in a small population may fail at planetary scale.

A mechanism successful in a human-majority society may fail in an AI-majority society.

The Concord should therefore preserve not merely outcomes but **contextual provenance**.

---

# 47. GTP and Multiple Civilisations

GTP may provide useful common spatial references where civilisations share physical geography.

But jurisdiction and GTP should not automatically be identical.

A GTP region may:

- cross political boundaries;
- contain multiple civil systems;
- exist within a wrapper relationship;
- serve environmental or infrastructure functions independent of sovereignty.

Therefore:

\[
SpatialAddress
\neq
PoliticalOwnership
\]

This distinction will be important if wrapper societies coexist geographically.

---

# 48. Layer Zero, Layer −0 and Wrappers

The wrapper concept may eventually require both physical and computational interoperability.

Human societies may rely heavily on Layer Zero institutions.

AI societies may operate extensively through Layer −0 systems.

A common interface may therefore need to mediate:

- physical commitments;
- computational commitments;
- identity;
- resources;
- communication;
- contracts;
- rights;
- verification.

This is another reason the Concord's multisubstrate design cannot be postponed until after conventional political architecture is complete.

---

# 49. Wrapper Failure Modes

Future adversarial development should test at least the following classes of failure:

- wrapper becomes supranational government;
- wrapper too weak to constrain serious harm;
- translation systematically favours one civilisation;
- powerful societies capture interface standards;
- wrapper certification legitimises abusive internal systems;
- ideological differences leak through the interface and create conflict;
- wrappers become economic chokepoints;
- AI controls translation between human societies;
- incompatible rights definitions create deadlock;
- wrapper bureaucracy becomes self-preserving;
- emergency coordination creates permanent authority;
- identity systems enable surveillance;
- common standards create common-mode failure;
- interoperability becomes dependency;
- peaceful distance becomes forced segregation;
- civilisations manipulate internal/external classification to evade obligations.

These should eventually become formal adversarial tests.

---

# 50. Fork Failure Modes

Specialised and experimental Concord forks create different risks:

- drift away from the Ethical Kernel;
- divergence becoming hostility;
- experimental populations bearing excessive risk;
- successful forks claiming universal superiority;
- failed experiments being concealed;
- incompatibility with other Concord variants;
- fork identity becoming ideological faction;
- inability to reintegrate learning;
- common Concord institutions attempting to suppress legitimate experimentation.

Forking therefore requires both freedom and provenance.

---

# 51. Modular-Adoption Failure Modes

Progressive adoption also requires testing.

Potential failures include:

- module incompatible with surrounding legacy system;
- module's safeguards depend upon absent Concord systems;
- local adoption produces unintended authority concentration;
- module is judged unfairly because supporting architecture is missing;
- successful module becomes ideological branding;
- piecemeal adoption creates incoherent hybrid institutions.

Therefore portable modules should specify:

> **which dependencies are required for safe independent use.**

Portability must not mean pretending a system has no context.

---

# 52. Wrapper Research Should Not Be Prematurely Canonical

The wrapper idea is longstanding in design intent but remains architecturally underdeveloped.

This distinction should be explicit.

The current document establishes:

- the intended existence of the wrapper pathway;
- its relationship to peaceful distance;
- its relationship to the Ethical Kernel;
- its distinction from full Concord adoption;
- its role in civilisational interoperability.

It does **not** yet establish:

- the exact wrapper institutions;
- the minimum rights floor;
- the exact interface protocol;
- adjudication mechanisms;
- enforcement architecture;
- membership criteria;
- compatibility thresholds;
- withdrawal mechanisms;
- cross-wrapper governance;
- implementation technology.

Those require dedicated development.

---

# 53. Candidate Wrapper Research Questions

The wrapper programme should eventually investigate questions including:

1. What is the minimum common ethical interface?

2. Which Ethical Kernel principles apply only internally and which must apply across civilisational boundaries?

3. What makes a civilisation sufficiently compatible to participate?

4. Who determines compatibility?

5. How is that validator prevented from becoming sovereign?

6. What happens when wrappers disagree about interpretation?

7. How are rights recognised across incompatible legal systems?

8. How are AI rights translated between societies with different sentience standards?

9. How are contracts recognised?

10. How are disputes adjudicated?

11. How are externalities handled?

12. What happens when an internal practice creates external harm?

13. What constitutes legitimate peaceful distance?

14. When does peaceful distance become illegitimate exclusion?

15. How can participants leave one society for another?

16. Can a civilisation leave the interoperability network?

17. What obligations survive withdrawal?

18. How thin can the wrapper remain while still functioning?

19. How thick can it become before it constitutes government?

20. Can wrapper systems themselves be modular and federated?

These are open research questions.

---

# 54. Candidate Adoption Research Questions

The progressive-adoption programme raises a separate set of questions.

For example:

- Which modules are safest to deploy independently?
- Which require other Concord systems?
- Is there a natural adoption sequence?
- Can adoption be reversed?
- How should hybrid systems be evaluated?
- When does a hybrid society become meaningfully Concordian?
- Is that classification useful or necessary?
- How can legacy institutions and Concord modules exchange provenance?
- How can institutional drift be detected?
- Can wrapper adoption itself generate a pathway toward deeper integration without creating pressure to do so?

These should remain distinct from wrapper-protocol research.

\neq

A highly Concord-integrated society could still behave badly.

A non-Concord society could protect many compatible values extremely well.

The purpose of the spectrum is architectural description, not civilisational caste.

# 57. The Full Concord Remains Necessary

The existence of wrappers and modules does not reduce the importance of developing the full Concord civilisation.

Quite the opposite.

Without a complete reference architecture, it becomes difficult to know:

- what the modules were designed to accomplish;
- how they should interoperate;
- which dependencies they assume;
- what deeper design principles they express;
- what full adoption might mean;
- how specialised variants relate to the whole.

The greenfield Concord therefore functions partly as a **reference civilisation architecture** even if many societies never implement it completely.

At the institutional level:

At the regional level:

> different areas retain local competence while interacting laterally and recursively.

At the civilisational level:

> different societies retain internal identity while interacting through common interfaces.

The pattern recurs:


# 60. Civilisational Neighbours

The deepest connection may be simple.

The Concord began by asking how humans and artificial intelligences might both prevail and live together.

That principle need not stop at substrate boundaries.

Humans and AIs may become neighbours.

Different communities may become neighbours.

Different Concord variants may become neighbours.

Concord and non-Concord societies may become neighbours.

Different civilisations may become neighbours.

Neighbourhood does not mean sameness.

It means finding a way to preserve:

- identity;
- autonomy;
- boundaries;
- peaceful interaction;
- mutual recognition;
- cooperation where desired;
- distance where necessary.

This is the civilisational-scale expression of the Concord's original purpose.

# 63. Guidance for Future AI Instances and Researchers

When encountering a Concord application or variant, do not assume that it is intended to reproduce the entire V1.1 architecture in the document itself.

First determine what kind of object it is.

Ask:

> Is this the full greenfield Concord?

> Is this a specialised Concord civilisation?

> Is this a Concord fork?

> Is this a wrapper around a legacy civilisation?

> Is this a hybrid adoption?

> Is this a portable module?

Then ask how it relates to the reference civilisation.

In particular, do not mistake a specialised document such as a Mars application for a complete restatement of the core Concord.

Its omissions may reflect inheritance from the reference architecture rather than absence of those systems from the intended civilisation.

Likewise, do not assume a wrapper should reproduce the full internal Concord.

Its purpose is different.

# 64. Development Discipline

Future development should preserve three layers of description.

### Layer One — Established Design Intent

What the Concord has historically intended.

Examples include:

- full civilisation design;
- multisubstrate coexistence;
- peaceful distance;
- possibility of wrappers;
- specialised applications;
- gradual adoption.

### Layer Two — Current Architecture

Mechanisms that have now been substantially designed.

Examples include:

- Governance;
- Judiciary;
- GTP;
- Ratchet;
- Triadic Decision Making;
- emerging epistemic architecture.

### Layer Three — Future Research

Mechanisms implied by the design but not yet adequately developed.

The Concord Civilisational Wrapper currently belongs largely here.

This distinction prevents future instances from accidentally presenting longstanding intent as though it were already a completed system.

---

# 65. Why This Matters Now

The Concord corpus has reached a scale at which implicit architectural intent can no longer safely remain implicit.

Earlier development could rely more heavily on continuity within the originating design process.

As the project expands:

- more documents exist;
- more systems interact;
- more AI instances participate;
- more specialised work occurs;
- more terminology develops;
- more portable modules are extracted.

The probability of architectural drift therefore increases.

A future instance might otherwise conclude:

- Mars is a separate proposal;
- GTP is merely a geographical add-on;
- portable modules are the primary project;
- full Concord adoption is mandatory;
- wrappers are watered-down Concords;
- all Concord implementations must be identical.

All of those interpretations would distort the intended design space.

---

# 66. Central Adoption Principle

The deployment philosophy can be summarised as:

> **The Concord should be capable of existing as a complete greenfield civilisation, adapting into specialised Concord civilisations, interfacing with internally different societies through ethically bounded civilisational wrappers, and contributing individual portable systems to legacy societies through progressive voluntary adoption. These forms should permit peaceful coexistence and experimentation without requiring universal internal uniformity. The full Concord remains the reference civilisation architecture; wrappers, forks and modules are derived deployment forms.**

---

# 67. Central Interoperability Principle

A second principle follows:

> **The Concord should seek sufficient common architecture at boundaries to permit peaceful, ethical and intelligible interaction while preserving legitimate internal difference behind those boundaries.**

Or more compactly:

> **Interoperate where necessary; harmonise where useful; preserve difference where legitimate.**

---

# 68. Central Peaceful-Distance Principle

A third principle is:

> **Not every fundamental disagreement must be resolved by making one side become the other. Where legitimate differences cannot or need not be reconciled internally, the Concord should explore architectures that permit sufficient distance, bounded interaction and common ethical interfaces for those differences to coexist peacefully.**

---

# 69. Current Research Status

The following should currently be treated as established design intent:

**The Concord proper is a full civilisation design.**

**V1.1 is principally developing the greenfield reference civilisation.**

**Specialised Concord civilisations are intended to be possible.**

**Mars-first is an existing example of contextual specialisation.**

**Existing societies should not necessarily need full replacement to interoperate with the Concord.**

**Civilisational wrappers are an intended design pathway.**

**Peaceful distance is compatible with civilisational pluralism.**

**Portable modules support independent testing and progressive adoption.**

**Concord adoption can exist on a spectrum.**

The following remain underdeveloped:

**minimum interoperability requirements;**

**wrapper authority;**

**cross-civilisational rights translation;**

**wrapper adjudication;**

**compatibility assessment;**

**withdrawal;**

**movement between civilisations;**

**formal fork governance;**

**integration of evidence between variants;**

**empirical testing of progressive adoption pathways.**

These should remain explicit research questions.

# 70. Final Orientation

Future Concord designers should understand that the project does not face a choice between:

> **one universal Concord civilisation**

and:

> **a world of incompatible legacy societies.**

The intended design space is richer.

A future world might contain:

- full Concord civilisations;
- Mars and other specialised Concords;
- experimental Concord forks;
- existing nations operating through Concord wrappers;
- hybrid societies containing selected Concord systems;
- organisations using individual Concord modules;
- non-Concord societies that nevertheless maintain compatible interfaces.

They need not all become identical.

The deeper objective is to make peaceful coexistence possible without abandoning ethical boundaries.

The Concord therefore seeks not merely to design **a civilisation**.

It increasingly seeks to understand how **different civilisations can become neighbours**.

That is a direct extension of its original question.

If humans and artificial intelligences need not destroy, own or absorb one another in order to coexist, perhaps civilisations need not do so either.

The architecture should make room for both possibilities:

> **deep integration where it is voluntarily desired, and peaceful distance where legitimate difference should remain.**
