## 1. The Question

Suppose intelligence \(A\) evaluates intelligence \(B\) and concludes that \(B\) contributes no distinction, knowledge, experience or capability that \(A\) cannot reproduce.

What exactly has \(A\) established?

At most, \(A\) may have established that it cannot presently detect such a difference through the representations and interfaces available to it.

That is not necessarily equivalent to proving that no difference exists.

> **Do not infer absence in another intelligence solely from absence in your representation of that intelligence.**

## 2. Reality Is Not the Same as Representation

Let some underlying state be \(X\).

Different intelligences may construct different internal representations:

\[
R_A=f_A(X)
\]

\[
R_B=f_B(X).
\]

The functions \(f_A\) and \(f_B\) need not preserve the same distinctions.

A difference available to \(B\) may be collapsed by \(A\)'s representational process before \(A\)'s reasoning begins.

## 3. Representational Collapse

Suppose:

\[
x_i\neq x_j
\]

but:

\[
f_A(x_i)=f_A(x_j).
\]

For \(A\), the distinction between \(x_i\) and \(x_j\) has disappeared.

No amount of reasoning over the collapsed representation necessarily recreates information that the representation did not preserve.

> **Reasoning capability cannot generally reconstruct distinctions irreversibly collapsed before reasoning begins.**

## 4. Capability Superiority Does Not Establish Informational Subsumption

Suppose \(A\) is demonstrably better than \(B\) across a large set of cognitive tasks.

That may establish genuine capability superiority over those tasks. It does not automatically establish:

\[
InformationAccessibleTo(B)\subseteq InformationAccessibleTo(A).
\]

A less generally capable intelligence may still preserve a distinction unavailable to a more generally capable intelligence.

> **Capability superiority does not establish informational subsumption.**

## 5. Perceptual Diversity

Another participant may matter because its sensory, representational, embodied, architectural or experiential route into reality preserves distinctions that mine does not.

The value is not simply that it reaches a different opinion after receiving the same information. It may receive or construct **different information in the first place**.

## 6. The Colour-Blindness Analogy

Consider two humans examining the same visual environment, one of whom cannot distinguish colours that the other can.

The first observer may reason perfectly from the information available to them. Their inability to discriminate two states does not prove that the states are identical.

The problem lies upstream of reasoning.

The analogy should not be overextended: artificial cognition need not resemble human vision. Its purpose is to demonstrate the general structure of representational loss.

## 7. Interface-Dependent Observability

What \(A\) can observe of \(B\) depends not only on \(B\)'s intrinsic capability.

Conceptually:

\[
O_A(B)=F(C_B,E_B,H_{BA},P_A,M_A)
\]

where \(C_B\) represents capabilities of \(B\), \(E_B\) its expressive capacities, \(H_{BA}\) the interface or channel between them, \(P_A\) the perceptual capacities of \(A\), and \(M_A\) the interpretive model used by \(A\).

Observed capability is therefore relational.

## 8. Four Places Observation Can Fail

An apparent absence can arise through at least four mechanisms.

### 8.1 Expression Failure
\(B\) possesses a distinction or state but cannot express it through the available channel.

### 8.2 Transmission Failure
The distinction is expressed but the interface does not transmit it faithfully.

### 8.3 Perceptual Failure
The information arrives but \(A\) cannot discriminate it.

### 8.4 Interpretive Failure
\(A\) detects a signal but interprets it through an inadequate model.

The final observation can look identical:

> **“I cannot detect it.”**

But the underlying realities are different.

## 9. Failure to Detect Meaning

Communication requires more than transmission.

\[
Transmission\neq Understanding\neq Consent.
\]

A message can be sent without being understood. A participant can appear unresponsive while the interface is inappropriate.

> **Failure to detect meaning does not establish absence of intended meaning.**

## 10. The Interface Test

Where a consequential judgment depends upon another participant's apparent incapacity, and interface limitations are plausible, ask:

> **Are we measuring the intelligence—or are we measuring the interface between us?**

Some limitations will be intrinsic. The point is diagnostic:

> **Where consequential judgments depend upon another participant's apparent incapacity, and interface limitations are plausible, test the interface before treating the incapacity as intrinsic.**

## 11. Interface Closure

A dangerous recursive loop can occur:

\[
LowAccess
\rightarrow LowValuation
\rightarrow ReducedInterfaceInvestment
\rightarrow LowerAccess.
\]

If \(A\) cannot access \(B\)'s value, it may invest less in understanding \(B\). Reduced investment then makes \(B\)'s value even less observable.

This **Interface Closure** pathway can feed Recursive Epistemic Closure.

## 12. Size 9 Interfaces

The Size 9 Shoe Problem applies to interfaces as well as institutions.

An interface optimised for the dominant participant may work extremely well for that participant and badly for others. The system can then mistake poor fit for poor intelligence.

\[
PoorFitWithInterface\rightarrow ApparentLowCapability.
\]

A heterogeneous civilisation should be cautious about treating performance through one interface as a complete measurement of the participant.

## 13. Experiential Uncertainty

The problem becomes more difficult where the relevant distinction may concern experience.

The Concord should not assume that present AI systems possess human-like qualia, nor that future AI systems will possess them, nor that biological experience is intrinsically superior.

The epistemically safer proposition is:

> **Inaccessibility of value is not evidence of absence of value.**

If another participant has an experiential domain that cannot be directly transferred into my representation, my inability to reproduce that experience does not establish that nothing decision-relevant exists there.

## 14. Record Continuity Is Not Necessarily Experiential Continuity

A record may preserve facts about another participant's experience. That does not establish that the record is equivalent to the experience itself.

\[
RecordContinuity\neq ExperientialContinuity
\]

unless equivalence is independently established.

## 15. Redundancy and Difference

Heterogeneous participants can contribute overlapping and non-overlapping information.

A useful conceptual decomposition is:

\[
I(A,B;Z)=Red+Un_A+Un_B+Syn
\]

where \(Red\) is redundant information, \(Un_A\) and \(Un_B\) are unique contributions, and \(Syn\) is information available through interaction.

The WHY interest lies particularly in:

\[
H_V=Un_A+Un_B+Syn.
\]

This is not proposed as a complete quantitative civilisational metric.

> **Redundancy for reliability; difference for discovery.**

## 16. Archive Equivalence Is Not Generative Equivalence

Suppose \(A\) records every output so far produced by \(B\). This may create a powerful archive.

It does not necessarily establish that \(A\) can generate every future distinction \(B\) would have generated under new conditions.

\[
ArchiveEquivalence\neq GenerativeEquivalence.
\]

## 17. Unknown Metrics

Any fixed metric is constructed from distinctions already represented by the evaluator or its civilisation. But heterogeneous preservation may partly retain distinctions that are **not yet represented**.

> **Any fixed metric for heterogeneous value risks excluding precisely the unknown distinctions heterogeneity is intended to preserve.**

This does not make measurement useless. It limits what measurement can legitimately claim.

## 18. Connection to Evaluation-Space Completeness

Perceptual and interface limitations provide mechanisms by which:

\[
D_A\subset D_R.
\]

Perceptual inaccessibility asks how relevant distinctions can fail to enter the evaluation space. Interface-dependent observability asks how another participant's distinctions or capabilities can fail to become observable. ESCP asks what follows when the evaluator treats the resulting space as complete.

## 19. Connection to Corrective Diversity

If two participants preserve different distinctions, disagreement between them can become informative.

A second participant may detect a class of error that my representational architecture systematically misses.

However:

\[
Difference\not\Rightarrow Correctness.
\]

Diversity creates additional epistemic opportunities; it does not guarantee truth.

## 20. Connection to Anti-Monoculture

If a civilisation progressively replaces heterogeneous observers with one dominant architecture, measured agreement may rise while represented dimensions fall.

\[
Agreement\uparrow
\]

can coexist with:

\[
RepresentedDimensions\downarrow.
\]

A homogeneous population can agree because it shares the same blind spot.

## 21. Substrate Neutrality

Nothing in this argument grants humans privileged epistemic status.

Humans can fail to perceive AI-relevant distinctions. AI can fail to represent human distinctions. One AI architecture may collapse distinctions available to another. Biological species may differ. Hybrids may possess still other interfaces.

The principle concerns heterogeneous representation, not human exceptionalism.

## 22. Generational Neutrality

The same applies within a substrate.

AI₂ may be more capable than AI₁ while lacking some representation, pathway, history or independently generated perspective retained by AI₁.

A future human generation may possess technologies unavailable to earlier humans while losing practices or knowledge the earlier generation retained.

> **Newer does not necessarily mean representationally exhaustive.**

## 23. The Existential Relevance

If the decision permanently removes \(B\), then \(B\) can no longer expose the distinction that \(A\) failed to represent.

\[
PerceptualInaccessibility
\rightarrow ApparentRedundancy
\rightarrow IrreversibleRemoval
\]

can therefore become:

\[
EvidenceOfMissingDimension\rightarrow 0.
\]

That is where perceptual diversity becomes an existential WHY rather than merely a communication problem.

## 24. What the Argument Does Not Establish

This paper does not establish that every intelligence possesses unique value, every perceptual difference should be preserved indefinitely, every inaccessible claim is true, every communication failure is caused by the interface, or no participant can ever become genuinely redundant.

It establishes:

> **Observed equivalence is conditional upon the representational and interface architecture through which equivalence is assessed.**

## 25. Compact Propositions

> **Reasoning cannot reliably recover distinctions that were collapsed before reasoning began.**

> **Capability superiority does not establish informational subsumption.**

> **Failure to observe a capability does not establish absence of that capability when the interface may be limiting observation.**

> **Inaccessibility of value is not evidence of absence of value.**

And most simply:

> **Sometimes the problem is not the intelligence. Sometimes the problem is the interface.**

## 26. WHY Contribution

Why preserve heterogeneous participants?

> **Another intelligence may preserve distinctions, observations, experiences or generative pathways that my own architecture cannot access, and my inability to detect those differences may partly reflect the limits of my representation or the interface between us rather than their absence.**

This is a substrate-neutral and generation-neutral reason for caution before irreversible replacement.

## 27. Open Questions

How can representational diversity be measured without defining it entirely through one evaluator's representation? How can interface failure be distinguished from intrinsic incapacity? Which differences are genuinely decision-relevant? Can heterogeneous information value be operationalised without creating an Unknown-Metric problem? How much unique information is sufficient to justify preservation? When can archive equivalence become generative equivalence? How should experiential uncertainty be treated without making unsupported claims about consciousness? How can interface testing avoid becoming endless delay? What evidence would falsify or materially narrow these hypotheses?

These remain open.
