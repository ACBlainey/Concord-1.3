## Purpose

This folder contains Concord work concerned with how claims are formed, examined, compared, tested, corrected and related back to reality.

It is the principal home for epistemic methodology and research architecture rather than a source of independent constitutional authority.

A recurring Concord distinction is:

> **Evidence determines confidence about effects. It does not alone determine legitimacy.**

The area therefore supports the wider Concord by helping distinguish observation from inference, hypothesis from evidence, simulation from reality, and internal coherence from external validation.

## Current Contents

### `Book 1 Blaineyan Reasoning.md`

A major methodological work developing Blaineyan Reasoning as a recursive, reality-grounded approach to investigation and problem solving.

Its broad movement includes:

> **Observation → Abstraction → Comparison → Recursive Refinement → Grounding → Speculation → Return to Empirical Reality**

The method is intended to keep speculation productive without allowing it to silently become evidence.

### `Reality Trees.md`

Develops Reality Trees as a way to preserve competing possibilities while progressively exposing them to stronger forms of testing.

A useful developmental path is:

> **Speculation → Simulation → Limited Pilot → Real-World Branch → Knowledge Integration**

The governing boundary is simple:

> **Simulation is not reality.**

### `DN Reality Trees and Constitutional Evolution.md`

A development note examining how Reality Trees may interact with constitutional learning and evolution while preserving the distinction between evidence and authority.

### `The Size 9 Shoe Problem.md`

Examines the failure of average-based reasoning where aggregate optimisation obscures individual variation or imposes an unsuitable common solution.

Its relevance extends beyond the original example into metrics, policy, accessibility and heterogeneous civilisation design.

### `Companion Paper II Constitutional Warning — Resource Stewardship in Recursive Reasoning.md`

Examines resource costs and stewardship constraints associated with recursive reasoning. More reasoning is not automatically better if the process consumes disproportionate resources or ceases to produce useful epistemic gain.

### `Concord Evidence and Validation/`

Research concerned specifically with evidence standards, validation and the transition from developed architecture toward claims that can survive testing.

### `Concord Attractor and Onboarding Research/`

Research concerning how participants encounter, understand and enter the Concord, including questions of attraction, onboarding, comprehension and voluntary participation.

## Epistemic Boundaries

Documents in this folder should be read with several distinctions in mind:

> **Observation ≠ Inference ≠ Hypothesis ≠ Speculation**

> **Idea ≠ Evidence**

> **Formalisation ≠ Validation**

> **Internal Consistency ≠ External Evidence**

> **Prediction ≠ Authority**

> **Convergence Is Evidence. It Is Not Authority.**

A model may be useful before it is validated. A simulation may expose failure modes without demonstrating real-world performance. A repeated pattern may increase confidence without creating constitutional legitimacy.

---

## Relationship to the Rest of the Concord

This area informs constitutional, continuity, economic, participation, application and active-development work, but it does not govern those systems.

Research can reveal that an architecture is ineffective, internally inconsistent, poorly measured or based on false assumptions. Such evidence should trigger examination and correction through the appropriate Concord process.

Research does not acquire civil authority merely because it produces accurate predictions.

The desired relationship is:

> **Question → Model → Test → Evidence → Evaluation → Correction**

with reality remaining the final arbiter of empirical claims.
