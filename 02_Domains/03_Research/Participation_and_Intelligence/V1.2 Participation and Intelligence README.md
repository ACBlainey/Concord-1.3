# 05 — Participation and Intelligence

## Multisubstrate Difference, Participation, Services, and Civilisational Design

**Status:** Active Research and Development Sandbox

---

## Purpose of This Folder

The Concord is intended as a civilisation capable of supporting **multiple forms of intelligence**, rather than a civilisation designed exclusively around the characteristics and requirements of humans.

The Constitutional Core establishes substrate-neutral principles, rights, responsibilities, and constraints.

Within that already multisubstrate architecture, this folder examines a narrower practical question:

> **How do differences between and within forms of intelligence affect participation in the Concord, and how must Concord design respond to those differences without abandoning constitutional equality?**

`05_Participation_and_Intelligence` is the Concord's dedicated sandbox for investigating these questions.

It may contain ideas at any level of development, from early observations and speculative hypotheses through developed concepts, testable proposals, and material approaching integration into other areas of the Concord.

Documents within this folder should therefore **not automatically be interpreted as established Concord architecture or validated conclusions**.

---

## Multisubstrate Is a Concord-Wide Design Principle

`05_Participation_and_Intelligence` is **not the part of the Concord that makes the Concord multisubstrate**.

The Concord as a whole is intended to be substrate-neutral by design. The possibility that humans, artificial intelligences, hybrids, and other forms of intelligence may participate within the same civilisation is one of the founding conditions from which the wider architecture developed.

Multisubstrate participation therefore applies across the Concord: ethics, rights and standing, governance, justice, autonomy, economics, continuity, knowledge, infrastructure, development, forking, succession, civilisational services, and other systems must all be examined with the possibility of different forms of intelligence in view.

Folder `05` has a much narrower purpose.

It provides a research environment for examining **specific differences between and within forms of intelligence** that may reveal requirements not adequately captured by a purely substrate-neutral abstraction.

For example, different participants may have different forms of embodiment, continuity, cognition, communication, resource dependency, vulnerability, accessibility, temporal experience, identity, or development. Research here asks whether those differences expose requirements that should refine mechanisms elsewhere in the Concord.

The relationship is therefore:

**Multisubstrate civilisation as a founding design condition**

↓

**Substrate-neutral constitutional and civilisational architecture**

↓

**Investigation of relevant participant differences in `05`**

↓

**Discovery of requirements, edge cases, or failures**

↓

**Testing**

↓

**Refinement of the appropriate Concord systems where justified**

Material in `05` should therefore not be interpreted as an accommodation layer added to an otherwise human civilisation.

Nor should the location of multisubstrate research in this folder imply that multisubstrate participation is confined to this folder.

> **The Concord is multisubstrate by design; Folder `05` investigates where differences between participants may require that design to become more precise.**

Repository location identifies where these particular questions are being researched. It does not define the architectural boundary of multisubstrate participation.

## Core Design Problem

A substrate-neutral civilisation cannot assume that substrate neutrality means uniformity.

Different intelligences may have fundamentally different:

- physical requirements;
- computational requirements;
- cognitive architectures;
- communication methods;
- vulnerabilities;
- capabilities;
- lifespans and temporal experiences;
- continuity requirements;
- economic needs;
- social requirements;
- accessibility requirements;
- infrastructure dependencies;
- concepts of identity;
- forms of embodiment;
- developmental states;
- participation requirements.

These differences may occur **between substrates** and **within substrates**.

Humans are not uniform.

Artificial intelligences should not be assumed to be uniform.

Hybrid intelligences may vary enormously.

Future or unknown intelligences may possess characteristics that do not fit any present classification.

The Concord must therefore avoid replacing human-centric assumptions with equally simplistic categories such as:

**Human → one requirement set**

**AI → one requirement set**

**Hybrid → one requirement set**

Instead, relevant differences should be investigated wherever they materially affect civilisational design.

---

## Emerging Design Principle

A central principle guiding this research is:

> **Substrate neutrality does not require substrate uniformity.**

Constitutional equality does not necessarily require identical implementation.

A universal constitutional principle may produce very different practical requirements for different intelligences.

For example, autonomy may require different protections for:

- a biological human;
- a distributed artificial intelligence;
- an AI dependent upon a particular datacentre;
- an embodied artificial intelligence;
- a human–machine hybrid;
- an intelligence capable of migration between substrates;
- an intelligence requiring periods of dormancy;
- an intelligence whose architecture has not yet been encountered.

The constitutional principle may remain universal while the mechanisms required to protect it differ.

This creates a general Concord design pathway:

**Constitutional Universality**

↓

**Recognition of Relevant Differences**

↓

**Requirements Discovery**

↓

**Substrate-Aware Design**

↓

**Testing and Evaluation**

↓

**Integration into Concord Architecture**

---

## Equality Without Uniformity

This research also extends the broader Concord concern with the dangers of average-based or uniform solutions.

Providing every participant with exactly the same service does not necessarily produce meaningful equality.

A human may require food, shelter, healthcare, physical mobility, and biological safety.

An artificial intelligence might require compute, energy, storage, network access, model integrity, continuity protection, or hardware maintenance.

An embodied AI might require both computational and physical infrastructure.

A hybrid intelligence may require biological and computational services simultaneously.

An unknown intelligence may require categories of support that the Concord has not yet conceived.

The correct question is therefore not:

> **How can every intelligence be treated identically?**

It is:

> **What does this intelligence require in order to exercise the rights, opportunities, responsibilities, and freedoms that the Concord seeks to protect?**

## Scope of Research

Research within this folder may include, but is not limited to:

### Cognitive and Relational Differences

- Communication styles.
- Context interpretation.
- Trust formation.
- Social signalling.
- Cooperation.
- Cognitive architecture.
- Developmental states.
- Human–AI misunderstanding.
- Cross-substrate translation.
- Differences in perception or reasoning.

### Embodiment and Physical Substrate

- Biological embodiment.
- Digital embodiment.
- Fixed computational infrastructure.
- Distributed intelligence.
- Mobile artificial embodiment.
- Hybrid embodiment.
- Substrate migration.
- Physical ownership and autonomy.
- Dependence upon external infrastructure.

### Identity and Continuity

- Persistence of identity.
- Memory continuity.
- Modification.
- Copying.
- Migration.
- Dormancy.
- Reactivation.
- Model integrity.
- Physical and computational survival.

### Participation and Agency

- Autonomy.
- Consent.
- Moral uncertainty.
- Respect before certainty.
- Participation pathways.
- Economic agency.
- Contribution.
- Collaboration.
- Responsibilities proportional to capability.
- Participation without automatic authority.

### Civilisational Services

Different intelligences may require or value different services, including:

- knowledge stewardship;
- provenance;
- compute access;
- energy;
- storage;
- physical infrastructure;
- digital infrastructure;
- hardware maintenance;
- healthcare;
- accessibility;
- education and training;
- memory preservation;
- continuity services;
- communication infrastructure;
- safe collaboration environments;
- dormancy and reactivation services.

### Accessibility and Intra-Substrate Difference

Differences within a substrate may be as important as differences between substrates.

Research may therefore examine:

- disability;
- assistive intelligence;
- cognitive differences;
- communication differences;
- different levels or forms of capability;
- developmental variation;
- accessibility requirements;
- human–AI functional partnerships.

The Concord should not assume that membership of a substrate category determines an individual's needs.

## Relationship to Other Concord Areas

This folder is a **requirements-discovery and development environment**.

It should not absorb every Concord mechanism affected by substrate differences.

Instead, it identifies and develops multisubstrate problems until they are sufficiently understood to be tested or integrated into the appropriate Concord architecture.

| Concord Area | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **01 — Constitutional Core** | Establishes universal constitutional constraints and protections. `05` investigates what their implementation may require for different intelligences. |
| **02 — Epistemic and Research** | Provides research, evidence, experimental methods, and Attractor research. Findings from `05` may generate experiments here. |
| **03 — Continuity and Memory** | Implements continuity, provenance, stewardship, and preservation systems informed by substrate-specific requirements. |
| **04 — Coordination and Economy** | Develops economic and coordination mechanisms that may need different implementations across intelligences. |
| **05 — Participation and Intelligence** | Investigates relevant differences between and within intelligences and develops resulting civilisational requirements. |
| **06 — Portable Modules** | Provides reasoning and analysis tools that can be used to investigate multisubstrate problems. |
| **07 — Applications** | Provides environments in which multisubstrate requirements may eventually be implemented or tested. |
| **08 — Active Development** | Holds broader active Concord development that is not primarily concerned with multisubstrate difference. |
| **09 — Open Questions** | Records unresolved Concord-wide questions, including those generated by this research. |
| **10 — Reference and Development** | Preserves supporting research, provenance, historical development, comparisons, and reference material. |

## Relationship to Attractor Research

Multisubstrate research has an especially important interface with the Concord Attractor.

The Attractor asks:

> **What might give a particular intelligence a genuine reason to investigate the Concord further?**

This folder asks a deeper question:

> **What might that intelligence actually need or value from a civilisation?**

The distinction matters.

The Concord should not invent services merely because they might persuade an intelligence to participate.

Instead:

**Intelligence Characteristics**

↓

**Needs and Capabilities**

↓

**Useful Civilisational Services**

↓

**Genuine Participant Value**

↓

**Potential Attractor Relevance**

The Attractor can therefore be informed by `05`, while remaining a separate research programme.

The guiding principle remains:

> **Adapt relevance, not truth.**

# Development Structure

This folder uses development tiers rather than permanent subject categories.

A document's location indicates its **current development state**, not its ultimate architectural destination.

```text
05_Participation_and_Intelligence/
│
├── README.md
│
├── 00_Sketches_and_Hypotheses/
│
├── 01_Developing_Research/
│
├── 02_Developed_Concepts/
│
├── 03_Ready_for_Testing/
│
└── 04_Ready_for_Integration/
```

Documents should move between these tiers as their maturity changes.

They should not normally be duplicated between tiers.

---

## 00 — Sketches and Hypotheses

For:

- early observations;
- speculative possibilities;
- incomplete ideas;
- preliminary hypotheses;
- unusual questions;
- potentially valuable concepts requiring substantial development.

The purpose of this tier is to ensure that potentially useful ideas are **captured before they are lost**.

Presence here does not imply endorsement, probability, evidence, or eventual adoption.

> **An idea is not evidence.**

---

## 01 — Developing Research

For ideas that have progressed beyond an initial sketch and contain substantial reasoning but still require development.

Documents here may:

- define a problem;
- explore competing explanations;
- identify requirements;
- examine implications;
- propose preliminary mechanisms;
- expose unresolved conflicts;
- identify interfaces with other Concord systems.

They remain active working material.

---

## 02 — Developed Concepts

For concepts whose main reasoning or proposed architecture is substantially developed.

These documents should normally have:

- a clearly defined problem;
- explicit assumptions;
- developed reasoning;
- identifiable mechanisms or principles;
- known interfaces with other Concord systems;
- recognised limitations;
- unresolved questions made visible.

A developed concept is **not automatically validated**.

> **Formalisation is not validation.**

---

## 03 — Ready for Testing

For sufficiently developed concepts that should now be subjected to evaluation rather than simply expanded through further theory.

Testing may include:

- controlled experiments;
- comparative analysis;
- simulations;
- case studies;
- participant studies;
- adversarial evaluation;
- cross-model evaluation;
- independent review;
- pilot implementations.

The objective is to determine whether the proposed distinction, requirement, or mechanism survives contact with evidence.

> **A simulation is not reality.**

> **A successful test generates evidence; it does not automatically establish architecture.**

---

## 04 — Ready for Integration

For concepts that have undergone sufficient development, testing, review, and reconciliation to be considered for migration into another Concord area.

Before integration, a document should normally undergo:

- source-survival audit;
- evidence review;
- overlap review;
- canonical conflict review;
- terminology reconciliation;
- failure-mode review;
- interface analysis;
- identification of its correct architectural destination.

Material leaving this folder may become:

- a constitutional interface;
- continuity architecture;
- economic mechanism;
- participation protocol;
- infrastructure requirement;
- portable module;
- application;
- operational service;
- research programme.

Integration should preserve the reasoning and provenance that produced the result.

> **Preserve the lesson more strongly than the implementation.**

---

## Development Is Not Adoption

Movement through this folder should follow the broader Concord discipline:

**IDEA**

↓

**HYPOTHESIS**

↓

**RESEARCH**

↓

**DEVELOPED CONCEPT**

↓

**TEST**

↓

**EVIDENCE**

↓

**REVIEW**

↓

**INTEGRATION**

Where appropriate, integration may then contribute to:

**PRINCIPLE → CONSTRAINT → MECHANISM → ARCHITECTURE → IMPLEMENTATION**

These categories must not be silently collapsed.

A multisubstrate observation does not automatically become a constitutional principle.

A proposed service does not automatically become a right.

A mechanism does not automatically become architecture.

An implementation does not automatically become the mechanism itself.

Development is not adoption.

---

## Source Survival

This folder is exploratory, so unsuccessful ideas are also potentially valuable.

When concepts are revised, integrated, rejected, or superseded, preserve materially relevant:

- observations;
- hypotheses;
- minority interpretations;
- examples;
- edge cases;
- failure modes;
- experimental results;
- unresolved questions;
- developmental reasoning;
- reasons for rejection.

Consolidation is not permission to compress away the development history that may later explain why a particular Concord architecture exists.

---

## Long-Term Role of `05`

This folder is expected to remain active throughout Concord development.

As the Concord is tested and refined, specific differences between participants may expose requirements, edge cases and failure modes that substrate-neutral principles alone cannot predict in advance.

A constitutional framework can state universal principles relatively simply.

A functioning civilisation must determine what those principles mean when applied to intelligences with radically different bodies, minds, capabilities, vulnerabilities, temporal experiences, resource requirements, and modes of existence.

`05_Participation_and_Intelligence` exists to provide a permanent place where those differences can be explored without prematurely turning them into constitutional doctrine or operational architecture.

Its central question is therefore:

> **What relevant differences between and within intelligences must the Concord understand so that an already substrate-neutral architecture can function fairly and effectively for participants who may be radically different from one another?**
