# Sketch Idea — Ethical Complexity, Social Scale and Comparative Small-Society Tests

**Status:** SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL
**Origin:** Unified Paths comparative backlog.

## Hypothesis
There may be a relationship between social/civilisational complexity and the number or complexity of explicit compound ethical rules required for stable interoperability.

The source note proposes Australian Aboriginal traditions and Amazonian Indigenous traditions as possible comparative cases following earlier African work.

## Critical Boundary
This must not presume that small-scale, Indigenous or non-industrial societies are ethically “less evolved.” Root count, textual explicitness, institutional complexity, oral transmission and civilisational advancement are different variables.

## Questions
- Does root-kernel size actually vary, or mainly compound expression?
- How do oral traditions affect extraction?
- Does social scale predict compound-rule complexity?
- Is there a cognitive saturation point where no individual can know the whole normative system?
- Could institutional/interface support solve such saturation?

## Possible Tests
Cross-cultural extraction using equivalent methods; control for corpus size, textual/oral preservation and researcher bias; compare root versus compound counts separately.
