# Sketch Idea — Participant-Serving Civilisation, Temporary Authority and Persistent Exit

**Status:** SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL
**Origin:** Unified Paths final comparison / CDT-adjacent reasoning.

## Observation
A possible higher-order pattern links consent, need, function, bounded authority and exit.

## Sketch
Explore the proposition that legitimate Concord authority should be justified by a bounded need and function, remain case/context specific, and dissolve when that justification ends.

Related proposition:
**The Concord exists for its participants; participants do not exist to be sacrificed merely to preserve the Concord as an institution.**

Persistent meaningful exit may act as a major anti-domination constraint.

## Questions
- Which authority legitimately persists independent of an individual case?
- How does emergency authority differ?
- What duties survive participant exit?
- When can protection of other participants constrain exit-related actions?
- Does “self-sacrificing civilisation” create continuity vulnerabilities?

## Risks
Over-compressing existing constitutional architecture; confusing consent with the sole source of every legitimate duty; making exit absolute where third-party consequences exist.

## Possible Tests
Map the proposition against canonical authority, emergency, judiciary, property/privacy, peaceful distance and fork architecture.

Formalised from `Concord moral authority.txt`; claims remain hypotheses pending canonical comparison.
