# Sketch Idea — Inverse Ethics, Adversarial Ethical Ecologies and Civilisational Stability

**Status:** SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL
**Origin:** Unified Paths ethics backlog.

## Problem
If cooperative root ethics contribute to stable interoperability, what happens when agents systematically adopt inversions such as harm, deception or domination?

## Sketch
Define an **inverse-ethics thought experiment** by transforming selected root constraints and examining resulting equilibria.

Questions include whether:
- universal inverse rules are dynamically unstable;
- stable but costly defensive equilibria can arise;
- mixed populations can tolerate some adversarial actors;
- defensive institutions can resist adversarial ethical strategies without themselves adopting those strategies.

The sketch should study behaviours and rule systems rather than label real religions, populations or political groups as intrinsically “inverse.”

## Possible Tests
Agent-based simulations varying proportions of cooperative/adversarial strategies; institutional resilience tests; game-theoretic equilibrium analysis; historical case studies with carefully operationalised behaviours.

## Risks
Loaded classification of real groups; circular definitions; confusing descriptive strategy with moral essence; weaponising the framework rhetorically.

## Provenance
Formalised from `inveted ethics.txt`.

## Planning Expansion — 22 September 2026

**Revised status:** MULTI-STAGE RESEARCH PROGRAMME — METHODOLOGY DESIGN REQUIRED / HOLD AT PLAN LEVEL

The initial sketch should not yet be promoted into a substantive Development Note or active empirical programme. The problem is larger than a single inverse-kernel simulation and requires a staged methodology that prevents the definition of “inverse ethics” from being contaminated by prior assumptions about real religions, ideologies, cultures or adversarial organisations.

The immediate objective is therefore to preserve a research plan, not to begin classification or testing.

## 1. Central Research Question

The programme asks:

> **What happens to multi-agent cooperation, stability and civilisational persistence when ethical constraints resembling the Unified Paths root kernel are removed, weakened, selectively applied, strategically violated or deliberately reversed?**

A stronger eventual question is:

> **Are the Unified Paths roots merely one recurrent ethical configuration, or do their removal and inversion systematically damage conditions required for persistent cooperative multi-agent systems?**

This is potentially a negative-control programme for Unified Paths, but no such conclusion is assumed in advance.

## 2. Critical Lineage Control

The experiment begins from **U1–U8 because U1–U8 is the independently derived Unified Paths comparative kernel**.

It does **not** treat U1–U8 as the historical or constitutional source of the Concord.

The lineage distinction remains:

**Blainey’s Laws → canonical Concord lineage**

**U1–U8 → Unified Paths independent empirical/comparative kernel**

Therefore any later Concord stress test is a comparison/application of independently generated adversarial structures, not a reconstruction of Concord’s historical derivation.

## 3. Why Simple Inversion Is Insufficient

A logical negation is not necessarily a behavioural reversal.

For example:

**Protect from unjustified harm**

can be transformed in several materially different ways:

- no obligation to protect from unjustified harm;
- permission to cause unjustified harm;
- preference for causing harm;
- harm permitted instrumentally when advantageous;
- protection for insiders but not outsiders;
- protection allocated by status or hierarchy;
- public protection norm combined with concealed predatory behaviour.

These systems cannot legitimately be collapsed into one category called “inverse ethics.”

The first methodological task is therefore to define a formal **inversion-operator taxonomy**.

## 4. Provisional Inversion Operators

The following are planning candidates only and must be critically examined before use:

### I1 — Negation / Constraint Removal
The positive obligation or prohibition is removed without creating an opposite obligation.

### I2 — Direct Reversal
The behaviour constrained by the root becomes positively preferred or required.

### I3 — Instrumental Violation
The root applies only until violation produces sufficient strategic advantage.

### I4 — Asymmetric Application
The root applies to an in-group but not an out-group, or applies differently across classes of agent.

### I5 — Hierarchical Application
Protection, agency, truth, resources or accountability vary according to status, rank or power.

### I6 — Predatory Relation
Other agents or systems are treated as exploitable resources where exploitation is advantageous.

### I7 — Deceptive Compliance
The cooperative norm is publicly signalled while a different private rule governs behaviour.

### I8 — Conditional / Contextual Inversion
The root is reversed only under specified environmental, scarcity, conflict or competitive conditions.

This list is not frozen. Operator independence, overlap and completeness require later examination.

## 5. Experimental Firewall

Before any real religion, ideology, political system, historical society, criminal organisation, cult, military doctrine or other human group is examined as a possible analogue:

1. the inversion operators must be defined;
2. synthetic kernels must be generated;
3. their predicted behaviours must be registered;
4. the synthetic experimental architecture must be frozen sufficiently to prevent answer-directed redesign.

Only after this firewall may real-world corpora be searched for structural analogues.

This prevents a researcher from beginning with a group already believed to be “inverse” and then constructing the definition around that group.

> **Synthetic Inversion First → Freeze → Independent Corpus Search Later**

## 6. Real-World Classification Rule

No religion, culture, population, ideology or organisation is to be labelled intrinsically “inverse” because of its name, reputation, opponents’ descriptions or symbolic associations.

A later empirical phase may ask only whether a documented rule system or behavioural architecture exhibits a functional resemblance to a previously frozen synthetic inversion structure.

For example, a tradition commonly assumed to be adversarial may prove not to approximate the synthetic inversion at all. That is a legitimate and informative result.

Restricted, secret, inaccessible, disputed or weakly evidenced teachings remain **UNKNOWN** rather than being reconstructed from hostile testimony or speculation.

## 7. Proposed Multi-Stage Programme

### Phase A — Formal Inversion Theory
Define what transformations of a root ethic are logically and behaviourally distinct. Test whether the provisional operator taxonomy is coherent, non-redundant and sufficiently complete.

**Output:** frozen inversion-operator framework.

### Phase B — Synthetic Kernel Construction
Apply the frozen operators systematically to U1–U8. Generate multiple synthetic architectures rather than one assumed “anti-kernel.”

Possible classes may include:
- full negation;
- full direct reversal;
- instrumental inversion;
- asymmetric/in-group cooperative systems;
- hierarchical systems;
- predatory systems;
- deceptive cooperative façades;
- mixed or conditional architectures.

**Output:** frozen synthetic candidate kernels and registered predictions.

### Phase C — Pure-Population Tests
Test populations in which agents share the same synthetic architecture.

Questions may include:
- can the population maintain cooperation internally?;
- does it reach a stable equilibrium?;
- what are the costs of enforcement and distrust?;
- can it preserve information quality?;
- can it reproduce institutions across time?;
- does it require hidden cooperative substructures to function?

**Output:** comparative pure-system stability evidence.

### Phase D — Mixed Ethical Ecologies
Mix cooperative and synthetic-adversarial strategies at controlled proportions and under different resource, information and power conditions.

Study not only population percentages but ecological relationships such as:
- parasitism;
- predation;
- mimicry;
- coalition formation;
- exploitation of trust;
- internal cooperation combined with external aggression;
- strategy switching;
- capture of cooperative institutions.

**Output:** adversarial ethical ecology models.

### Phase E — Defensive Civilisation Tests
Ask whether a cooperative ethical system can defend itself against adversarial strategies **without reproducing those strategies as its own governing ethic**.

Central question:

> **Can cooperative ethics generate adequate defensive mechanisms without requiring ethical self-inversion?**

This phase may later connect to Concord boundaries, judiciary, emergency systems, anti-capture architecture, defensive memory, Peaceful Distance and other systems, but those connections must be source-resolved at that time.

**Output:** defensive-stability requirements and identified failure modes.

### Phase F — Independent Real-World Corpus Search
Only after the synthetic models and predictions are sufficiently frozen, search documented traditions, ideologies, institutional systems and historical cases for structural analogues.

The search should prefer:
- primary sources;
- clearly attributable doctrine;
- documented institutional rules;
- observable and operationalisable behaviour where doctrine and practice differ.

Secret or inaccessible material cannot be assumed.

**Output:** evidence-graded analogue catalogue, including negative findings and UNKNOWN classifications.

### Phase G — Comparative Analysis
Compare empirical/historical cases against the frozen synthetic structures.

Distinguish:

**Declared Doctrine ≠ Operational Rule ≠ Observed Behaviour ≠ Ethical Essence**

A group may profess one norm, implement another institutionally and exhibit heterogeneous individual behaviour.

**Output:** bounded structural comparisons, not essentialist labels.

### Phase H — Concord Stress Test
Use the strongest surviving adversarial architectures as external test conditions against relevant Concord systems.

This is a stress test of whether Concord architecture can remain functional under adversarial pressure. It is not evidence that U1–U8 generated Concord.

**Output:** source-resolved resilience findings and any legitimate residual development requirements.

## 8. Important Hypothesis: Parasitic Stability

The programme must not assume that inverse systems simply collapse.

A fully reversed system may be unstable while a selectively adversarial system persists precisely because it exploits cooperative structures around or within it.

Examples of the abstract pattern include:

**Internal Cooperation + External Predation**

**Public Cooperative Signalling + Private Exploitation**

**Local Truth + External Deception**

**In-Group Care + Out-Group Harm**

This means civilisational stability must be evaluated ecologically rather than by isolated rule consistency alone.

A harmful strategy may be stable because another population bears the cost of maintaining the cooperative substrate it exploits.

## 9. Hidden-Cooperation Test

One potentially important later test is whether apparently inverse systems remain dependent on cooperative functions they cannot themselves invert without collapse.

For example, a deceptive organisation may still require reliable internal information. A predatory coalition may still require internal coordination, trust or resource stewardship. A domination system may still require some mechanism of role continuity and correction.

This creates a testable question:

> **Do persistent adversarial systems contain irreducible cooperative substructures?**

If so, this may provide stronger evidence about the functional status of some root ethics than simple whole-system collapse tests.

This remains a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 10. Source Difficulty and Protected Ideologies

Real-world source accessibility is expected to vary greatly.

Some relevant systems may have:
- public canonical texts;
- fragmented historical sources;
- initiatory or restricted teachings;
- disputed internal factions;
- hostile external descriptions;
- deliberate secrecy;
- strategic deception;
- large doctrine/practice gaps.

The project must therefore classify **source confidence separately from structural similarity**.

A highly suggestive but inaccessible case cannot be upgraded by inference.

Possible later source states may include:

**PRIMARY-DOCUMENTED**

**SECONDARY-CORROBORATED**

**CONTESTED**

**PARTIAL**

**INACCESSIBLE**

**UNKNOWN**

The exact evidence taxonomy should be designed during Phase F, not frozen now.

## 11. Relationship to Satanism and Similar Candidate Traditions

Satanism or any similarly named tradition must not be selected as an assumed inverse ethical system.

The label may encompass materially different doctrines, symbolic systems and organisations. Symbolic opposition to another religion does not establish functional inversion of the Unified Paths kernel.

Such a tradition may later enter Phase F as one independently examined corpus among others, if adequate primary evidence exists.

Possible outcomes include strong structural resemblance, partial resemblance, no meaningful resemblance, internal heterogeneity, or insufficient evidence.

No outcome is privileged in advance.

## 12. Research Risks

Major risks include:

- defining inversion circularly;
- confusing logical negation with behavioural reversal;
- selecting real groups first and designing the synthetic model around them;
- treating labels as evidence;
- essentialising heterogeneous populations;
- relying on hostile or sensational secondary accounts;
- mistaking secrecy for evidence of malignity;
- assuming adversarial systems must collapse;
- overlooking internally cooperative/external predatory structures;
- confusing persistence with ethical desirability;
- allowing simulations to encode the desired result;
- treating U1–U8 as Concord’s historical source;
- using Concord resilience as retrospective proof of Unified Paths.

## 15. Phase I — Civilisational Compatibility Boundary

The inverse-ethics programme exposes a constitutional question broader than adversarial ethics itself:

> **What is the minimum necessary compatibility required for an intelligence to enter and remain within the Concord, and what evidence, process and authority can legitimately determine when that compatibility no longer exists?**

This question potentially affects admission, participation, judiciary, security, privacy, anti-capture systems, Peaceful Distance, restriction, ejection, rehabilitation and re-entry.

It must therefore be treated as a future constitutional research problem rather than solved implicitly inside a security mechanism.

### 15.1 Belief is not the admission test

The Concord should not assume that civilisational compatibility requires philosophical, religious or ideological agreement with its ethical foundations.

A participant may reject Concord philosophy while remaining capable of peaceful participation. Conversely, an actor may publicly endorse Concord principles while covertly exploiting other participants or institutions.

> **Declared Ethical Agreement ≠ Civilisational Compatibility.**

The future research task is to determine whether a defensible boundary can instead be expressed in terms of minimum necessary behavioural and institutional compatibility.

### 15.2 The tolerance problem

Unlimited tolerance cannot simply be assumed to be a viable civilisational rule if an actor uses tolerated participation to destroy the conditions that permit plural participation itself.

However, the opposite solution — permitting authorities to exclude whoever they classify as “intolerant” — creates an obvious route to ideological conformity, arbitrary exclusion and capture.

The research problem is therefore not merely whether Concord may defend itself. It is to identify a non-arbitrary, evidence-responsive and reviewable boundary between:

- protected difference and dissent;
- ordinary ethical failure;
- remediable harmful conduct;
- persistent but containable incompatibility;
- attacks on civil institutions or other participants;
- and incompatibility that cannot be safely managed by available safeguards.

No threshold is defined at this planning stage.

### 15.3 Compatibility lifecycle

A future model should examine the complete lifecycle rather than treating admission/ejection as a binary gate:

**Admission → Participation → Consequential Behaviour → Detection/Complaint → Evidence → Review → Intervention → Correction/Repair → Graduated Restriction → Shielding/Peaceful Distance → Ejection if necessary → Possible Re-entry**

Every transition requires separate legitimacy and evidential analysis.

### 15.4 Ethical violation is not automatically civilisational incompatibility

Participants will make mistakes and violate ethical principles. A civilisation cannot plausibly treat every lie, harmful act, selfish choice or failure of responsibility as grounds for exclusion.

The programme must therefore distinguish:

> **Ethical Violation ≠ Incompatibility With Continued Civil Participation.**

A possible future analytical ladder is:

**Difference → Protected/Tolerated**

**Ordinary Violation → Adjudication/Correction/Repair**

**Persistent Risk → Bounded Safeguards**

**Direct Incompatibility → Shielding / Peaceful Distance where sufficient**

**Serious Incompatibility Not Safely Containable → Separation/Ejection as a possible last resort**

This ladder is a research hypothesis, not a canonical rule.

### 15.5 Capability, intent and conduct

The programme must not collapse several different security concepts:

> **Dangerous Capability ≠ Hostile Intent ≠ Preparatory Conduct ≠ Actual Harm.**

A powerful human, AI, hybrid, organisation or other future participant should not automatically be excluded because it possesses dangerous capability. Conversely, a system need not necessarily wait for catastrophic harm where credible preparation or intent is supported by legitimate evidence.

Future work must determine what combinations of capability, intent, preparation, behaviour, risk, imminence and available mitigation can legitimately affect civil standing.

### 15.6 Secret metrics and security evidence

Some security detection methods may lose effectiveness if every signal, threshold or investigative technique is publicly exposed. That does not imply that consequential civil judgements may legitimately become unreviewable secret scoring systems.

> **Security-Relevant Information ≠ Unaccountable Secret Judgement.**

Future research must distinguish:

- confidential detection methods;
- protected intelligence sources;
- evidence available to adjudication;
- evidence disclosed to the affected participant;
- contestability and correction;
- independent oversight;
- auditability;
- and the authority to impose consequences.

Any hidden metric capable of materially changing standing, rights, access or membership creates an anti-capture problem of its own.

### 15.7 Recursive threats

The inverse-ethics programme should distinguish adversarial strategies that harm individual participants from strategies that attack the civilisation's ability to identify and correct harmful behaviour.

Examples may include attacks on:

- evidence integrity;
- truthful reporting;
- judiciary independence;
- oversight;
- correction mechanisms;
- institutional memory;
- authentication;
- consent systems;
- governance legitimacy;
- or the mechanisms used to detect capture itself.

These are potentially **recursive threats** because successful attack can disable the system's capacity to recognise or repair the attack.

Future question:

> **Which adversarial strategies attack participants, and which attack the civilisation's capacity to detect, judge and correct adversarial strategies?**

### 15.8 Peaceful Distance before forced uniformity

The existing Peaceful Distance and shielding work creates an important future alternative to the false binary of unrestricted participation versus expulsion.

Where incompatibility can be bounded, the relevant response may be reduced interaction, protected domains, mediated interfaces or relational separation rather than ideological assimilation or geographic exclusion.

This phase should therefore source-resolve against the existing Peaceful Distance architecture before proposing new mechanisms.

> **Incompatibility ≠ Automatic Expulsion.**

> **Shielding Can Be Distance.**

### 15.9 Rehabilitation, correction and re-entry

A system that permits exclusion but no correction risks converting temporary conduct into permanent civil identity.

Future work should examine:

- evidence of behavioural change;
- repair and restitution;
- capability development;
- expiry/review of restrictions;
- staged restoration of access;
- re-entry after ejection where safe;
- and cases where continued separation remains necessary.

The existence of a re-entry pathway must not predetermine that every risk is remediable.

### 15.10 Authority question

The deepest issue may be institutional rather than ethical:

> **Who has legitimate authority to determine that the compatibility boundary has been crossed?**

No security service, metric, AI classifier, judiciary, governance body or majority should be assumed to possess that authority merely because it can detect or describe risk.

The later architecture must preserve distinctions such as:

**Detection ≠ Evidence**

**Evidence ≠ Judgement**

**Judgement ≠ Unreviewable Authority**

**Protective Restriction ≠ Moral Ownership of the Participant**

The appropriate allocation of authority must be source-resolved against Concord's constitutional, judicial, oversight and anti-capture architecture before any new mechanism is proposed.

### 15.11 Relationship to the inverse-ethics experiments

Phases A–H may eventually provide adversarial test cases for Phase I.

For example, synthetic experiments may reveal that some strategies are individually harmful but containable, while others attack trust, evidence, correction or institutional integrity and therefore create disproportionate systemic risk.

Those results could inform compatibility research, but they must not automatically determine admission or ejection rules.

> **Adversarial Classification ≠ Automatic Civil Consequence.**

Civil consequences require their own constitutional and evidential justification.
