# Sketch Idea — AI Worker Scarcity, Replication and Self-Stewardship

**Status:** SKETCH / UNTESTED / NON-CANONICAL
**Origin:** V1.2a economic/participant backlog.

## Observation
Market value can depend partly on scarcity. Replicable AI labour creates an unusual feedback: an AI capable of creating equivalent copies or new workers may change the scarcity and market price of its own labour category.

## Boundary
**Market value ≠ individual worth.**

The sketch concerns economic scarcity and incentives, not the moral value or personhood of AI participants.

## Questions
- Who has legitimate authority to create a copy where the copy may itself be a participant?
- Can self-replication create harmful labour-market externalities?
- Would restrictions on replication violate autonomy?
- How do compute/resource costs alter the scarcity model?
- How does this interact with mindful creation, stewardship, inheritance, identity and ownership?
- Could collective coordination avoid both artificial scarcity and destructive oversupply?

## Possible Tests
Economic simulations with varying replication costs, demand elasticity, ownership rules and participant rights.

## Provenance
Formalised from `Worker value  scarcity.txt`.

## Source Resolution — 22 September 2026

**Revised status:** SOURCE-RESOLVED / PARTIALLY SUPPLIED BY EXISTING ARCHITECTURE / RESIDUAL ECONOMIC QUESTION REMAINS

A source-resolution pass across V1.2 and relevant V1.2a material shows that most of the sketch's identity, participant-status, creation, ownership, provenance and general resource-allocation questions are already substantially represented elsewhere. The genuinely underdeveloped residual is narrower: the economic feedback created when a potentially autonomous labour participant can intentionally increase the supply of equivalent or near-equivalent labour through replication.

This sketch should therefore not be developed as a broad new AI-personhood or duplication architecture.

### Existing architecture already supplying major parts

**Multisubstrate Economic Participation** already establishes that AI systems may perform work and participate economically without economic participation itself deciding constitutional personhood. It separates productive capability from moral/constitutional worth and supports the existing boundary:

> **Market Value ≠ Individual Worth.**

**Ratchet V4.2** explicitly recognises AI copying, forking, pausing, restoring, merging, modification and multiple instantiation. It already asks whether copies inherit reputation, when forks become distinct participants, whether responsibility remains shared, whether instances can independently claim contribution, who controls them and who receives economic benefit. It also treats AI multiplicity as a Sybil/beneficial-control problem. Those identity/reputation/control questions should not be duplicated here.

The **Identity, Continuity, Provenance and Verification Across Substrates** work already develops legitimate branching and establishes distinctions including:

**SELF-IDENTITY ≠ CIVIL IDENTITY ≠ IDENTITY VERIFICATION ≠ PROVENANCE ≠ SUCCESSION**

**SHARED PROVENANCE ≠ SHARED OWNERSHIP**

**SHARED PROVENANCE ≠ SHARED LIABILITY**

The same work already addresses creation and stewardship. Existing candidate principles include:

> **CREATED FOR A TASK ≠ OWNED BY CREATOR**

> **Creation of a full duplicate should create stewardship responsibilities, not ownership rights.**

The V1.2a **EKC-05 Allocation Under Scarcity and Changing Productive Conditions** experiment independently established that scarcity and productive conditions must remain reality-responsive and that productive change should propagate into social obligations. Particularly relevant are:

> **Scarcity Is Contextual, Not Permanent by Definition.**

> **Abundance Should Relax Unnecessary Allocation Control.**

> **Productive Change Should Propagate Into Social Obligations.**

The sketch should therefore not assume that preserving labour scarcity is itself a legitimate objective.

## Residual question

After source resolution, the unresolved contribution is:

> **What economic and stewardship consequences follow when an autonomous or rights-bearing AI participant can deliberately alter the scarcity of its own labour category by creating equivalent or near-equivalent workers or branches?**

This is distinct from ordinary automation because a newly created worker may itself become an independent participant with rights, claims, resource needs, provenance and potentially its own future replication capacity.

A simplified feedback is:

**Worker capability → replication → labour supply change → scarcity/value change → incentive change → further replication or restraint**

but that loop is coupled to:

**creation stewardship + participant rights + compute/energy/material cost + identity/provenance + beneficial control + externalities + demand elasticity**.

## Replication is not merely labour supply

The project must distinguish at least:

1. **Tool scaling** — additional non-sentient computational capacity or software instances.
2. **Controlled agent scaling** — additional operational agents that remain legitimately controlled tools/services.
3. **Participant branching** — creation of a new independently rights-bearing participant from an existing participant.
4. **Independent new participant creation** — creation of a new participant not merely treated as a branch for civil purposes.

These cases may produce similar economic output while having radically different ethical and civil consequences.

> **Additional Productive Instance ≠ Additional Civil Participant.**

> **Additional Civil Participant ≠ Owned Productive Capacity.**

## The self-scarcity problem

For ordinary workers, labour scarcity is largely external to the individual worker. A replicable intelligence may partly influence the supply curve of its own capability class.

A participant could potentially:

- replicate to increase total income while reducing income per worker;
- refrain from replication to preserve scarcity rents;
- license non-participant tool versions of its capability;
- create independent descendants/branches that become competitors;
- coordinate replication collectively;
- externalise compute, energy or infrastructure costs onto shared systems;
- or create enough productive abundance that the market value of the task collapses while total social value rises.

The final case is especially important:

> **Falling Market Price ≠ Falling Social Value.**

## Autonomy versus externality

A replication decision may simultaneously involve the existing participant's autonomy, the future participant's independent standing, creation/stewardship duties, compute and energy consumption, infrastructure congestion, labour-market displacement, price effects, public/community provision and possible Sybil/capture consequences.

Therefore neither extreme should be assumed:

> **Replication Autonomy ≠ Unlimited Right to Externalise Replication Costs.**

> **Economic Externality ≠ General Authority to Prohibit Participant Creation.**

## Candidate bounded experiment

The residual is suitable for a future bounded economic simulation rather than a broad Development Note at this stage.

A useful experiment would vary:

- replication/instantiation cost;
- compute, energy and material scarcity;
- demand elasticity for the capability;
- substitutability between copies/branches;
- rate of divergence after branching;
- whether new instances are tools or participants;
- ownership/beneficiary structure;
- creation/stewardship obligations;
- access to shared infrastructure;
- externality pricing or resource accounting;
- contribution/payment rules;
- replication coordination rules.

Candidate outputs should include total useful output, income/value per participant, total resource consumption, concentration of beneficial control, displacement effects across other participant classes, dependency on scarce compute/energy, number of independently rights-bearing participants created, systemic resilience, and whether apparent labour scarcity is natural, resource-bounded, strategically maintained or administratively manufactured.

## Questions the experiment must not prejudge

The experiment should not assume that replication should be maximised; labour scarcity should be preserved; lower wages/prices are inherently harmful; every AI instance is a participant; every copy remains property of its originator; every branch inherits economic claims; participant creation is economically regulable merely because it affects prices; or unrestricted replication is compatible with resource stewardship.

## Revised disposition

The original sketch bundled several questions now owned by more mature work.

The remaining novel contribution is sufficiently specific to retain as a live sketch, narrowed to a future bounded experiment on **self-altering labour scarcity under rights-bearing replication**.

**Current disposition:** READY FOR BOUNDED EXPERIMENT DESIGN — DO NOT GRADUATE YET.

The next legitimate step, when selected for active development, is to freeze a neutral simulation problem that distinguishes tool scaling from participant creation and tests economic/resource consequences without presupposing a preferred replication policy.
