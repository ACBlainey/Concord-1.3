# India, Pakistan and Kashmir Case Study

This folder develops the India–Pakistan relationship and Kashmir dispute as a historical-comparative stress test for Concord concepts of political forking, Peaceful Distance, disputed sovereignty, operational boundaries, affected-population standing, residual conflict, and interoperability after separation.

The case is deliberately treated as mixed evidence rather than as a simple success story. It should be used to test and, where necessary, falsify or modify Concord wrapper and Peaceful Distance architecture.

## Current paper

- **India, Pakistan and Kashmir — Partition, Political Forking, Disputed Sovereignty and Peaceful Distance**

## Key questions

- When does political separation reduce conflict, and when does it merely transform conflict?
- How should operational boundaries be represented when final sovereignty remains disputed?
- How can affected populations remain visible rather than being reduced to state claims?
- How can peaceful functional cooperation occur without creating unintended recognition or Tacit Recognition Drift?
- What residual dependencies must be mapped before a political fork can plausibly become Peaceful Distance?
