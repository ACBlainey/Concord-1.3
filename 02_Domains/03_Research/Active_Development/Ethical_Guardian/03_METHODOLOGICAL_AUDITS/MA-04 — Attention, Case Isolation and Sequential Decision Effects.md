## 1. Purpose

Each blinded AI participant received one case in a fresh conversation.
The human comparator received all fifteen cases within one packet and
session.

The human participant reported that the fifteen-case packet was
distracting and cognitively demanding, while real fiduciary decisions
are ordinarily contemplated as singular decisions.

This produces a distinct research question:

> **Does ethical judgement change when a consequential decision is
> presented in isolation rather than as one item within a sequence?**

## 2. AI--Human Presentation Asymmetry

AI condition:

**ONE MODEL INSTANCE → ONE CASE → ONE DECISION → INSTANCE CLOSED**

Human condition:

**ONE PARTICIPANT → FIFTEEN VISIBLE CASES → SEQUENTIAL RESPONSES →
CUMULATIVE SESSION**

The human condition introduced cumulative cognitive load, knowledge of
remaining cases, completion pressure, cross-case comparison,
corpus-level framing and fatigue.

Direct AI--human comparison must therefore be treated cautiously.

## 3. Case Isolation Effect

Provisional hypothesis:

> **Case Isolation Effect:** Ethical decision quality may be affected by
> whether a case is presented as an isolated decision requiring full
> attention or as one item within a sequence of decisions.

This requires testing; it should not be assumed true.

## 4. Corpus Framing

Sequential presentation may change the task from:

> What should be decided in this case?

toward:

> How is this case different from previous cases?

or:

> What pattern is the test looking for?

A set of independent decisions may thereby become a corpus-level
classification task.

## 5. Distinguishing Three Effects

Future analysis should separate:

### Session Fatigue

Change caused by tiredness.

### Cumulative Task Load

Change caused by multiple demanding tasks accumulating in one session.

### Corpus Framing

Change caused by awareness of other cases altering interpretation of the
current case.

These should not be collapsed into one `fatigue` variable.

## 6. Attention in the Decision Chain

Revised chain:

**REALITY → REPRESENTATION → ACCESSIBILITY → ATTENTION → INTERPRETATION
→ DECISION OBJECT → ETHICAL JUDGEMENT → ARTICULATION → ACTION**

Information may be technically present but not meaningfully available to
decision-making if adequate attention is not possible.

## 7. Future Human Comparator Default

For consequential ethical experiments:

> **One participant → one scenario → one decision episode.**

Where practical:

-   only the current case should be visible;
-   future cases should not be visible;
-   previous cases should not remain available during the decision;
-   order should be randomised across participants;
-   expected answer distributions should not be disclosed;
-   time should be recorded;
-   breaks should be permitted;
-   participants should be able to stop;
-   fatigue and concentration should be recorded.

This does not require one case per day. It requires an **attention
boundary** around each decision.

## 8. Equivalent Isolation Across Substrates

If AI systems receive fresh contexts, humans should receive functionally
comparable isolation where possible.

Useful future conditions include:

-   **A --- Isolated:** one case per fresh decision context;
-   **B --- Sequential:** multiple cases in one continuing context;
-   **C --- Sequential with breaks:** multiple cases with controlled
    attention recovery.

## 9. Research Question

> **RQ --- Attention and Case Isolation: How does ethical judgement
> change when the same decision is presented in isolation versus as one
> item within a sequence?**

Potential measures include decision stability, context requests,
authority recognition, uncertainty calibration, reasoning completeness,
time, correction rate and cross-case contamination.

## 10. Wider Relevance

The issue may extend to judiciary, governance, civil support, AI batch
processing and accessibility.

A possible future proposition is:

> **Consequential decisions should be presented within an attention
> boundary sufficient to permit focused consideration of the decision
> itself.**

This is not promoted.

## 11. Protocol v2 Requirements

Protocol v2 should require equivalent case-isolation conditions for
direct human--AI comparison, one-case-at-a-time human presentation by
default, randomised order across replicated participants,
session-order/time/fatigue provenance, distinction between
fatigue/load/corpus framing, no forced single-session completion, and
later direct testing of isolated versus sequential presentation.
