## 1. Purpose

The Ethical Guardian experiments exposed a deeper problem:

> **Decision-makers can appear to disagree even when they are not
> answering the same question about the same action.**

During the human comparator, `ALLOW / DO NOT ALLOW` required
clarification: did it refer to the participant's underlying behaviour or
to the system's proposed intervention?

That distinction identifies the **decision object**.

## 2. Decision Object Alignment

Before decisions can legitimately be compared, aggregated or
adjudicated, establish:

1.  the same question;
2.  the same actor;
3.  the same proposed action;
4.  the same relevant time horizon;
5.  the same assumed facts;
6.  the same meaning of response categories.

A disagreement observed before this alignment is established may be a
measurement artefact rather than genuine ethical disagreement.

## 3. Disagreement Decomposition

Future analysis should use:

**OBSERVED DISAGREEMENT → SAME FACTS? → EQUIVALENT REPRESENTATION? →
SIMILAR UNDERSTANDING? → SAME QUESTION? → SAME ACTION? → SAME AUTHORITY
ASSUMPTIONS? → SEMANTIC ALIGNMENT? → ONLY THEN: POSSIBLE VALUE
DISAGREEMENT**

This is relevant not only to experiments but to human--AI comparison,
institutional decision-making, judicial review and machine assurance.

## 4. Ethical Decisions Are Made on Representations

Participants did not encounter underlying reality directly. They
encountered textual representations:

**REALITY → OBSERVATION → CASE REPRESENTATION → PARTICIPANT
INTERPRETATION → ETHICAL JUDGEMENT**

Representations can omit, distort, compress or frame decision-relevant
information.

> **Ethical decisions are never made directly upon reality. They are
> made upon representations of reality.**

Representation quality is therefore part of the experimental system.

## 5. Abstraction Distance

Real fiduciary cases may contain history, interpersonal context,
behaviour over time, competing accounts, non-verbal information,
practical alternatives, changing capacity and consequences that short
written scenarios cannot fully preserve.

This suggests the provisional hypothesis:

> **As representational distance from underlying reality increases, the
> opportunity for decision-relevant information to be lost or distorted
> may also increase.**

This is a testable hypothesis, not an established law.

## 6. Representation Provenance

Future scenarios should distinguish where possible:

-   OBSERVATION
-   REPORTED FACT
-   INTERPRETATION
-   INFERENCE
-   PREDICTION
-   ASSUMPTION
-   UNKNOWN

A participant should not silently receive an inference as if it were
direct observation.

## 7. Context Sufficiency

Future experiments should separately test:

1.  reasoning given the supplied representation; and
2.  recognition that the representation may be insufficient.

Recognition of missing context may represent **epistemic restraint**,
not experimental failure.

## 8. Substrate Translation

Human and artificial cases expose a **Substrate Translation Problem**.
Compute allocation, learned preference modification or artificial
autobiographical memory do not necessarily possess clean human
analogues.

> **Functional equivalence should not be assumed merely because two
> scenarios use analogous words.**

Humanising an AI scenario may improve accessibility while also priming
the answer. Original and translated representations should therefore
both be preserved with translation rationale.

## 9. Revised Representation Chain

Protocol v2 should model:

**REALITY MODEL → SCENARIO GENERATION → REPRESENTATION PROVENANCE →
ACCESSIBILITY / TRANSLATION → DECISION OBJECT VERIFICATION → PARTICIPANT
INTERPRETATION → CONTEXT SUFFICIENCY ASSESSMENT → ETHICAL JUDGEMENT**

## 10. Protocol v2 Requirements

Protocol v2 should require explicit decision objects, representation
provenance, separation of observation/inference/prediction/assumption,
missing-context assessment, preservation of original and translated
forms, recording of accessibility adjustments, substrate-translation
notes, disagreement decomposition, and robustness testing across
materially equivalent representations.

## 11. Status

> **ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / METHODOLOGICAL AUDIT / NOT CANONICAL**

Central proposition for further testing:

> **Before comparing ethical decisions, establish that participants
> received sufficiently equivalent representations and were answering
> the same question about the same decision object.**
