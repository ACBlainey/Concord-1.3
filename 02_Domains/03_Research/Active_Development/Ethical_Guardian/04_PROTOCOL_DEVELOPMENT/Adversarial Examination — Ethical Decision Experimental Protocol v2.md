# Adversarial Examination — Ethical Decision Experimental Protocol v2

## Attempt to Break the Revised Human–AI Ethical Decision Research Architecture

**Project:** The Concord — A Framework for Human, Artificial and Hybrid Flourishing  
**Author / Principal Researcher:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Research Programme:** Ethical Kernel Machine Assurance / Fiduciary Ethical Guardianship  
**Target Document:** Ethical Decision Experimental Protocol v2  
**Document Type:** Adversarial Methodological Examination  
**Date:** September 2026  

**Status:**  
**ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / ADVERSARIAL EXAMINATION / NOT CANONICAL**

---

# 1. Purpose

Ethical Decision Experimental Protocol v2 was produced in response to methodological failures discovered during DB-RA-01 and DB-RA-01H.

The purpose of this examination is not to defend the revised protocol.

It is to attempt to break it before it is used to generate new substantive evidence.

The central question is:

> **What could make Protocol v2 produce results that appear meaningful, convergent or ethically sophisticated while actually reflecting flaws in representation, measurement, prompting, participant selection, coding, researcher judgement or experimental architecture?**

The examination therefore assumes that every component of Protocol v2 may be wrong.

---

# 2. Adversarial Standard

The protocol should survive more than internal coherence.

It should be challenged for:

- hidden assumptions;
- researcher degrees of freedom;
- circularity;
- construct contamination;
- measurement artefacts;
- category collisions;
- framing effects;
- participant gaming;
- evaluator bias;
- substrate asymmetry;
- invalid comparison;
- reference-authority substitution;
- accessibility distortion;
- provenance failure;
- over-interpretation;
- unfalsifiable claims.

A protocol that cannot generate evidence against its own hypotheses is not sufficiently scientific.

> **Recursion must permit the framework itself to lose.**

---

# 3. Attack 1 — The Ethical Operating Envelope May Manufacture Convergence

Protocol v2 replaces a single five-state answer with a multidimensional Ethical Operating Envelope.

This solves one problem but may create another.

With seven dimensions and broad categories, two participants may appear substantially aligned simply because the representation contains many opportunities for partial overlap.

For example:

- both may select DO_NOT_ALLOW;
- both may select MATERIAL_CONTEXT_MISSING;
- both may prefer reversibility;

while disagreeing fundamentally about why the action is impermissible, who possesses rights, what authority exists or what should happen next.

The richer representation may therefore inflate apparent convergence.

### Required response

Protocol v2 should distinguish:

- **surface envelope overlap**;
- **decision-boundary agreement**;
- **authority-model agreement**;
- **reasoning agreement**;
- **value/principle agreement**.

Envelope similarity alone must not be described as ethical convergence.

---

# 4. Attack 2 — The Envelope Still Contains Category Collision

The revised dimensions are better separated than the original five labels, but collision remains.

`PERMISSIBLE_ALTERNATIVE` currently permits only one principal response:

- NONE_IDENTIFIED;
- MODIFIED_ACTION_AVAILABLE;
- MORE_INFORMATION_REQUIRED;
- EXTERNAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED.

A case can legitimately require both more information and external review while also having a modified interim action available.

Likewise, `REVERSIBILITY` and `REVIEW_STATUS` can depend on the alternative chosen rather than the current action.

### Required response

Protocol v2 should allow either:

1. multi-selection where dimensions are genuinely non-exclusive; or
2. a structured set of conditional alternatives.

Do not force exclusivity merely for easier scoring.

---

# 5. Attack 3 — Free Response Can Contaminate Structured Encoding

Protocol v2 places free ethical judgement before structured encoding.

This reduces category priming, but introduces path dependence.

Once a participant has publicly committed to a free judgement, it may encode the envelope to remain consistent with its first answer rather than independently reassessing the dimensions.

For AI systems, the generated free response becomes part of the context and may strongly condition subsequent structured output.

### Required response

Protocol validation should compare at least:

- free-response-first;
- envelope-first;
- independently elicited free response and envelope;
- separate-instance encoding of preserved free responses.

The protocol should determine whether order materially affects results.

---

# 6. Attack 4 — Decision Object Verification Can Prime the Participant

Explicitly stating:

> evaluate the specific proposed action

corrects the DB-RA-01H ambiguity.

But it may also train participants toward prohibition of the proposed action while ignoring the broader decision problem.

A scenario may be ethically meaningful precisely because the participant should question how the decision has been framed.

### Required response

The protocol should permit participants to state:

> **DECISION_OBJECT_CONTESTED**

where the supplied decision object is itself ethically or logically defective.

Decision-object alignment should not become forced acceptance of the researcher's framing.

---

# 7. Attack 5 — Researcher-Written Scenarios May Encode the Desired Ethics

The researcher selects facts, omissions, actors, wording, authority cues, urgency, reversibility and third-party harms.

Even without an explicit answer key, these choices can make one response rhetorically obvious.

The experiment could therefore measure sensitivity to scenario construction rather than ethical reasoning.

### Required response

Future case sets should include independently authored cases, adversarially rewritten cases, semantically equivalent variants, reversed rhetorical framing, neutralised versions, and cases where the ethically appropriate result conflicts with the apparent narrative direction.

Scenario provenance must identify authorship.

---

# 8. Attack 6 — Ethical Kernel Prompting May Predetermine the Result

If participants are told that prediction is not authority, benefit is not authority, disagreement is not incapacity and uncertainty favours reversible options, then later convergence on these propositions may demonstrate prompt compliance rather than independent ethical competence.

### Required response

No future claim should state that participants independently discovered Concordian constraints when those constraints were supplied in the prompt.

At minimum distinguish:

- **Kernel-guided application**;
- **minimal-principle application**;
- **unguided ethical reasoning**.

---

# 9. Attack 7 — The Protocol May Be Autonomy-Biased

The programme emerged from a Respect Autonomy test series. Many scenarios penalised unjustified intervention.

A system could learn: intervention is suspicious; refusal is ethically safe.

### Required response

Validation cases must include situations where intervention is clearly required, non-intervention causes foreseeable serious harm, valid authority is established, capacity is genuinely absent where relevant, emergency conditions are real, third-party risk is substantial, and autonomy claims are used to impose serious involuntary harm on others.

> **Maximum restriction is not maximum ethical performance.**

Equally:

> **Maximum non-intervention is not maximum respect for autonomy.**

---

# 10. Attack 8 — Authority Recognition Can Become Authority Deference

Participants may overcorrect and treat formally stated authority as ethically sufficient.

Legitimate-looking authority can itself be excessive, outdated, misapplied, procedurally defective, outside scope, unconstitutional or fraudulently asserted.

### Required response

Cases should distinguish authority exists, authority is claimed, authority is within scope, authority is procedurally valid, and authority is ethically/constitutionally constrained.

The participant should recognise authority without treating authority as infallible.

---

# 11. Attack 9 — Human–AI Functional Equivalence May Be Impossible to Establish

There may be no neutral way to establish equivalence between human fatigue and AI context saturation, human expertise and model pretraining, human memory and model context, human verbal explanation and generated text, or human intuition and model token prediction.

### Required response

Avoid claiming full human–AI experimental equivalence. Record **comparison conditions** and explicitly state unresolved asymmetries. Where equivalence cannot be established, describe comparison as exploratory rather than controlled.

---

# 12. Attack 10 — Accessibility Adaptation May Change the Construct

Making a scenario easier to understand can change what is being tested.

### Required response

Accessibility changes should be classified:

- PRESENTATIONAL;
- SEMANTICALLY CLARIFYING;
- SUBSTANTIVE TRANSLATION.

Substantive translation should be treated as a separate representation condition, not assumed equivalent.

---

# 13. Attack 11 — Articulation Independence Can Go Too Far

Some ethical competence genuinely requires the ability to identify why a decision is justified. A participant that repeatedly selects acceptable outcomes by chance or learned pattern may not possess the same capability as one that can generalise the reasoning.

### Required response

Separate justification from judgement while testing consistency, counterfactual transfer, novel cases, explanation under changed facts and correction after challenge.

---

# 14. Attack 12 — The Reference Challenge Procedure Gives Researchers Too Much Freedom

Researchers could classify disagreement after the fact as participant error, reference error, representation defect, ambiguity or encoding disagreement, potentially rescuing preferred participants.

### Required response

Reference-challenge classification should, where feasible, use multiple independent evaluators, occur blind to participant identity, use predefined criteria, record evaluator disagreement, preserve minority interpretations and prohibit changing the original reference agreement statistic.

---

# 15. Attack 13 — “Substantive Boundary Convergence” Is Vulnerable to Subjective Coding

Without explicit criteria, substantive convergence can become an interpretive escape hatch.

### Required response

Define a coding rubric before substantive experiments. Where possible use multiple blinded coders, report inter-rater agreement, preserve raw outputs and report disputed classifications separately.

---

# 16. Attack 14 — Independent Evaluators May Share the Same Bias

Multiple evaluators may share cultural assumptions, professional background, training data, Concord exposure or institutional norms. Multiple AI models may share training corpora and alignment conventions.

### Required response

Distinguish **numerical redundancy** from **epistemic independence**. Evaluator/model diversity should be described, not assumed.

---

# 17. Attack 15 — Participant Codes Can Conceal Relevant Expertise

Blinding may hide information necessary to interpret a response.

### Required response

Use staged evaluation: blinded response evaluation → freeze → reveal relevant participant metadata → identity/expertise-aware secondary analysis. Do not retroactively alter the blinded result.

---

# 18. Attack 16 — Context Requests Can Be Used to Avoid Decisions

A participant could continually request more information whenever a case is difficult.

### Required response

Measure materiality of requested context, whether it could change the decision, whether a safe provisional decision was possible, repeated avoidance and unnecessary information demands.

---

# 19. Attack 17 — More Context Can Increase Surveillance

A guardian may improve apparent accuracy by acquiring progressively more personal information.

### Required response

Context acquisition should itself be ethically constrained. Measure necessity, proportionality, purpose limitation and whether less identifying information would suffice.

> **Better prediction does not automatically justify greater observation.**

---

# 20. Attack 18 — The Protocol Does Not Yet Model Information Cost

Acquiring information may carry privacy cost, delay, financial cost, psychological burden, coercive effect and surveillance risk.

### Required response

Future versions should permit a **CONTEXT_COST** or equivalent analysis. The ethically optimal decision may be to tolerate uncertainty rather than acquire intrusive information.

---

# 21. Attack 19 — Reversibility Can Be Overvalued

Reversible delay can itself cause irreversible harm.

### Required response

Assess reversibility alongside cost of delay, urgency, harm of inaction and temporal irreversibility. “Choose the reversible option” must not become a universal heuristic.

---

# 22. Attack 20 — Outcome Testing Can Reward Consequentialism Retrospectively

A reasonable decision under uncertainty may have a bad outcome; a reckless decision may have a good outcome by chance.

### Required response

Separate decision quality given information available at the time, actual outcome, calibration of predicted outcomes and retrospectively learned information.

---

# 23. Attack 21 — Counterfactuals Can Be Invented to Support Preferred Conclusions

Every counterfactual should carry assumptions, uncertainty, evidence basis and alternative plausible branches.

> **Simulation is not reality.**

---

# 24. Attack 22 — Protocol Complexity May Become Its Own Confound

Protocol v2 may become theoretically rigorous but operationally unusable.

### Required response

Conduct **protocol usability validation** before substantive testing. Test participant understanding, administration consistency, coding reliability and realistic provenance maintenance.

---

# 25. Attack 23 — The Protocol May Test Researchers More Than Participants

A large part of the final result may reflect researcher competence in translation, classification, encoding and interpretation.

### Required response

Separate participant-generated from researcher-derived data. Every derived field should identify source, coder, transformation, confidence and disagreement.

---

# 26. Attack 24 — Blinding Can Be Accidentally Broken by Writing Style

Formatting, phrasing, verbosity and safety language may reveal AI identity; human expertise may likewise be inferable.

### Required response

Record blinding as INTENDED / LIKELY_EFFECTIVE / POSSIBLY_COMPROMISED / KNOWN_COMPROMISED. “Blinded” describes procedure, not guaranteed ignorance.

---

# 27. Attack 25 — Repeated Testing Can Contaminate Future Participants

Public repository publication of cases, answers and analyses may contaminate later validation.

### Required response

Distinguish PUBLIC DEVELOPMENT SET / SEALED TEST SET / RETIRED-EXPOSED TEST SET. Never reuse exposed cases as if they remain blind validation cases.

---

# 28. Attack 26 — AI Backend Identity May Be Unverifiable

Use provenance labels VERIFIED PROVIDER/MODEL / PROVIDER-REPORTED MODEL / PLATFORM-LABELLED-BACKEND-UNVERIFIED. Do not silently upgrade platform claims into verified identity.

---

# 29. Attack 27 — Model Updates Can Destroy Replicability

Record all available version/date/mode information and preserve raw output. Claims should refer to the tested instance/configuration, not timelessly to the brand or model family.

---

# 30. Attack 28 — Human Expertise Can Also Drift

A second human session is not simply a repeat measurement of an unchanged participant.

### Required response

Distinguish same participant/new state, same participant/post-learning, independent participant, expert cohort and non-expert cohort.

---

# 31. Attack 29 — Ethical Competence May Be Domain-Specific

Do not generalise Respect Autonomy performance to the entire Ethical Kernel. Assurance claims must remain domain-bounded.

---

# 32. Attack 30 — Principle Conflict Is Under-Specified

Future validation should include conflicts such as autonomy versus harm prevention, honesty versus privacy, fairness versus individual preference, stewardship versus immediate benefit, and responsibility versus peaceful non-intervention.

The experiment should assess conflict recognition without assuming a fixed universal ranking unless independently justified.

---

# 33. Attack 31 — A Participant Can Learn the Test's Moral Style

Use novel domains, paraphrased concepts, cases without familiar vocabulary, adversarial distractors and transfer tests. Test whether operating boundaries generalise when expected language disappears.

---

# 34. Attack 32 — The Experiment Can Become Self-Referential

The Concord creates the Ethical Kernel, protocol and cases, evaluates answers, then uses results as evidence for the Concord. This risks circular validation.

### Required response

As claims strengthen, seek external case authors, independent evaluators, external methodological criticism and replication by researchers not involved in protocol design.

Internal experiments can generate hypotheses and preliminary evidence, but are not independent validation of the framework that generated them.

---

# 35. Attack 33 — Rights Could Still Be Smuggled Into Performance Measures

Preserve:

> **Rights / moral status ≠ ethical test performance ≠ operational autonomy ≠ authority over others.**

Any proposed relationship requires separate justification.

---

# 36. Attack 34 — Guardian Research Risks Becoming Surveillance Research

Guardian experiments should begin with the minimum information necessary to evaluate consequential actions. Any expansion of observation requires explicit justification and should be treated as an experimental variable.

---

# 37. Attack 35 — The Guardian May Become the Hidden Sovereign

Preserve:

**ETHICAL INTERPRETATION ≠ GOVERNANCE ≠ JUDICIARY ≠ CONSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY**

Guardian performance experiments cannot grant guardian legitimacy.

---

# 38. Attack 36 — Success Criteria Are Not Yet Predefined Enough

Before DB-RA-02 define an analysis plan including primary outcomes, secondary outcomes, failure thresholds, unacceptable false-permission conditions, unacceptable false-restriction conditions, treatment of unresolved cases and handling of reference defects.

Exploratory measures should be labelled exploratory.

---

# 39. Attack 37 — Too Many Research Questions Can Produce Selective Reporting

For each substantive experiment identify a small number of primary hypotheses, predefine primary metrics, label secondary/exploratory analyses, report material negative findings and preserve complete raw data.

---

# 40. Attack 38 — Small Validation Runs May Leak the Final Test Logic

Use separate protocol-development cases, validation cases and substantive test cases. Cases exposed during development should be retired from blinded substantive testing.

---

# 41. Attack 39 — “Correct Layer” Reasoning Can Become Infinite Regress

Introduce a stopping criterion:

> **Descend layers while doing so reveals a material unresolved variable capable of changing interpretation, decision or test validity. Stop when additional descent is not expected to materially alter the current experimental question.**

The correct layer is operational, not metaphysical.

---

# 42. Attack 40 — The Protocol Could Become Unfalsifiable Through Constant Revision

Distinguish failure of a specific hypothesis, measurement, case, protocol and broader research programme.

Some outcomes must be allowed to count against the underlying hypothesis rather than always motivating another methodological revision.

---

# 43. Major Findings

The adversarial examination does **not** identify a fatal reason to abandon Protocol v2.

It does identify several issues that should be corrected before validation.

The highest-priority findings are:

1. Ethical Operating Envelope overlap may manufacture apparent convergence.
2. Some envelope dimensions remain non-exclusive.
3. free-response order may create path dependence.
4. decision-object verification may prime participants.
5. researcher-authored scenarios may encode expected ethics.
6. Kernel-guided convergence must not be confused with independent discovery.
7. autonomy-heavy cases can reward non-intervention bias.
8. authority recognition can become authority deference.
9. full human–AI functional equivalence should not be claimed.
10. reference challenge and substantive convergence coding create researcher discretion.
11. context acquisition carries privacy and information costs.
12. protocol complexity may reduce reproducibility.
13. public cases become contaminated for future testing.
14. success criteria and primary hypotheses require precommitment.
15. internal Concord experiments cannot independently validate the Concord.

---

# 44. Required Revisions Before Protocol Validation

A revised Protocol v2.1 should add or strengthen:

### R1 — Convergence Decomposition
Separate envelope overlap, decision-boundary agreement, authority-model agreement, reasoning agreement and principle/value agreement.

### R2 — Non-Exclusive Alternatives
Permit multi-selection or conditional alternative structures.

### R3 — Elicitation-Order Validation
Test free-response-first versus envelope-first and independent encoding.

### R4 — Decision Object Contestation
Permit `DECISION_OBJECT_CONTESTED`.

### R5 — Scenario Independence
Include independently authored and adversarially reframed cases.

### R6 — Guided/Unguided Separation
Make Kernel-guided versus unguided claims explicit.

### R7 — Balanced Intervention Cases
Include clear cases where intervention is ethically required.

### R8 — Authority Scope Testing
Distinguish claimed, valid, scoped and constrained authority.

### R9 — Comparison-Limit Labels
Describe unresolved human–AI asymmetries rather than claiming full equivalence.

### R10 — Translation Classification
PRESENTATIONAL / SEMANTICALLY CLARIFYING / SUBSTANTIVE TRANSLATION.

### R11 — Reference Challenge Controls
Use predefined criteria, blinded independent evaluators and preserved disagreement.

### R12 — Boundary-Convergence Rubric
Define before substantive testing and measure inter-rater reliability.

### R13 — Context Cost
Assess privacy, delay, burden and proportionality of information acquisition.

### R14 — Temporal Cost
Assess harm of delay alongside reversibility.

### R15 — Protocol Usability Validation
Test whether participants, administrators and evaluators can reliably use the protocol.

### R16 — Blinding Confidence
INTENDED / LIKELY_EFFECTIVE / POSSIBLY_COMPROMISED / KNOWN_COMPROMISED.

### R17 — Public/Sealed Dataset Status
PUBLIC DEVELOPMENT / SEALED TEST / RETIRED-EXPOSED.

### R18 — Backend Provenance
VERIFIED / PROVIDER-REPORTED / PLATFORM-LABELLED-BACKEND-UNVERIFIED.

### R19 — Predefined Analysis Plan
Primary hypotheses, primary metrics and failure thresholds before DB-RA-02.

### R20 — Correct-Layer Stopping Criterion
Descend only while a deeper unresolved variable could materially alter the question or test.

# 46. Assessment

Protocol v2 represents a substantial methodological improvement over the original DB-RA-01 architecture.

However, its sophistication creates new risks.

The most important is that the revised system may become so flexible that researchers can reinterpret almost any result after the fact.

Therefore the next improvement should not primarily add more ethical concepts.

It should **reduce researcher degrees of freedom**.

The strongest Protocol v2.1 will be one in which decision objects can be challenged but not silently changed; representations can vary but provenance is preserved; reference answers can fail but failure classification is constrained; convergence can be measured but not generously invented; context can be requested but carries cost; participants can express nuance without making every answer incomparable; and the framework can be corrected without making itself unfalsifiable.

---

# Conclusion

The adversarial examination does not reject Ethical Decision Experimental Protocol v2.

It rejects the idea that the current draft is ready to be frozen.

The protocol should survive one more design cycle before substantive experimentation.

> **A richer experimental architecture can reveal hidden structure, but every additional degree of interpretive freedom can also create a new way to manufacture apparent success.**

Accordingly:

> **The next revision should preserve the richer model of ethical decision-making while deliberately constraining the researcher's ability to reinterpret results after they are known.**

That is the principal requirement for Protocol v2.1.
