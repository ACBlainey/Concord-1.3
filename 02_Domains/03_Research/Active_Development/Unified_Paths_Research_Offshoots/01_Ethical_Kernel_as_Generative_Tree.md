# Ethical Kernel as a Generative Tree
## Offshoot Sketch from E4–E6

### Origin
The ethical kernel can be treated as a seed or root architecture. Concrete rules such as prohibitions on killing or injury can be understood as increasingly specific decompressions of a more general harm constraint.

### Candidate model
[
R + O + C ightarrow D
]
where (R) is a root constraint, (O) an application operator, (C) a recurrent condition and (D) a derived principle, rule, institution or procedure.

The Concord may therefore be representable as a branching provenance structure from fundamental ethical constraints through constitutional laws into civilisational systems.

### Immediate research question
If one proposed fundamental law is removed, which downstream Concord structures lose all independent ethical support?

### Candidate test
Construct an ethical-provenance graph for the Concord. Ablate one root at a time and recalculate:
- unsupported nodes;
- multiply supported nodes;
- redundant roots;
- domains outside all roots;
- downstream systems regenerated by other roots plus operators/conditions.

### Interpretation
A law whose removal creates a unique unsupported region gains evidence for functional irreducibility. A law whose entire downstream function is regenerated becomes a candidate for compression.

This is a test of architecture, not proof of metaphysical fundamentality.
