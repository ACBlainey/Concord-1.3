# Religious Ethical Evolution and Root-Law Depth
## Historical Hypothesis Sketch

### Origin
The comparative programme raises a possible relationship between tradition age, ethical compression and later elaboration.

### Candidate hypothesis
Older ethical traditions may sometimes encode a smaller or more compressed set of deep functional constraints, while later traditions may repartition, elaborate or operationalise those constraints under new social and environmental conditions.

### Important non-conclusion
The present E3–E6 root counts do **not** establish an age-to-root-count relationship. The corpora, textual histories and compression geometries differ.

Religious branching also cannot presently be inferred to result from discovery of additional ethical roots.

### Prospective tests
Before future extraction, register age/depth predictions separately from the extraction team/process. Then test:
- root count;
- root functional coverage;
- operator count;
- condition/domain elaboration;
- residual roots;
- textual age versus abstraction level.

### African-tradition question
A particularly useful future holdout is whether selected African Traditional ethical corpora produce a more compressed root architecture.

A lower count would be compatible with the hypothesis but would not prove ancestral priority or religious lineage. An equal or higher count would also require interpretation rather than rescue.

Historical borrowing, later transmission, corpus preservation and colonial/post-colonial recording are confounds that must be investigated independently.

No assumption about civilisational or ethical development should be inferred from material culture, technology or housing form.
