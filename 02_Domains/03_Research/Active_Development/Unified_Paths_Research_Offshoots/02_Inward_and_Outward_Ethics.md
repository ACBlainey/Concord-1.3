# Inward and Outward Ethics
## Self-Stewardship as Directional Application

### Origin
Ethical analysis in the Concord has predominantly described how an agent should act toward other agents. The same constraints may also apply reflexively.

[
E_i(Aightarrow B)
qquad	ext{and}qquad
E_i(Aightarrow A)
]

Examples include outward harm versus self-harm, outward deception versus self-deception, and stewardship of external resources versus stewardship of one's own capabilities and continuity.

### Hypothesis
Inward ethics may be a **directional application of existing roots**, not an additional root family.

### Connection to E4–E6
This could help explain why self-regulation, reflective self-scrutiny, capability development and correction repeatedly appear without necessarily surviving as independent social roots.

### Questions
- Does every root admit a coherent reflexive form?
- Are some roots intrinsically relational?
- Can self-directed application conflict with outward application?
- Does self-stewardship generate distinct operators or merely conditions?
- How should autonomy constrain paternalistic intervention when an agent is harming itself?

### Status
Conceptual offshoot. It must not be used to alter frozen kernels without a separate derivation.
