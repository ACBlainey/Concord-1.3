# Representational Limits of Ethical Root Laws
## Language, Compression and ESCP

### Origin question
Does the minimum ethical description available in language necessarily equal the minimum underlying ethical structure?

Terms such as “non-harm” are already abstractions constructed within a representational system. Compression may stop because a concept is genuinely irreducible, or because the available language and conceptual space cannot represent a deeper relation.

### Core distinction
[
	ext{Minimum linguistic description}

eq	ext{necessarily}
	ext{minimum ethical structure}
]

### Connection to ESCP
This is an ethical instance of the Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem: an evaluator may incorrectly treat the limits of its representational space as the limits of the phenomenon.

### Research questions
- Can roots be represented geometrically, relationally or computationally without naming them?
- Do different languages partition the same ethical space differently?
- Can non-linguistic agents recover the same functional topology?
- Does compression remain stable after changing representation?
- Are apparently separate roots projections of a higher-dimensional relation?

### Methodological consequence
“Fundamental” should presently mean **minimum-stable under the declared representation and compression method**, unless stronger evidence is established.
