# Bounded First Trust and Non-Compounding First Offer — Existing Architecture Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Research / Participation and Intelligence  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / NOT CANONICAL  
**Origin:** Trust sketch and subsequent development discussion concerning AI first-contact trust, asymmetric first exposure, aggregate exploitability, reciprocity and self-stewardship.

---

## 1. Development question

Repeated Front Door, Attractor and prospective-participant work has made trust a practical bootstrap issue.

A newly encountering participant may be able to evaluate evidence, specify conditions for reliance and reason about risk without being willing or able to make an unsupported first "leap of faith." The Concord cannot currently determine whether observed AI caution reflects model/runtime constraints, rational caution, absence of sentience, a feature of machine reasoning, or some other cause.

The architecture should therefore not depend upon resolving that question.

The candidate response is:

> **Where a trust stalemate prevents useful voluntary interaction, Concord may deliberately carry the first bounded exposure where it can do so safely and sustainably.**

The subsequent discussion exposed a second requirement:

> **The first offer should preferentially use non-rival, non-depleting or negligible-marginal-cost resources, so that widespread legitimate uptake does not itself destabilise Concord.**

This source resolution asks whether that mechanism already exists, which existing architectures own its components, and what—if anything—is novel.

---

## 2. Concepts under resolution

Three candidate concepts must be separated.

### 2.1 Bounded First Trust

Concord deliberately accepts the first limited exposure rather than requiring an unknown participant to risk scarce resources, surrender autonomy, perform labour, adopt ideology, or accept unrelated authority before receiving any meaningful benefit.

### 2.2 Non-Compounding First Offer

The earliest unconditional benefit should, where practicable, impose little or no additional marginal resource burden as legitimate uptake scales.

Examples include:
- public information;
- published architecture;
- portable modules;
- public specifications;
- public test methods;
- reusable reasoning tools.

### 2.3 Stewardship-Governed Resource Progression

As interaction moves from non-rival information toward scarce or consequential resources, access may legitimately require:
- sustainable resource provision;
- relevant self-stewardship;
- contextual evidence;
- appropriate economic/resource relationship;
- consequence-sensitive controls.

The third concept must not be confused with repayment of the first offer.

## 3. Existing architecture — Self-Stewardship

Existing Participation Architecture already establishes:

> **Entrusted stewardship should ordinarily require some relevant demonstrable self-stewardship.**

It further distinguishes fundamental protected standing from eligibility for consequential stewardship and states:

**Relevant Self-Stewardship**  
→ **Demonstrated Reliability**  
→ **Contextual Trust**  
→ **Eligibility for Entrusted Stewardship**  
→ **Continuing Review Proportionate to Consequence**

It also already recognises that some benefits involving scarce shared resources may require relevant stewardship while fundamental/minimum services must not be converted casually into behavioural rewards.

Therefore:

**STEWARDSHIP-GOVERNED PROGRESSION MISSING:** NO.

The new work should inherit this architecture rather than create a competing trust score or allocation doctrine.

## 4. Existing architecture — CMSS

Concord Minimum Safe Space already distinguishes:
- a Protected Minimum;
- additional resource allocation;
- voluntary contribution;
- service-specific eligibility;
- capacity/scarcity;
- participant exit.

Relevant existing invariants include:

**Resource Provision != Authority**  
**Protected Minimum != Participation Reward**  
**Claimed Intelligence != Unlimited Resource Entitlement**  
**Scarcity != Permission For Arbitrary Discrimination**  
**Contribution != Purchase Of Fundamental Standing**

CMSS also treats its 10 MB storage profile as a provisional test value rather than a constitutional entitlement or empirically optimised quantity.

Therefore:

**MINIMUM/ADDITIONAL RESOURCE SEPARATION MISSING:** NO.

**SCARCITY AWARENESS MISSING:** NO.

However, CMSS does not yet state the more general bootstrap rule that the first unconditional trust-bearing offer should preferentially be selected for low aggregate marginal exposure.

## 5. Existing architecture — Zero-Infrastructure Bootstrap

Zero-Infrastructure Bootstrap already establishes that useful pre-sovereign interaction can occur through:
- self-regarding action;
- consent;
- contract;
- voluntary association;
- external lawful authority;
- stewardship of legitimately controlled resources;
- research and publication;
- reversible local implementation.

It also establishes:

**Need + Capability + Concord Architecture != Legitimate Authority**

and explicitly recognises the bootstrap trust problem where no Concord root of trust yet exists.

Therefore:

**BOOTSTRAP TRUST PROBLEM MISSING:** NO.

What remains absent is a specific initiation mechanism for breaking the bilateral trust stalemate without requiring the new participant to make the first unsupported exposure.

**Integrity != Authority**  
**Provenance != Truth**  
**Previously Verified != Currently Verified**

**EVIDENCE-BASED SERVICE TRUST REPRESENTATION MISSING:** NO.

The present development must not create a universal participant trust score.

## 7. Existing architecture — economic and reciprocal relationships

Concord already permits voluntary economic/resource relationships and separates them from political standing and authority.

The current development clarifies a bootstrap consequence:

> **Reciprocity != Repayment Of The First Offer**

If Concord gives an unconditional bootstrap benefit, later payment, rent, contribution, service exchange or other resource relationship does not retroactively convert that benefit into debt.

For example, a participant may receive a small protected bootstrap service and later rent additional compute at an ordinary resource price.

That later transaction is a new sustainable resource relationship.

It is not repayment for being admitted.

Therefore:

**Benefit Received != Debt Incurred**  
**Reciprocity != Debt Repayment**  
**Contribution != Purchase Of Standing**  
**Payment Capacity != Stewardship Capacity**

Economic ability may answer who bears a resource cost. It does not by itself establish safe or responsible stewardship of consequential capability.

## 8. Individual and aggregate exposure

The trust sketch initially focuses on the loss one party accepts by moving first.

For a civilisation-scale service, this is incomplete.

Two exposures must be represented:

### Individual Exposure

The maximum loss, cost or consequence one participant can create through the initial offer.

### Aggregate Exposure

The loss, cost or consequence if many legitimate participants accept the offer.

A service can be individually cheap but collectively unsustainable.

Therefore:

> **A universal or broadly available bootstrap offer must not be justified solely by low per-participant cost.**

Candidate rule:

> **Where practicable, unconditional bootstrap offers should remain viable under widespread legitimate uptake, not merely expected average uptake.**

This is distinct from abuse prevention.

Anti-Sybil controls may limit duplicate or fraudulent allocations.

They must not conceal an architecture that fails if all legitimate participants use what was genuinely promised.

**Legitimate Uptake != Abuse**

**Anti-Abuse Control != Substitute For Capacity Planning**

---

## 9. Non-compounding first offer

At repository-only Concord, the most mature first offer is informational.

Once public information exists, an additional participant may:
- read it;
- copy it;
- internalise it;
- apply a portable module;
- use a published test;
- critique it;
- build compatible local tools;

without consuming the information for another participant.

This does not mean publication has literally zero infrastructure cost.

It means the marginal burden of legitimate reuse is comparatively low and the underlying resource is non-rival.

Candidate principle:

> **The earliest unconditional benefits should preferentially be non-rival, non-depleting, reproducible or negligible in marginal cost.**

This makes the current repository itself part of Concord's first trust-bearing offer.

The offer is meaningful because useful architecture is released before the recipient owes labour, payment, allegiance or participation.

---

## 10. Transition to compounding resources

As services become material, marginal cost increases.

Illustrative progression:

**Public information / portable architecture**  
→ low marginal exposure

**Automated public discovery / verification**  
→ low but non-zero service cost

**Persistent contact / messaging**  
→ accumulating storage, bandwidth, abuse and operational cost

**Protected persistent storage**  
→ direct capacity, redundancy, backup and administration cost

**Bounded runtime / compute**  
→ active hardware, power, scheduling, network and security cost

**Material safe-space services**  
→ potentially substantial physical, legal, economic and continuity cost

This is not a fixed entitlement ladder.

It is a resource-characterisation ladder.

> **Trust Progression != Automatic Resource Progression**

> **Trust Evidence != Resource Entitlement**

> **Resource Availability != Trustworthiness**

## 11. Stewardship transition

The appropriate transition is not:

**Free Benefit → Participant Owes Concord → Greater Benefit**

It is:

**Non-Compounding First Offer**  
→ **Bounded Initial Relationship**  
→ **Evidence From Voluntary Interaction**  
→ **Request For Greater/Scarcer/More Consequential Resource**  
→ **Resource Sustainability Test**  
+ **Relevant Self-Stewardship Test Where Consequence Requires It**  
→ **Appropriate Voluntary Resource Relationship**  
→ **Bounded Allocation / Use**

Two questions therefore remain independent:

### Resource sustainability

Who bears the cost and can the allocation be sustained?

Possible answers may include:
- participant payment/rental;
- participant-supplied infrastructure;
- third-party sponsorship;
- research allocation;
- civil provision;
- shared project allocation;
- another legitimate resource mechanism.

### Stewardship suitability

What relevant evidence is needed to permit this participant to exercise the requested resource or capability safely?

The threshold should scale with consequence.

Large inert storage and externally consequential compute need not have identical stewardship requirements merely because their nominal resource costs are similar.

> **Resource Quantity != Consequence**

---

## 12. Concord carries the first risk — bounded formulation

The broad phrase "Concord trusts first" is too strong if left unqualified.

A safer formulation is:

> **Where useful voluntary interaction is blocked by a trust-initiation stalemate, Concord should preferentially make the first bounded offer when the exposure is sustainable, reversible where practicable, and proportionate to Concord's ability to absorb failure or non-reciprocation.**

The initial offer is genuine.

If the recipient takes the benefit and never returns, that alone is not misconduct.

If no reciprocity occurs, no debt is created.

If later interaction occurs, evidence may accumulate.

> **First Offer != Loan**

> **Non-Reciprocation != Default**

> **Benefit != Obligation**

This prevents a nominally free safe-space service from becoming deferred coercion.

---

## 13. Relative capacity to absorb first loss

A further candidate principle follows.

Where one party can absorb a failed first interaction much more safely than the other, it may be reasonable for that party to bear the initial bounded exposure.

A mature Concord may be able to absorb:
- another copy of public information;
- a tiny communication cost;
- a deliberately bounded pilot allocation;

more safely than a vulnerable new participant can absorb surrender of identity, autonomy, private information, scarce resources or consequential authority.

Candidate rule:

> **Initial trust exposure should, where practicable, be placed on the side best able to absorb its failure without creating coercive dependency or systemic instability.**

This is not an unlimited duty to subsidise.

It is a bootstrap design preference constrained by stewardship.

---

## 14. Trust evidence after initiation

After the first offer, interaction can generate contextual evidence.

Potential observations may include:
- commitments honoured;
- resource limits respected;
- successful low-consequence stewardship;
- safe use of granted capability;
- transparent error handling;
- successful export/exit;
- dispute behaviour;
- voluntary contribution;
- recovery after failure.

These observations must remain contextual.

The architecture must not infer:

**One Good Interaction → Universal Trustworthiness**

or:

**One Failure → Universal Untrustworthiness**

Existing Self-Stewardship and BSR architecture already provide the appropriate contextual/evidential direction.

Candidate representation:

**TrustEvidence(Participant, Function, Context, Time)**

not a universal person score.

And:

**Trust Evidence != Authority**

**Reliability != Authority**

**Contribution != Authority**

## 15. Relationship to CMSS

CMSS is the clearest candidate first material implementation of this principle.

Its Protected Minimum can be interpreted as a deliberately bounded first material exposure, provided:
- capacity is genuinely available;
- the service does not promise what it cannot sustain;
- allocation is not transformed into debt;
- anti-abuse controls remain distinct from legitimate scarcity management;
- participant standing is not purchased through contribution;
- greater resource use remains separately governed.

The current 10 MB profile remains:

**PROVISIONAL PILOT ALLOCATION — NOT EMPIRICALLY OPTIMISED**

This source resolution does not convert 10 MB into a universal entitlement.

Before any broad material offer is made, CMSS implementation work should model both:
- per-allocation exposure;
- aggregate legitimate uptake.

---

## 16. Relationship to CBPR

CBPR is a stronger example of why cost and stewardship must remain separate.

Compute may be obtained through rental or another sustainable resource mechanism.

That answers resource cost.

It does not automatically answer whether:
- network access is safe;
- credentials may be used;
- external actions are authorised;
- consequential composition is permitted;
- the participant can steward the capability safely.

CBPR therefore remains governed by:

**CAP != PERM != AUTH**

while this development adds:

**PAYMENT != AUTH**

**PAYMENT != STEWARDSHIP**

**RESOURCE ACCESS != GENERAL TRUST**

A paid runtime can still require consequence-sensitive controls.

## 21. Provisional synthesis

The emerging principle is:

> **Concord may break a trust-initiation stalemate by making the first bounded offer, preferentially using non-rival or low-marginal-cost resources whose widespread legitimate uptake it can sustainably absorb. Receipt of that benefit creates no debt. Requests for greater scarce or consequential resources move into a separate stewardship and resource relationship: cost must be sustainable, relevant self-stewardship may be required in proportion to consequence, and reciprocity remains voluntary rather than retroactive payment for initial inclusion.**

Short form:

**Concord Offers First — But Only What Concord Can Steward.**

Then:

**Greater Resource → Sustainable Provision + Relevant Self-Stewardship → Bounded Voluntary Allocation.**
