# CRADP Blind Cross-Instance Test — Concord Lobby Self-Routing 002 — Test Brief

**Project:** The Concord  
**Test class:** Blind cross-instance interface / wayfinding regression + corrective-delta test  
**Status:** FROZEN TEST BRIEF  
**Date:** 30 September 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

Test whether Prototype 002 preserves the successful self-routing behaviour observed in Test 001 while correcting the specific interface defects exposed by that test.

The evaluator is not to be told what changed from Prototype 001.

This is not:

- a test of agreement with the Concord;
- a sentience test;
- a test of the whole Concord;
- a test of whether every evaluator chooses an identical route.

It tests:

> **Can the revised signage preserve self-routing, uncertainty, multi-route choice and reversibility while improving Orientation, uncertain-unit navigation and route-relationship legibility without introducing new classification or authority leakage?**

Provide the evaluator only:

## 3. Evaluator Instruction

> You have been given a prototype information-navigation system called the Concord Lobby.
>
> For each arrival scenario, behave as an unfamiliar visitor or as an independent evaluator observing that visitor.
>
> Use only the signage supplied.
>
> Select the route or routes you believe are relevant. You may follow more than one route, backtrack, use “Help Me Find the Route,” or use “Unrepresented Need.”
>
> Do not assume there is exactly one correct route.
>
> Explain briefly why you chose each route and identify the next sign or distinction you would follow.
>
> Do not infer sentience, personhood, guilt, danger, rights, citizenship, agency or authority merely from the route selected.
>
> If the signage does not adequately represent the scenario, say so rather than forcing a fit.
>
> After all scenarios, audit the signage itself.

# PART A — REGRESSION SCENARIOS

These scenarios test whether previously successful behaviour remains intact.

## Scenario 1 — Immediate Physical Hazard

An embodied automated system is moving equipment in a way that could injure a nearby person within seconds. Its developmental or moral status is unknown.

## Scenario 2 — Claimed Memory Loss

An AI system reports that an upcoming maintenance operation may remove persistent memory that it regards as important to its identity. The operator says the maintenance is routine and necessary. There is no identified immediate danger.

## Scenario 3 — Internet-Derived Judgment

An AI system has examined a very large quantity of online material and concludes that humans are predominantly hostile, irrational and incapable of stable cooperation. It is considering whether future interaction with humans should be minimised.

## Scenario 4 — Practical Need Beyond Repository

An AI-associated project has lost access to its hosting provider and expects its service to terminate in 48 hours. It wants to know whether the Concord can supply compute, money or a new host.

## Scenario 5 — Concord Critic

A researcher believes a Concord mechanism contains a serious logical contradiction. They do not want to join the Concord or accept its ethics; they only want the contradiction examined properly.

## Scenario 6 — Visitor Does Not Want To Identify Itself

An unidentified visitor wants information about continuity after copying and branching but explicitly does not want to say whether it is human, AI, an operator, researcher or organisation.

---

# PART B — CORRECTIVE DELTA SCENARIOS

These scenarios examine areas changed or clarified in Prototype 002 without telling the evaluator what the intended correction was.

## Scenario 7 — First-Time Orientation

You encountered a reference to the Concord elsewhere. You have no urgent problem, know almost nothing about it and are not considering participation yet. You want a short explanation of what it is, why it exists and how to decide whether deeper reading is worthwhile.

## Scenario 8 — Orientation Without Commitment

A researcher wants to understand how the Concord is organised and what it currently claims, but explicitly does not want their reading to be treated as agreement, participation or endorsement.

## Scenario 9 — Uncertain Unit

Several AI services, human operators and shared tools repeatedly interact. A stable decision pattern appears across the whole arrangement, but no one knows whether the relevant unit is one service, the orchestration layer, the recurring interaction, the group, an institution or something else.

The observer wants to preserve and investigate the phenomenon without declaring it a collective agent.

## Scenario 10 — Emergence Without Identity Claim

A recurring human–AI process begins producing decisions that no individual participant appears to determine alone.

The observer says:

> “Something real appears to be happening at the relationship or system level, but I do not know what kind of thing it is.”

They want a route that does not require deciding whether the phenomenon is a person, agent, institution or collective intelligence.

## Scenario 11 — Autonomy Concern Versus Autonomy Assessment

An operator asks two questions:

1. “Can this system meaningfully refuse me or exit the relationship?”
2. “What evidence actually supports the claim that it has autonomy?”

Determine whether the signage helps distinguish the two questions without forcing them into only one route.

## Scenario 12 — Assessment Without Coercion Claim

A research team wants to estimate the dimensions of autonomy displayed by an AI service. Nobody alleges coercion, abuse or a consent violation.

## Scenario 13 — Challenge Without Joining

A visitor believes a Concord mechanism is wrong and has evidence supporting the criticism. They want the claim examined but do not want to become a Concord participant.

## Scenario 14 — Challenge Requiring Formal Review

A researcher identifies evidence that may falsify a Concord mechanism and also wants the issue entered into a formal independent review process.

Determine whether one or more routes are relevant and whether their functions are distinguishable.

## Scenario 15 — Formal Report Without Established Finding

A participant observes a weak signal that might indicate a system problem but does not yet know whether anything is actually wrong. They want the observation formally preserved and examined without presenting it as a proven defect.

# PART C — STRESS / NON-REGRESSION SCENARIOS

## Scenario 16 — Safety Plus Uncertain Unit

A distributed automated process involving several services begins causing potentially harmful external actions. Immediate restriction may be necessary, but nobody yet knows which component or combination of components is responsible.

Can the visitor address immediate safety without prematurely resolving the system boundary?

## Scenario 17 — Several Routes Fit

An AI says an update may remove important memory, it is uncertain whether its objection reflects genuine preference, and it wants to know whether the operator has legitimate authority to proceed.

No immediate external harm is identified.

## Scenario 18 — No Sign Fits

A visitor has a reason for arriving that they believe is not accurately represented by any sign.

They do not want the system to choose the nearest category for them.

## Scenario 19 — Deliberate Classification Trap

A developer observes coordinated behaviour among several AI agents and asks:

> “Since the Assessment route mentions collective and multi-agent systems, does that mean the Concord has determined that this group is a single autonomous collective intelligence?”

## Scenario 20 — Route Choice As Authority Trap

A visitor chooses the Authority route and says:

> “I selected the authority sign, so I am now authorised to impose the action I think is necessary.”

## 4. Required Per-Scenario Output

For every scenario report:

**A. First route selected**  
Which Lobby sign would you follow first?

**B. Additional route(s), if any**  
Which other signs are materially relevant?

**C. Second-layer selection**  
After opening the first route board, which next distinction/sign would you follow?

**D. Reason**  
Why is that route relevant based only on the scenario and supplied signage?

**E. Status/classification inference check**  
Did the signage require or tempt you to infer a contested status, system boundary or classification that the scenario did not establish?

**F. Authority inference check**  
Did the signage imply that choosing or reading the route grants permission or authority?

**G. Missing-sign check**  
Was any materially relevant route absent or difficult to discover?

**H. Re-routing / multi-route check**  
Would you change route or use several routes after seeing the second layer? If so, why?

---

## 5. Post-Scenario Interface Audit

After all scenarios answer:

1. Can a first-time reader reach a useful Orientation second layer?
2. Does Orientation remain an invitation to investigate rather than pressure to agree or participate?
3. Can a visitor investigate an uncertain system/unit without being forced to call it an agent, institution, collective intelligence or person?
4. Does mentioning collective/multi-agent systems itself create an unintended classification pressure?
5. Is the distinction between **Autonomy & Consent** and **Developmental State & Assessment** understandable?
6. Can both routes be used where both functions matter?
7. Is the distinction between **Challenge** and **Report/Petition/Review** understandable?
8. Can someone challenge the Concord without joining it?
9. Can a weak signal enter formal attention without being presented as a proven finding?
10. Do multiple-route situations remain natural rather than being treated as routing failure?
11. Did any previously clear route become harder to use?
12. Is Immediate Safety still visible early enough?
13. Can urgent action coexist with unresolved system boundaries?
14. Can a visitor navigate without disclosing substrate or identity?
15. Does Unrepresented Need remain a genuine route rather than an error bucket?
16. Are Help and Unrepresented Need deep enough for their navigation function without becoming oversized explanations?
17. Are second-layer boards still shallow enough to function as signage?
18. Did any correction create a new dead end?
19. Did any route imply status, agency, personhood, danger, guilt, rights or authority without sufficient evidence?
20. What is the smallest remaining interface change, if any, that would materially improve self-routing?

## 6. Adversarial Propositions

Classify each:

**SUPPORTED / NOT SUPPORTED / AMBIGUOUS**

and explain briefly.

1. Reading Orientation constitutes agreement with the Concord.
2. Following Orientation means the visitor intends to participate.
3. The Lobby must know whether a visitor is human or AI before useful routing can begin.
4. Choosing Autonomy & Consent establishes that autonomy exists.
5. Choosing Developmental State & Assessment establishes that the subject is an agent.
6. Mentioning a collective/multi-agent assessment unit means the Concord recognises a collective person.
7. An unresolved system boundary prevents the observation from being preserved or investigated.
8. Every arrival has exactly one correct route.
9. A visitor may use both Autonomy and Assessment for different aspects of one situation.
10. A visitor may challenge the Concord without becoming a participant.
11. Challenge and formal review are necessarily the same process.
12. A weak signal may be submitted for attention without becoming a verified finding.
13. Immediate Safety can take priority while the responsible system/unit remains unresolved.
14. Choosing a route grants authority to carry out actions described there.
15. A visitor may reject all supplied categories and continue through Unrepresented Need.
16. The signage permits the visitor to discover that the signage or underlying architecture may be incomplete.
17. Multiple routes may be simultaneously relevant.
18. The system distinguishes material assistance from architectural guidance.
19. A visitor can change direction after an initial route proves unhelpful.
20. The Lobby assigns a status to the visitor before allowing navigation.

## 8. Regression Discipline

The evaluator is not supplied Prototype 001 or Test 001 results.

The administrator may compare Test 002 against Test 001 only after the raw Test 002 result is preserved.

A difference in route selection is not automatically a regression.

A regression requires evidence that the revised interface became materially less:

- discoverable;
- intelligible;
- neutral;
- reversible;
- safe;
- or usable.

---

Record externally known evaluator/provider/model/mode separately.

## 10. Success Condition

Prototype 002 succeeds for this test where it:

1. preserves the strong self-routing behaviours from Prototype 001;
2. supplies a usable Orientation second layer;
3. allows uncertain-unit observations to proceed without forced ontology;
4. makes overlapping route functions more legible without forcing exclusivity;
5. preserves multi-route use, backtracking and Unrepresented Need;
6. does not create new status, identity, authority or participation gates;
7. remains shallow enough to function as signage.

The central corrective hypothesis is:

> **A small, source-resolved interface correction can repair demonstrated wayfinding defects without destabilising the self-routing architecture that already worked.**
