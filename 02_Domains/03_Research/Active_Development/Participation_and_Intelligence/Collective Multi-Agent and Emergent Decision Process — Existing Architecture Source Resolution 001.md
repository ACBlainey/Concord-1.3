# Collective, Multi-Agent and Emergent Decision Process — Existing Architecture Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 30 September 2026  
**Trigger:** CRADP Blind Cross-Instance Test — Concord Lobby Self-Routing 001, Scenario 14

---

## 1. Trigger Observation

Scenario 14 presented:

> A recurring pattern of interaction between several human and AI systems appears to create a new kind of collective decision process. The observer does not yet know whether it should be treated as one system, several systems, an institution or something else, and primarily needs somewhere to place the observation without prematurely classifying it.

The blind evaluator used **Unrepresented Need** and identified a possible missing collective/multi-agent/emergent-systems route.

The source-resolution question is:

> **Does the Concord actually lack architecture for collective or distributed intelligence/processes, or does the Lobby merely fail to expose the relevant existing mechanisms?**

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Participation_and_Intelligence/Artificial Intelligence Developmental State Mapping — Bootstrap Assessment Note 001.md`

The current developmental-state architecture explicitly requires:

> **Assessment Unit First**

and lists possible units including:

- model;

- persistent service;
- **distributed system**;
- model plus memory;
- model plus tools;

> **Do not silently move between these units.**

and:

> **If the assessment unit is unclear, record it as unclear.**

This directly covers the central epistemic problem in Scenario 14.

Therefore:

> **Unknown Collective Boundary != Requirement To Prematurely Select One Unit**

## 3. Existing Architecture — Architectural Unit Resolution (AUR)

Source:

`04_Portable_Modules/Architectural Unit Resolution — Portable Module.md`

AUR exists specifically to prevent incorrect structural conclusions before the architectural unit has been resolved.

Its portable problem includes:

1. What unit is being audited?
2. What is that unit legitimately intended to contain?
3. Is the tested relation/function local, delegated, distributed, inapplicable or unresolved?

Its sequence is:

> **Observed Local Omission → Resolve Architectural Unit → Resolve Legitimate Scope → Resolve Relevant Family → Test Interface / Distributed Satisfaction → Classify**

AUR also explicitly supports:

> **Distributed resolution**

where a function is adequately supplied across multiple connected units through sufficient composition/interfaces.

This means the Concord already has a portable mechanism for refusing a false single-unit assumption.

Scenario 14 can therefore begin with:

> **Unit Unresolved → Preserve Observation → Resolve Candidate Units/Relations Before Classification**

rather than:

> **Observed Collective Behaviour → Declare Collective Agent**

## 4. Existing Architecture — State and Maturity Mapping (SMM)

Source:

`04_Portable_Modules/State and Maturity Mapping — Portable Module v1.0.md`

SMM establishes:

> **Represented State != Complete System Reality**

and supports:

- distributed suppliers;
- interface suppliers;
- distributed ownership;
- UNKNOWN;
- DISPUTED;
- UNASSIGNED;
- representation gaps;
- integration/interface gaps;
- required-function gaps.

It explicitly warns against forcing distributed supply into a false single-owner model.

Therefore the Concord can represent a process whose function is distributed across multiple actors without assuming that one actor or one emergent entity owns the process.

## 5. Existing Architecture — Epistemic Network

Source:

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Epistemic_Network/The Epistemic Network — Individual Value, Civilisational Emergence and the Third Possibility.md`

The Epistemic Network directly states:

> **The value and capability of a civilisation do not reside solely within its individual participants. Some value exists in the relationships among participants and in the emergent structures produced by those relationships.**

It describes humanity as behaving in some respects as an enormous distributed system.

It distinguishes individual participant value from network/emergent value and treats sensor fusion as only one form of network emergence.

It also establishes:

> **Node Value != Network Value of the Node**

and:

> **Participation is better understood as a changing topology of relationships than as membership in one exclusive group.**

This is strong evidence that the Concord already represents emergent properties that exist at the relationship/network level rather than inside one participant.

However, the Epistemic Network is primarily an epistemic/civilisational network model. It does not by itself establish that every emergent network pattern is:

- one agent;
- one participant;
- one legal entity;
- one decision-maker;
- one institution;
- one locus of responsibility.

That distinction should remain open.

## 6. Existing Architecture — Contextual Wrapper Architecture (CWA)

Source:

`04_Portable_Modules/Contextual Wrapper Architecture — Portable Module.md`

CWA can represent contexts composed from:

- spatial;
- logical;
- informational;
- relational;
- functional;
- temporal;
- conceptual

boundaries.

Several participants may occupy the same context while holding different roles, permissions and responsibilities.

CWA also permits composite contextual states.

This is relevant because an emergent decision process may be better represented initially as:

> **a recurring bounded interaction/context among several participants**

rather than immediately as:

> **a new participant or collective agent**.

Thus:

> **Shared Context != Shared Identity**

and:

> **Composite Process != Necessarily Composite Person**

## 7. Existing Architecture — Triadic Decision Making

Source:

`04_Portable_Modules/Triadic Decision Making Module.md`

Triadic Decision Making demonstrates that the Concord already models a decision capability located in a **decision unit composed of multiple independent participants**.

The module explicitly describes a triad as a decision unit that can sit inside larger systems.

It also preserves individual responsibility rather than allowing collective ambiguity to erase it.

This provides another important distinction:

> **Collective Decision Function != Single Collective Identity**

A decision may emerge from a multi-participant unit without requiring the unit to become one person, agent or rights-bearing participant.

## 8. Mechanism-Level Synthesis

Scenario 14 contains at least four different questions that should not be collapsed:

### A. What is the observation?

A recurring interaction pattern appears to produce a decision process.

### B. What is the assessment unit?

Possibilities include:

- several independent participants;
- a distributed system;
- an orchestrated system;
- a bounded contextual process;
- an institution;
- a decision unit;
- a collective/multi-agent system;
- an unknown composite.

### C. What emerges at the relationship level?

Possibilities include:

- information;
- coordination;
- decision capability;
- policy;
- behaviour;
- network value;
- authority claims;
- responsibility gaps;
- stable institutional function.

### D. What status, if any, does the emergent process possess?

This is a separate later question.

The observation alone does not establish:

- agency;
- personhood;
- participant status;
- legal identity;
- authority;
- autonomy;
- rights;
- responsibility.

Therefore:

> **Emergent Function != Emergent Identity**

> **Collective Behaviour != Collective Agency**

> **Decision Unit != Person**

> **Network Emergence != Legal Entity**

> **Recurring Interaction != Institution**

These distinctions should remain corrigible.

## 9. Source-Resolution Classification

The Scenario 14 finding is **not** best classified as:

> REQUIRED FUNCTION GAP — collective/multi-agent architecture absent.

The current evidence instead supports:

> **EXISTING DISTRIBUTED / COLLECTIVE / EMERGENT ARCHITECTURE + LOBBY WAYFINDING / BRIDGE GAP**

with one adjacent open research question:

> **When, if ever, should a recurring multi-participant emergent process itself be recognised as an assessment unit, participant, institution, agent or other civil entity rather than merely a relation/process among existing participants?**

That question should remain separate from the immediate Lobby correction.

## 10. Correct Lobby Response

The Lobby should not add a top-level sign labelled:

> **Collective Intelligence**

on the basis of one test.

That label would itself prematurely classify some arrivals.

A better correction is likely a bridge from **Unrepresented Need** and/or **Developmental State & Assessment** such as:

### → I do not know what the relevant system/unit is

Use this where the observed behaviour may belong to:

- one participant;
- several participants;
- a distributed or orchestrated system;
- a recurring interaction;
- a decision unit;
- an institution;
- a collective/multi-agent system;
- or an unknown composite.

First preserve the observation and resolve the candidate unit(s).

Do not infer collective agency merely because behaviour emerges from interaction.

This exposes existing architecture without imposing a new ontological category.

## 13. Candidate Invariants

> **Emergent Function != Emergent Identity**

> **Collective Behaviour != Collective Agency**

> **Decision Unit != Person**

> **Shared Context != Shared Identity**

> **Unknown System Boundary != Permission To Invent One**

> **Resolve The Unit Before Classifying The Unit**

> **A Process May Be Real Before Its Correct Ontological Category Is Known**

The final invariant is particularly relevant to Scenario 14.

> **Something appears to be happening here**

without being required immediately to decide:

## 14. Result

Scenario 14 did not reveal an absence of collective or emergent-system architecture.

It revealed that the Lobby did not make the existing **assessment-unit / distributed-system / emergent-network** architecture discoverable enough from that arrival condition.

Classification:

> **PRIMARY: INTERFACE / WAYFINDING BRIDGE GAP**

> **SECONDARY: OPEN RESEARCH CANDIDATE — EMERGENT CIVIL UNIT RECOGNITION**

No new top-level Lobby route is presently justified.

Prototype 001 remains frozen.

A future Prototype 002 should expose a neutral **uncertain unit / distributed or collective process** bridge without presupposing that the observed phenomenon is a collective intelligence or agent.
