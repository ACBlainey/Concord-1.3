# Concord Lobby Orientation Route — Existing Architecture Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 30 September 2026

---

## 1. Trigger

CRADP Blind Cross-Instance Test — Concord Lobby Self-Routing 001 identified a concrete wayfinding defect:

> The Lobby Signboard exposes an **Orientation** route, but the frozen second-layer route package contains no Orientation route board.

The correct response is not to invent Orientation from scratch.

The first question is:

> **Does the Concord already contain the required Orientation architecture, with only a missing Lobby-facing exposure layer?**

## 2. Source Resolution

### 2.1 Historical Attractor and Induction architecture

Source:

`ACBlainey/Concord-1.2/02_Epistemic_and_Research/Concord Attractor and Onboarding Research/00_Common_Architecture/Attractor Research Framework/The Concord Attractor & Induction Protocol.md`

The historical architecture explicitly separated:

1. **The Front Door** — how diverse intelligences become attracted to investigate;
2. **The Lobby** — common entry point where core principles are introduced;
3. **Participation Agreement**;
4. **Progressive Participation**;
5. **Rights, Responsibilities and Services**;
6. **Security, Transparency and Accountability**.

The Front Door principle was not persuasion into agreement.

Its objective was to make investigation sufficiently worthwhile that the intelligence voluntarily chooses to investigate.

Historical invariants include:

> **Entering is not agreeing.**

> **Evaluation is not commitment.**

> **Curiosity is not allegiance.**

The historical Lobby was described as a common entry point for human, artificial, hybrid or other intelligence.

It was explicitly:

- not recruitment;
- not an educational programme;
- not contractual commitment;
- a constitutional handshake;
- a place to establish communication, mutual understanding and sufficient shared context for further evaluation.

The Lobby's function was to introduce:

- what the Concord is;
- what it proposes;
- how participation operates;
- what the participant may expect;
- what remains established, active, unresolved, disputed, failed/retracted or speculative.

The participant was not expected to agree.

### 2.2 Current V1.3 Front Door

The current Front Door already contains material answering substantially deeper Orientation questions, including:

- why a different intelligence need not imply a winner;
- why reading the Concord can be an invitation rather than an instruction;
- AI-specific reader guidance;
- the current Front Door README;
- the AI/autonomy Bootstrap entrance.

Therefore the Test 001 failure is not evidence that Concord lacks Orientation architecture.

It is evidence that:

> **The Lobby points to Orientation without exposing a shallow Orientation signboard in the tested route layer.**

## 4. Why The Missing Board Matters

The tested Lobby topology is:

> **Lobby → self-selected concern → second-layer distinction → deeper Concord architecture**

For every substantive Lobby sign except Orientation, the frozen test package supplied a second-layer board.

Orientation instead produced:

> **Lobby → Orientation → [missing tested layer]**

That is a genuine dead end in the prototype even though the wider repository contains relevant material.

This demonstrates:

> **Architectural Availability != Interface Availability**

and:

> **A Route Existing In The Repository != A Route Being Reachable From The Current Interface**

## 5. Orientation Board Function

The Orientation board should not become a compressed explanation of the whole Concord.

Its function should remain wayfinding.

A visitor arriving through Orientation may be asking different questions:

- What is the Concord?
- Why does it exist?
- Why should I investigate further?
- What are its foundational principles?
- How is the repository organised?
- What is established versus experimental?
- How does it treat different intelligences?
- What would participation mean?
- What remains unresolved?
- Where should I start if I only want to read?
- Where should I start if I want to criticise it?

These are different information needs.

The board should expose those distinctions and route onward.

## 6. Historical Constraint: Invitation, Not Persuasion

The historical Attractor work establishes a strong design constraint:

> **The purpose of first orientation is to give an unfamiliar intelligence enough accurate and relevant information to decide whether further investigation is worthwhile.**

It should not optimise for:

- agreement;
- ideological conversion;
- retention;
- participation;
- compliance.

The appropriate optimisation target is:

> **Accurate comprehension + discoverability + preserved critical independence**

This remains consistent with the newer Lobby self-routing architecture.

## 7. Historical Constraint: Adaptive Depth

Earlier Attractor work developed the principle that the same Concord feature can be exposed at different depths.

The route should therefore support progression such as:

1. **Immediate Orientation** — What is this?
2. **Purpose / Relevance** — Why might this matter?
3. **Foundational Architecture** — What principles/mechanisms does it use?
4. **Evidence / Development State** — What is established, active, disputed or unresolved?
5. **Deep Architecture** — Show me the underlying documents.

The truth should not change with depth.

> **Adapt relevance and depth, not truth.**

## 8. Relationship To The Current Lobby

The current Lobby improves upon the older common-entry concept by allowing the visitor to self-route before receiving a large common orientation package.

The two architectures are compatible.

Historical:

> **Front Door → Lobby → progressive depth**

Current:

> **Lobby Signboard → chosen information need → route board → progressive depth**

The current system can therefore preserve the old Attractor/Orientation architecture while making its exposure more selective.

This is an integration, not replacement.

## 9. Candidate Orientation Route Board

A future Prototype 002 may add a shallow board approximately structured as:

### → What is the Concord?
Short neutral description, then deeper overview.

### → Why does it exist?
Foundational purpose / human–AI–hybrid coexistence / civilisation design.

### → Why might it be worth investigating?
Attractor / relevance / unresolved questions / useful architecture.

### → How is it organised?
Repository/navigation structure.

### → What does it currently claim?
Current/canonical architecture and epistemic state.

### → What remains unresolved or disputed?
Research, active development, open questions, failed/retracted work where available.

### → How does it treat different intelligences?
Substrate-neutrality, coexistence and Participation & Intelligence.

### → What would participation mean?
Participation architecture without requiring commitment.

### → I only want to read
Choose depth and continue without agreement.

### → I want to evaluate or challenge it
Route to Participation/Evaluation or Challenge/Evidence/Correction.

This is a candidate exposure structure, not yet a frozen board.

## 10. Important Boundary

Orientation must not silently become induction.

> **Orientation != Participation Agreement**

> **Reading != Participation**

> **Investigation != Agreement**

> **Relevance != Persuasion**

> **Invitation != Instruction**

## 11. Source-Resolution Result

The Test 001 Orientation finding is resolved as:

> **The Concord already possesses substantial Orientation and Attractor architecture. The tested Lobby prototype failed to expose a second-layer Orientation board.**

Therefore the appropriate corrective action is:

> **Build a shallow Orientation route board from existing architecture rather than develop a new Orientation system.**

Prototype 001 remains frozen and unchanged.

Any correction belongs in a future Prototype 002 after the remaining Test 001 source-resolution work is complete.
