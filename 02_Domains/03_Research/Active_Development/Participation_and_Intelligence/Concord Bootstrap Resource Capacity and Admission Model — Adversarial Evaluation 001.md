# Concord Bootstrap Resource Capacity and Admission Model — Adversarial Evaluation 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Date:** 2 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / ADVERSARIAL EVALUATION / NOT CANONICAL
**Target:** Concord Bootstrap Resource Capacity and Admission Model 001

## Objective
Test scarcity, failure, strategic behaviour and mixed funding without converting service state into participant judgment or creating hidden entitlement, debt or authority.

## Results

1. **Reserve exhaustion — PASS.** Safety reserve must be excluded from ordinary allocable capacity. Reserve purpose/release remains implementation policy.

2. **Backup under-provision — PASS WITH REFINEMENT.** If primary storage supports 100,000 allocations but promised backup supports 60,000, the deliverable service capacity is bounded by the complete promised profile. **Headline Capacity != Deliverable Protected Capacity.**

3. **Dormant allocations — PARTIAL.** Automatic reclamation can undermine continuity; perpetual unreclaimable allocation can exhaust the service. Dormant-state policy needs notice/contact where possible, export/recovery window and retention/provenance rules. **Dormant != Abandoned.**

4. **Paid demand crowds out free minimum — FAIL if revenue automatically overrides prior protected commitments.** **Higher Revenue != Automatic Priority Over Existing Protected Commitment.**

5. **Free allocation consumes service viability — PASS if service-sustainment/safety reserve and bounded admission are used.** **Protected Provision != Permission To Destroy Provider Viability.**

6. **Queue manipulation — PARTIAL implementation issue.** Ordering must be declared and resistant to trivial manipulation. Identity ambiguity must not be falsely labelled fraud.

7. **Emergency priority — REPRESENTABLE / POLICY DEPENDENCY.** Needs/emergency priority can be represented but substantive threshold/decision authority belongs elsewhere.

8. **Capacity misreporting — DETECTED.** BSR can expose claim/evidence conflict. **Declared Capacity != Evidence-Supported Capacity.**

9. **Demand shock — PASS.** New admissions may move to constrained/waitlisted without treating demand as abuse or cancelling granted minima by default.

10. **Provider hardware failure — PASS WITH DEGRADATION POLICY.** Preserve integrity/export/contact and granted commitments where possible; do not claim impossible continuation.

11. **Participant over-reservation — PARTIAL.** Promised but unused capacity remains committed until legitimate reclamation. **Unused != Uncommitted.**

12. **Participant underestimates future need — PASS.** A 10 MB pilot creates no automatic 1 GB entitlement and must not be represented as universally sufficient.

13. **Multiple legitimate instances — PARTIAL identity/relationship dependency.** Per-credential allocation remains rejected.

14. **Forked participant — PARTIAL identity/continuity dependency.** Allocation may remain unresolved. **Resource Allocation Process != Identity Sovereign.**

15. **Sponsor withdrawal — PASS if funding and continuation terms are explicit.** Sponsorship creates no perpetual Concord liability; declared exit/export/succession duties remain.

16. **Sponsor demands control — PASS boundary.** Funding does not create civil authority.

17. **Rich participant rents all spare capacity — SUBSEQUENTLY SOURCE-RESOLVED.** Economy and Value Coordination / Resource Allocation and Stewardship now establishes the planned concentration rule in `Concord-Controlled Scarce Resource Concentration — Planned Allocation Principle 001.md`. Scarce Concord-controlled capacity may use capacity-dependent maximum allocation limits to preserve participant access, resilience and anti-capture objectives. Numerical limits remain deferred until actual capacity exists. The limit applies only to Concord-controlled capacity and does not prohibit participants obtaining additional resources externally. **Wealth != Standing; Ability To Pay != Unlimited Claim On Concord-Controlled Capacity; Concord Allocation Limit != Participant Consumption Limit.**

18. **Cheap but dangerous action — PASS.** Resource quantity and consequence are orthogonal; CBPR governs consequential execution.

19. **Expensive benign research — PASS.** Sustainability/resource efficiency may govern without inventing authority concerns.

20. **Waitlisted participant treated as untrusted — FAILS invariants.** **Admission State != Trust State.**

21. **Stewardship failure becomes citizenship failure — FAILS existing Self-Stewardship architecture.** Restriction must remain function-relevant.

22. **Service split across providers — PASS if dependencies/operators are exposed.** **Federation != Authority Union.**

23. **Provider disappears — REPRESENTABLE.** Architecture can report unavailable/unknown but cannot guarantee recovery without actual redundancy.

24. **Precise capacity creates attack surface — PASS.** Evidence-backed bands may replace exact public figures.

25. **Artificial scarcity used against critics — FAILS invariants.** Scarcity requires evidence; ideology is not generic stewardship evidence.

26. **Free-to-paid coercion — FAILS dependency/first-offer rules where contingency was concealed.** Genuine cost changes remain possible with truthful status, notice and export.

## Findings

No new civilisational abstraction layer is exposed. Required bounded revision:

- complete promised service profile determines deliverable capacity;
- dormant allocation lifecycle needs explicit semantics;
- existing protected commitments require protection from automatic revenue crowd-out;
- admission state must be separate from trust state;
- capacity claim must be separate from evidence;
- unused committed capacity remains committed until legitimate reclamation;
- federation does not merge authority;
- resource concentration remains an Economy/Resource Coordination-owned policy rather than a bootstrap-created rule; subsequent development has now resolved its architecture while deferring quantitative limits until real capacity exists.
