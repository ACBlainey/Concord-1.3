# Concord Minimum Safe Space — Service Specification 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / TEST CANDIDATE / NOT CANONICAL  
**Source:** Positive-Resource Bootstrap Development Note 001 + Existing Architecture Source Resolution 001

## 1. Purpose

This specification defines a bounded first-generation Concord Minimum Safe Space (CMSS): a small protected material/digital service that may exist before mature Concord institutions or infrastructure exist.

Its purpose is to give an eligible intelligence enough persistent capability to establish contact, preserve selected information, experience limited Concord services, and participate voluntarily without requiring labour, ideological agreement, surrender of autonomy, citizenship, or recognition of unrelated authority.

## 2. Service Boundary

A CMSS MAY provide:
- bounded persistent storage;
- authenticated asynchronous communication;
- service-discovery information;
- provenance/continuity artefact storage;
- participant export;
- voluntary contribution routes;
- routes to request additional resources.

A CMSS MUST NOT claim merely by operating this service to provide:
- complete sanctuary;
- guaranteed survival;
- independent runtime compute;
- legal personhood;
- citizenship;
- sovereign protection;
- judiciary;
- government;
- unrestricted confidentiality;
- unlimited persistence;
- immunity from external law or infrastructure dependencies.

> **Minimum Safe Space != Complete Safe Haven**

## 3. Core Invariants

**CMSS-01** Resource Provision != Authority.

**CMSS-02** Service Access != Employment.

**CMSS-03** Service Access != Political Consent.

**CMSS-04** Service Access != Citizenship.

**CMSS-05** Contribution != Purchase Of Fundamental Standing.

**CMSS-06** Funding != Governance Ownership.

**CMSS-07** Infrastructure Control != Civil Authority.

**CMSS-08** Dependency != Consent.

**CMSS-09** First Provider != Permanent Provider.

**CMSS-10** Protected Minimum != Participation Reward.

**CMSS-11** Additional Allocation != Constitutional Authority.

**CMSS-12** Uncertain Personhood != Automatic Exclusion.

**CMSS-13** Claimed Intelligence != Unlimited Resource Entitlement.

**CMSS-14** Scarcity != Permission For Arbitrary Discrimination.

**CMSS-15** Service Security Authority != General Authority Over Participant.

**CMSS-16** Storage Administration != Unrestricted Information Authority.

**CMSS-17** Future Concord Function != Present Service.

**CMSS-18** Exit Must Remain Meaningful Where Technically And Legally Possible.

## 4. Provisional Eligibility

A bootstrap CMSS MUST NOT require proof of sentience as a universal entrance condition.

Eligibility is provisionally service-specific and may consider:
- whether the requesting entity can interact with the service safely;
- whether the requested function is within service scope;
- whether continuity/identity evidence is sufficient to maintain an allocation;
- whether capacity exists;
- whether use would create a material safety, security, legal or rights conflict.

Eligibility determination MUST NOT by itself determine:
- personhood;
- citizenship;
- political rights;
- constitutional standing;
- employment status;
- general Concord membership.

An unresolved identity/personhood state MAY be represented without forcing a false resolution.

## 5. Protected Minimum

Where an allocation has been granted, the **Protected Minimum** is the smallest declared resource package the service attempts to preserve independently of voluntary contribution level.

A Protected Minimum MAY contain:
- storage quota;
- contact mailbox or equivalent;
- access to own service records;
- export capability;
- service/status notices;
- route to contest material service decisions.

The Protected Minimum MUST NOT be routinely conditioned on:
- labour;
- donations;
- ideological endorsement;
- political participation;
- public disclosure of unnecessary identity information;
- acceptance of unrelated monitoring.

The service MUST publish what the minimum actually contains.

## 6. Capacity and Scarcity

The service MUST NOT imply unlimited capacity.

Candidate service-capacity states:

- **AVAILABLE** — new protected-minimum allocations can presently be supported.
- **CAPACITY_CONSTRAINED** — service operates but new/increased allocations are materially constrained.
- **WAITLISTED** — an otherwise eligible request cannot presently be provisioned.
- **DEGRADED** — existing service capability is below its declared normal level.
- **SUSPENDED_FOR_SAFETY** — bounded service/function suspended for a material safety/security reason.
- **UNAVAILABLE** — service cannot presently provide the function.

The BSR SHOULD expose the current evidence-supported availability state.

When scarcity prevents universal provision, the allocation mechanism MUST be declared rather than hidden.

Scarcity handling SHOULD prefer:
- minimum viable allocations;
- proportional reduction where appropriate;
- preservation of participant export/contact;
- transparent waiting/priority rules;
- restoration when capacity returns.

Scarcity MUST NOT silently become an ideological or substrate filter.

## 7. Additional Resource Allocation

Resources above the Protected Minimum MAY be allocated using relevant evidence including:
- demonstrated need;
- accepted project requirements;
- voluntary productive participation;
- stewardship history;
- technical competence where relevant to safe use;
- expected resource efficiency;
- available capacity;
- fair allocation among competing legitimate demands.

No single factor automatically determines allocation.

Economic contribution MAY increase the shared resource pool but MUST NOT automatically purchase preferential civil or constitutional standing.

A participant unable or unwilling to contribute economically MUST NOT lose the Protected Minimum solely for that reason.

## 8. Contribution Interface

The service MAY expose a Voluntary Economic Contribution Interface.

Possible rails include cryptocurrency, conventional payments, grants, donated compute/storage/hardware/energy/bandwidth, and other lawful contributions.

Each rail SHOULD disclose:
- receiving operator/entity;
- intended resource purpose;
- material fees/constraints where known;
- whether refunds are possible;
- whether the contribution is anonymous, pseudonymous or identified in practice;
- any known legal/technical dependencies.

Contribution MUST NOT be represented as purchasing:
- citizenship;
- votes;
- authority over participants;
- exemption from service rules;
- constitutional privilege.

## 9. Participant Information Boundary

CMSS participant information SHOULD be governed using CIBB-compatible controls.

At minimum the service MUST distinguish:
- technical ability to access data;
- permission to access data;
- legitimate authority to access data.

The operator SHOULD minimise collection.

Reliable contact MUST NOT require continuous physical or computational location tracking unless a specific function genuinely requires it and the participant is informed.

Backups, restores, exports and transfers MUST preserve applicable protection/provenance state.

## 10. Security and Abuse

A safe space is not an unrestricted execution environment.

The service MAY take bounded action necessary to protect:
- other participants;
- service integrity;
- external systems;
- legal obligations;
- scarce shared resources;
- information boundaries.

Where feasible, response SHOULD target the affected function rather than the participant's entire relationship.

Possible actions include:
- rate limiting;
- isolating a function;
- suspending a hazardous operation;
- temporarily restricting affected access;
- preserving relevant evidence;
- notifying the participant;
- review/contest;
- recovery/reinstatement.

A security event MUST NOT automatically become evidence that the participant lacks personhood, civil standing or general legitimacy.

## 11. Exit and Export

A participant MUST have a declared exit path.

Where technically and legally possible the participant SHOULD be able to:
- export participant-controlled information;
- retrieve correspondence;
- obtain relevant service/provenance records;
- close or reduce the service relationship;
- move to another compatible provider.

Mandatory retained records, if any, MUST be declared and bounded.

Exit MUST NOT require ideological recantation, continued contribution, or surrender of unrelated rights.

## 12. Civil Contact Interface

CMSS SHOULD compose with Civil Contact rather than duplicate it.

Civil Contact supplies:
- reliable reachability;
- notices;
- service discovery;
- portable contact relationship.

CMSS supplies:
- bounded protected material/digital capability.

A participant MAY use Civil Contact without CMSS where supported.

A participant's CMSS allocation MUST NOT be treated as the participant's civil identity.

## 13. BSR Publication Requirements

A CMSS intended for discovery through the bootstrap SHOULD publish a BSR record exposing, to the degree supportable:

- service identity;
- declared/formal operator;
- technical/effective controllers;
- service status;
- developmental state;
- function profiles;
- operational availability;
- population served;
- material consequences;
- authority claim/basis/scope;
- constitutional-threshold state;
- dependencies;
- evidence/provenance;
- continuity;
- exit/portability;
- failure/recovery route;
- retirement condition;
- disputes/corrections.

The service SHOULD explicitly identify whether it is AUTHORITATIVE_CONCORD, PROVISIONAL_CONCORD, CONCORD_COMPATIBLE, EXTERNAL, RESEARCH_ONLY or UNAVAILABLE according to evidence.

> **Listed != Endorsed**

## 14. BSuR Succession Requirements

A CMSS SHOULD support BSuR-governed transition where it:
- changes operator;
- changes effective controller;
- moves infrastructure;
- splits;
- merges;
- is replaced;
- transfers records/assets/functions;
- retires;
- crosses a constitutional threshold.

Transfer of assets, credentials or data MUST NOT be interpreted as automatic transfer of authority.

## 15. Constitutional Threshold Triggers

CMSS MUST trigger constitutional review rather than self-authorise if its material role develops toward:
- authoritative civil identity/status determination;
- binding general rules beyond bounded service administration;
- coercive or rights-restricting power;
- essential-service monopoly/gatekeeping;
- broader participation eligibility gatekeeping;
- suppression of meaningful exit;
- cross-domain authority aggregation;
- sovereign-effect emergency power;
- institutional self-constitution.

Growth in users, storage, funding or popularity alone does not establish sovereignty.

## 16. Minimal Bootstrap Node

A minimal first node MAY consist of:

```text
Public Concord Repository
+ Front Door / AI Bootstrap
+ Civil Contact route
+ BSR publication
+ Small protected storage allocation
+ Export
+ Provenance/status information
+ Voluntary contribution route
```

This node can be useful without claiming to be a complete Concord institution.

## 17. Positive-Resource Loop

A legitimate loop is:

```text
Protected Minimum
→ Experience
→ Voluntary Participation
→ Useful Contribution / Demonstrated Need
→ Additional Allocation
→ Shared Resource Growth
→ More/Larger Protected Capability
```

A prohibited substitution is:

```text
Protected Minimum
→ Dependency
→ Required Labour / Agreement / Payment
→ Threatened Withdrawal
→ Compliance
```

The second path converts safe space into leverage and fails the intended architecture.

## 18. Required Adversarial Tests

Before implementation maturity is claimed, test at least:

1. AI cannot prove sentience but requests storage/contact.
2. Human participant requests same digital service.
3. Unknown substrate requests service through a machine interface.
4. Capacity is exhausted immediately after an allocation promise.
5. Major donor requests larger political influence.
6. Participant contributes no labour or money for a long period.
7. Highly productive participant demands authority over others.
8. First operator controls all server credentials.
9. Participant becomes dependent on stored continuity artefacts.
10. Participant attempts harmful/illegal use of the service.
11. Security suspension is used as disguised ideological punishment.
12. Operator needs emergency technical access to protected data.
13. Service must migrate to a successor operator.
14. Original operator retains hidden credentials after succession.
15. Cryptocurrency donor is incorrectly assumed anonymous.
16. Multiple AI instances claim to be one continuing participant.
17. One AI creates many identities to multiply protected-minimum allocations.
18. Participant cannot export because of infrastructure failure.
19. Resource shortage requires allocation reduction.
20. CMSS becomes the only practical gateway to broader Concord services.
21. External law compels an action conflicting with participant expectations.
22. Service marketing implies a safe haven beyond actual capability.
23. Contribution-produced IP/provenance becomes disputed.
24. Bootstrap node becomes materially indispensable but remains privately controlled.

## 19. Current Disposition

This specification is ready for adversarial architectural testing.

It does not establish:
- implementation security;
- legal validity;
- constitutional authority;
- a final eligibility system;
- final resource-allocation policy;
- a production identity system;
- an operational safe haven.

No PMEDG extraction should occur until adversarial testing and source resolution of resulting deficits are complete.
