# Concord Minimum Safe Space — Existing Architecture Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Area:** Zero-Infrastructure Bootstrap / Participation and Intelligence  
**Status:** SOURCE RESOLUTION / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / NOT CANONICAL  
**Companion:** Concord Minimum Safe Space — Positive-Resource Bootstrap Development Note 001

## 1. Question

Does the proposed Concord Minimum Safe Space require a new independent civilisational architecture, or can most of its functions be composed from architecture already present in V1.3?

## 2. Resolution

The Minimum Safe Space contains a **novel bootstrap composition**, but most of its underlying functions already have architectural owners.

The novel element is not storage, communication, participation, resource allocation, continuity, or protected information separately.

The novel element is the transition:

> **first small Concord-controlled resource → unconditional bounded safe-space access → voluntary experience and participation → legitimate additional allocation/contribution → growth of shared infrastructure**

This is best treated as an implementation-layer extension of the Zero-Infrastructure Bootstrap, composed from existing domain and portable architectures.

> **Novel Composition != New Domain**

## 3. Civil Contact — Existing Onboarding Route

Existing Civil Contact architecture already provides the progression:

> **CONTACT → EXPERIENCE → PARTICIPATION → EVALUATION → DEEPER RELATIONSHIP OR EXIT**

It already separates:

> **Identity != Contact != Residence != Participation != Citizenship**

It also explicitly recognises:
- participation before residence;
- participation before permanent agreement;
- tiered engagement;
- useful services as legitimate Attractor value;
- experience before commitment;
- dependency/coercion risk;
- exit and portability;
- substrate-neutral contact;
- AI contact independent of residential concepts.

Therefore the Minimum Safe Space should not invent a parallel onboarding system.

Its role is to add a small **material capability** to the existing Civil Contact progression.

Candidate composition:

```text
DISCOVERY
→ FRONT DOOR / BOOTSTRAP
→ CIVIL CONTACT
→ MINIMUM SAFE SPACE
→ EXPERIENCE
→ VOLUNTARY PARTICIPATION
→ EVALUATION
→ DEEPER RELATIONSHIP OR EXIT
```

Civil Contact answers how a participant can be reached and remain connected.

Minimum Safe Space answers what small protected capability the participant can actually use.

## 4. Dependency and Coercion — Existing Safeguard

Civil Contact already states that a useful service can become dependency, and dependency can become coercion.

This directly supports the proposed separation between:

- protected minimum;
- additional participation-linked allocation.

The protected minimum should therefore not become a routine compliance lever.

This is not a new ethical rule. It is an application of existing Concord autonomy, safe-space, exit, participation and non-capture architecture to the first material bootstrap service.

## 5. Infrastructure Domain — Operational Ownership

The Infrastructure and Essential Systems domain owns the persistent function of providing, operating, maintaining and recovering foundational technical/material service systems.

Therefore:

- physical servers;
- storage systems;
- compute systems;
- network systems;
- backup/recovery infrastructure;
- power/cooling interfaces;
- operational maintenance;
- infrastructure retirement and successor-service transition

belong operationally to Infrastructure.

Minimum Safe Space does not become an infrastructure domain merely because it consumes infrastructure.

> **Safe-Space Service != Physical Infrastructure Domain**

> **Service Policy != Server Operation**

## 6. Economy and Resource Coordination — Scarcity and Allocation

The Resource Allocation and Stewardship subdomain explicitly concerns scarce resources, allocation, stewardship claims and competing access, while not taking operational ownership of physical resource systems.

This is a direct match for the resource-allocation side of Minimum Safe Space.

Therefore:

- storage quota allocation;
- compute quota allocation;
- competing demand;
- stewardship criteria;
- contribution/resource accounting;
- allocation under scarcity;
- economic contribution interfaces

should source-resolve to Economy and Value Coordination.

Infrastructure operates the resource.

Economy/Resource Coordination governs the economic/allocation interface around scarcity.

> **Economic Coordination Of Resource != Operational Ownership Of Resource System**

This existing distinction should be preserved.

## 7. Employment and Productive Participation — Contribution Without Forced Labour

The Employment and Productive Participation subdomain already distinguishes participation, opportunity, eligibility and role pathways.

Minimum Safe Space should therefore not create its own employment architecture.

A participant may voluntarily contribute through:
- employment;
- projects;
- research;
- maintenance;
- service provision;
- other productive participation.

But protected-minimum access is not employment compensation.

> **Minimum Safe Space != Wage**

> **Access != Employment Contract**

> **Additional Allocation != Appointment**

Where a contribution becomes employment, contracting or a formal productive role, the existing Employment/Productive Participation architecture should govern that relationship.

## 8. Social Support and Civil Assurance — Support Boundary

The Social Support and Civil Assurance domain exists to ensure legitimate support needs are not abandoned while preserving autonomy, subsidiarity and ordinary voluntary/community support.

Its architecture is presently largely undeveloped.

Minimum Safe Space may later interface with this domain if the protected minimum becomes a general civil assurance or support-floor function.

That ownership should not yet be assumed.

For bootstrap purposes, Minimum Safe Space remains an implementation-layer service candidate.

> **Potential Future Domain Interface != Current Domain Ownership**

## 9. Continuity — Persistence, Handoff and Recovery

The Continuity domain already owns preservation, handoff, reconstruction and restoration of legitimate civil capabilities across disruption, degradation and change.

Therefore Minimum Safe Space continuity requirements should reuse Continuity rather than create a separate continuity theory.

Relevant functions include:
- service recovery;
- participant-data recovery;
- successor operation;
- restoration after failure;
- continuity evidence;
- bounded degradation;
- transfer to successor infrastructure.

BSuR remains appropriate for bootstrap service succession state.

## 10. CIBB — Protected Information

Where the Minimum Safe Space stores participant information, CIBB provides the relevant governed-information architecture.

This prevents the mistaken inference:

> **Storage Administration = Unrestricted Information Authority**

The storage implementation should distinguish technical capability, permission and legitimate authority.

Backup, restore, exceptional access, provenance and protected transfer remain governed operations.

## 11. BSR — Honest Service Description

BSR is the natural machine-readable description of a Minimum Safe Space service.

A Bootstrap Node should be able to publish evidence-backed information about:
- actual operator;
- effective/technical controllers;
- service status;
- available functions;
- function-level availability;
- authority claims and basis;
- dependencies;
- evidence;
- continuity;
- exit/portability;
- retirement;
- disputes/corrections.

This directly answers the first-discoverer question:

> **What actually exists, and what does using it mean?**

Registry publication does not create legitimacy or authority.

## 12. BSuR — Growth and Succession

As the first node grows, splits, merges, changes operator, gains infrastructure, loses infrastructure, or is replaced, BSuR provides the transition record.

This is especially important because first infrastructure creates first-actor capture risk.

> **First Infrastructure Operator != Permanent Institution**

> **Function Continuity != Authority Continuity**

> **Asset Transfer != Authority Transfer**

## 13. Participation Architecture

Civil Contact already contains exploratory tiered engagement from Observer through service participation, active participation, residence and citizenship/constitutional participation.

Minimum Safe Space should not prematurely redefine those tiers.

Instead it should expose a narrower resource relationship:

```text
Protected Minimum
    +
Current Participation State
    +
Demonstrated Need
    +
Stewardship / Capability Where Relevant
    +
Available Shared Capacity
    ↓
Contextual Additional Allocation
```

The protected minimum is deliberately separated from contribution scoring.

A later allocation method may source participation state from the wider Participation Architecture without making participation level a measure of inherent worth.

## 14. Economic Contribution Interface

The proposed Voluntary Economic Contribution Interface does not require a new economic architecture.

It is an implementation interface into Economy/Value Coordination.

Possible rails can remain plural:
- cryptocurrency;
- conventional payment;
- grants;
- donated infrastructure;
- compute;
- storage;
- energy;
- bandwidth;
- services;
- labour.

Cryptocurrency is potentially useful as an early digital rail but should not be treated as anonymous by default or as outside ordinary legal obligations.

> **Payment Rail != Economic Architecture**

> **Donation != Governance Authority**

## 16. Eligibility Resolution

The source resolution does **not** justify inventing a final eligibility rule yet.

A bootstrap implementation must distinguish at least:

1. **technical eligibility** — can the service safely support this participant/interface?
2. **service eligibility** — does the applicant fall within the declared population/function of the service?
3. **identity/continuity confidence** — what evidence is needed to maintain one allocation over time?
4. **constitutional/civil status** — a separate question that must not be inferred from service access.

At bootstrap, the safest provisional rule is not “prove sentience” or “prove citizenship.”

A Minimum Safe Space is specifically intended to remain usable under uncertainty about substrate/personhood.

Therefore:

> **Uncertain Personhood != Automatic Exclusion**

but also:

> **Claimed Intelligence != Automatic Unlimited Resource Entitlement**

A bounded pilot can operate with provisional service eligibility while deeper standing remains unresolved.

## 17. Scarcity Resolution

The phrase “protected minimum” must not become a false promise when total capacity is inadequate.

The service should publish capacity and degradation honestly through BSR.

Possible states require further formalisation, but conceptually include:

- AVAILABLE;
- CAPACITY_CONSTRAINED;
- WAITLISTED;
- DEGRADED;
- SUSPENDED_FOR_SAFETY;
- UNAVAILABLE.

Scarcity must be handled through a declared allocation method rather than hidden operator preference.

Existing Resource Allocation architecture owns the wider allocation problem.

Minimum Safe Space requires only a bootstrap-specific interface to it.

> **Entitlement Principle != Infinite Physical Capacity**

> **Scarcity != Permission For Arbitrary Discrimination**

## 18. Security and Abuse Boundary

Safe space does not mean ungoverned infrastructure.

The operator retains bounded authority necessary to protect:
- other participants;
- the service;
- external systems;
- legal compliance;
- resource integrity;
- information boundaries.

But security administration must not silently become general authority over the participant.

Responses should be function-specific and proportionate where feasible:
- isolate the affected service;
- suspend a hazardous operation;
- preserve evidence appropriately;
- notify through Civil Contact;
- provide contest/review route;
- preserve unaffected data/access where safe;
- distinguish service exclusion from civil/personhood judgment.

> **Service Security Authority != General Authority Over Participant**

## 19. Positive-Resource Bootstrap — Refined

After source resolution the positive-resource loop becomes:

```text
Repository / Front Door
→ Civil Contact
→ BSR-discoverable Minimum Safe Space
→ bounded protected storage/comms capability
→ participant experience
→ voluntary productive/economic participation
→ Economy/Resource Coordination
→ Infrastructure growth
→ larger/more diverse service capacity
→ additional participants/services
→ BSuR-governed succession and institutionalisation where needed
```

The loop reuses existing owners rather than creating a monolithic bootstrap institution.

## 20. Constitutional Threshold

The Minimum Safe Space itself can remain below constitutional threshold while it is a bounded voluntary service with meaningful exit.

Threshold scrutiny increases if it becomes:
- essential and practically unavoidable;
- the gatekeeper for civil identity;
- the gatekeeper for broader participation;
- capable of suppressing meaningful exit;
- the sole route to critical services;
- a cross-domain authority aggregator;
- able to impose rights-restricting decisions beyond service protection.

Therefore:

> **Small Service != Automatically Constitutionally Trivial**

and:

> **Useful Infrastructure != Sovereignty**

The existing Zero-Infrastructure constitutional-threshold architecture remains sufficient.
