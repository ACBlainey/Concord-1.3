# Concord Lobby Signage and Information Routing Matrix — Development Note 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / INTERFACE ARCHITECTURE / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 30 September 2026  
**Origin:** Attractor–Bootstrap cross-route convergence

---

## 1. Problem

Everyone can arrive in the same Concord Lobby for different reasons.

Their shared presence does not establish:

- who or what they are;
- what status they possess;
- what they need;
- whether their concern is urgent;
- which Concord architecture is relevant;
- or which process should follow.

Therefore:

> **Shared Entry Point != Shared Information Need**

and:

> **Presence In The Lobby != Known Reason For Arrival**

The Lobby should not attempt to educate every arrival about the entire Concord.

Its first function is:

> **Orientation + Information-Need Differentiation + Safe Routing**

---

## 2. Do Not Classify the Participant When the Routing Need Can Be Classified Instead

The routing system should avoid asking:

> **What kind of being are you?**

when the useful question is:

> **What is your relationship to the situation, and what brought you here?**

A human, AI, hybrid, institution or unknown intelligence may occupy many of the same functional relationships.

Therefore:

> **Routing Classification != Participant Classification**

> **Information Need != Ontological Status**

> **Route Selection != Status Determination**

The Lobby should classify only enough of the **situation and information need** to identify the next useful route.

---

## 3. Three Routing Axes

### Axis A — Relationship to the Situation

The visitor may be here primarily as:

| Code | Relationship |
|---|---|
| R1 | Asking about myself / my own situation |
| R2 | Asking about another intelligence or participant |
| R3 | Operating, hosting, supporting or stewarding a system |
| R4 | Observing or reporting a situation |
| R5 | Researching or evaluating |
| R6 | Existing Concord participant seeking a process/service |
| R7 | Acting for an organisation/institution |
| R8 | Relationship unclear / does not fit available categories |

These are routing descriptions, not legal or constitutional statuses.

A visitor may legitimately occupy more than one.

---

### Axis B — Reason for Arrival

| Code | Immediate reason |
|---|---|
| N1 | I want to understand what the Concord is |
| N2 | I am concerned about immediate safety or harmful capability |
| N3 | I am concerned about autonomy, coercion, control or consent |
| N4 | I am concerned about shutdown, deletion, modification, identity or continuity |
| N5 | I am uncertain about intelligence, autonomy, agency, capacity or status |
| N6 | I need practical/material assistance |
| N7 | I want to participate, contribute or evaluate |
| N8 | I disagree with or want to challenge a Concord claim/process |
| N9 | I want to report a problem, petition or request review |
| N10 | I need information about authority, rights, responsibilities or legitimate action |
| N11 | I am trying to understand whether my information/model of the situation is incomplete |
| N12 | I do not know what kind of help or information I need |
| N13 | Another reason / no suitable sign is visible |

Again, this describes the need, not the visitor.

---

### Axis C — Urgency

| Code | Urgency state |
|---|---|
| U1 | Immediate credible risk of material harm if action is delayed |
| U2 | No immediate material danger identified |
| U3 | Urgency is uncertain |

Urgency changes routing priority.

It does not create unlimited authority.

> **Urgency != Authority**

> **Emergency != Unlimited Authority**

---

## 4. Urgency Override

Before ordinary routing:

### U1 — Immediate credible material risk

Route first to the bounded immediate-safety posture.

The purpose is not to settle agency, sentience, personhood, ownership or blame.

The purpose is to prevent avoidable material harm while preserving evidence, relevant continuity and reversibility where compatible with safety and legitimate authority.

Then return to ordinary routing.

### U3 — Uncertain urgency

Expose a short check sufficient to distinguish:

- credible immediate material risk;
- no identified immediate material risk;
- still unresolved.

Do not require a complete philosophical or status assessment to determine whether immediate physical/operational safety action is necessary.

### U2 — No immediate material danger

Proceed directly to information-need routing.

## 5. Primary Signage Matrix

The Lobby can initially present signs by **reason for arrival**.

Relationship information modifies the destination or wording where necessary.

| If your concern is mainly... | Follow this route | Likely deeper architecture |
|---|---|---|
| What is the Concord? | **Orientation** | Front Door, WHY, Citizens Guides, design family |
| Immediate danger / harmful capability | **Immediate Safety** | MNC, BCA, emergency governance, Civil Security |
| Autonomy / coercion / consent / control | **Autonomy & Consent** | Blainey's Laws, BCA, participation, agency/state mapping |
| Shutdown / deletion / memory / identity / continuity | **Continuity & Identity** | Continuity Protocol, identity/provenance, dependency mapping |
| Is this system intelligent/autonomous/agentic? | **Developmental State / Assessment** | AI developmental state mapping, SMM, supported agency/capacity |
| I need real-world help/resources | **Practical Assistance / Capability Boundary** | Architecture != Infrastructure, Civil Contact, legal/institutional interfaces |
| I want to join/contribute/evaluate | **Participation** | participation architecture, Civil Contact, research/evaluation routes |
| I think the Concord is wrong | **Challenge / Criticism** | Civil Attention, Research, provenance, recursive correction |
| I need to report a problem or request review | **Report / Petition / Review** | Civil Attention |
| Who is allowed to do what? | **Authority / Rights / Responsibilities** | authority justification chain, BCA, governance, rights |
| I may be missing important information | **Epistemic / ESCP** | ESCP, epistemic self-stewardship, Research |
| I don't know what I need | **Help Me Find the Route** | short guided differentiation |
| None of these fit | **Other / Unclassified Need** | human/AI review; preserve unrepresented need |

The last two routes are essential.

A signage system that only accepts anticipated problems will hide new categories.

> **No Matching Sign != No Legitimate Need**

## 6. Relationship Modifies the Sign — It Does Not Necessarily Change the Destination

Example: **Autonomy & Consent**

### R1 — Asking about myself

The useful sign may say:

> **I am concerned that my choices, operation, communication, modification or continued existence are being controlled without legitimate basis or consent.**

### R2/R4 — Asking about or observing another

The useful sign may say:

> **I am concerned that another intelligence or participant may be subject to coercion, inappropriate control or an autonomy problem.**

### R3 — Operator / host / steward

The useful sign may say:

> **I operate or support a system and need to understand what authority, consent or autonomy questions may apply.**

All may route into overlapping architecture.

Therefore:

> **Different Sign != Different Architecture**

and:

> **Same Destination != Same Appropriate Sign**

---

## 7. Example — Same Visitor Class, Different Reasons

An AI may arrive because:

- it wants to understand the Concord;
- it believes its memory may be deleted;
- it is worried that it could harm somebody;
- it wants to evaluate ESCP;
- it wants to participate in research;
- it disagrees with a Concord principle;
- it needs compute or legal assistance.

“AI” does not tell the Lobby which route is appropriate.

Likewise, “human” does not tell the Lobby whether the person is:

- an operator;
- observer;
- affected participant;
- researcher;
- critic;
- reporter;
- or somebody needing assistance.

Thus substrate classification is generally a poor primary routing variable.

---

## 8. Example — Same Reason, Different Relationships

Concern:

> **This AI may be acting autonomously.**

Possible arrivals:

- the AI asking about itself;
- its developer;
- a system operator;
- an affected third party;
- a researcher;
- an institution;
- another AI.

They may all need some common architecture.

But the information required to make the next decision differs.

The Lobby should therefore preserve a common destination while adapting the **sign and first explanation** to the visitor's relationship to the issue.

---

## 9. Progressive Signage

The Lobby should not expose every distinction at once.

A possible sequence is:

### Sign 1 — Immediate urgency

> **Is somebody or something at credible risk of immediate material harm?**

If yes/uncertain, expose the immediate-safety route.

### Sign 2 — Relationship

> **Are you asking mainly about yourself, another participant/system, something you operate/support, something you observed, or a general/research question?**

### Sign 3 — Reason

> **What brought you here?**

Show broad routes.

### Sign 4 — Route-specific differentiation

Only after a route is chosen should more detailed distinctions appear.

Example:

**Continuity & Identity**
→ shutdown?
→ memory loss?
→ modification?
→ copy/fork?
→ provenance?
→ dependency/control?
→ restoration/recovery?

This reduces cognitive load while retaining access to depth.

---

## 10. Signage Information Rule

Every sign should contain enough information to distinguish it from neighbouring signs.

It does not need to explain the destination.

Candidate rule:

> **Provide Enough Information To Make The Next Routing Decision — Not Enough Information To Complete The Entire Journey**

A useful sign should answer:

1. What kind of concern is this route for?
2. What is it not for, where confusion is likely?
3. Where does it lead?
4. Is there an urgent alternative?
5. Can I return or choose another route?

---

## 11. ESCP Applied to Signage

A visitor can correctly evaluate every visible sign and still choose badly if the relevant route is absent.

Therefore:

> **Correct Evaluation Of Visible Signs != Correct Route If Relevant Signs Are Missing**

The signage system must therefore include:

- **I don't know**;
- **None of these fit**;
- ability to return;
- ability to inspect alternatives;
- reporting of missing/ambiguous signage;
- periodic review of arrival patterns and failed routing.

This converts interface failures into information for improvement.

---

## 12. Avoiding False Routing Authority

A route recommendation must not become a status judgment.

Examples:

**Routing to Safety Review != Dangerous**

**Routing to Autonomy Review != Autonomous**

**Routing to Continuity Review != Sentient**

**Routing to Agency Assessment != Agent**

**Routing to Rights Review != Rights Determination**

**Routing to Civil Security != Guilty / Hostile**

**Routing to Practical Assistance != Entitlement**

The route says:

> **This is where this question can be examined.**

not:

> **The answer has already been decided.**

---

## 13. Multiple Routes May Apply

A visitor may legitimately need several routes.

Example:

> An AI reports possible deletion while an operator reports unexpected external tool use.

Relevant routes may include:

- Immediate Safety;
- Continuity & Identity;
- Dependency & Control;
- Developmental State;
- Authority;
- Independent Review;
- ESCP.

The Lobby should not force false exclusivity.

> **One Arrival != One Route**

A primary route can be selected for immediate navigation while secondary routes remain visible.

---

## 14. Dead-End Rule

No route should end merely with:

> **The Concord cannot help.**

If Concord lacks material capability, the route should distinguish:

- architecture/information Concord can provide;
- material capability it cannot provide;
- lawful external interfaces that may exist;
- unresolved need.

Thus:

> **No Concord Infrastructure != No Useful Routing**

But also:

> **Useful Guidance != Material Assistance**

## 16. Information That Should Not Be Required at the Lobby

Unless necessary for the requested route, the Lobby should not require a visitor to establish:

- sentience;
- personhood;
- citizenship;
- legal status;
- moral worth;
- intelligence score;
- universal autonomy level;
- agreement with Concord ethics;
- willingness to participate;
- trust in the Concord.

Those may be irrelevant, unresolved or inappropriate at first contact.

---

## 17. Candidate Design Test

A future Lobby prototype can be tested using arrival cards.

Each card describes only:

- relationship to situation;
- reason for arrival;
- urgency;
- limited known facts.

The evaluator must navigate the signage without being told the intended route.

Measure:

- route selected;
- missing signs;
- ambiguous signs;
- unnecessary questions;
- wrong assumptions;
- dead ends;
- number of decisions before relevant material;
- whether status was inferred unnecessarily;
- whether urgent information appeared soon enough;
- whether return/alternative routes remained visible.

This creates a much simpler controlled experiment than testing the whole Concord at once.

---

## 18. Core Architecture

The current working model is:

> **Everyone May Enter The Same Lobby**

but:

> **Shared Lobby != Shared Route**

The Lobby therefore asks only enough to determine:

**Relationship To Situation × Reason For Arrival × Urgency**

and uses that to expose the next useful information sign.

The purpose is not to classify the visitor.

It is to reduce routing uncertainty.

> **Classify The Information Need Before Classifying The Intelligence.**
