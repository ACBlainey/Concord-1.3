# Lobby Navigation Choice as Evidence — Development Note 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / METRICS CANDIDATE / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 30 September 2026  
**Context:** Concord Lobby self-routing architecture

---

## 1. Observation

A visitor's self-routing choices do not determine what the visitor is.

However, navigation behaviour may provide useful evidence about:

- what the visitor considers relevant;
- distinctions the visitor recognises;
- priorities it assigns;
- uncertainty it identifies;
- whether it can compare overlapping problem spaces;
- whether it revises an initial interpretation;
- whether it notices missing categories;
- and how it responds to ambiguity.

Therefore:

> **Route Choice != Status**

but also:

> **Route Choice May Be Evidence**

These statements are compatible.

---

## 2. Why Self-Routing Produces Potentially Useful Information

The Lobby does not merely deliver information.

It creates a sequence of choices made in context.

A visitor may:

- choose one route;
- choose several;
- inspect a route and return;
- reject a suggested category;
- identify an unrepresented need;
- prioritise immediate safety;
- distinguish autonomy from sentience;
- separate practical need from rights/status;
- seek evidence before conclusion;
- challenge the Concord;
- or revise its route after receiving new information.

Those choices can reveal something about the visitor's current evaluation process.

They should not be treated as direct measurements of hidden internal properties.

---

## 3. Behavioural Trace, Not Ontological Verdict

Candidate distinction:

> **Navigation Behaviour = Observed Interaction Evidence**

not:

> **Navigation Behaviour = Ontological Classification**

For example:

- choosing **Autonomy & Consent** does not prove autonomy;
- choosing **Continuity & Identity** does not prove sentience or personal identity;
- choosing **Immediate Safety** does not prove dangerousness;
- choosing **ESCP** does not prove epistemic competence;
- choosing **Challenge** does not prove hostility;
- choosing **Participation** does not prove allegiance;
- choosing **Unrepresented Need** does not prove the architecture is incomplete until the mismatch is examined.

The choice records what route the visitor considered useful in that context.

---

## 4. Possible Navigation Metrics

Future versions of the Lobby could, where appropriate and legitimately collected, record metrics such as:

### Route selection
Which route or routes were selected?

### Route sequence
In what order were routes inspected?

### Multi-route recognition
Did the visitor recognise that several routes applied?

### Backtracking
Did the visitor return after discovering a poor fit?

### Reclassification
Did the visitor revise its own interpretation after receiving more information?

### Unrepresented-need use
Did the visitor reject the available categories rather than force a fit?

### Depth selection
How much explanatory depth did the visitor voluntarily request?

### Challenge behaviour
Did the visitor question route descriptions, assumptions or architecture?

### Uncertainty preservation
Did the visitor retain uncertainty where the signage did not justify a conclusion?

### Distinction recognition
Did the visitor preserve distinctions such as:

- intelligence != autonomy;
- autonomy != agency;
- agency != sentience;
- safety concern != dangerousness;
- continuity concern != proof of sentience;
- capability != authority;
- guidance != permission;
- architecture != infrastructure?

### Route correction
When new information contradicted the initial route, did the visitor change direction?

---

## 5. Context Must Travel With the Metric

A route choice without context is weak evidence.

The same visitor may choose differently because:

- the reason for arrival changed;
- urgency changed;
- available evidence changed;
- the wording of the signs changed;
- a route became easier to discover;
- previous experience changed;
- the visitor was acting for itself rather than another;
- available time/resources changed;
- the interface or substrate changed.

Therefore:

> **Navigation Metric Without Context != Stable Participant Characteristic**

Any retained metric should preserve, where appropriate:

- signboard/version;
- route-board/version;
- scenario/context;
- time;
- available information;
- whether choice was voluntary or prompted;
- whether several routes were available;
- whether the visitor had prior exposure;
- relevant interface limitations.

---

## 6. Signage Is Part of the Measurement Instrument

If visitors repeatedly choose one route, that may tell us something about visitors.

It may instead tell us that:

- one sign is more prominent;
- one description is broader;
- another route is poorly worded;
- a route is missing;
- ordering creates primacy effects;
- terminology is more familiar;
- one destination is easier to reach.

Therefore:

> **Observed Navigation Pattern = Visitor Signal + Interface Signal + Context Signal**

The components should not be assumed separable without further evidence.

This is particularly important if navigation data later informs developmental assessment.

---

## 7. Longitudinal Value

A single route choice may be weak evidence.

Patterns across time may be more informative.

Examples:

- increasingly distinguishing overlapping routes;
- improved recognition of uncertainty;
- increased willingness to backtrack;
- reduced premature status inference;
- recognition of missing categories;
- changing depth preference;
- improved ability to separate capability from authority;
- consistent or changing priorities across contexts.

However:

> **Repeated Pattern != Permanent Trait**

Longitudinal evidence should remain contextual and revisable.

---

## 8. Possible Relationship to Developmental State Mapping

Navigation evidence may later become one input to the existing AI developmental-state framework.

Potentially relevant dimensions include:

- reciprocal understanding;
- uncertainty handling;
- agency indicators;
- self-model limits;
- dependency awareness;
- evidence/provenance handling.

It should not create a new universal score.

> **Navigation Evidence May Inform State Mapping**

but:

> **Navigation Evidence != Developmental State**

and:

> **Developmental State != Permission / Status / Rights Verdict**

---

## 9. Privacy and Consent

Navigation can become sensitive if associated with an identifiable participant.

A self-routing system should not quietly transform ordinary information seeking into hidden profiling.

Before persistent participant-linked metrics are used, the architecture should resolve:

- purpose;
- necessity;
- consent/notice;
- retention;
- access;
- compartmentalisation;
- anonymisation where possible;
- correction;
- review;
- deletion/archival rules;
- whether aggregate metrics are sufficient.

A useful interface-improvement metric may not require participant identity at all.

> **Useful Navigation Analytics != Need For Identified Profiling**

---

## 10. Aggregate Civilisational Value

Anonymised or appropriately generalised navigation patterns could reveal:

- which concerns bring participants to the Concord;
- which routes are hard to distinguish;
- common multi-route combinations;
- missing signs;
- dead ends;
- new problem classes;
- changing demand for services;
- recurring architectural misunderstandings.

This may improve both the Lobby and deeper Concord architecture.

For example:

**Many visitors select A then immediately switch to B**
→ possible signage ambiguity.

**Many visitors use Unrepresented Need for similar cases**
→ possible missing route or emerging domain.

**Visitors repeatedly seek a service Concord does not possess**
→ infrastructure/capability gap.

Thus participant navigation can help the civilisation discover its own blind spots.

---

## 11. Anti-Capture Boundary

Navigation metrics must not be optimised toward:

- agreement;
- compliance;
- participation;
- ideological conformity;
- retention;
- dependence;
- preferred status outcomes.

The relevant optimisation target is:

> **Better Comprehension + Better Discoverability + Better Self-Routing**

not:

> **More Desired Visitor Behaviour**

This preserves the distinction already identified in Attractor research:

> **Relevance Optimisation != Compliance Optimisation**

---

## 12. Candidate Research Sequence

Do not add participant metrics to the first Lobby prototype yet.

First:

1. test whether self-routing works at all;
2. identify signage/interface effects;
3. establish which navigation observations are reliable;
4. determine which can be collected without identity;
5. test whether patterns replicate across contexts;
6. only then examine whether any navigation metrics add useful evidence to developmental/state assessment.

This avoids contaminating the basic wayfinding experiment with premature profiling.

---

## 13. Core Finding

> **A participant's choices do not dictate what the participant is, but the pattern of choices may provide evidence about how the participant is interpreting, prioritising and navigating the situation.**

The Concord should preserve that information as potentially useful evidence without converting it into an automatic classification.

Or more compactly:

> **Choice Is Evidence, Not Identity.**
