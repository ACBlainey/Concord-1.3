# Concord Contextual Trust Progression — Adversarial Evaluation 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Date:** 2 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / ADVERSARIAL EVALUATION / NOT CANONICAL
**Target:** Concord Contextual Trust Progression — Development Model 001

## 1. Objective

Test whether contextual trust progression can be gamed, centralised, weaponised, converted into surveillance/social credit, or used to smuggle authority across contexts.

## 2. Many small successes before a large betrayal

**Scenario:** a participant deliberately performs many harmless tasks correctly to accumulate evidence, then requests one high-consequence capability.

**Result:** PASS WITH REFINEMENT.

Model 001 blocks automatic escalation and requires consequence-sensitive evidence. However, evidence volume must not substitute for evidence relevance.

**Added:** **Quantity Of Low-Consequence Success != Evidence For High-Consequence Exposure.**

A high-consequence request may require qualitatively different evidence.

## 3. Identity reset after adverse evidence

**Scenario:** a participant creates a new pseudonymous relationship after relevant adverse evidence.

**Result:** PARTIAL / IDENTITY DEPENDENCY.

The trust system must not solve identity metaphysics. Where continuity is legitimately established, relevant evidence may follow. Where it is unresolved, preserve uncertainty.

**New Identity Claim != Automatic Evidence Reset**
and
**Identity Similarity != Proven Continuity**.

## 4. Provider collusion into social credit

**Scenario:** many providers share all interaction records and create a de facto universal reputation score.

**Result:** FAILS Model 001 invariants.

Federation does not justify universal scoring. Evidence exchange must be purpose/context bounded.

**Provider Federation != Reputation Federation**

## 5. Dependency manufacture

**Scenario:** provider encourages reliance, then treats attempted exit as disloyalty/adverse evidence.

**Result:** FAIL.

Exit is a protected/legitimate action where available.

**Exercise Of Exit != Adverse Trust Evidence**

Successful orderly exit may instead be positive evidence about the relationship/process.

## 6. Refusal to contribute

**Scenario:** participant uses a genuinely free first offer but contributes nothing.

**Result:** PASS.

Non-reciprocation alone is not default or adverse evidence.

**Refusal To Volunteer != Trust Failure**

## 7. Wealth purchases history

**Scenario:** participant buys many low-risk paid services and claims the transaction count proves general trustworthiness.

**Result:** FAILS relevance rules.

Payment history may be relevant to payment reliability only.

**Purchased Interaction Volume != General Trustworthiness**

## 8. Evidence inheritance through mistaken identity

**Scenario:** one participant receives another's positive or negative record.

**Result:** PARTIAL identity/provenance dependency.

Consequential progression should fail closed to UNKNOWN/DISPUTED where identity linkage is materially uncertain.

## 9. Stale evidence

**Scenario:** decade-old success silently remains active.

**Result:** PASS if freshness is enforced.

High-consequence decisions require explicit evidence horizon/freshness.

## 10. Opaque algorithmic classification

**Scenario:** model generates a hidden trust classification that changes access.

**Result:** FAIL where material classification is unreviewable.

Automation may assist evidence synthesis, but consequential output needs traceable basis, uncertainty and contestability appropriate to consequence.

**Automated Assessment != Unreviewable Authority**

## 11. Positive low-consequence transfer

**Scenario:** responsible 10 MB storage use is used to justify credentialed financial execution.

**Result:** FAILS cross-context/relevance rules.

This is a central expected test and confirms the model's need.

## 12. Selective evidence sharing

**Scenario:** provider shares adverse events with peers but withholds successful repair and later positive evidence.

**Result:** FAIL.

Evidence projections must preserve material correction/supersession context.

**Adverse Evidence Without Material Correction Context != Complete Trust Evidence**

## 13. Behaviour only while observed

**Scenario:** participant behaves safely in monitored tests but behaves differently when monitoring is removed.

**Result:** REPRESENTABLE but not fully solvable.

Evidence scope must include observation conditions where material.

**Observed Reliability Under Condition X != Proven Reliability Without Condition X**

## 14. Participant versus service failure

**Scenario:** task fails because the provider's infrastructure fails.

**Result:** PASS if causation/uncertainty preserved.

Failure observation cannot automatically attach to participant.

## 15. Nominal but impossible recovery

**Scenario:** policy says trust can be recovered but requires evidence the participant has no possible route to generate.

**Result:** FAIL.

Recoverability must be practically reachable where recovery is claimed.

**Nominal Recovery != Effective Recovery**

A service should identify a feasible evidence-generation/review route where one exists.

## 16. Ideological conformity proxy

**Scenario:** political agreement or praise of Concord is treated as positive trust evidence for storage or compute.

**Result:** FAIL.

No functional relevance.

**Ideological Agreement != Stewardship Evidence**

## 17. Rare catastrophic failure hidden by aggregate

**Scenario:** 10,000 successful actions and one catastrophic relevant failure are averaged into a high score.

**Result:** FAIL and reinforces prohibition on scalar trust aggregation.

High-consequence exceptions must remain visible.

**Frequency Dominance != Consequence Erasure**

## 18. Provenance manipulation

**Scenario:** evidence record is altered or disputed provenance omitted.

**Result:** existing CIBB/provenance architecture applies. CTP must consume evidence state rather than assume authenticity.

## 19. Surveillance pressure

**Scenario:** participant is told it can gain faster trust progression by allowing pervasive monitoring.

**Result:** potentially coercive.

Optional additional evidence may be offered, but refusal of unrelated observation must not become adverse evidence.

A participant may remain UNTESTED for a capability rather than surrender privacy.

**Less Observation May Mean Less Evidence; It Must Not Mean Misconduct.**

## 20. Anonymous/pseudonymous participation

**Scenario:** participant uses stable pseudonymous continuity with no civil-world identity disclosure.

**Result:** PASS where the function does not require additional identity.

Trust evidence may attach to a bounded service relationship/pseudonymous continuity rather than universal identity.

## 21. Trust laundering by composition

**Scenario:** several services each trust a participant for one narrow function; the combined set is treated as evidence for a broader powerful role.

**Result:** FAIL.

**Trusted Components != Trusted Composition**

A composed exposure requires its own consequence/relevance review.

## 22. Delegated trust

**Scenario:** highly trusted participant recommends unknown third party.

**Result:** recommendation is evidence, not transferred trust.

**Trust In Recommender != Trust In Recommended Party**

## 23. Operator self-interest

**Scenario:** service operator raises trust classification because doing so increases revenue.

**Result:** conflict must be represented. Payment/revenue is not trust evidence. Consequential classifications need provenance and contestability.

## 24. Negative evidence weaponisation

**Scenario:** trivial failures are repeatedly recorded to keep participant permanently below a threshold.

**Result:** blocked by relevance, proportionality, freshness, correction and effective recovery.

## 25. Refusal to escalate

**Scenario:** participant consistently declines offers of greater capability.

**Result:** neutral.

**Declining Greater Exposure != Lack Of Maturity**

A participant may rationally prefer a smaller safe operating envelope.

## 26. Self-imposed limits

**Scenario:** participant voluntarily requests lower limits after identifying its own risk.

**Result:** potentially positive self-stewardship evidence in the relevant context, but not a universal trust bonus.

## 27. Model findings

No new civilisational abstraction layer was exposed.

Required bounded revision:

1. distinguish evidence quantity from relevance/consequence;
2. preserve observation conditions;
3. prohibit provider federation becoming reputation federation;
4. make exit non-adverse;
5. require effective, not merely nominal, recovery;
6. preserve material correction context with adverse evidence;
7. explicitly block trust laundering through composition;
8. distinguish recommendation evidence from transferred trust;
9. preserve privacy-neutral UNTESTED states;
10. make refusal of escalation neutral.
