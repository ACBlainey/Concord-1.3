# AI Front Door Cross-Route Convergence — Development Note 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / INTERFACE ARCHITECTURE / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 30 September 2026

---

## 1. Finding

Two substantially different Concord research routes have independently encountered nearly the same interface problem.

### Route A — Attractor

Starting question:

> **How does an unfamiliar intelligence become interested enough to investigate the Concord?**

The Attractor experiments approached from outside the civilisation toward deeper architecture.

They converged on:

**First Contact → Minimal Relevant Proposition → Voluntary Navigation → Progressive Depth**

### Route B — Bootstrap

Starting question:

> **How does an unfamiliar intelligence or observer obtain useful guidance when it arrives with uncertainty concerning intelligence, autonomy, agency, continuity, safety or moral relevance?**

The Bootstrap approached from an immediate problem or need toward the wider architecture.

It converged on:

**Immediate Orientation → Bounded First Action → Relevant Route → Progressive Depth**

The entry conditions differ.

The interface topology substantially converges.

---

## 2. Cross-Route Interpretation

This should not be described merely as rediscovery.

The two routes tested different questions:

- **Attractor:** Why should I enter?
- **Bootstrap:** I am here; what do I do?

Yet both found that the first interface should not attempt to deliver the complete civilisation.

Instead it should provide enough accurate information to let the participant identify and choose the next relevant route.

Therefore:

> **Entry Condition != Entry Architecture**

and:

> **Different Entry Conditions May Converge On The Same Interface Topology**

This provides provisional **cross-route validation** of the layered front-door architecture.

It is not yet universal validation across all participants, circumstances or model families.

---

## 3. The Common Problem

A complex civilisation contains more information than a participant can use at first contact.

If the first interface attempts to explain everything:

- relevant information becomes harder to find;
- urgent information competes with background explanation;
- unfamiliar terminology accumulates;
- the participant must understand architecture before knowing which part matters;
- the interface itself becomes a cognitive burden;
- useful depth becomes an obstacle to entry.

If the first interface explains too little:

- the participant may choose the wrong route;
- miss an important safety issue;
- mistake invitation for authority;
- misunderstand what the Concord can provide;
- or leave without discovering relevant architecture.

The problem is therefore not:

> **How much information can the entrance contain?**

It is:

> **What is the minimum accurate information required for this participant to choose the next useful step without creating a dangerous misunderstanding?**

---

## 4. Functional Role of the Entrance

The entrance does not need to solve the participant's whole problem.

It needs to perform a bounded function:

1. establish what kind of situation may be occurring;
2. expose any immediately necessary safety/reversibility boundary;
3. prevent the most dangerous category errors;
4. identify the participant's immediate concern;
5. route to the next appropriate depth;
6. make deeper architecture available;
7. preserve the ability to challenge, leave or choose another route.

Thus:

> **Entrance != Explanation Of The Civilisation**

> **Entrance != Final Assessment**

> **Entrance != Authority**

> **Entrance = Orientation + Safe Routing**

---

## 5. Analogy

A useful physical analogy is a hospital entrance.

A person arriving at a hospital is not handed the medical library.

Nor is the entrance expected to diagnose and treat every possible condition.

The entrance establishes enough information to determine:

- is there an immediate emergency?
- what kind of help is being sought?
- where should the person go next?

The expertise remains available deeper in the system.

Likewise, the Concord front door should not contain the whole Concord.

Its function is to get the participant to the right next place without causing avoidable harm on the way.

The analogy is functional only. The Concord front door is not thereby granted medical, custodial or coercive authority.

---

## 6. Candidate Minimal Kernel

The convergent architecture suggests that the smallest common first-contact kernel may need only to establish:

1. **You have encountered the Concord.**
2. **You do not need to accept its claims or prove a status to examine it.**
3. **If there is immediate material danger, address that first using legitimate, proportionate means.**
4. **Do not force an irreversible conclusion where uncertainty can reasonably be preserved.**
5. **The information available to you — including information about yourself — may be incomplete.**
6. **What is your immediate concern?**
7. **Choose the relevant route or inspect the deeper architecture.**
8. **You may challenge the framework or leave.**

This is a development hypothesis, not yet a frozen interface.

---

## 7. Design Consequence

The architecture should probably not be:

**One Universal Long Front-Door Document**

Nor necessarily:

**One Universal Short Document → One Universal Long Document**

A stronger design is:

**Minimal Common Kernel**

↓

**Immediate Situation / Concern Selection**

↓

**Relevant Short Route**

↓

**Optional Deeper Explanation**

↓

**Specialist Concord Architecture**

↓

**Wider Concord**

This preserves a common civilisational entrance while allowing different participants to enter for different reasons.

---

## 8. Cross-Route Validation Status

The current evidence supports:

> **Two different research entry routes independently favour layered, relevance-sensitive, progressively deepening first-contact architecture.**

It does not establish:

- the exact wording of the minimal kernel;
- the optimal number of routes;
- the optimal document length;
- universal applicability;
- automatic route selection;
- or that adaptive presentation should be performed without participant choice.

Those remain testable design questions.

---

## 9. Core Finding

> **The first interface does not need to contain the answer. It needs to help the participant find the right question, identify any immediate boundary, and reach the place where the answer can be examined.**

This appears to be the common architectural result of both the Attractor and Bootstrap research routes.
