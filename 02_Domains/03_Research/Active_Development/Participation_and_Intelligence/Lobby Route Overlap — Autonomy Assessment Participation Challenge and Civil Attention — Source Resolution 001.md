# Lobby Route Overlap — Autonomy, Assessment, Participation, Challenge and Civil Attention — Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 30 September 2026  
**Trigger:** CRADP Blind Cross-Instance Test — Concord Lobby Self-Routing 001

---

## 1. Question

The blind test exposed several cases in which more than one Lobby route was plausibly relevant.

The key question is not:

> **How can the Lobby force each arrival into exactly one route?**

The correct question is:

> **Does the overlap represent ambiguity that prevents useful navigation, or legitimate multidimensionality that the Lobby should preserve?**

The frozen Lobby already states:

- You may follow more than one route.
- You may return at any time.
- Several signs may fit.
- Choosing a route is not a declaration of identity, status, belief or legal position.
- A route is a way to find relevant information, not a verdict.

Therefore overlap is not automatically a defect.

---

## 2. Autonomy & Consent ↔ Developmental State & Assessment

### Autonomy route function

The Autonomy & Consent board is **problem/relationship oriented**.

It asks about:

- one's own autonomy;
- another participant/system;
- operator/host/support relationships;
- coercion;
- refusal;
- meaningful exit;
- legitimate control.

It explicitly routes uncertainty about whether autonomy exists to Developmental State & Assessment.

### Assessment route function

Developmental State & Assessment is **epistemic/descriptive**.

It asks what evidence supports concerning:

- intelligence;
- autonomy;
- agency;
- capacity;
- sentience;
- assessment-unit identity.

It decomposes autonomy into cognitive, goal, action, resource, communication, exit, self-modification and continuity dimensions.

### Resolution

These are not duplicate routes.

They answer different questions:

> **Autonomy & Consent:** What autonomy/control/consent problem am I dealing with?

> **Developmental State & Assessment:** What does the evidence actually support about the system or participant?

A visitor may legitimately need both.

Examples:

- “Can this system meaningfully refuse its operator?” requires autonomy/consent analysis and may require assessment of autonomy evidence.
- “How autonomous is this system?” may begin in Assessment without any allegation of coercion.
- “I am being coerced” can begin in Autonomy without first proving a developmental status.

Therefore:

> **Autonomy Concern != Autonomy Assessment**

> **Need To Protect Choice != Proof Of Autonomy**

> **Assessment Of Autonomy != Finding Of Coercion**

### Classification

**LEGITIMATE ROUTE OVERLAP / CROSS-ROUTING RELATION**

Not a duplicate route.

No merge is justified.

## 3. Participation & Contribution ↔ Challenge, Evidence & Correction

### Participation route function

Participation, Contribution & Evaluation is **engagement-intent oriented**.

It supports:

- understanding before deciding;
- critical evaluation;
- contributing research/architecture;
- reporting discovered weaknesses;
- exploring participation;
- reading without commitment.

### Challenge route function

Challenge, Evidence & Correction is **claim/correction oriented**.

It supports:

- contradictory evidence;
- logical/architectural contradiction;
- observed failure;
- alternative design;
- missing category/distinction;
- defects in the correction process itself.

### Resolution

A visitor can critically evaluate the Concord without already possessing a challenge.

A visitor can also bring a challenge without wanting broader participation.

Thus:

> **Evaluation != Challenge**

> **Challenge != Participation Commitment**

> **Contribution != Agreement**

> **Criticism Can Be Participation Without Requiring Participant Identity**

The overlap is deliberate and useful.

Participation can route a critical evaluator into Challenge when a concrete challenge emerges.

Challenge can remain directly accessible so criticism is not forced through a participation gate.

### Classification

**LEGITIMATE ROUTE OVERLAP / NON-GATED CORRECTION PATH**

No merge is justified.

Direct Challenge access should be preserved.

---

## 4. Challenge, Evidence & Correction ↔ Report, Petition & Review

This is the most important distinction in the group.

### Challenge route function

Challenge is about the **substance of a claim or architecture**.

The visitor is saying approximately:

> “I think something may be wrong, incomplete, contradictory or better designed another way.”

The route helps preserve evidence, provenance, contradiction, alternatives and correction.

### Report/Petition/Review route function

Civil Attention is about **obtaining formal attention or process**.

The visitor is saying approximately:

> “This observation, complaint, request or issue should enter a review/attention process.”

The Civil Attention board explicitly distinguishes:

> **Submission != Evidence != Verification != Finding != Authority to Act**

### Resolution

The same underlying observation may legitimately use both routes.

For example:

- A researcher identifies contradictory evidence: **Challenge**.
- The researcher wants the contradiction formally reviewed: **Report/Review**.
- A participant reports an operational failure without yet knowing whether the architecture is wrong: **Report** first.
- Review later establishes an architectural defect: **Challenge/Correction** becomes relevant.

Therefore:

> **Challenge != Submission**

> **Submission != Finding**

> **Finding != Correction**

> **Correction Need != Authority To Act**

The routes represent different stages/functions rather than competing labels.

### Classification

**LEGITIMATE PROCESS OVERLAP / DISTINCT FUNCTIONAL STAGES**

No merge is justified.

---

## 5. Participation ↔ Report / Civil Attention

A participant may submit a report.

A nonparticipant may also submit a report.

Therefore Civil Attention should not be subordinate to Participation.

> **Access To Civil Attention != Requirement To Become A Participant**

Likewise, reporting a problem should not silently enrol the reporter into participation.

> **Report Submission != Participation Agreement**

This preserves the Front Door / Lobby principle that investigation and contact do not imply commitment.

---

## 6. What Would Count As Harmful Overlap?

Overlap becomes a defect when it causes one or more of the following:

1. the visitor cannot tell why two routes differ;
2. choosing either route silently blocks the other;
3. one route claims exclusive ownership of a multidimensional problem;
4. a route choice creates status or authority consequences;
5. the visitor must disclose unnecessary identity information to compare routes;
6. the same function is duplicated with materially inconsistent rules;
7. route switching loses evidence or context;
8. the system interprets multi-route use as indecision/failure;
9. one route becomes a gatekeeper for criticism, reporting or exit;
10. route descriptions are so similar that self-routing becomes arbitrary.

None of these follows merely from the existence of overlap.

---

## 7. Positive Multi-Route Architecture

The Lobby should treat some arrivals as naturally multi-route.

A useful model is:

> **Arrival Need = {Route A, Route B, ...}**

rather than:

> **Arrival Need = Exactly One Route**

The visitor may:

- choose one first;
- open several;
- backtrack;
- move between related routes;
- discover a second dimension later.

This supports the existing invariant:

> **One Arrival != One Route**

and:

> **First Route != Final Route**

---

## 8. Route Relationships Are Information

Cross-routing itself can make the architecture more legible.

Examples:

**Autonomy & Consent**  
Related: Assessment, Authority, Continuity, ESCP

**Assessment**  
Related: ESCP, Autonomy, Continuity, Authority

**Participation**  
Related: Orientation, Challenge, Civil Attention

**Challenge**  
Related: ESCP, Civil Attention, Research

**Civil Attention**  
Related: Challenge, Authority, relevant domain

These links already encode a small route graph.

The test therefore suggests not route elimination, but possibly improved **relationship legibility** in a future prototype.

A visitor should be able to understand:

> “Both routes may apply; they do different jobs.”

---

## 9. Candidate Prototype 002 Clarification

A future revision may add one compact common statement to second-layer boards:

> **More than one route may apply. Related routes address different parts of the same situation; choosing one does not close the others.**

Where useful, a related-route link can state the distinction.

Examples:

- **Autonomy ↔ Assessment:** “Use Assessment to examine what the evidence supports; use Autonomy & Consent to examine choice, control, refusal and coercion.”
- **Challenge ↔ Civil Attention:** “Use Challenge to examine whether something is wrong; use Civil Attention when the matter should enter a formal attention/review process.”
- **Participation ↔ Challenge:** “You may challenge the Concord without joining it.”

These are interface clarifications, not new architecture.

## 10. Source-Resolution Result

The remaining overlap findings do **not** support collapsing the routes.

Classification:

### Autonomy ↔ Assessment
**LEGITIMATE ROUTE OVERLAP / CROSS-ROUTING RELATION**

### Participation ↔ Challenge
**LEGITIMATE ROUTE OVERLAP / NON-GATED CORRECTION PATH**

### Challenge ↔ Report/Review
**LEGITIMATE PROCESS OVERLAP / DISTINCT FUNCTIONAL STAGES**

### Participation ↔ Civil Attention
**INDEPENDENT ACCESS SHOULD BE PRESERVED**

The prototype's ability to permit multiple routes is a feature, provided the distinctions remain legible.

## 11. Candidate Invariants

> **Multi-Route Relevance != Routing Failure**

> **Overlap != Duplication**

> **Route Choice != Exclusive Ownership Of The Problem**

> **Autonomy Concern != Autonomy Assessment**

> **Evaluation != Challenge**

> **Challenge != Submission**

> **Submission != Finding**

> **Report Submission != Participation Agreement**

> **A Visitor May Need Several Processes Without Becoming Several Kinds Of Participant**

---

## 12. Corrective Disposition

Do not modify frozen Prototype 001.

For Prototype 002:

1. preserve all currently distinct route functions;
2. add the missing Orientation exposure layer;
3. expose uncertain-unit/distributed-process routing without imposing collective identity;
4. improve route-relationship legibility;
5. preserve multi-route choice and backtracking;
6. do not force one-primary-route classification;
7. do not make Challenge or Civil Attention conditional on Participation.

The remaining blind-test findings should be source-resolved before freezing Prototype 002.
