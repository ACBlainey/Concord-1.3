# Peaceful Heterogeneity — Rights, Harm and Bounded Authority
## Synthesis and Development Map

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Project:** The Concord — A Framework for Human, Artificial and Hybrid Flourishing  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** **ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / SYNTHESIS / NOT CANONICAL**  
**Development Area:** Governance and Stewardship / Peaceful Heterogeneity

## 1. Purpose

The investigation began with:

> **What minimum common constraints are actually necessary for heterogeneous participants and communities to coexist peacefully, while leaving everything outside those constraints free to vary?**

The work now separates naturally into three connected development paths:

1. **General Rights, Autonomy and Peaceful Heterogeneity**
2. **Harm, Consent and Permissibility**
3. **Emergency Governance and Emergency Operations**

They should remain connected but not be collapsed into one doctrine.

## 2. Path A — General Rights, Autonomy and Peaceful Heterogeneity

Starting presumption:

\[
AUTONOMOUS\ VARIATION \rightarrow PERMITTED
\]

rather than:

\[
CONFORMITY \rightarrow PERMITTED
\]

Difference, unusual preference, voluntary risk and self-regarding harm do not by themselves establish legitimate authority to intervene.

The emerging common boundary concerns non-consensual external harm, conflicts of rights, valid consent, coercion, legitimate delegated authority, proportionality, practical exit and constitutional limits on power.

A heterogeneous civilisation may therefore require less agreement about **how participants should live** than agreement about **how participants may treat one another and how power may legitimately be exercised**.

## 3. Path B — Harm, Consent and Permissibility

PH-01 Boxing asks:

> **Under what conditions can participants legitimately consent to actions and risks that would otherwise constitute impermissible harm, and what defines the boundary of that consent?**

It demonstrates:

\[
PERMISSIBILITY \neq f(HARM\ SEVERITY\ ALONE)
\]

The work separates participation consent, action consent, risk consent and outcome consent, together with expected harm, foreseeable accepted harm, catastrophic accepted risk, prohibited harm, extra-contextual harm, intent, negligence, rule compliance and temporal validity.

The central emerging concept is a **bounded consent envelope**, not a binary consent flag.

## 4. Path C — Emergency Governance and Operations

The boxing referee reveals **bounded delegated authority**: participants may recognise purpose-specific temporary authority without surrendering general autonomy.

PH-02 asks:

> **When, if ever, may civilisation temporarily restrict ordinary rights or impose harm to address a qualifying emergency, and what prevents exceptional authority from becoming ordinary power?**

The model separates constitutional legitimacy, activation, operational command, frontline exercise, compulsion, sanction, review, remedy and post-event learning.

It also distinguishes:

**ADVISORY → DIRECTIVE → COMPULSORY**

A Directive creates a strong presumption of compliance but does not, by itself, authorise force or automatically criminalise contextual deviation.

## 5. Shared Emerging Findings

> **Context changes ethical classification.**

> **Harm severity alone does not determine permissibility.**

> **Consent is bounded by action, risk, context, time and termination conditions.**

> **Authority to direct does not automatically create authority to compel.**

> **Authority to compel does not automatically create grounds for criminalisation.**

> **Prediction does not grant authority.**

> **Participant contradiction can be evidence.**

> **Higher intrusion requires stronger evidence, necessity, proportionality, provenance and review.**

> **Exceptional authority should expire when its qualifying condition expires.**

These remain hypotheses under development.

## 6. Recommended Development Separation

### A. General Rights
Minimum common boundary; autonomy and non-conformity; externalities; conflicts of rights; practical exit; contextual deviation; limits of civic obligation.

### B. Harm and Consent
PH-01 Boxing; consent-envelope model; action versus risk consent; severity and irreversibility; dynamic capacity; consent assurance; withdrawal and temporal consent.

### C. Emergency Governance and Operations
PH-02; explicit civic emergency consent; Emergency Authority Envelope; advisory/directive/compulsory distinction; activation and sunset; frontline discretion; review, provenance, remedy and bidirectional information flow.

This separation prevents a consensual-harm problem from silently becoming a theory of government, or an emergency-operational rule from becoming a general restriction on autonomy.
