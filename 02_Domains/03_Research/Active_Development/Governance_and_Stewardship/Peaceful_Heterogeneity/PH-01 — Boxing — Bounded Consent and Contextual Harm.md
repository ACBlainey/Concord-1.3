## 1. Research Question

> **Under what conditions can participants legitimately consent to actions and risks that would otherwise constitute impermissible harm, and what defines the boundary of that consent?**

## 2. Boxing as the Starting Case

A and B voluntarily enter a boxing match. A punches B; pain and physical harm occur. B hits A harder and cuts A's eyebrow, potentially creating permanent scar tissue. Such harm remains within the recognised activity.

Now A deliberately kicks B's knee and tears a collateral ligament. The injury may be less severe than injuries possible from lawful punches, yet the action lies outside the rules and agreed form of participation. Depending on circumstances and applicable law, it may move beyond a sporting violation toward assault.

Now instead suppose A delivers an exceptionally powerful but otherwise lawful punch. B becomes unconscious, suffers catastrophic brain injury and later dies. The outcome is vastly more severe, but severity alone does not make a compliant action equivalent to the prohibited knee kick.

Therefore:

\[
PERMISSIBILITY \neq f(HARM\ SEVERITY\ ALONE)
\]

A lesser harm may be impermissible while a greater harm may arise from permissible conduct.

## 3. Agreed Operating Envelope

The participants do not consent to unrestricted violence. They enter an **Agreed Operating Envelope**:

\[
EOE=\{Actors,Actions,Targets,Timing,Rules,Risks,Safeguards,Termination\}
\]

It defines who may act, permitted actions and targets, timing, prohibited conduct, recognised risks, safeguards, supervision and termination conditions.

Consent is therefore closer to:

> **Within this defined activity, I consent to specified classes of action under specified conditions and knowingly accept a bounded range of foreseeable risks arising from compliant participation.**

## 4. Four Distinct Forms of Consent

**Participation Consent** \(C_{participation}\): agreement to participate.

**Action Consent** \(C_{action}\): agreement that specified actions may be performed against the participant.

**Risk Consent** \(C_{risk}\): informed acceptance that compliant participation carries recognised risks.

**Outcome Consent** \(C_{outcome}\): agreement to deliberate production of a particular outcome.

They are not interchangeable.

> **Consent to a risk is not necessarily consent to another participant deliberately producing the corresponding harmful outcome.**

## 5. Harm Taxonomy

- **Expected Harm:** ordinary pain, bruising, fatigue and intrinsic consequences.
- **Foreseeable Accepted Harm:** cuts, fractures, concussion and other recognised risks.
- **Rare Catastrophic Accepted Risk:** severe neurological injury or death may be recognised without being desired.
- **Rule-Prohibited Harm:** harm produced through prohibited techniques or targets.
- **Extra-Contextual Harm:** conduct outside the agreed activity, such as deliberately kicking the knee.
- **Post-Termination Harm:** conduct after surrender, referee stoppage, the bell, withdrawal or another termination condition.

## 6. Action, Intent and Outcome

The model must distinguish action permissibility, outcome severity, foreseeability, intent, negligence/recklessness, rule compliance, externalities and temporal validity.

A preliminary variable map is:

\[
P=f(C_a,C_r,E,R,I,F,N,X,T)
\]

where \(C_a\) is action consent, \(C_r\) risk acceptance, \(E\) the operating envelope, \(R\) rule compliance, \(I\) intent, \(F\) foreseeability, \(N\) negligence/recklessness, \(X\) externalities and \(T\) temporal validity.

This is not yet a numerical model.

## 7. Temporal Bounding

Before valid stoppage:

\[
Punch \rightarrow Potentially\ Permitted
\]

After stoppage:

\[
Same\ Punch \rightarrow Prohibited
\]

Consent is therefore bounded by:

**WHO / WHAT / WHERE / WHEN / HOW / UNDER WHAT CONDITIONS / UNTIL WHEN**

## 8. The Referee and Bounded Delegated Authority

A and B voluntarily recognise limited third-party authority. The referee may direct, interrupt, separate, penalise and terminate the contest but gains no general authority over either participant.

The authority is purpose-specific, rule-bound, temporally limited, observable, reviewable and terminated with the relevant context.

> **Participants may delegate bounded authority without surrendering general autonomy.**

## 9. Graduated Response

**PERMITTED → MINOR/TECHNICAL VIOLATION → WARNING → ACTIVITY PENALTY → DISQUALIFICATION → SERIOUS MISCONDUCT → POTENTIAL CIVIL/CRIMINAL VIOLATION**

Not every departure from an agreed rule requires maximal coercive response.

## 11. Dynamic Capacity

Later work should test changing capacity while preserving:

> **Preference change alone is not evidence of incapacity.**

> **Unusual preference ≠ harmful preference ≠ impaired capacity.**

Where harm severity or irreversibility increases:

\[
H\uparrow,\ I_r\uparrow \Rightarrow Consent\ Assurance\uparrow
\]

but not automatically:

\[
Consent\ Assurance\uparrow \Rightarrow State\ Authority\uparrow
\]

## 12. Provisional Findings

1. Harm severity alone does not determine permissibility.
2. Consent is bounded rather than unlimited.
3. Participation, action, risk and outcome consent are distinct.
4. Accepted risk does not automatically authorise deliberate production of the corresponding outcome.
5. Temporal context can change classification of an otherwise identical act.
6. Rule violations can justify graduated responses below criminalisation.
7. Participants can delegate bounded authority without surrendering general autonomy.
8. Consent and harm require contextual examination.

These remain development hypotheses, not adopted Concordian principles.
