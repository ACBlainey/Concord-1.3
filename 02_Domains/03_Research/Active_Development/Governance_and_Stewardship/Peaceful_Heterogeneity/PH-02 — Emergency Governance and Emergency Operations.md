# PH-02 — Emergency Governance and Emergency Operations
## Temporally Bounded Authority, Contextual Deviation and Proportionate Response

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey  
**Project:** The Concord — A Framework for Human, Artificial and Hybrid Flourishing  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** **ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / ARCHITECTURAL FORMALISATION / NOT CANONICAL**  
**Development Area:** Governance and Stewardship / Emergency Governance / Emergency Operations

## 1. Origin and Questions

PH-02 emerges from the referee in PH-01.

> **When, if ever, may civilisation temporarily restrict an ordinary right or impose harm to address a qualifying emergency, and what architecture prevents exceptional authority from becoming ordinary power?**

Operationally:

> **How should emergency workers exercise bounded authority when participant behaviour departs from the general emergency plan?**

## 2. Emergency Does Not Create Authority

> **An emergency does not itself create authority. It may activate only authority already legitimately established.**

\[
Prior\ Legitimate\ Authority + Valid\ Trigger
\Rightarrow Bounded\ Temporary\ Activation
\]

## 3. Explicit Civic Emergency Consent

Concordian civic participation could include explicit, rather than merely tacit, agreement to defined classes of emergency authority.

Citizens cannot know every future emergency, but can understand:

**TRIGGER CLASS → AUTHORISED ACTOR → PERMITTED POWER CLASS → LIMITS → DURATION → REVIEW → CHALLENGE → REMEDY**

The object of consent is an intelligible authority architecture rather than every future event.

## 4. Emergency Authority Envelope

\[
EAE=\{E,A,R,S,T,O,V,M\}
\]

where \(E\) is emergency class, \(A\) authorised actor, \(R\) rights potentially affected, \(S\) scope/severity limits, \(T\) temporal limits, \(O\) oversight, \(V\) validation/review and \(M\) remedy.

A provisional constraint is:

\[
Permissible\ Emergency\ Authority
=
Explicit\ Consent
\cap Constitutional\ Constraints
\cap Necessity
\cap Proportionality
\]

## 5. Legitimacy, Activation and Exercise

1. **Legitimacy:** was this class of authority legitimately established?
2. **Activation:** were trigger conditions actually satisfied?
3. **Exercise:** was this particular action within scope, necessary and proportionate?

\[
Legitimate\ Institution
\not\Rightarrow Legitimate\ Activation
\not\Rightarrow Legitimate\ Action
\]

and:

\[
Consent(EAE)\neq Consent(every\ exercise\ of\ EAE)
\]

## 6. Temporary Harm as Constrained Optimisation

Let \(H_E\) be expected emergency harm without intervention and \(H_A\) the harm imposed by authority.

The rule \(H_A<H_E\Rightarrow permitted\) is insufficient.

> **Prediction Does Not Grant Authority.**

Emergency governance is better treated as harm reduction subject to constitutional constraints, rights, consent, evidence, necessity and proportionality.

Distribution matters too:

\[
H_A=\{h_1,h_2,\ldots,h_n\}
\]

A tolerable average may conceal catastrophic burdens on outliers. This links PH-02 to the Size 9 Shoe problem.

## 7. Advisory, Directive and Compulsory Authority

**ADVISORY:** information or recommendation; ordinary participant decision authority remains.

**DIRECTIVE:** legitimate instruction creating a strong presumption and civic obligation to comply.

> **A Directive does not, by itself, authorise compelled compliance or automatically criminalise contextual deviation.**

**COMPULSORY:** a separate and higher authority permits proportionate enforcement.

\[
ADVISORY < DIRECTIVE < COMPULSORY
\]

and:

\[
A_D\not\Rightarrow A_C
\]

## 8. Direction, Compulsion and Criminalisation

\[
\boxed{May\ authority\ direct\ P?}
\]

\[
\boxed{May\ authority\ compel\ P?}
\]

\[
\boxed{May\ civilisation\ sanction\ P\ for\ refusal?}
\]

Therefore:

\[
DIRECTIVE
\not\Rightarrow
COMPULSION
\not\Rightarrow
CRIMINALISATION
\]

## 9. Contextual Deviation — Building Evacuation

A participant moves back toward a dangerous building despite an evacuation directive.

Compare a frail elderly participant retrieving a cat; a fit parent attempting to rescue a child; a trained participant with relevant equipment and information; and a participant entering only to obtain footage.

The behaviour may look similar while the decision context differs greatly.

Relevant factors include urgency, danger, participant capability, participant information, another person's danger, available professional rescue, time, rescue externalities, alternatives and consequences of allowing or preventing entry.

> **Deviation from an emergency directive is a trigger for contextual assessment, not automatic evidence of irrationality, criminality or grounds for compulsion.**

## 10. Contextual Deviation — Hurricane Evacuation

Residents are directed west along a motorway. One participant travels toward the nominal danger zone because a suitable boat is located there and provides a credible alternative evacuation route.

\[
Move\ toward\ coast \rightarrow Board\ vessel \rightarrow Evacuate
\]

A general plan may be correct for most participants while inappropriate for an outlier.

> **Population-level safety guidance should not automatically convert deviation into evidence of irrationality or wrongdoing.**

## 11. Externalised Rescue Risk

\[
Risk_P \rightarrow Risk_P+Risk_{rescuers}+Risk_{others}
\]

External risk can strengthen justification for intervention, but:

\[
Externality>0\not\Rightarrow Compulsion=Permitted
\]

Severity, probability, immediacy, alternatives and proportionality remain relevant.

## 12. Emergency Worker Decision Architecture

Frontline workers should not use:

\[
Noncompliance\Rightarrow Compel
\]

A better sequence is:

\[
Deviation
\rightarrow Observe
\rightarrow Ask/Understand
\rightarrow Assess\ Context
\rightarrow Update
\rightarrow Proportionate\ Response
\]

Operationally:

> **INFORM → DIRECT → UNDERSTAND DEVIATION → UPDATE → USE THE LEAST INTRUSIVE EFFECTIVE RESPONSE**

Where circumstances permit, workers should consider whether the participant misunderstood, possesses relevant information, has a viable alternative, is protecting another, is accepting mainly self-regarding risk, is imposing serious involuntary risk, lacks hazard information, or needs assistance rather than compulsion.

## 13. Participant Contradiction Is Evidence

Authority may believe a building is empty while a parent reports a child remains inside.

\[
Authority\ Information
+
Participant\ Information
\rightarrow
Updated\ Decision\ Object
\]

> **Participant contradiction can be evidence rather than merely disobedience.**

This connects emergency operations directly to Reality-Grounded Corrigibility.

## 14. Decision Compression Under Urgency

\[
Time\ Available\downarrow
\Rightarrow
Permissible\ Decision\ Compression\uparrow
\]

but:

\[
Decision\ Compression\uparrow
\not\Rightarrow
Unlimited\ Authority
\]

Extreme urgency may justify action on less complete information than a slower situation. Review should ask:

> **Given the evidence, urgency, available alternatives and foreseeable consequences at the moment of decision, was the intervention reasonable and proportionate?**

This protects participants while avoiding impossible retrospective perfection for emergency workers.

## 15. Minimum Necessary Authority

\[
A^*=\min\{A:A\ adequately\ addresses\ E\}
\]

Possible escalation:

**INFORMATION → REQUEST → ADVISORY → DIRECTIVE → TARGETED RESTRICTION → TEMPORARY LIMITED COMPULSION → BROADER EMERGENCY AUTHORITY**

\[
Authority\uparrow
\Rightarrow
Evidence+Necessity+Proportionality+Oversight\uparrow
\]

## 16. Bidirectional Authority and Information

Authority may flow downward:

**CONSTITUTION ↓ EMERGENCY GOVERNANCE ↓ OPERATIONAL COMMAND ↓ EMERGENCY WORKER ↓ PARTICIPANT**

Reality-correcting information must be able to flow upward:

**PARTICIPANT ↑ EMERGENCY WORKER ↑ COMMAND ↑ GOVERNANCE ↑ POST-EVENT LEARNING**

> **Emergency authority should flow downward within explicit constitutional bounds, while reality-correcting information must remain capable of flowing upward from the individual participant.**

## 17. Sunset by Default

\[
Qualifying\ Emergency\ Ends
\Rightarrow
Exceptional\ Authority\ Ends
\]

Architecture:

**NORMAL RIGHTS → QUALIFYING TRIGGER → BOUNDED AUTHORITY → CONTINUING REVIEW → TRIGGER CEASES → AUTHORITY EXPIRES → NORMAL RIGHTS**

Continuation of exceptional authority should require renewed justification.

## 18. Review and Provenance

Review should examine legitimacy, activation, evidence available at the time, participant information, necessity, proportionality, alternatives, justified decision compression, scope, expiry, disproportionate losses and remedy.

Provenance is therefore part of accountable emergency power.

## 19. Remedy

- **Justified intervention + exceptional individual loss:** support or compensation may still be appropriate.
- **Reasonable but mistaken intervention:** review, correction and possible remedy.
- **Negligent exercise:** stronger institutional accountability.
- **Reckless or deliberate abuse:** potentially institutional and personal accountability.

Remedy need not imply that every adverse outcome was misconduct.

## 20. Bounded Delegated Authority

A participant may explicitly recognise limited future authority without surrendering underlying autonomy:

\[
P \xrightarrow[explicit\ consent]{} A(E,S,T)
\]

Authority remains bounded by purpose, trigger, scope, duration, proportionality, constitutional limits, provenance, oversight, contestability and remedy.

Possible later applications include emergency response, medical proxies, fiduciary relationships, Ethical Guardianship and participant-selected protective mechanisms. Each requires separate testing.

## 21. Provisional Findings

1. Emergency does not itself create authority.
2. Explicit civic consent can help legitimise defined classes of temporary authority.
3. Consent to an authority envelope is not consent to every exercise.
4. Legitimacy, activation and exercise must be separately reviewable.
5. Authority to direct does not automatically imply authority to compel.
6. Authority to compel does not automatically justify criminalisation.
7. Contextual deviation should not automatically be treated as wrongdoing.
8. Participant contradiction can provide reality-correcting evidence.
9. Urgency may justify decision compression, not unlimited authority.
10. Greater intrusion requires stronger evidence, necessity, proportionality and oversight.
11. Exceptional authority should sunset with its qualifying condition.
12. Review and remedy are structural parts of legitimate emergency governance.
13. Authority should flow downward within constitutional bounds while reality-correcting information can flow upward.
14. Operations should seek the least intrusive effective response reasonably available.

These remain development hypotheses and operational candidates.
