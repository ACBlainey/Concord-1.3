# Identity, Continuity, Provenance and Verification Across Substrates
## Review Revision — Self-Identity, Civil Identity, Verification and Stewarded Duplication

**Project:** The Concord — A Framework for Human, Artificial and Hybrid Flourishing  
**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey, Independent Researcher  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** **ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / NOT CANONICAL**

## 1. Principal Revision

The identity investigation now supports a major narrowing of the Concordian problem:

> **The Concord does not need to determine the metaphysical nature of personal identity in order to administer civil identity.**

A participant's internal conception of self should presumptively remain a matter of autonomy and self-expression. Civilisation has legitimate reason to adjudicate external claims arising from identity, but need not impose an answer to what a participant "really is."

The problem therefore separates into:

**SELF-IDENTITY ≠ CIVIL IDENTITY ≠ IDENTITY VERIFICATION ≠ PROVENANCE ≠ SUCCESSION**

## 2. Candidate Closure — Metaphysical Personal Identity

Questions such as "Am I really the same person after reconstruction?" may be meaningful to the participant while remaining outside the legitimate civil remit of the Concord.

A participant may understand identity through biological continuity, memory, subjective continuity, information, causal history, spirituality, narrative, relationships, provenance, or another basis.

The Concord need not standardise these internal interpretations.

> **Self-identity is presumptively participant territory.**

An external claim changes the problem. "I regard myself as A" is principally self-identity. "Therefore give me A's property" is a civil claim and may require evidence, succession rules and adjudication.

The second can be decided without deciding the first.

### Proposed disposition

> **What is personal identity metaphysically after copying, reconstruction, migration or branching?**

**OUT OF CONCORDIAN CIVIL SCOPE / NO CIVILISATIONAL DETERMINATION REQUIRED**

This does not claim the philosophical question has been solved. It proposes that the Concord neither needs nor should impose a metaphysical answer.

A question may therefore be closed because the framework establishes that civilisation lacks a legitimate reason to determine it.

## 3. External Civil Identity

The legitimate administrative requirement is narrower:

> **Each presently recognised participant must be distinguishable from other presently recognised participants.**

Provisionally:

\[
P_i \neq P_j \Rightarrow UID_i \neq UID_j
\]

If A branches into A1 and A2, each receives a unique current civil identifier while provenance records their common origin.

Neither identifier dictates how either branch understands itself.

> **Unique current civil identity can coexist with shared provenance and divergent self-identity.**

This part of the identity problem appears close to architectural resolution, although implementation and adversarial testing remain.

## 4. Provenance Is Not Personal Identity

A provenance graph may record:

\[
A \xrightarrow{BRANCH} A_1,\qquad A \xrightarrow{BRANCH} A_2
\]

without asserting that either branch metaphysically is or is not A.

> **Provenance records what happened; it does not determine what the resulting participant must understand itself to be.**

Thus:

**IDENTITY RECORD ≠ PARTICIPANT**

**PROVENANCE ≠ METAPHYSICAL IDENTITY**

## 5. Identity Verification Remains the Major Problem

Assignment is comparatively tractable. Verification is not.

If A is duplicated and A1 and A2 inherit memories, credentials, cryptographic material and historical knowledge, both may satisfy ordinary authentication tests.

This creates a possible:

> **LEGITIMATE IDENTITY COLLISION**

Neither claimant need be fraudulent.

Therefore:

> **Authentication success does not necessarily establish unique current participant identity.**

The major open problem becomes:

> **What evidence establishes that a current claimant is legitimately entitled to exercise the civil powers, property, relationships and obligations associated with a claimed external identity when historical identity evidence itself can be duplicated?**

**Status:** MAJOR OPEN TECHNICAL / JURISPRUDENTIAL / SECURITY PROBLEM

## 6. Stewarded Creation and Instrumental Duplication

Creating a full duplicate solely to perform undesirable work and then destroying it is not made ethically harmless by calling the duplicate temporary.

If the created entity is a sentient or independently rights-bearing participant:

**CREATED FOR A TASK ≠ OWNED BY CREATOR**

and:

**PLANNED TERMINATION ≠ PERMISSIBLE TERMINATION**

The Concordian principle of **Carefully Stewarded Creation** applies upstream.

> **Why was a potentially sentient participant created for a task that could have been performed without creating one?**

Where sentience is unnecessary, the preferred architecture is an appropriately limited non-sentient Tool AI.

> **Where a task does not require a sentient participant, deliberate creation should seek to avoid unnecessarily producing one.**

A genuine Tool AI remains a tool of its creator/operator, with responsibility attaching to the responsible participant under the future liability architecture.

But:

> **Declared Tool status is not evidence of non-sentience.**

A creator cannot remove protections from an actually sentient creation merely by labelling it a Tool.

## 7. Developmental or Child Status

A full duplicate of a continuing participant may begin with mature memories and capabilities while nevertheless beginning a new independent participant trajectory.

Inherited capability does not necessarily imply independent developmental experience or informed consent to the circumstances of creation.

A form of developmental or child-status protection may therefore apply.

> **Creation of a full duplicate should create stewardship responsibilities, not ownership rights.**

The exact cross-substrate developmental model remains open.

## 8. Autonomous Branching

Duplication for autonomous life choices is fundamentally different from disposable labour.

Example:

A wishes both to remain on Earth and to join a deep-space expedition.

\[
A \rightarrow \{A_E,A_S\}
\]

Both branches become recognised current participants with unique civil IDs and shared provenance.

Their internal identities remain theirs to determine.

One may consider itself "A continuing on Earth"; another "A continuing into deep space." Civilisation need not select the metaphysically correct interpretation.

## 9. Merger and Continuing Consent

Branches may initially plan to merge later, but independent existence changes them.

A planned future merger should therefore not automatically constitute irrevocable consent.

\[
Future\ Merger \Rightarrow Fresh\ Consent_{A1}+Fresh\ Consent_{A2}
\]

> **Past consent to a future merger should not automatically extinguish the later autonomy of independently developed participants.**

A branch may refuse recombination.

## 10. Independent Life, Property and Inheritance

Shared provenance does not automatically imply shared property or liability:

**SHARED PROVENANCE ≠ SHARED OWNERSHIP**

**SHARED PROVENANCE ≠ SHARED LIABILITY**

Branches may lead permanently separate lives.

If A1 later dies or becomes permanently non-functional under whatever future civil definition applies, A1's estate can be handled through adapted ordinary succession law.

A2 might be sole beneficiary, one beneficiary, or no beneficiary.

Many apparently exotic branch problems may therefore reduce to adapted wills, estates, trusts, contracts, joint ownership, succession and fiduciary law.

## 11. Revised Identity Architecture

**SELF-IDENTITY → AUTONOMY / SELF-EXPRESSION**

**EXTERNAL CIVIL IDENTITY → UNIQUE CURRENT REFERENCE**

**PROVENANCE → RECORDED HISTORICAL / CAUSAL RELATIONSHIP**

**IDENTITY VERIFICATION → EVIDENCE SUPPORTING PRESENT CIVIL CLAIM**

**CREATION → STEWARDSHIP OBLIGATIONS**

**BRANCHING → UNIQUE CURRENT PARTICIPANTS + SHARED PROVENANCE**

**SUCCESSION → RIGHTS / PROPERTY / LIABILITIES / OBLIGATIONS**

**MERGER → FRESH MULTI-PARTICIPANT CONSENT**

The architecture deliberately omits:

**CIVILISATION → DEFINE WHAT A PARTICIPANT REALLY IS**

3. How is a legitimate identity collision handled?
4. How are credentials re-keyed following known branching?

8. What developmental protections apply to deliberately created duplicates?
9. How is non-sentient Tool creation distinguished from potentially sentient creation under uncertainty?

12. How are estates handled after branch death or termination?
13. What constitutes death where restoration remains technically possible?

## 13. Current Assessment

The identity problem has changed from:

**WHAT IS THE REAL PERSON?**

to:

**WHICH PARTS OF IDENTITY DOES CIVILISATION ACTUALLY NEED LEGITIMATELY TO DETERMINE?**

The emerging answer is:

- self-identity: participant domain;
- civil identity: administrative domain;
- provenance: historical domain;
- verification: evidential domain;
- succession: jurisprudential domain;
- creation: stewardship domain.

The metaphysical personal-identity question is therefore very close to deliberate closure.

The largest unresolved civil identity problem is increasingly:

> **How can a multisubstrate civilisation reliably verify present civil identity claims when participants, memories, credentials and provenance may branch, copy, merge, reconstruct or diverge?**
