## 1. Architecture Statement

> **A civil predictive architecture should provide a protected modelling environment in which plausible trajectories and alternative interventions can be explored before consequential real-world action, while preserving the distinction between model, evidence, prediction, authority and reality.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This architecture consolidates:

- Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment;
- Participant Digital Twin System.

It also uses:

- Reality Trees;
- Blaineyan Reasoning;
- vectoral trajectory analysis.

It interfaces with:

- CA-01 Participant Support and Autonomy;
- CA-03 Civilisational Learning and Metrics;
- CA-04 Purpose-Limited Participant Information;
- CA-05 Predictive System-Steering and Resource Allocation.

## 3. Civilisational Problem Addressed

Civil systems often act after failure has already occurred. Predictive modelling may make earlier action possible, but also creates dangers:

- prediction becoming authority;
- models displacing participants;
- self-fulfilling prediction;
- one model spreading error across many systems.

This architecture seeks useful foresight without allowing modelled futures to become civil facts.

## 4. Intended Scope

Applicable to modelling:

- participants;
- populations;
- infrastructure;
- institutions;
- services;
- resource systems;
- settlement systems;
- AI behaviour;
- emergency trajectories.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This architecture does not:

- create authority;
- determine legitimate purpose by itself;
- require a Digital Twin for all modelling;
- establish guilt;
- permit punishment for predicted acts;
- convert simulation into reality.

## 6. Principal Components

### 6.1 Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment
A pre-action modelling space separate from Layer Zero reality.

### 6.2 Participant Digital Twin
A participant-specific model where legitimate and useful, with correction, freshness and uncertainty.

### 6.3 Reality Trees
Represent alternative explanations, future branches and interventions.

### 6.4 Blaineyan Reasoning
Separates observation, inference, hypothesis and speculation.

### 6.5 Vectoral Analysis
Examines direction, magnitude, continued trajectory and thresholds.

## 8. Information Flow

Predictions should carry sufficient:

- source;
- model version;
- purpose;
- uncertainty;
- confidence;
- domain limits;
- prohibited-use constraints where necessary.

A prediction should not silently become universal civil truth.

## 9. Authority Flow

The architecture may inform:

- attention;
- preparation;
- voluntary support;
- system-level adaptation.

It should not create or automatically escalate authority over a participant.

Consequential authority must arise independently.

## 10. Required Boundaries

### 10.1 Simulation Is Not Reality
Counterfactual branches remain possibilities.

### 10.2 Prediction Does Not Grant Authority
Prediction is evidence, not a source of power.

### 10.3 Domain-Limited Prediction
Confidence should not transfer automatically outside domains where the model has been meaningfully validated.

### 10.4 Competing Models
Where practical, high-consequence decisions should not rely blindly upon a single epistemic monopoly.

### 10.5 Digital Twin Freshness
Participant models require versioning, uncertainty and correction.

## 11. Intervention Provenance

Where a prediction leads to consequential intervention, the record should preserve:

- pre-intervention state;
- prediction;
- confidence;
- intervention;
- expected intervention effect;
- actual outcome;
- plausible intervention-caused effects.

This reduces self-fulfilling prediction and false validation.

## 12. Failure Behaviour

If predictive modelling becomes unavailable:

- CA-01 should revert to non-predictive support;
- CA-05 should revert to conventional observation and planning;
- CA-03 should continue learning from actual outcomes.

Prediction is an enhancement, not a prerequisite for ordinary civilisation.

## 13. Anti-Capture Safeguards

Key risks:

- model monopoly;
- automation bias;
- participant displacement;
- prediction cascade;
- political or manipulative use.

Safeguards:

- contestability;
- competing branches;
- provenance;
- purpose constraints;
- independent testing;
- correction.

## 14. Cross-Substrate Considerations

Future development should examine:

- AI model validity;
- distributed identities;
- forks;
- hybrids;
- collective intelligences.

Predictive uncertainty should increase where models extrapolate outside validated substrate domains.

## 15. Adversarial Findings

The architecture survived first-stage failure testing.

New mandatory findings:

- intervention provenance;
- domain-limited prediction;
- Digital Twin freshness;
- protection against model monopoly.

## 16. Unresolved Questions

- How should Digital Twin correction work for forked identities?
- What domains should be prohibited from individual predictive use?
- How should prediction-linked intervention effects be causally analysed?
