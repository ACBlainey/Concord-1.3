## 1. Architecture Statement

> **A civil support architecture should connect actual participant conditions to accessible and proportionate support while preserving or restoring the greatest practicable degree of participant autonomy, and should not allow support relationships to become covert pathways to suspicion, dependency or exceptional authority.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This Candidate Architecture emerged from the Layer-Zero / Layer −0 development cycle and consolidates:

- Layer-Zero Systems Interface;
- Predictive Support System;
- Handrail Civil Support Architecture;
- Supported Decision-Making and Capacity Architecture.

It interfaces closely with:

- Guardian Stewardship;
- Civil Contact Points;
- CA-02 Layer −0 Predictive and Counterfactual Architecture;
- CA-04 Purpose-Limited Participant Information Architecture;
- CA-06 Exceptional Access, Provenance and Accountable Power Architecture.

## 3. Civilisational Problem Addressed

Civil support often arrives only after crisis, or arrives in forms that reduce agency. Support systems may also generate institutional dependency, convert refusal into suspicion, or become data sources for unrelated control systems.

This architecture seeks to make support useful before crisis without turning early support into early control.

## 4. Intended Scope

Applicable to:

- healthcare;
- homelessness;
- mental health;
- disability;
- employment disruption;
- civil participation;
- emergency recovery;
- AI support;
- hybrid support;
- other circumstances where participants may need civil assistance.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This architecture does not:

- create authority merely because support is offered;
- treat refusal as incapacity;
- require predictive modelling;
- assume all dependency is undesirable;
- guarantee unlimited services;
- replace Judiciary or emergency law;
- define one substrate-specific model of wellbeing.

## 6. Principal Components

### 6.1 Layer-Zero Systems Interface
Connects actual participant conditions to services, coordination, Metrics and higher civil architecture.

### 6.2 Predictive Support
Uses legitimate foresight primarily to increase useful options rather than reduce autonomy.

### 6.3 Handrail Civil Support
Provides support that is available, visible, accessible and normally optional before crisis.

### 6.4 Supported Decision-Making and Capacity
Provides a graduated path from autonomous choice through supported decision-making and only, where independently justified, temporary substituted authority.

## 8. Information Flow

Support information should remain within the legitimate support purpose unless:

- identity is no longer necessary and information is minimised for civil learning; or
- a separate legitimate authority justifies exceptional access.

The preferred learning path is:

**CA-01 SUPPORT**

↓

**CA-04 PURPOSE BOUNDARY**

↓

**IDENTITY MINIMISATION**

↓

**CA-03 CIVILISATIONAL LEARNING**

## 9. Authority Flow

Authority must not arise from:

- service dependence;
- refusal;
- prediction;
- staff preference;
- administrative convenience.

Where higher intervention becomes necessary, authority must come from an independently justified civil basis and should be proportionate, bounded and reviewable.

## 10. Required Boundaries

### 10.1 Support Is Not Suspicion
Seeking, receiving or refusing support should not by itself create suspicion, guilt, dangerousness or diminished civil standing.

### 10.2 Refusal Is Not Incapacity
Refusal should remain analytically separate from incapacity unless independently supported by relevant evidence.

### 10.3 Support-to-Investigation Firewall
Support data should not silently become investigative data.

### 10.4 Exit Can Be Success
A participant no longer needing or choosing support may represent successful operation rather than institutional failure.

### 10.5 Emergency Escalation Must Be Separate
Emergency intervention should require its own legitimate authority rather than be manufactured by the support relationship.

## 11. Failure Behaviour

If CA-02 predictive systems fail:

- ordinary support continues.

If CA-03 Metrics fail:

- direct participant support continues.

If CA-04 information boundaries fail:

- unnecessary downstream data transfer should stop rather than become unrestricted.

If oversight of emergency authority weakens:

- extraordinary authority should narrow to minimum necessity where practical.

## 12. Anti-Capture Safeguards

Key capture risks include:

- caseload incentives;
- permanent dependency;
- surveillance through support;
- refusal escalation;
- institutional retention.

Safeguards include:

- exit pathways;
- autonomy restoration;
- voluntary support;
- contestability;
- purpose separation;
- independent authority for coercive action.

## 13. Cross-Substrate Considerations

The architecture must not assume human-default wellbeing.

Future development should address:

- AI refusal of updates or monitoring;
- degraded AI capacity;
- distributed identities;
- hybrids;
- collective intelligences;
- off-world participants.

## 14. Adversarial Findings

The architecture survived first-stage failure testing.

Mandatory findings:

- Support-to-Investigation Firewall is a required safety boundary.
- Refusal must not become automatic evidence of incapacity.
- Support exit should be capable of counting as success.
- Emergency escalation must remain separately justified.

## 15. Unresolved Questions

- How should support entitlement be allocated under scarcity?
- What counts as meaningful consent under dependency?
- How should capacity be assessed across substrates?
- When is long-term interdependence healthy rather than dependency?
- How should support systems handle malicious gaming without creating universal suspicion?

## 16. Validation Requirements
