## 1. Architecture Statement

> **Legitimately necessary exceptional exercises of civil authority should remain specific, bounded, purpose-limited, provenance-visible and independently reviewable, and should become more constrained rather than less constrained when ordinary accountability weakens.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This architecture consolidates:

- Support-to-Investigation Firewall;
- Exceptional Identifiable Access System;
- Dual-Provenance and Exceptional-Power Oversight System.

It interfaces with:

- CA-04 Purpose-Limited Participant Information;
- Judiciary;
- Recursive Oversight;
- Guardian Stewardship;
- emergency authority.

It does not include HR-01 Evidence-Conditioned Investigative Modelling, which remains high-risk research.

## 3. Civilisational Problem Addressed

Civilisation may sometimes require exceptional power:

- emergency intervention;
- identifiable access;
- secret investigation;
- temporary substituted authority;
- urgent safety measures.

The danger is that exceptional capability becomes:

- routine;
- invisible;
- permanent;
- unreviewable.

This architecture seeks to preserve legitimate exceptional power without normalising it.

## 4. Intended Scope

Applicable to:

- exceptional identifiable access;
- emergency powers;
- secret or restricted investigation;
- substituted decision-making;
- high-impact institutional authority;
- AI or hybrid intervention;
- controlled re-identification.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This architecture does not:

- create Judiciary;
- create authority merely from technical access;
- authorise predictive policing;
- make oversight universally public;
- permit unrestricted oversight access;
- absorb Recursive Oversight as a subordinate system;
- promote HR-01.

## 6. Principal Components

### 6.1 Support-to-Investigation Firewall
Prevents support relationships from silently becoming investigative pathways.

### 6.2 Exceptional Identifiable Access
Provides a bounded route for legitimate access outside ordinary purpose.

### 6.3 Controlled Identity Bridge
Allows re-identification only under defined authority.

### 6.4 Operational Provenance
Records case-specific identity and operational authority where legitimately necessary.

### 6.5 Oversight Provenance
Allows oversight of power while minimising unnecessary identity exposure.

### 6.6 Independent Oversight
Provides review separate from the actor exercising power.

### 6.7 Judiciary Interface
Provides interpretation, adjudication and remedy.

Ordinary information remains governed by CA-04.

Exceptional access should be:

- specific;
- separately justified;
- authorised;
- minimum scope;
- purpose-limited;
- provenance-recorded.

Re-identification itself counts as access.

## 10. Required Boundaries

### 10.1 Exceptional Must Remain Exceptional
Standing capability does not create standing entitlement.

### 10.2 Observable Power
Significant power should remain reviewable by legitimate oversight.

### 10.3 Oversight Is Also Power
Oversight access must itself be bounded and provenance-recorded.

### 10.4 Functional Independence
Oversight independence must be real, not merely organisationally labelled.

### 10.5 Authority Narrows When Oversight Fails
Loss of accountability should not automatically expand authority.

### 10.6 Emergency Power Expires
Emergency authority should remain temporary and retrospectively reviewable.

### 10.7 Post-Hoc Justification Must Be Distinguishable
Records should distinguish pre-authorised power, emergency action, retrospective review and post-hoc rationalisation.

## 11. Metrics About Exceptional Power

Aggregate Metrics should examine:

- frequency;
- duration;
- category;
- authority source;
- outcomes;
- reversals;
- errors;
- recurrence;
- oversight findings.

These should avoid unnecessary participant identification.

## 12. Provenance Integrity

The architecture requires provenance that is:

- tamper-evident or independently verifiable;
- time-linked;
- authority-linked;
- purpose-linked;
- accessible to legitimate oversight.

Exact technical mechanisms remain undeveloped.

## 13. Failure Behaviour

If oversight fails:

- exceptional authority should narrow toward minimum emergency necessity.

If communications fail:

- local legitimate emergency authority may act within bounded scope and later reconcile provenance.

If provenance systems fail:

- ordinary exceptional use should reduce rather than continue invisibly where practical.

## 14. Anti-Capture Safeguards

Key risks:

- exception normalisation;
- oversight capture;
- secret law;
- authority laundering;
- provenance manipulation;
- retrospective legitimisation.

Safeguards:

- aggregate exception Metrics;
- expiry;
- functional independence;
- dual provenance;
- Judiciary;
- recursive review;
- auditable oversight access.

## 15. Cross-Substrate Considerations

Exceptional intervention against AI or hybrid participants may include:

- shutdown;
- memory inspection;
- compute restriction;
- forced isolation;
- model-state cloning;
- fork control;
- forced merge.

These may require substrate-specific severity and reversibility standards.

## 16. Adversarial Findings

The architecture survived first-stage failure testing.

Mandatory additions:

- Metrics about exceptional power;
- functional oversight independence;
- tamper-evident provenance;
- authority narrowing under oversight failure;
- explicit treatment of retrospective justification.
