## 1. Architecture Statement

> **Aggregate civil evidence and counterfactual modelling may be used to adapt infrastructure, preparedness and resource availability before avoidable system failure, provided predictive optimisation does not collapse into unnecessary individual control, excessive centralisation or resilience-destroying efficiency.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This architecture is centred on:

- System-Steering and Predictive Resource Allocation System.

It interfaces with:

- CA-02 Layer −0 Predictive and Counterfactual Architecture;
- CA-03 Civilisational Learning and Metrics;
- economic architecture;
- Ratchet/vector analysis;
- Pathfinder alternatives.

## 3. Civilisational Problem Addressed

Civil systems frequently react after demand becomes crisis.

Examples:

- housing shortages;
- healthcare capacity;
- emergency supplies;
- energy demand;
- compute demand;
- transport;
- off-world resources.

Predictive system steering may reduce avoidable crisis, but creates risks of central planning, brittle optimisation and minority neglect.

## 4. Intended Scope

Applicable to:

- infrastructure;
- public-service capacity;
- emergency preparedness;
- energy;
- transport;
- housing;
- healthcare;
- compute;
- communications;
- settlement resources.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This architecture does not:

- replace the Concord economy;
- assign individual life outcomes;
- grant authority over participants from aggregate prediction;
- justify removal of all redundancy;
- require central planning;
- assume all optimisation is desirable.

## 8. Information Flow

CA-05 should primarily receive:

- aggregate;
- anonymised;
- structural information.

Individual identity should normally be unnecessary.

Where individual-level data are proposed, CA-04 purpose constraints apply.

## 9. Authority Flow

CA-05 should steer:

- systems;
- infrastructure;
- availability;
- preparedness.

It should not automatically steer:

- individual behaviour;
- civil standing;
- rights.

## 10. Required Boundaries

### 10.1 System Steering Before Individual Control
Where legitimate goals can be achieved through environment or infrastructure, this should normally be considered before unnecessary individual restriction.

### 10.2 Anti-Central-Planning Boundary
Predictive coordination should not silently become comprehensive control of civil life.

### 10.3 Resilience Before Maximum Efficiency
Spare capacity, redundancy and local autonomy may be valuable resilience rather than waste.

### 10.4 Tail-Risk Protection
Rare but critical needs should not disappear because average demand is low.

### 10.5 Subsidiarity
Allocation should occur at the lowest competent level consistent with wider system constraints.

### 10.6 Reversibility
Where uncertainty is material, reversible adjustments should normally be preferred where practical.

## 11. Failure Behaviour

If prediction fails:

- fall back to observational planning;
- preserve reserves;
- retain local capacity;
- use reversible adjustments.

If Metrics become unreliable:

- reduce automated steering.

If central coordination fails:

- local systems should retain sufficient capability to operate.

## 12. Anti-Capture Safeguards

Key risks:

- central planning;
- manipulated Metrics;
- optimisation monopoly;
- minority resource suppression;
- efficiency destroying resilience.

Safeguards:

- subsidiarity;
- reserves;
- plural allocation mechanisms;
- outlier visibility;
- Pathfinder alternatives;
- rollback.
