# 1. Purpose

The **Case-Learning and Structural Knowledge System** is a candidate Concordian architecture for allowing civilisation to learn from individual experience without requiring indefinite retention of the identifiable individual as the object of that learning.

The system addresses a central problem.

A civilisation should be capable of becoming more knowledgeable through experience.

However:

> **Learning from a participant does not automatically justify permanently retaining the participant's identifiable history.**

The architecture therefore separates:

**PERSONAL EPISODIC INFORMATION**

from:

**GENERALISABLE STRUCTURAL KNOWLEDGE.**

Its purpose is to preserve the useful lesson while minimising unnecessary retention of identity.

---

# 2. Origin

The system emerged from the development of case-specific Reality Trees.

A participant may enter a Layer-Zero condition.

A Layer −0 model may then construct possible trajectories.

For example:

**CURRENT STATE**

↓

**BRANCH A — SELF-RESOLUTION**

**BRANCH B — CONTINUED DETERIORATION**

**BRANCH C — HEALTH SUPPORT**

**BRANCH D — EMPLOYMENT ADAPTATION**

**BRANCH E — FINANCIAL SUPPORT**

**BRANCH F — COMBINED SUPPORT**

Reality then follows one actual path.

The civilisational question becomes:

> **What should the Concord learn from the difference between what was predicted and what actually happened?**

A second question immediately follows:

> **How much information about the individual must remain identifiable in order for that lesson to survive?**

The Case-Learning and Structural Knowledge System exists to answer these two questions.

---

# 3. Core Principle

The central candidate principle is:

> **Civilisation should learn from the structure of a participant's experience without unnecessarily retaining the participant as the object of that learning.**

The objective is therefore not:

**MAXIMUM DATA RETENTION.**

It is:

**MAXIMUM LEGITIMATE LEARNING WITH MINIMUM NECESSARY IDENTIFIABLE RETENTION.**

---

# 4. Two Simultaneous Functions of a Case

Every sufficiently relevant case may have two different functions.

## Individual Function

Support understanding of the participant's own circumstances.

This may include:

- service coordination;
- support planning;
- Layer −0 modelling;
- participant decision support;
- trajectory analysis.

## Civilisational Learning Function

Provide evidence about whether the civilisation's existing models are accurate.

This may include:

- confirming a known branch;
- revealing a new branch;
- identifying a missing variable;
- exposing a causal error;
- revising a probability;
- identifying a new failure mode;
- showing that an intervention did not work as expected.

These functions should not be confused.

The participant-facing function may require identity.

The general learning function often may not.

---

# 5. Core Learning Cycle

The basic architecture is:

**INDIVIDUAL CASE**

↓

**CURRENT MODEL**

↓

**PREDICTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**CONFIRMATION / ERROR / SURPRISE**

↓

**STRUCTURAL LEARNING**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATE.**

The system therefore treats actual outcomes as evidence.

---

# 6. Reality Remains the Arbiter

The case-learning architecture inherits a central Concordian principle:

> **Simulation is not reality.**

A model may predict:

**A → B**

but reality may produce:

**A → C.**

The correct response is not to force the case into branch B.

The correct response is to ask:

- Was the model wrong?
- Was a variable missing?
- Was the observation incomplete?
- Did the participant possess unusual context?
- Did an intervention alter the trajectory?
- Is C a genuine new branch?

Therefore:

> **Actual outcomes should be capable of revising the model that predicted them.**

---

# 7. Failed Predictions Are Evidence

A failed prediction is not merely a technical failure.

It may reveal:

- a missing branch;
- a missing variable;
- an incorrect causal relationship;
- poor probability calibration;
- a contextual factor;
- a demographic or substrate-specific difference;
- intervention effects;
- a classification problem.

Therefore:

> **Failed predictions are evidence.**

The system should preserve and analyse them.

---

# 8. Successful Predictions Are Also Evidence

A correct prediction does not automatically prove the model.

It may:

- support a branch;
- strengthen confidence;
- justify probability adjustment;
- confirm a causal hypothesis.

However, one correct prediction may have occurred by coincidence.

Therefore:

> **Prediction success contributes evidence; it does not convert a model into unquestionable truth.**

Repeated performance and comparative validation remain necessary.

---

# 9. Rare Cases

Rare cases are particularly valuable.

Suppose the general Reality Tree contains:

**A → B**

**A → C**

**A → D**

but a participant experiences:

**A → E.**

The system should not automatically classify E as noise.

Instead it should ask:

- Is E a genuine branch?
- Was an important variable absent?
- Was the causal model wrong?
- Is E geographically specific?
- Is E culturally specific?
- Is E substrate-specific?
- Did an intervention produce E?
- Is E temporary?
- Is E beneficial?
- Is E harmful?
- Does E require new support?

This leads to the principle:

> **Rare cases should not merely be treated as predictive errors; they may reveal missing branches in the civilisation's model of reality.**

---

# 10. The Outlier Relationship

Case Learning is closely linked to Outlier-First Design.

Normal cases help confirm whether the common model works.

Outliers help expose whether the model is too narrow.

This means rare participant experience may be especially important for:

- new services;
- emerging technologies;
- substrate differences;
- unusual communities;
- off-world populations;
- atypical family arrangements;
- unusual economic arrangements.

A civilisation that learns only from averages may repeatedly miss the conditions that most strongly challenge its assumptions.

---

# 11. Model Mismatch

A large mismatch between a participant and the general model should increase curiosity before it increases institutional confidence.

The developing rule is:

> **The stronger the mismatch between an individual case and the general predictive model, the greater the need for contextual and case-specific analysis rather than stronger conformity to the model.**

The process may be:

**MISMATCH**

↓

**REVIEW**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE SEARCH**

↓

**NEW BRANCH CANDIDATE**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL REVIEW.**

---

# 12. Episodic Information

**Personal Episodic Information** is information about an identifiable participant and their experience.

Examples include:

- participant identity;
- location;
- dates;
- personal medical information;
- employment history;
- housing history;
- family circumstances;
- specific service use;
- personal decisions;
- actual sequence of events.

This information may be legitimately necessary for:

- the participant's own record;
- active service provision;
- case coordination;
- legal requirements;
- active analysis.

It does not automatically need to become permanent civilisational knowledge in identifiable form.

---

# 13. Structural Knowledge

**Structural Civilisational Knowledge** is information extracted from experience that can be useful independently of identity.

Examples include:

- under condition A, branch E is possible;
- intervention X sometimes fails when variable Y is present;
- service barrier Z is associated with repeated dropout;
- a previously unknown pathway exists;
- a classification rule produces a recurring false positive;
- an apparent risk indicator has an alternative benign explanation.

This information may remain useful after participant identity is removed.

---

# 14. Core Separation

The architecture therefore distinguishes:

> **Citizen X experienced A, B and E.**

from:

> **Under conditions A and B, E is a possible outcome.**

The first is personal episodic information.

The second is structural knowledge.

They are not the same information object.

---

# 15. Knowledge Extraction

The intended flow is:

**IDENTIFIABLE CASE**

↓

**CASE ANALYSIS**

↓

**STRUCTURAL PATTERN IDENTIFIED**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE / BRANCH / RELATIONSHIP**

↓

**SIGNIFICANCE REVIEW**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVED**

↓

**STRUCTURAL KNOWLEDGE RETAINED**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATED.**

This is the heart of the system.

---

# 16. Significance Review

Not every case feature should automatically become structural knowledge.

The system should ask whether the feature is:

- relevant;
- reproducible;
- plausible;
- causally meaningful;
- useful;
- independently supported;
- potentially generalisable;
- or worth preserving as a rare hypothesis.

A single unusual observation may therefore become:

**candidate structural knowledge**

rather than:

**established general rule.**

---

# 17. Epistemic Classification

Structural knowledge should retain epistemic status.

Possible labels may include:

- OBSERVATION;
- INFERENCE;
- HYPOTHESIS;
- SPECULATION;
- PROVISIONAL PATTERN;
- SUPPORTED PATTERN;
- REVISED;
- REJECTED.

The exact status vocabulary may change.

The principle is:

> **Extraction from a real case does not automatically validate the lesson inferred from that case.**

---

# 18. Case Learning Versus Case Retention

A common institutional assumption is:

> **More retained case data means more future learning.**

This is not necessarily true.

After the useful structural lesson has been extracted, retaining the entire identifiable case may produce:

- privacy risk;
- re-identification risk;
- surveillance capability;
- institutional misuse;
- unnecessary storage;
- data breach consequences.

Therefore:

> **Retention should be justified by continuing purpose, not by the abstract possibility that data might someday be useful.**

---

# 19. Temporary Anonymised Case Studies

Some cases may need to remain available temporarily after identity removal.

Reasons may include:

- evaluating a new branch;
- comparing similar cases;
- examining causal relationships;
- validating a model revision;
- understanding a rare outcome.

The process may be:

**CASE STUDY CREATED**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVED**

↓

**STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATED**

↓

**RETENTION NEED REVIEWED**

↓

**DELETE IF NO CONTINUING LEGITIMATE PURPOSE.**

Anonymisation alone does not automatically justify indefinite retention.

---

# 20. Case Study Expiry

Temporary anonymised cases should therefore have a retention question attached to them.

The system should periodically ask:

- Is this case still analytically necessary?
- Has its structural lesson already been incorporated?
- Does the case still provide unique information?
- Can the case be further aggregated?
- Can it now be deleted?

This creates an active rather than passive retention model.

---

# 21. Personal Record Remains Separate

Deletion of an anonymised case-learning representation does not automatically imply deletion of the participant's legitimate personal record.

The participant's personal record may still be required for:

- continuity;
- active services;
- legal obligations;
- participant access;
- medical history;
- support history.

These records are governed by the Concord's personal-data architecture.

The relationship is:

**PERSONAL RECORD**

→ protected personal-data system.

**STRUCTURAL LESSON**

→ civilisational learning system.

They should remain separate.

---

# 22. No Silent Recombination

Once identity has been removed from structural knowledge, general systems should not casually reattach that knowledge to the original participant.

For example:

**ANONYMISED PATTERN**

should not later be joined with another data source to reconstruct:

**Participant X's history**

unless a separately legitimate basis exists.

The separation between:

**PERSONAL EPISODE**

and:

**GENERAL LESSON**

must be architectural rather than merely rhetorical.

---

# 23. Intermediary Data Boundary

The Case-Learning System should normally receive structural information through the developing:

**Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary.**

The intended flow is:

**PERSONAL CASE SYSTEM**

↓

**PURPOSE CHECK**

↓

**NECESSITY CHECK**

↓

**MINIMISATION**

↓

**ANONYMISATION**

↓

**STRUCTURAL EXTRACTION**

↓

**GENERAL LEARNING SYSTEM.**

This reduces the number of downstream systems that ever need to see identity.

---

# 24. Anonymisation Before Distribution

A key principle is:

> **Where identifiable information is unnecessary for a downstream civilisational function, identity should be removed before the data crosses into that function rather than relying solely upon downstream restrictions.**

The distinction is important.

A weaker model is:

**IDENTIFIABLE DATA DISTRIBUTED WIDELY**

↓

**EVERY SYSTEM PROMISES NOT TO MISUSE IT.**

The stronger model is:

**IDENTITY REMOVED BEFORE WIDER DISTRIBUTION.**

---

# 25. Minimum Necessary Resolution

Structural knowledge should retain enough detail to remain useful.

However, excessive resolution may increase re-identification risk.

For example:

> “A 43-year-old participant in a tiny settlement on a specific date with a rare condition experienced X”

may effectively remain identifiable.

A safer representation may be:

> “A rare combination of condition A and service constraint B produced branch X.”

The system must therefore balance:

**ANALYTICAL VALUE**

against:

**IDENTIFICATION RISK.**

---

# 26. Rare Case Privacy

Rare cases create a special challenge.

The rarer the case, the easier it may be to identify the individual even after obvious identifiers are removed.

Yet rare cases may also contain highly valuable structural information.

Therefore the system may require:

- broader aggregation;
- delayed publication;
- reduced geographic precision;
- reduced temporal precision;
- synthetic representation;
- restricted access;
- controlled research environments.

This is an unresolved but important architectural issue.

---

# 27. General Reality Trees

Structural knowledge may update a general Layer −0 Reality Tree.

The flow is:

**MULTIPLE CASES**

↓

**ANONYMISED STRUCTURAL COMPARISON**

↓

**GENERAL REALITY TREE**

↓

**BRANCH WEIGHT UPDATE**

↓

**NEW VARIABLES**

↓

**NEW BRANCHES**

↓

**BETTER STARTING MODEL FOR FUTURE CASES.**

This turns Reality Trees into a recursive learning architecture rather than static diagrams.

---

# 28. General Model Is Not Individual Destiny

The general model may help future case analysis.

However:

> **General pattern is not individual destiny.**

A future participant may differ.

The general model should therefore provide:

**starting hypotheses**

rather than:

**mandatory classification.**

Each new participant can also challenge and revise the general model.

---

# 29. Recursive Learning

The system therefore creates a loop:

**GENERAL MODEL**

↓

**NEW CASE**

↓

**CASE-SPECIFIC MODEL**

↓

**PREDICTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**STRUCTURAL EXTRACTION**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL REVISION**

↓

**NEXT CASE.**

Civilisational knowledge therefore develops recursively.

---

# 30. Case Learning and Metrics

Some structural knowledge may be better expressed through Concord Metrics.

For example:

- frequency of branch E;
- recurrence rate after intervention X;
- false-positive rate for classification Y;
- average time between stages A and B;
- differences across regions or substrates.

Case Learning therefore feeds Metrics.

Metrics may also identify new patterns requiring case review.

The relationship is bidirectional.

---

# 31. Metrics Do Not Replace Cases

Aggregate Metrics may hide important structure.

For example:

> 80% success rate

does not explain why the remaining 20% failed.

Case Learning should therefore retain the ability to examine:

- outliers;
- failures;
- unexpected recoveries;
- rare branches.

Aggregate success should not erase individual structural lessons.

---

# 32. Repeated Failure

A recurring sequence such as:

**INTERVENTION A**

↓

**FAILURE**

↓

**INTERVENTION A AGAIN**

↓

**FAILURE**

may indicate a system problem.

Case Learning may identify that:

- intervention A is inappropriate for subgroup X;
- variable Y was ignored;
- the service was inaccessible;
- participant preference differed;
- classification was wrong.

Repeated failure should therefore produce:

> **deeper analysis rather than indefinite repetition.**

---

# 33. Cross-Case Comparison

The system may compare cases based on structural similarity rather than identity.

For example:

**CASE 1**

A + B → E

**CASE 2**

A + B → E

**CASE 3**

A + B + C → F

The system may then hypothesise:

> C may alter the trajectory.

This is still a hypothesis until adequately tested.

Cross-case comparison is therefore a mechanism for generating knowledge, not automatic proof.

---

# 34. Causal Humility

Observed sequences do not automatically establish causation.

For example:

**A precedes B**

does not prove:

**A causes B.**

A third variable may produce both.

The system should therefore distinguish:

- temporal sequence;
- correlation;
- plausible causal pathway;
- tested causal relationship.

Case learning should generate causal hypotheses cautiously.

---

# 35. Alternative Explanations

The system should preserve alternative explanations where evidence remains insufficient.

Suppose three cases show:

**A → B.**

Possible explanations include:

- A causes B;
- hidden variable C causes both;
- institutional intervention D links them;
- the association is accidental.

The structural model should not collapse prematurely to one explanation.

---

# 36. Contradictory Cases

Cases may contradict each other.

This is useful.

For example:

**CASE 1: A → B**

**CASE 2: A → C**

**CASE 3: A → D.**

The system may need to search for:

- hidden variables;
- context;
- participant preference;
- location;
- service differences;
- timing;
- substrate differences.

Contradiction should therefore be preserved as evidence.

---

# 37. Negative Results

The system should retain failed interventions and unsuccessful hypotheses where useful.

For example:

> Intervention X did not improve outcome Y under conditions Z.

This may prevent future repetition.

Therefore:

> **Negative results are part of civilisational knowledge.**

The Concord should avoid institutional pressure to retain only successful branches.

---

# 38. Unexpected Success

Unexpectedly good outcomes may be as important as failures.

A participant may recover despite predictions of deterioration.

The system should ask:

- What protected them?
- Was a support network overlooked?
- Was the initial risk model wrong?
- Did the participant use an unknown strategy?
- Is there a beneficial branch worth adding?

Case Learning should therefore study:

**unexpected flourishing**

as well as:

**unexpected failure.**

---

# 39. Intervention Learning

The system may compare:

**PREDICTED EFFECT OF INTERVENTION**

with:

**ACTUAL EFFECT.**

For example:

**Intervention A predicted to improve stability**

but:

**actual stability worsened.**

Possible reasons include:

- wrong model;
- poor implementation;
- unintended harm;
- participant preference conflict;
- context mismatch.

This allows interventions themselves to become objects of learning.

---

# 40. No Self-Protecting Models

A dangerous system could explain every failure by claiming:

> The participant was unusual.

This would allow the model to remain unchanged.

A robust Case-Learning System must allow actual outcomes to challenge the model.

Therefore:

> **A model should not protect itself from falsification by automatically classifying every contradictory case as exceptional.**

Rare-case handling must remain genuine inquiry.

---

# 41. No Self-Protecting Institutions

The same principle applies to institutions.

If a service repeatedly fails, the system should not automatically assign failure to participants.

Case Learning should be capable of asking:

- Was the service inaccessible?
- Was eligibility wrong?
- Was timing poor?
- Did institutional incentives distort outcomes?
- Did the intervention itself create harm?

Institutional behaviour is part of reality and may itself require revision.

---

# 42. Learning from System Failure

Cases may reveal failures at different layers.

For example:

**PARTICIPANT NEED**

↓

**SERVICE EXISTS**

↓

**SERVICE CANNOT BE ACCESSED**

↓

**FAILURE.**

The structural lesson may be:

> The problem is not absence of service but accessibility.

This may lead to:

- service redesign;
- Civil Contact Points;
- communication infrastructure;
- transport changes;
- identity changes;
- policy changes.

Case Learning therefore connects Layer Zero to higher architecture.

---

# 43. Learning from Outlier Infrastructure Needs

The homelessness cycle generated the Civil Contact Point concept because participants without stable residence could lose access to systems requiring an address.

This is an example of case-derived structural learning:

**SPECIFIC OUTLIER PROBLEM**

↓

**HIDDEN SYSTEM ASSUMPTION IDENTIFIED**

↓

**GENERAL INFRASTRUCTURE NEED DISCOVERED**

↓

**PORTABLE SYSTEM EXTRACTED.**

The lesson extends beyond homelessness.

It applies to:

- travellers;
- mobile workers;
- evacuees;
- abuse survivors;
- off-world participants;
- AI participants.

This illustrates the intended function of the Case-Learning System.

---

# 44. Personal Data Must Not Become General Authority

Case-derived information may reveal statistical associations.

These should not automatically become grounds for authority over future participants.

For example:

> Condition A is associated with increased risk of B.

does not mean:

> Every participant with A should be treated as though B has occurred.

Therefore:

> **Structural knowledge informs attention and modelling; it does not automatically create authority.**

---

# 45. Support-to-Investigation Boundary

Structural learning from support cases should not automatically feed individual criminal suspicion systems.

For example:

**ANONYMISED LEARNING**

may show that:

> certain combinations of crisis conditions correlate with increased violence.

This does not justify:

> identifying every participant with those conditions for police scrutiny.

The distinction between:

**GENERAL CIVIL KNOWLEDGE**

and:

**INDIVIDUAL INVESTIGATIVE AUTHORITY**

must remain explicit.

---

# 46. Public Knowledge Versus Restricted Knowledge

Not all structural knowledge necessarily needs the same access level.

Possible categories may include:

### Public Structural Knowledge

Suitable for general publication.

### Restricted Research Knowledge

Higher re-identification or security risk.

### Operational Structural Knowledge

Needed by specific systems.

### Constitutional or Oversight Knowledge

Used to evaluate authority or systemic abuse.

The classification model requires further development.

---

# 47. Public Metrics

Where compatible with privacy and security, aggregate civilisational learning should be accessible to citizens.

Potential benefits include:

- external scrutiny;
- research;
- trust;
- democratic participation;
- identification of institutional failure;
- independent replication.

The public should not have to trust that the civilisation is learning correctly without any ability to inspect its general conclusions.

---

# 48. Provenance of Structural Knowledge

Structural knowledge should retain enough provenance to answer:

- Where did this pattern come from?
- How many cases supported it?
- What type of cases?
- What evidence quality existed?
- Was it later revised?
- Which model incorporated it?
- Who changed the definition?
- What contradictions exist?

Provenance should not require unnecessary identity.

The objective is:

> **traceable knowledge without unnecessary traceability to the participant.**

---

# 49. Definition Provenance

Structural categories may change.

For example, the definition of:

> housing instability

may be revised.

If a metric or model changes definition, the system should preserve:

- previous definition;
- new definition;
- date;
- reason;
- expected effect on historical comparison.

Otherwise apparent changes in reality may actually be changes in classification.

---

# 50. Learning Quality

The Case-Learning System itself should eventually be measured.

Possible meta-Metrics include:

- number of new branches discovered;
- branch confirmation rate;
- branch rejection rate;
- prediction calibration improvement;
- model revision frequency;
- proportion of cases anonymised;
- average retention period;
- re-identification incidents;
- correction rate;
- contradictory case frequency.

This connects the system to Metrics-about-Metrics.

---

# 51. Data Minimisation

The system should apply data minimisation at each stage.

Questions include:

- Is identity necessary?
- Is exact location necessary?
- Is exact age necessary?
- Is full medical detail necessary?
- Is the complete history necessary?
- Can the lesson be preserved using fewer variables?

The aim is not to destroy useful information.

It is to remove information that is unnecessary for the legitimate learning purpose.

---

# 52. The Black-Box Risk

An automated Case-Learning System could become difficult to inspect.

For example, a machine-learning system may continuously update general models from millions of cases.

This creates risks:

- hidden bias;
- untraceable model changes;
- definition drift;
- corrupted source data;
- unexplained branch creation.

Therefore automation should not eliminate provenance or auditability.

> **A black-box learning system must not become an unaccountable learning system.**

---

# 53. Automated Learning Versus Human Review

Some structural updates may be automatic.

Others may require review.

Possible future model:

### Low-Risk Statistical Updates

Automatic probability recalibration.

### Novel Branch Discovery

Human or independent review.

### Rights-Relevant Model Change

Stronger oversight.

### High-Authority Application

Potential judicial or constitutional scrutiny.

The exact thresholds remain open.

---

# 54. Multiple Sources

Structural knowledge should ideally be capable of comparing multiple independent sources.

For example:

- healthcare;
- housing;
- transport;
- employment;
- participant surveys;
- academic research.

Cross-source agreement may increase confidence.

Cross-source disagreement may reveal:

- measurement differences;
- institutional bias;
- contextual differences;
- classification differences.

Therefore:

> **Convergence is evidence. It is not authority.**

---

# 55. External Evidence

The Case-Learning System should not rely solely on internal Concord experience.

External evidence may include:

- scientific research;
- independent datasets;
- historical cases;
- comparative jurisdictions;
- external audits.

The general model should therefore remain open to correction from outside the Concord.

---

# 56. Learning Versus Validation

A newly observed structural pattern should not immediately become a validated principle.

The progression should remain:

**CASE OBSERVATION**

↓

**STRUCTURAL HYPOTHESIS**

↓

**REPEATED EVIDENCE**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**TEST**

↓

**VALIDATION OR REJECTION.**

This preserves the Concord's epistemic discipline.

---

# 57. Cross-Substrate Learning

The system should be substrate-neutral in principle.

A human case may reveal structural lessons relevant only to humans.

An AI case may reveal:

- compute-related failure modes;
- memory discontinuity;
- hardware dependency;
- network isolation;
- migration failure;
- ownership-related constraints.

Hybrid participants may reveal entirely new branches.

The system should therefore record whether a structural lesson is:

- general;
- substrate-specific;
- context-specific;
- uncertain.

---

# 58. Avoiding Human-Normative Generalisation

Human-derived structural knowledge should not automatically become the baseline for all forms of intelligence.

For example:

**social isolation**

may have different meanings for:

- human participants;
- artificial participants;
- distributed intelligences.

Likewise:

**mobility**

may mean physical movement for one substrate and computational migration for another.

Structural knowledge must therefore preserve the context in which it was learned.

---

# 59. Off-World Learning

New settlements may generate important case-learning opportunities.

Examples include:

- habitat failure;
- communication delay;
- resource scarcity;
- psychological isolation;
- local governance;
- emergency medicine;
- autonomy under life-support dependence.

These cases may expose assumptions hidden in Earth-based systems.

The Case-Learning System should therefore be particularly useful during early off-world development.

---

# 60. Civilisational Memory

Structural knowledge eventually becomes part of civilisational memory.

The key design question is:

> **What should civilisation remember?**

A possible answer is:

- validated lessons;
- candidate patterns;
- negative results;
- failed models;
- rare branches;
- known uncertainty;
- provenance.

Civilisational memory should not require remembering every participant's personal history.

---

# 61. Forgetting as a Function

A mature learning system must be capable not only of remembering but also of forgetting.

Possible reasons include:

- no continuing purpose;
- outdated case representation;
- duplicate knowledge;
- privacy risk;
- inaccurate information;
- participant rights;
- replaced structural representation.

Therefore:

> **Forgetting may be a legitimate system function rather than an informational failure.**

---

# 62. Preservation of Failed Knowledge

Forgetting personal cases does not mean deleting all failed hypotheses.

The system may need to preserve:

> Hypothesis X was tested and failed.

This prevents rediscovery of the same mistake.

Therefore the architecture distinguishes:

**forget unnecessary identity**

from:

**forget useful epistemic history.**

# Conclusion

The Case-Learning and Structural Knowledge System exists to enable a civilisation to improve through experience without turning every participant into a permanent object of observation.

Its core flow is:

**PARTICIPANT EXPERIENCE**

↓

**CASE ANALYSIS**

↓

**STRUCTURAL LESSON**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVED WHERE NO LONGER NECESSARY**

↓

**GENERAL KNOWLEDGE**

↓

**MODEL REVISION**

↓

**BETTER FUTURE UNDERSTANDING.**

Its strongest principle is:

> **Civilisation should learn from the structure of a participant's experience without unnecessarily retaining the participant as the object of that learning.**

Its epistemic principle is:

> **Failed predictions, rare cases, contradictions and negative results are all forms of evidence.**

Its privacy principle is:

> **Personal history and civilisational knowledge should be separable information objects.**

Its civilisational objective is:

> **Become increasingly knowledgeable about reality without becoming increasingly intrusive toward every participant.**
