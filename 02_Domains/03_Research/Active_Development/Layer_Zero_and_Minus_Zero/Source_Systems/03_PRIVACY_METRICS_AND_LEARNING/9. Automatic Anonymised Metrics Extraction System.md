# 1. Purpose

The **Automatic Anonymised Metrics Extraction System** is a candidate Concordian architecture for converting operational observations into civilisational Metrics without unnecessarily exposing the identities of the participants represented within those observations.

Its central purpose is:

> **to allow reality to enter Concord Metrics as directly as practical while allowing identity to enter only where necessary.**

The system therefore attempts to connect:

**WHAT IS ACTUALLY HAPPENING**

with:

**WHAT THE CIVILISATION BELIEVES IS HAPPENING**

while preserving:

- privacy;
- provenance;
- evidence status;
- correction;
- definition integrity;
- appropriate resolution.

---

# 2. Origin

The system emerged from the Layer-Zero and Case-Learning development cycle.

That work exposed a recurring problem.

A civilisation may have extensive operational records but still possess poor civilisational understanding because information remains trapped inside separate systems.

For example:

- hospitals know about injuries;
- housing systems know about instability;
- transport systems know about failures;
- support systems know about access barriers;
- employment services know about labour transitions.

If those observations do not feed general Metrics, Governance may operate with an incomplete picture of reality.

However, simply centralising all identifiable operational records would create severe privacy and surveillance risks.

The Automatic Anonymised Metrics Extraction System attempts to bridge those competing requirements.

---

# 3. Core Principle

The strongest source-derived principle is:

> **Reality should enter Metrics as directly as practical, while identity should enter only where necessary.**

This means the system should maximise:

- fidelity to operational reality;

while minimising:

- unnecessary identity;
- unnecessary personal history;
- unnecessary resolution.

---

# 4. Core Architecture

The basic architecture is:

**OPERATIONAL REALITY**

↓

**OPERATIONAL RECORD**

↓

**SOURCE-SYSTEM VALIDATION**

↓

**INTERMEDIARY PERSONAL-DATA BOUNDARY**

↓

**PURPOSE CHECK**

↓

**MINIMISATION**

↓

**ANONYMISATION / AGGREGATION**

↓

**METRIC DEFINITION**

↓

**CONCORD METRICS**

↓

**ANALYSIS / GOVERNANCE / RESOURCE PLANNING**

↓

**REAL-WORLD OUTCOME**

↓

**METRIC REVIEW.**

The system is therefore both an extraction mechanism and part of a larger feedback loop.

---

# 5. Operational Reality as Source

The system should favour information generated through real operations where such information is legitimate and useful.

Examples may include:

- emergency department admissions;
- transport breakdowns;
- housing service requests;
- service waiting times;
- infrastructure failures;
- employment transitions;
- energy demand;
- resource shortages;
- participant corrections;
- verified service outcomes.

This does not mean every operational record should automatically become a Metric.

It means operational records can be valuable evidence about actual conditions.

---

# 6. Operational Records Remain Operational

A source system should continue to hold the information necessary for its own legitimate function.

For example, a hospital may require:

- participant identity;
- detailed diagnosis;
- treatment;
- medication;
- clinician notes.

The Metrics system may need only:

- injury category;
- severity;
- treatment outcome;
- broad demographic category;
- broad location;
- time period.

Therefore:

> **The existence of detailed operational data does not justify copying the full operational record into Metrics.**

---

# 7. Extraction Rather Than Replication

The preferred architecture is:

**SOURCE RECORD**

↓

**EXTRACT RELEVANT STRUCTURE**

↓

**REMOVE UNNECESSARY IDENTITY**

↓

**SEND METRIC-RELEVANT OUTPUT.**

It is not:

**SOURCE RECORD**

↓

**COPY EVERYTHING TO CENTRAL DATABASE.**

The distinction is foundational.

---

# 8. Automatic Extraction

Where definitions and safeguards are sufficiently mature, extraction may be automated.

For example:

**SOURCE EVENT**

↓

**RULE-BASED OR MODEL-BASED EXTRACTION**

↓

**STANDARD METRIC EVENT**

↓

**AGGREGATION.**

Automation may improve:

- timeliness;
- consistency;
- scale;
- responsiveness.

However, automation also introduces new risks and must remain auditable.

---

# 9. Black-Box Anonymiser

The source development introduced a possible **Black-Box Anonymiser** between operational systems and general Metrics.

Its conceptual form is:

**RAW OPERATIONAL DATA**

↓

# BLACK-BOX ANONYMISER

↓

**MINIMISED / ANONYMISED METRIC INPUT.**

The purpose is to prevent downstream Metrics systems from requiring routine access to identifiable operational data.

However:

> **A black-box anonymiser must not become an unaccountable anonymiser.**

Its rules and transformations must remain auditable.

---

# 10. Minimum Necessary Variables

The extractor should ask:

- Which variables are needed for this Metric?
- Which are useful but unnecessary?
- Which increase re-identification risk?
- Which add no analytical value?

For example, a road-safety Metric may require:

- road category;
- collision type;
- injury severity;
- time band.

It may not require:

- participant name;
- exact home address;
- unrelated medical history.

---

# 11. Minimum Necessary Resolution

The system should preserve the:

> **minimum useful resolution**

rather than:

> **maximum available resolution.**

Examples include:

**Exact age**

may become:

**age band.**

**Exact address**

may become:

**district.**

**Exact minute**

may become:

**hour or day.**

The correct level depends on the Metric's purpose.

---

# 12. Resolution Must Remain Sufficient

Over-anonymisation can also destroy meaning.

For example:

> all incidents occurred somewhere in the country

may be too coarse to detect a dangerous junction.

The system must therefore balance:

**PRIVACY**

with:

**ACTIONABLE REALITY.**

The objective is not to make data vague.

It is to retain only the detail required to understand the relevant phenomenon.

---

# 13. Resolution Layers

Different users may legitimately receive different resolution levels.

For example:

### Public Level

National or regional aggregate.

### Operational Planning Level

District or service-level detail.

### Restricted Safety Analysis

Specific site detail where justified.

### Personal Operational Record

Identifiable individual detail held by source system.

This allows the same underlying reality to support different functions without universal access to maximum detail.

---

# 14. Metric Definition

Extraction requires a clear definition of what is being measured.

For example:

**Homelessness**

may have multiple meanings:

- rough sleeping;
- temporary accommodation;
- couch surfing;
- vehicle living;
- housing insecurity.

Without a definition, the Metric is ambiguous.

Therefore every significant Metric should eventually preserve:

- definition;
- scope;
- exclusions;
- extraction rule;
- source;
- version.

---

# 15. Definition Provenance

Definitions change.

If:

> homelessness

is defined differently in 2026 and 2030, a trend may appear even if reality did not change.

Therefore:

> **Changes to metric definitions, extraction methods or classifications should remain provenance-visible.**

A system should be able to distinguish:

**REAL-WORLD CHANGE**

from:

**MEASUREMENT-DEFINITION CHANGE.**

---

# 16. Observation Versus Interpretation

The system should distinguish:

**MEASUREMENT**

from:

**INTERPRETATION.**

For example:

### Measurement

73 emergency admissions occurred.

### Interpretation

Emergency-care access is deteriorating.

The first may be directly observed.

The second requires reasoning.

This distinction should remain visible.

---

# 17. Context

A Metric may be technically accurate but misleading without context.

For example:

**emergency department attendance increased by 20%.**

Possible explanations include:

- worse health;
- population growth;
- local clinic closure;
- improved hospital accessibility;
- coding changes;
- seasonal variation.

Therefore Metrics should not automatically contain their own causal interpretation.

---

# 18. Exposure Denominators

Raw frequency may be misleading.

For example:

**100 injuries**

means something different among:

- 1,000 users;
- 1,000,000 users.

Where relevant, Metrics should preserve denominator or exposure information.

Possible examples include:

- injuries per journey;
- failures per operating hour;
- homelessness cases per population;
- service failures per request;
- faults per device-hour.

---

# 19. Severity

Frequency alone may also be insufficient.

For example:

**100 minor faults**

may be less serious than:

**one catastrophic failure.**

Metrics may therefore need to represent:

- frequency;
- severity;
- reversibility;
- duration;
- population affected.

---

# 20. Cross-Source Confirmation

A Metric may be stronger when multiple independent sources indicate the same pattern.

For example:

**hospital data**

+

**ambulance data**

+

**transport incident data**

may collectively support a road-safety concern.

This creates:

> **convergence as evidence.**

However:

> **Convergence is evidence. It is not authority.**

Multiple systems can share the same underlying error.

---

# 21. Provisional Data

Operational data may initially be provisional.

For example:

- preliminary diagnosis;
- initial collision classification;
- first incident report.

The system should preserve this epistemic status.

The flow may be:

**PROVISIONAL OBSERVATION**

↓

**PROVISIONAL METRIC INPUT**

↓

**LATER REVIEW**

↓

**CONFIRMED / REVISED / REJECTED.**

---

# 22. Provisional-to-Final Tracking

The system should support changes such as:

**INITIAL CLASSIFICATION: A**

↓

**REVIEW**

↓

**FINAL CLASSIFICATION: B.**

The downstream Metrics system should be capable of reflecting that revision.

It should not freeze first reports as permanent truth.

---

# 23. Correction Propagation

A candidate principle is:

> **Where source observations are provisional, Metrics should preserve their epistemic status and propagate legitimate later corrections rather than treating first reports as permanent truth.**

This creates a recursive correction architecture.

---

# 24. Source Correction Example

Suppose an emergency department initially records:

> accidental injury.

Later evidence shows:

> occupational injury.

The system should be capable of:

**SOURCE CORRECTION**

↓

**EXTRACTION UPDATE**

↓

**METRIC UPDATE**

↓

**TREND RECALCULATION IF MATERIAL.**

---

# 25. Participant Correction

A participant may also correct source data.

For example:

a record states:

> unemployed

but participant is:

> self-employed.

A legitimate correction should, where relevant, propagate into anonymised Metrics.

This creates:

**PARTICIPANT CONTESTABILITY**

↓

**SOURCE CORRECTION**

↓

**ANONYMISED EXTRACTION**

↓

**BETTER CIVILISATIONAL METRIC.**

---

# 26. Correction Without Identity Exposure

A technical challenge is enabling downstream correction without giving the Metrics system unnecessary identity.

Possible architectures may include:

- pseudonymous update keys;
- recomputation from source;
- replacement aggregates;
- controlled linkage at intermediary boundary.

The exact implementation remains unresolved.

---

# 27. Metrics About Metrics

The system itself should be measured.

Possible meta-Metrics include:

- extraction error rate;
- correction rate;
- missing-data rate;
- source disagreement;
- anonymisation failure;
- re-identification incidents;
- definition-change frequency;
- stale-data rate;
- time from source correction to Metric correction.

This creates:

> **Metrics about Metrics.**

---

# 28. Measurement Error

Every measurement system has error.

Possible sources include:

- sensor failure;
- human coding error;
- incomplete reporting;
- software bugs;
- classification ambiguity;
- sampling bias;
- inaccessible services;
- deliberate falsification.

The Metrics architecture should represent uncertainty where material.

---

# 29. Missing Data

Absence of recorded data does not always mean absence of the underlying phenomenon.

For example:

low homelessness-service use may mean:

- low homelessness;

or:

- inaccessible services;
- low trust;
- participants not engaging.

Therefore:

> **No recorded event**

is not automatically equivalent to:

> **No real-world event.**

---

# 30. Service-Visibility Bias

Systems often measure what enters institutions.

They may miss what occurs outside them.

For example:

a healthcare system sees:

> people who reach healthcare.

It does not automatically see:

> people who need healthcare but cannot access it.

Metrics therefore need to consider visibility bias.

---

# 31. Outliers

Aggregate Metrics can hide rare but significant cases.

The extractor should therefore preserve mechanisms for unusual patterns to enter:

- case-learning systems;
- restricted analytical datasets;
- rare-event indicators.

This prevents averages from erasing structurally important failures.

---

# 32. Goodhart Risk

A Metric can distort behaviour when institutions are rewarded for improving the number rather than the underlying reality.

For example:

> Reduce homelessness count.

An institution might improve the Metric by:

- redefining homelessness;
- moving people between categories;
- reducing reporting.

The system therefore needs:

- definition provenance;
- cross-source comparison;
- outcome measures;
- participant feedback;
- meta-Metrics.

---

# 33. Metric Gaming

Potential gaming behaviours include:

- reclassification;
- under-reporting;
- delayed reporting;
- changing denominator;
- excluding difficult cases;
- moving problems between systems.

The extraction architecture should make such manipulation harder or at least more visible.

---

# 34. Direct Extraction and Goodhart Resistance

Automatic extraction from operational reality may reduce some gaming.

For example:

if injury Metrics are derived directly from clinical treatment records, it may be harder to manipulate them than manually reported performance figures.

However, automatic extraction does not eliminate Goodhart risk.

Source systems themselves may still change behaviour.

---

# 35. Public Metrics

A candidate principle is:

> **Aggregate civilisational Metrics should be accessible to citizens where compatible with privacy, security and re-identification risk.**

Public access may support:

- scrutiny;
- independent research;
- trust;
- comparison;
- accountability.

The level of detail must remain privacy-safe.

---

# 36. Public Transparency and Rare Cases

A public dataset may require more aggregation than an internal analytical system.

For example:

**specific rare incident**

may be useful to authorised safety analysts.

Publishing the same detail could identify the participant.

Multiple output resolutions may therefore be necessary.

---

# 37. Product-Specific Learning

The source development included product-specific learning as an example.

Suppose a manufactured product repeatedly causes:

- injury type A;
- mechanical failure B.

Operational systems may detect the pattern.

The extraction system can produce:

**ANONYMISED PRODUCT-RELATED METRIC**

↓

**FAILURE TREND**

↓

**SAFETY REVIEW.**

The system does not need the identities of all injured participants to establish the product-level pattern.

---

# 38. Infrastructure Learning

The same approach may apply to:

- roads;
- buildings;
- transport systems;
- energy systems;
- communication infrastructure;
- habitats.

For example:

**RECURRENT INCIDENTS AT LOCATION X**

may indicate:

> infrastructure design failure.

The response can then steer the system rather than targeting individuals.

---

# 39. System Steering

Automatic Metrics can support:

**OBSERVATION**

↓

**AGGREGATE PATTERN**

↓

**RESOURCE OR SYSTEM CHANGE**

↓

**OUTCOME.**

This enables:

> **system steering before individual steering**

where practical.

---

# 40. Resource Planning

Metrics may support prediction of:

- hospital capacity;
- housing demand;
- transport demand;
- emergency resources;
- energy demand;
- education capacity;
- support services.

The purpose is to make resources available before systemic failure becomes severe.

---

# 41. Civilisational Sensory Function

The Automatic Metrics Extraction System can be understood as part of a civilisational sensory layer.

Individual institutions detect local reality.

The extraction system converts these observations into:

> **shared civilisational awareness.**

The analogy should not be interpreted as justification for universal surveillance.

A mature sensory system should know:

> **what is happening**

without always needing to know:

> **who exactly is involved.**

---

# 42. Three Information Pathways

The source development distinguished different information pathways.

A mature architecture may contain:

### Personal Operational Pathway

Identifiable information needed to serve the participant.

### Anonymised Civil Learning Pathway

Structural and aggregate information used for Metrics and learning.

### Exceptional Identifiable Pathway

Specific identifiable access under separately justified authority.

These pathways should remain architecturally distinct.

---

# 43. Civil Learning Without Universal Observation

A key design objective is:

> **Where a civil objective can be achieved through aggregate system-level learning, unnecessary individual observation should be avoided.**

For example:

if aggregate data establish that a junction is dangerous, there may be no need to monitor every driver individually.

---

# 44. Privacy Boundary

The system should normally receive inputs through the Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary.

It should not directly become a universal collector of raw identifiable records.

This preserves:

**SOURCE SYSTEM**

→ identity where necessary.

**METRICS SYSTEM**

→ structure where sufficient.

---

# 45. Purpose Limitation

A Metric extracted for one purpose should not silently be reused for another incompatible purpose.

For example:

**HEALTH-SERVICE DEMAND METRIC**

should not automatically become:

**INDIVIDUAL CRIMINAL RISK SYSTEM.**

Aggregate information can still create indirect harms and therefore requires governance.

---

# 46. No Automatic Authority

A statistical pattern may reveal risk.

It does not itself authorise intervention against individuals.

Therefore:

> **A statistical pattern creates grounds for contextual examination, not automatic intervention.**

This carries forward the wider Concord principle:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

---

# 47. Metric-to-Action Separation

The preferred flow is:

**METRIC**

↓

**INTERPRETATION**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**GOVERNANCE / SERVICE ANALYSIS**

↓

**PROPORTIONATE RESPONSE.**

Not:

**METRIC THRESHOLD**

↓

**AUTOMATIC INDIVIDUAL CONTROL.**

Metrics inform decisions.

They do not become sovereignty.

---

# 48. Governance Interface

Governance may use Metrics to:

- identify system failure;
- allocate resources;
- compare interventions;
- detect trends;
- evaluate policy.

However:

> **Metric output is evidence for governance, not governance itself.**

The distinction between observation and authority must remain intact.

---

# 49. Judiciary Interface

Judiciary may need Metrics for:

- system-level evidence;
- discriminatory-pattern analysis;
- institutional performance;
- rights-impact review.

Aggregate Metrics do not necessarily establish facts about a specific individual case.

Individual adjudication requires appropriate case evidence.

---

# 50. Recursive Oversight

Recursive Oversight may examine:

- Metric definition changes;
- manipulation;
- access abuse;
- anonymisation failure;
- systematic blind spots;
- institutional gaming.

Metrics systems themselves must remain subject to oversight.

---

# 51. Provenance

Every significant Metric should eventually be traceable to:

- source category;
- extraction rule;
- version;
- time period;
- definition;
- anonymisation method;
- aggregation method;
- correction history.

This does not mean traceable to an identifiable participant where unnecessary.

The objective is:

> **traceable evidence without unnecessary personal traceability.**

---

# 52. Metric Versioning

A Metric should have a version history where material.

For example:

**Road Injury Metric v1**

→ included hospital admissions only.

**v2**

→ added ambulance-treated non-admissions.

The system should preserve the difference so longitudinal comparisons remain meaningful.

---

# 53. Uncertainty

Metrics should express uncertainty where relevant.

Potential sources include:

- incomplete coverage;
- sampling error;
- classification ambiguity;
- provisional data;
- unknown denominator;
- source disagreement.

False precision can be as misleading as poor measurement.

---

# 54. Confidence and Evidence Status

Future Metrics may use evidence labels such as:

- PRELIMINARY;
- PROVISIONAL;
- STABLE;
- REVISED;
- DISPUTED.

The exact terminology remains undeveloped.

The important principle is:

> **The Metric should preserve what is known about the quality of the evidence underlying it.**

---

# 55. Cross-Substrate Metrics

The system should support different participant substrates.

For artificial participants, operational Metrics might include:

- compute availability;
- continuity interruptions;
- hardware failures;
- network exclusion;
- energy access;
- memory corruption.

These Metrics may require different anonymisation because technical fingerprints can identify particular AI participants.

---

# 56. Human-Normative Metric Risk

Metrics designed for human life may be inappropriate for AI or hybrid participants.

For example:

**employment rate**

may be central to one population but meaningless for another.

Therefore the Metrics system should not treat one substrate's indicators as universal measures of flourishing.

---

# 57. Off-World Metrics

Off-world settlements may require operational Metrics concerning:

- oxygen;
- radiation;
- power;
- habitat reliability;
- food;
- water;
- medical demand;
- communication delay.

Small populations increase re-identification risk.

Therefore public resolution may need to be significantly lower than internal operational resolution.

---

# 58. Small-Population Problem

In a population of 30 participants, reporting:

> one participant with rare condition X

may effectively identify the individual.

Aggregation thresholds must therefore consider:

- population size;
- rarity;
- cross-data linkability.

This becomes especially important in early settlements and rare-substrate populations.

---

# 59. Security

The Metrics extractor may become an attractive target.

Threats include:

- source manipulation;
- extraction-rule manipulation;
- false Metric injection;
- deanonymisation;
- data poisoning;
- definition tampering.

Security must therefore cover both:

- privacy;
- epistemic integrity.

---

# 60. Data Poisoning

A malicious actor may attempt to alter operational records in order to influence civil Metrics.

For example:

- fabricated incidents;
- false classifications;
- coordinated reporting.

The system should therefore eventually consider:

- source authentication;
- anomaly detection;
- cross-source comparison;
- provenance.

---

# 61. Failure Modes

Potential failure modes include:

- unnecessary identity enters Metrics;
- wrong anonymisation;
- over-aggregation destroys important signal;
- under-aggregation enables re-identification;
- provisional data treated as final;
- corrections fail to propagate;
- definitions change invisibly;
- source bias;
- missing-data bias;
- Goodhart gaming;
- Metric used outside original purpose;
- raw numbers interpreted without context;
- correlation treated as causation;
- aggregate risk converted into individual authority;
- black-box extraction becomes unaccountable.

---

# 62. Abuse Cases

Future adversarial testing should include:

- agency changes metric definition to improve performance;
- rare participants are re-identified from public dashboard;
- employer uses public Metrics to discriminate against a district;
- automated extractor systematically misses inaccessible populations;
- false incident data manipulate resource allocation;
- provisional accusations enter permanent civil Metrics;
- anonymisation system secretly retains identity mapping;
- aggregate support data become criminal-risk indicators.

These should be treated as foreseeable architectural risks.

---

# 63. Candidate Principles

The architecture currently supports several candidate principles:

> **Reality should enter Metrics as directly as practical, while identity should enter only where necessary.**

> **Where source observations are provisional, Metrics should preserve their epistemic status and propagate legitimate later corrections rather than treating first reports as permanent truth.**

> **Aggregate civilisational Metrics should be accessible to citizens where compatible with privacy, security and re-identification risk.**

> **Civilisational Metrics should preserve sufficient provenance to establish where they came from and how they were constructed.**

> **Changes to metric definitions, extraction methods and classification rules should remain provenance-visible.**

> **Where a civil objective can be achieved through aggregate system-level learning, unnecessary individual observation should be avoided.**

> **A statistical pattern creates grounds for contextual examination, not automatic intervention.**

These remain candidate principles.

# 65. Explicit Non-Scope

The Automatic Anonymised Metrics Extraction System does **not** itself:

- govern;
- adjudicate;
- determine guilt;
- determine individual incapacity;
- authorise investigation;
- define constitutional rights;
- replace operational records;
- establish causation automatically;
- convert aggregate patterns into individual authority.

Its role is:

> **to transform relevant operational reality into privacy-preserving, provenance-aware civilisational Metrics.**

**REALITY TESTS THE RESULT.**

Its central principle is:

> **Reality should enter Metrics as directly as practical, while identity should enter only where necessary.**

Its privacy objective is:

> **Know what the civilisation is experiencing without unnecessarily knowing who every experience belongs to.**

Its epistemic objective is:

> **Preserve definition, provenance, uncertainty, correction and context so that a Metric remains evidence rather than becoming an unquestioned number.**

And its wider civilisational objective is:

> **Create a civilisation capable of observing itself accurately enough to learn, while remaining constrained enough not to turn observation into universal surveillance.**
