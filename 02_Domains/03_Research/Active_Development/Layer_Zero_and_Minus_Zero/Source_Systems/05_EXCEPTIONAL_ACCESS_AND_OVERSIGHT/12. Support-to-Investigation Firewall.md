# 1. Purpose

The **Support-to-Investigation Firewall** is a candidate Concordian architecture for preventing civil support systems from silently becoming sources of suspicion, investigation, enforcement or coercive authority.

Its central purpose is:

> **to preserve the ability of participants to seek help without ordinary support automatically becoming investigative intelligence.**

The architecture therefore separates:

**SUPPORT**

from:

**INVESTIGATION.**

The two systems may sometimes interact under separately justified conditions.

They should not silently become the same system.

---

# 2. Origin

The architecture emerged from the development of:

- Predictive Support;
- Handrail systems;
- Participant Digital Twins;
- mental-health support;
- addiction support;
- housing assistance;
- financial assistance;
- civil-support Metrics.

A critical problem became apparent.

If a participant knows that asking for help may automatically create:

- a police flag;
- an employer flag;
- a risk score;
- insurance consequences;
- loss of rights;
- investigative attention,

then seeking support may itself become dangerous.

The participant may rationally avoid assistance.

This can cause earlier voluntary support to fail and later crisis to worsen.

The architecture therefore protects the distinction between:

> **“I need help.”**

and:

> **“I am a subject of investigation.”**

---

# 3. Core Principle

The strongest source-derived principle is:

> **Support is not suspicion.**

A closely related principle is:

> **Support data should not silently become predictive authority data.**

And the architectural warning is:

> **Handrail must not become Tripwire.**

---

# 4. The Core Category Error

The architecture exists to prevent this sequence:

**PARTICIPANT SEEKS HELP**

↓

**SYSTEM RECORDS VULNERABILITY**

↓

**VULNERABILITY IS REINTERPRETED AS RISK**

↓

**RISK IS REINTERPRETED AS SUSPICION**

↓

**SUSPICION CREATES AUTHORITY**

↓

**PARTICIPANT IS INVESTIGATED.**

Each step may appear administratively convenient.

Together, they can convert civil support into surveillance infrastructure.

---

# 5. Preferred Architecture

The preferred architecture is:

**SUPPORT SYSTEM**

↓

**SUPPORT PURPOSE**

↓

# SUPPORT-TO-INVESTIGATION FIREWALL

║

**NO ROUTINE IDENTIFIABLE FLOW**

║

**INVESTIGATIVE SYSTEM**

The firewall may permit exceptional crossings only where separately justified.

---

# 6. What the Firewall Protects

The firewall may protect information generated through:

- housing support;
- financial support;
- mental-health support;
- addiction treatment;
- employment assistance;
- Civil Contact Points;
- supported decision-making;
- Participant Digital Twins;
- Handrail services.

The exact categories remain subject to later development.

The general rule is:

> **information obtained because a participant sought support should not automatically become investigative material.**

---

# 7. Why This Matters

Without a firewall, support systems may become self-defeating.

For example:

**MENTAL-HEALTH SERVICE**

is intended to encourage early help.

But if use automatically triggers:

**SECURITY REVIEW**

then high-risk participants may avoid the service.

The support system becomes less effective precisely because it is integrated too closely with enforcement.

---

# 8. Handrail Versus Tripwire

A **Handrail** is:

- available;
- useful;
- voluntary where possible;
- safe to approach.

A **Tripwire**:

- detects;
- flags;
- triggers;
- escalates.

If ordinary use of a support system triggers unrelated authority, it has become a Tripwire.

Therefore:

> **Handrail must not become Tripwire.**

---

# 9. Firewall Does Not Mean Absolute Secrecy

The firewall should not be interpreted as:

> support information can never be accessed under any circumstances.

Some exceptional circumstances may justify access.

Examples might include:

- specific lawful investigation;
- immediate serious threat;
- judicially authorised access;
- participant-requested transfer.

The important distinction is:

> **exceptional access must be a separate authorised event, not the default architecture.**

---

# 10. Default Separation

The default should be:

**SUPPORT DATA**

→ remains within support purpose.

Not:

**SUPPORT DATA**

→ automatically copied into general investigative systems.

This creates structural privacy rather than privacy by promise alone.

---

# 11. Purpose Limitation

Information collected for:

> **support**

should normally be used for:

> **support.**

If another purpose is proposed, the system should ask:

1. What is the new purpose?
2. Is identifiable data necessary?
3. What authority permits the transfer?
4. Is the scope proportionate?
5. Is less intrusive information sufficient?
6. Is provenance recorded?

---

# 12. Support Data Versus Investigative Evidence

The architecture should distinguish:

**SUPPORT INFORMATION**

from:

**INVESTIGATIVE EVIDENCE.**

Support information may be:

- incomplete;
- contextual;
- self-reported;
- disclosed under expectations of assistance;
- highly sensitive.

It should not automatically acquire investigative evidentiary status merely because it exists in a civil system.

---

# 13. Vulnerability Versus Suspicion

A participant experiencing:

- depression;
- addiction;
- housing instability;
- debt;
- unemployment

may be vulnerable.

That does not mean they are suspicious.

The architecture therefore rejects the silent transformation:

**VULNERABILITY**

→ **SUSPICION.**

---

# 14. Prediction Versus Suspicion

Predictive Support may identify possible deterioration.

For example:

> employment instability may indicate increased risk of housing loss.

That is not equivalent to:

> participant is likely to commit an offence.

Even where criminal risk is modelled, prediction alone should not become investigative authority.

---

# 15. Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

The broader Concord principle applies:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

Within this architecture:

> **Predictive support information does not itself establish grounds for investigation.**

If legitimate investigative grounds exist, they should arise through the relevant evidence and authority pathway.

---

# 16. No Suspicion from Refusal

Refusing support should not automatically become:

- suspicion;
- risk escalation;
- evidence of concealment.

A participant may refuse because they:

- do not need the service;
- prefer another provider;
- value privacy;
- distrust institutions;
- intend to solve the issue themselves.

Therefore:

> **Refusal is not automatically suspicious.**

---

# 17. No Compliance Score

The architecture should resist creation of a general:

> **cooperation score**

based on whether participants accept support.

A system in which:

- accepts assistance = cooperative;
- refuses assistance = suspicious

would undermine voluntary support.

---

# 18. Support Records and Law Enforcement

Routine law-enforcement access to full support records would likely destroy the separation this architecture seeks to create.

The preferred model is:

**NO GENERAL BROWSING OF SUPPORT DATABASES.**

Specific access should require separately legitimate justification.

---

# 19. Specificity

Exceptional access should normally concern:

> **a specific participant, case, event or evidential purpose.**

Not:

> “Give us every support record in case something useful appears.”

The more general the request, the greater the surveillance risk.

---

# 20. Necessity

Exceptional access should ask:

> **Is identifiable support information actually necessary?**

An investigation may sometimes be satisfied through:

- aggregate information;
- confirmation of one event;
- limited metadata;
- another source.

The firewall should encourage the minimum necessary disclosure.

---

# 21. Proportionality

The scope of access should be proportionate to the justification.

For example:

a narrow question should not automatically justify:

> the participant's entire lifetime support history.

Possible limits include:

- date range;
- data category;
- specific service;
- specific event.

---

# 22. Authority

Exceptional crossing of the firewall should require a recognised authority basis.

Possible bases might include:

- judicial authorisation;
- statutory power;
- immediate emergency authority;
- participant consent.

The exact legitimate authorities remain unresolved.

The architectural principle is that authority must be explicit.

---

# 23. Provenance

Every legitimate exceptional crossing should create provenance.

A future record might include:

- requester;
- authority;
- case reference;
- purpose;
- data requested;
- data released;
- time;
- approver;
- expiry;
- later review.

This enables accountability.

---

# 24. The Authority–Provenance Relationship

A source-derived relationship is:

**AUTHORITY ↑**

→ **JUSTIFICATION ↑**

→ **PROVENANCE ↑**

→ **OVERSIGHT ↑.**

Intrusive access should create stronger records and review requirements.

---

# 25. Support Staff

Support workers should not automatically become investigative agents.

Otherwise participants may reasonably perceive every conversation as potential evidence collection.

The architecture should therefore define whether support staff:

- may report;
- must report;
- cannot report;
- may report only specified categories.

The exact rules require later development.

---

# 26. Mandatory Reporting

Some domains may legitimately contain mandatory reporting duties.

These should be:

- explicit;
- narrow;
- understandable;
- legally grounded;
- provenance-recorded.

A participant should not be told support is confidential if broad hidden reporting rules exist.

---

# 27. Transparency of Limits

Support services should clearly explain important confidentiality limits.

For example:

> information remains within support system except under specific emergency or lawful-access conditions.

The exact wording will depend on implementation.

The underlying principle is:

> **participants should not be deceived about the privacy boundary.**

---

# 28. Mental-Health Example

A participant seeks help for anxiety.

The default flow should be:

**MENTAL-HEALTH SUPPORT**

↓

**TREATMENT / SUPPORT.**

Not:

**MENTAL-HEALTH SUPPORT**

↓

**AUTOMATIC POLICE RISK RECORD.**

If a genuinely specific serious threat emerges, a separate exceptional process may become relevant.

---

# 29. Addiction Example

A participant voluntarily seeks addiction treatment.

That should not automatically become:

- criminal suspicion;
- employment flag;
- general civil-risk classification.

Otherwise the system punishes the very behaviour it intends to encourage:

> seeking help before crisis.

---

# 30. Housing Example

A participant seeks emergency housing support.

Housing instability should not automatically create:

- policing scrutiny;
- child-custody assumptions;
- financial-risk classifications;
- criminal-risk scores.

Any separate action requires separate evidence.

---

# 31. Financial Support Example

A participant requests debt advice.

That should not automatically make them:

> financially untrustworthy

for unrelated purposes.

Support information should remain purpose-limited.

---

# 32. Employment Example

A participant requests workplace stress or career-transition support.

The support service should not automatically notify the employer of sensitive details unless legitimately required or authorised.

Otherwise voluntary employment support becomes a hidden performance-monitoring system.

---

# 33. Civil Contact Points

Civil Contact Points may serve participants experiencing:

- homelessness;
- mobility;
- displacement.

Using one should not automatically mark the participant as:

- unstable;
- criminally risky;
- socially suspect.

A contact mechanism is not a risk label.

---

# 34. Digital Twin Interface

Participant Digital Twins may contain highly sensitive:

- context;
- predictions;
- support history;
- hypotheses.

The investigative system should not receive routine access to the full Digital Twin.

A model of possible futures is especially dangerous if treated as evidence of acts that never occurred.

---

# 35. Unrealised Branches

A simulated or predicted branch must never be treated as though the participant has already:

- chosen;
- caused;
- intended;
- committed

the events represented within it.

This is a critical boundary.

---

# 36. No Punishment for Simulated Futures

The architecture supports the principle:

> **A simulated future must never be treated as though the participant has already chosen, caused or committed the acts represented within it.**

This applies strongly to investigative use.

Layer −0 branches are models, not evidence of completed acts.

---

# 37. General Metrics

Aggregate support Metrics may legitimately inform:

- service planning;
- resource allocation;
- systemic review.

For example:

> addiction-support demand increased 20%.

That does not require transferring participant identities to law enforcement.

---

# 38. Aggregate Learning Versus Individual Investigation

The architecture therefore distinguishes:

**CIVILISATIONAL LEARNING**

from:

**INDIVIDUAL INVESTIGATION.**

Aggregate patterns may help identify:

- service failures;
- infrastructure needs;
- social trends.

They should not silently recreate person-level investigative databases.

---

# 39. Anonymous and Pseudonymous Support

Where feasible, some support may be provided:

- anonymously;
- pseudonymously;
- with minimal identity.

This may be especially useful for:

- information services;
- early counselling;
- initial guidance.

Not every support interaction requires complete civil identity.

---

# 40. Minimum Necessary Identity

Where identity is required, the system should still ask:

> **How much identity is necessary?**

Examples:

- contact information may suffice;
- full civil dossier may not.

This follows the wider data-minimisation architecture.

---

# 41. Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary

The Support-to-Investigation Firewall depends closely on the Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary.

The preferred architecture is:

**SUPPORT SYSTEM**

↓

**PERSONAL-DATA BOUNDARY**

↓

**ANONYMISED CIVIL LEARNING**

while:

**EXCEPTIONAL IDENTIFIABLE ACCESS**

uses a separate controlled path.

---

# 42. Exceptional Identifiable Access

System 13 develops the exceptional path in more detail.

At this extraction stage, the firewall establishes only that identifiable investigative crossing should be:

- specific;
- justified;
- authorised;
- necessary;
- proportionate;
- purpose-limited;
- provenance-recorded.

---

# 43. Firewall Crossing Should Be an Event

A key architectural distinction is:

> **exceptional access should occur as a discrete event.**

It should not be an ambient permanent connection.

This makes it possible to:

- log;
- review;
- challenge;
- expire;
- audit

each access.

---

# 44. Persistent Access

Persistent investigative access to support systems creates greater risks.

Examples include:

- direct database integration;
- live feeds;
- continuous law-enforcement browsing.

Such architectures should require much stronger justification, if permitted at all.

The default should favour specific bounded access.

---

# 45. Data Replication Risk

If support data are copied into an investigative database, later deletion or correction becomes difficult.

The firewall should therefore consider whether access can occur without creating uncontrolled copies.

Possible future designs include:

- query-based access;
- temporary view;
- controlled export;
- automatic expiry.

---

# 46. Correction Propagation

If exceptional information is copied and the source later changes, the investigative copy may become stale.

The architecture should therefore consider:

- correction notifications;
- provenance links;
- expiry;
- source verification.

This remains technically unresolved.

---

# 47. Investigative Context

Even legitimately accessed support information may require contextual interpretation.

For example:

> participant said “I cannot go on.”

may have different meanings depending on context.

Support records should not be stripped of all context and treated as literal investigative conclusions.

---

# 48. Evidentiary Status

A support record may contain:

- participant statement;
- support-worker inference;
- hypothesis;
- assessment.

These categories should remain distinguishable.

A support worker's speculation should not silently become:

> verified fact.

This mirrors Concord's broader Observation / Inference / Hypothesis distinctions.

---

# 49. Evidence-Conditioned Investigation

A legitimate investigation should be conditioned by evidence independent of mere support participation.

The source development describes an **Evidence-Conditioned Investigation Method**.

The general sequence is:

**SPECIFIC EVIDENCE**

↓

**INVESTIGATIVE JUSTIFICATION**

↓

**AUTHORITY**

↓

**PROPORTIONATE ACCESS.**

Not:

**SUPPORT USE**

↓

**INVESTIGATION.**

---

# 50. High-Risk Predictive Investigation

Using predictive models to initiate investigation is particularly high risk.

This belongs to the later:

**Evidence-Conditioned Investigative Modelling**

research system.

It should not be silently introduced through support infrastructure.

---

# 51. Emergency Exceptions

A genuine immediate emergency may require rapid information sharing.

Potential examples include:

- imminent serious harm;
- medical emergency;
- life-support failure.

Emergency access should still be:

- scoped;
- logged;
- reviewable afterward.

Emergency speed does not eliminate later accountability.

---

# 52. Emergency Expiry

Emergency access authority should end when the emergency ends.

Otherwise:

**TEMPORARY EXCEPTION**

can become:

**PERMANENT BACKDOOR.**

This should be explicitly tested.

---

# 53. Judicial Interface

Judiciary may be required for some exceptional access.

Possible functions include:

- authorisation;
- review;
- challenge;
- remedy.

The exact judicial threshold remains unresolved.

The firewall merely establishes the need for an authority boundary.

---

# 54. Recursive Oversight

Recursive Oversight may examine:

- access frequency;
- denied requests;
- emergency exceptions;
- repeated requesters;
- unusual patterns;
- scope creep;
- purpose drift.

The firewall itself must remain auditable.

---

# 55. Dual Provenance

Exceptional access may generate two distinct provenance records:

### Restricted Investigative Provenance

Contains necessary identity and case detail.

### External Oversight Provenance

Shows that exceptional power was exercised while protecting participant identity where appropriate.

This is developed further in System 14.

---

# 56. Transparency of Procedure

A powerful principle from the source architecture is:

> **Transparency of procedure without unnecessary transparency of identity.**

The public may need to know:

- how often powers are used;
- under what authority;
- whether oversight occurs.

They do not necessarily need the identities of participants subject to those powers.

---

# 57. Public Trust

The firewall supports trust by making a credible claim:

> **Ordinary support does not automatically become investigation.**

That trust may increase:

- early help-seeking;
- honest disclosure;
- voluntary engagement.

The firewall therefore has practical as well as rights-protective value.

---

# 58. Chilling Effects

Without strong separation, participants may alter behaviour because they fear surveillance.

Potential chilling effects include:

- avoiding mental-health care;
- avoiding addiction treatment;
- avoiding financial support;
- withholding information;
- avoiding Civil Contact Points.

These effects should become measurable in future pilots.

---

# 59. False Negatives

Strong privacy can also create a risk:

important information may not reach investigators when legitimately necessary.

The architecture therefore must balance:

- privacy;
- support trust;
- public safety.

The answer should not be unlimited sharing.

It should be a well-defined exceptional access route.

---

# 60. False Positives

Weak separation creates the opposite risk:

innocent support activity generates suspicion.

This may disproportionately affect vulnerable populations.

The firewall should reduce false investigative escalation caused merely by use of civil support.

---

# 61. Discrimination Risk

If support use becomes a risk proxy, systems may discriminate against groups more likely to use support.

For example:

- disabled participants;
- poor participants;
- displaced participants;
- certain AI substrates.

This is another reason support status should not automatically become suspicion data.

---

# 62. AI Participants

Artificial participants may need:

- maintenance support;
- compute assistance;
- memory restoration;
- safe-space services.

Using those services should not automatically be interpreted as:

- instability;
- dangerousness;
- grounds for shutdown.

The same firewall principle applies across substrates.

---

# 63. AI Diagnostic Data

AI support records may contain:

- errors;
- memory anomalies;
- capability changes;
- hardware faults.

These may be highly sensitive.

An investigative system should not automatically interpret technical maintenance data as evidence of harmful intent.

---

# 64. Hybrid Participants

Hybrid participants may generate combined:

- medical;
- cognitive;
- computational

support data.

The firewall should protect against cross-domain repurposing.

For example:

medical support information should not silently become general behavioural surveillance.

---

# 65. Off-World Settlements

Small off-world settlements may require closer coordination for genuine survival reasons.

However:

> **shared vulnerability does not automatically justify unlimited information sharing.**

Life-support exceptions should be narrow and connected to actual survival requirements.

Institutional convenience is not equivalent to survival necessity.

---

# 66. Security Agencies

National or settlement security agencies may legitimately require exceptional access in some circumstances.

This makes the firewall especially important.

The architecture should prevent a security exception from becoming:

> **a universal default access channel.**

---

# 67. Secret Access

Some investigations may require temporary secrecy.

Where participant notification is impossible or harmful, stronger independent oversight becomes important.

A source-derived relationship is:

**REQUIRED SECRECY ↑**

→ **INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT ↑.**

---

# 68. Later Notification

Future architecture may consider whether participants should be notified after secret access is no longer sensitive.

Possible reasons include:

- contestability;
- correction;
- accountability.

The exact exceptions and timelines remain unresolved.

---

# 69. Firewall Audit Metrics

Potential Metrics include:

- number of exceptional access requests;
- approvals;
- denials;
- emergency accesses;
- average scope;
- duration;
- later review outcomes;
- unauthorised access attempts;
- support participation changes after policy changes.

These Metrics should usually be aggregated to protect identity.

---

# 70. Chilling-Effect Metrics

Possible future indicators include:

- reduction in voluntary support uptake;
- increased incomplete disclosures;
- participant trust surveys;
- withdrawal after access-policy changes.

These may reveal whether the firewall is functioning socially, not merely technically.

---

# 71. Abuse Detection

Repeated requests from one agency or official may indicate:

- legitimate operational need;
- scope creep;
- fishing behaviour.

Recursive Oversight should be able to detect patterns.

---

# 72. Separation of Duties

A useful future safeguard may separate:

- support provider;
- access-authorising authority;
- investigator;
- oversight body.

This makes unilateral self-authorisation harder.

---

# 73. Auditability

A firewall that cannot prove when it was crossed is not a strong firewall.

Therefore exceptional access must, where possible, be technically or procedurally auditable.

Potential methods include:

- immutable logs;
- cryptographic records;
- independent audit trails.

Technical implementation remains open.

---

# 74. Failure Modes

Potential failure modes include:

- support databases directly accessible to investigators;
- vague emergency exception;
- refusal treated as suspicion;
- predictive support converted into risk scoring;
- participants not informed of confidentiality limits;
- access logs missing;
- copied data never corrected;
- emergency access never expires;
- support staff used as covert investigators;
- one agency authorises its own access;
- aggregate support Metrics re-identify individuals;
- support participation used as adverse eligibility data.

---

# 75. Abuse Cases

Future adversarial testing should include:

- participant seeks addiction help and is automatically investigated;
- homeless participant's support record becomes a criminal-risk profile;
- police query all mental-health records within a district;
- AI maintenance request triggers mandatory containment;
- secret emergency access continues after emergency ends;
- support worker is pressured to collect unrelated investigative information;
- insurer indirectly obtains support-use status;
- refusal of counselling becomes evidence of dangerousness;
- predictive branch is treated as evidence of future crime.

These should be treated as foreseeable architectural abuses.

---

# 76. Candidate Principles

The architecture currently supports several candidate principles:

> **Support is not suspicion.**

> **Support data should not silently become predictive authority data.**

> **Handrail must not become Tripwire.**

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

> **Exceptional access to support information should be specific, justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited and provenance-recorded.**

> **A simulated future must never be treated as though the participant has already chosen, caused or committed acts represented within it.**

> **Where investigative secrecy is legitimately required, independent oversight should increase rather than decrease.**

These remain candidate principles.

---

# 77. System Outputs

The Support-to-Investigation Firewall may eventually output:

- permitted or denied data-transfer decisions;
- purpose-boundary decisions;
- access-scope limits;
- exceptional-access referrals;
- provenance events;
- emergency-expiry requirements;
- audit alerts;
- misuse alerts.

It should not itself conduct investigation.

---

# 78. Explicit Non-Scope

The Support-to-Investigation Firewall does **not** itself:

- investigate;
- establish guilt;
- determine criminal suspicion;
- conduct policing;
- adjudicate;
- define every emergency;
- authorise unlimited access;
- guarantee absolute confidentiality;
- prevent all legitimate exceptional access.

Its role is:

> **to preserve a strong default boundary between support and investigative authority.**

# Conclusion

The Support-to-Investigation Firewall exists because a civilisation that makes asking for help dangerous will eventually make people afraid to ask for help.

Its preferred architecture is:

**PARTICIPANT SEEKS SUPPORT**

↓

**SUPPORT IS PROVIDED**

↓

**SUPPORT INFORMATION REMAINS PURPOSE-BOUND**

║

**FIREWALL**

║

**INVESTIGATION REQUIRES SEPARATE EVIDENCE, JUSTIFICATION AND AUTHORITY.**

Its shortest principle is:

> **Support is not suspicion.**

Its warning is:

> **Handrail must not become Tripwire.**

Its authority rule is:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

And its larger civilisational objective is:

> **Make support safe enough to seek, while preserving a separately accountable pathway for genuinely justified exceptional access.**
