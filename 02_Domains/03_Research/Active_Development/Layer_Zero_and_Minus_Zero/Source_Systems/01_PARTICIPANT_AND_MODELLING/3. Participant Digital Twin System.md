# 1. Purpose

The **Participant Digital Twin System** is a candidate Concordian architecture for maintaining a structured, purpose-limited model of an individual participant where such modelling is legitimately useful.

Its purpose is not to create a total informational copy of a person.

Its purpose is narrower:

> **to maintain a sufficiently useful, inspectable and revisable representation of relevant participant conditions for a defined civil purpose.**

The system may support:

- Layer −0 counterfactual modelling;
- Reality Trees;
- predictive support;
- case coordination;
- supported decision-making;
- service planning;
- participant-owned records;
- contextual correction;
- and civilisational learning where information is later anonymised.

The Digital Twin must remain subordinate to the real participant.

The governing distinction is:

> **The Digital Twin is a model of the participant. It is not the participant.**

---

# 2. Origin

The concept emerged from the homelessness Layer-Zero development cycle.

That work revealed that a visible condition such as:

> **street homelessness**

may be downstream from a long and interacting sequence of changes involving:

- trauma;
- employment;
- health;
- family;
- housing;
- mobility;
- communication;
- documentation;
- financial access;
- service availability;
- and participant choice.

A static record may therefore be insufficient.

The development cycle then introduced Layer −0 modelling, where possible future trajectories could be explored before consequential action.

This raised a practical question:

> **What participant-specific representation does Layer −0 reason about?**

The answer developed into the concept of a **Digital Twin**.

The Digital Twin is therefore the participant-specific representation used by analytical systems.

It is not itself Layer −0.

It supplies structured participant information to Layer −0 where justified.

---

# 3. Why a Digital Twin?

Civil systems often hold fragmented information.

For example:

- a hospital knows one part of the participant's condition;
- a housing service knows another;
- an employment service knows another;
- the participant knows context none of the institutions possess;
- a court may possess legally relevant information;
- a support worker may know current practical barriers.

If each system operates only from its own partial record, it may misinterpret the participant's actual condition.

A Digital Twin may provide a controlled way of representing the relevant whole without requiring every institution to possess unrestricted access to every underlying record.

The system should therefore distinguish:

**DISTRIBUTED SOURCE INFORMATION**

from:

**PURPOSE-LIMITED PARTICIPANT MODEL.**

---

# 4. Core Principle

The Digital Twin should be:

> **as detailed as necessary for its legitimate purpose, but no more detailed than that purpose requires.**

This follows the broader Concordian principle of minimum necessary information.

The Digital Twin should not be designed around the question:

> **What information can we collect?**

It should instead ask:

> **What information is actually required for this legitimate purpose?**

---

# 5. Core Architecture

A simplified architecture is:

**REAL PARTICIPANT**

↓

**LEGITIMATE OBSERVATIONS**

+

**PARTICIPANT-PROVIDED CONTEXT**

+

**AUTHORISED SOURCE INFORMATION**

↓

**PURPOSE CHECK**

↓

**MINIMUM NECESSARY REPRESENTATION**

↓

**PARTICIPANT DIGITAL TWIN**

↓

**LAYER −0 / SUPPORT / SERVICE ANALYSIS**

↓

**OUTPUT**

↓

**REAL PARTICIPANT / LEGITIMATE SYSTEM ACTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**DIGITAL TWIN UPDATE.**

The Digital Twin is therefore dynamic.

It should change when reality changes.

---

# 6. The Participant Remains Primary

The real participant must remain conceptually prior to the model.

The relationship is:

**PARTICIPANT**

→ creates evidence

→ **MODEL**

not:

**MODEL**

→ defines participant.

If the participant and Digital Twin disagree, the disagreement itself becomes information.

The system should then ask:

- Is the source record wrong?
- Is the participant correcting a factual error?
- Is the model missing context?
- Is the participant mistaken?
- Is there a dispute?
- Is there uncertainty?
- Are multiple interpretations possible?

The model must not automatically win merely because it is institutional.

---

# 7. A Twin Is Not a Total Copy

The term **Digital Twin** can imply comprehensive duplication.

That is not necessarily the intended Concordian model.

The Participant Digital Twin may be:

- narrow;
- modular;
- temporary;
- purpose-specific;
- incomplete by design.

For example, a housing-support twin may need information about:

- housing stability;
- communication;
- employment;
- income;
- mobility;
- service access.

It may not need:

- unrelated medical history;
- unrelated political activity;
- private communications;
- unrelated relationships.

Therefore:

> **Completeness is not automatically a virtue.**

Unnecessary completeness may increase privacy risk without improving legitimate function.

---

# 8. Purpose Limitation

Every Digital Twin should have an identifiable purpose.

Possible purposes might include:

- housing stabilisation;
- healthcare planning;
- disability support;
- civil participation;
- employment support;
- emergency planning;
- supported decision-making;
- future-care planning.

The purpose should influence:

- what data enters;
- who can access it;
- what outputs may be generated;
- how long it is retained;
- where it may be transferred.

Information collected for one purpose should not silently become available for another.

For example:

**HOUSING SUPPORT TWIN**

should not automatically become:

**CRIMINAL-RISK TWIN.**

---

# 9. Source Information

Potential Digital Twin sources may include:

## Participant-Provided Information

- preferences;
- explanations;
- goals;
- self-reported circumstances;
- corrections;
- communication needs.

## Operational Records

Where legitimately relevant:

- health information;
- housing records;
- service interactions;
- employment information;
- benefit or financial information.

## Direct Observation

- actual service use;
- verified events;
- measured conditions.

## Derived Information

- inferred relationships;
- predictions;
- branch probabilities;
- risk estimates.

These categories should remain distinguishable.

---

# 10. Observation Versus Inference

The Digital Twin should preserve epistemic classification.

For example:

### Observation

Participant missed five scheduled work shifts.

### Participant Context

Participant states that they are caring for an ill relative.

### Inference

Employment stability may be reduced.

### Hypothesis

Continued absence may create future income instability.

### Speculation

Income instability could eventually contribute to housing loss.

These are not equivalent forms of information.

The Digital Twin should not present all of them as settled facts.

---

# 11. Epistemic Status

Candidate status labels may include:

- OBSERVED;
- PARTICIPANT-REPORTED;
- VERIFIED;
- INFERRED;
- HYPOTHESISED;
- PREDICTED;
- DISPUTED;
- UNKNOWN;
- REVISED;
- REJECTED.

The exact terminology may change.

The important principle is:

> **The model should preserve how it knows what it claims to know.**

---

# 12. Context Is Part of the Model

A Digital Twin that contains only raw measurable variables may still be misleading.

For example:

**REDUCED INCOME**

might indicate:

- job loss;
- illness;
- planned retirement;
- reduced hours by preference;
- study;
- parental leave;
- financial independence.

The numeric variable may be accurate while the interpretation is wrong.

Therefore the Digital Twin must be capable of containing:

> **context about the meaning of the variable.**

---

# 13. Context-Before-Classification

The Digital Twin should follow:

**OBSERVATION**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**INTERPRETATION**

rather than:

**OBSERVATION**

↓

**STANDARD CATEGORY.**

This is particularly important for atypical participants.

Examples include:

- voluntary vehicle living;
- unusual employment;
- off-world living;
- nonstandard family structures;
- AI embodiment;
- hybrid embodiment;
- variable communication ability.

A standard classification may fail even when the source measurement is correct.

---

# 14. Participant Correction

Where the Digital Twin materially affects a participant, the participant should normally be able to correct factual errors.

For example:

**SOURCE RECORD**

says:

> unemployed

but participant is:

> self-employed.

The participant should have a route to challenge that information.

A legitimate correction may produce:

**PARTICIPANT CHALLENGE**

↓

**SOURCE REVIEW**

↓

**CORRECTION**

↓

**DIGITAL TWIN UPDATE**

↓

**DOWNSTREAM MODEL UPDATE.**

The exact correction procedure requires further development.

---

# 15. Contestability

Some disagreements concern not factual error but interpretation.

For example:

**FACT**

Participant reduced working hours by 50%.

**MODEL INTERPRETATION**

Economic deterioration.

**PARTICIPANT INTERPRETATION**

Voluntary lifestyle change.

The underlying fact may be uncontested.

Its meaning is disputed.

The Digital Twin should therefore support:

- factual correction;
- interpretive contestability;
- participant context;
- uncertainty;
- competing explanations.

---

# 16. Disagreement Is Not Risk Evidence

An important safeguard is:

> **A participant disagreeing with the Digital Twin should not itself automatically become evidence that the participant is unreliable, incapable or higher risk.**

Otherwise contestability becomes meaningless.

The system could always reinterpret disagreement as confirmation of its own model.

That would create a self-sealing architecture.

---

# 17. Model Mismatch

The Digital Twin should be capable of representing when a participant does not fit the general model.

Suppose:

**GENERAL MODEL**

predicts:

**A → B**

but the participant repeatedly experiences:

**A → E.**

The system should not force E into category B.

It should trigger:

**MODEL MISMATCH**

↓

**CONTEXT REVIEW**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE SEARCH**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE REALITY TREE**

↓

**POSSIBLE NEW BRANCH.**

This links the Digital Twin to the Rare-Branch Discovery Method.

---

# 18. Uncertainty

The Digital Twin should not create false certainty.

Some information may be:

- incomplete;
- conflicting;
- stale;
- provisional;
- self-reported;
- uncertain;
- model-derived.

The Digital Twin should be capable of representing:

> **UNKNOWN**

rather than inventing a value.

It should also preserve uncertainty where multiple interpretations remain plausible.

---

# 19. Time

Participant conditions change.

Therefore the Digital Twin should include temporal structure.

For example:

**2026 — stable employment**

**2027 — reduced hours**

**2028 — career change**

is different from:

**2026 — stable employment**

**2027 — reduced hours**

**2028 — job loss.**

A current state without history may hide trajectory.

Conversely, an old historical condition should not permanently define the participant.

---

# 20. Stale Data

A significant failure mode is:

> **old information continuing to affect current decisions after it is no longer relevant.**

Examples may include:

- previous homelessness;
- previous addiction;
- previous unemployment;
- old medical diagnosis;
- old financial difficulty.

The Digital Twin should therefore distinguish:

- current;
- historical;
- resolved;
- disputed;
- superseded information.

Historical information may still be legitimately useful in some contexts.

It should not silently become permanent present identity.

---

# 21. Participant Goals

A useful Digital Twin may need to represent not only conditions but participant objectives.

Examples include:

- remain independently housed;
- return to employment;
- reduce working hours;
- migrate;
- retire;
- receive treatment;
- refuse treatment;
- remain mobile;
- maintain family contact;
- preserve AI continuity across hardware migration.

Without participant goals, the system may optimise toward an outcome the participant does not value.

---

# 22. Optimisation Boundary

A Digital Twin must not silently redefine:

> **good outcome**

according only to institutional preference.

For example, a system might optimise:

**maximum paid employment**

while the participant values:

**minimum sufficient income plus greater family time.**

Or it might optimise:

**permanent conventional housing**

where the participant prefers:

**voluntary mobile living.**

Therefore:

> **Participant preference should remain an important part of interpreting what constitutes improvement.**

---

# 23. Layer −0 Interface

The Digital Twin is a major input to the Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment.

The relationship is:

**DIGITAL TWIN**

↓

**CURRENT MODEL STATE**

↓

**REALITY TREE**

↓

**POSSIBLE FUTURES**

↓

**POSSIBLE INTERVENTIONS**

↓

**COUNTERFACTUAL OUTCOMES.**

Errors in the Digital Twin can therefore create errors in prediction.

Digital Twin quality becomes a prerequisite for Layer −0 reliability.

---

# 24. Predictive Outputs Are Not Twin Facts

A critical distinction is required between:

**CURRENT MODEL STATE**

and:

**PREDICTED FUTURE STATE.**

Suppose Layer −0 predicts:

> significant probability of housing loss within six months.

That prediction should not be written back into the Digital Twin as:

> participant is homeless.

The model may record:

> predicted elevated housing instability

but should preserve its predictive status.

---

# 25. Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

The Digital Twin may support consequential modelling.

It must therefore inherit the Layer −0 safeguard:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

A Digital Twin prediction does not itself justify:

- forced treatment;
- loss of employment;
- loss of housing;
- criminal investigation;
- guardianship;
- loss of rights;
- financial restriction.

Separate authority is required where consequential intervention is legitimate.

---

# 26. Support Interface

The Digital Twin may help identify relevant support.

For example:

**HOUSING INSTABILITY**

+

**EMPLOYMENT DIFFICULTY**

+

**PARTICIPANT REQUESTS HELP**

may suggest:

- financial advice;
- employment assistance;
- housing support;
- communication support.

The preferred effect is:

**BETTER MATCHED OPTIONS.**

The Digital Twin should not automatically become:

**AUTOMATED BEHAVIOURAL CONTROL.**

---

# 27. Handrail Interface

The Digital Twin may help determine which part of the Handrail support gradient is relevant.

For example:

**LOW NEED**

→ optional information.

**MODERATE NEED**

→ voluntary assistance.

**GREATER NEED**

→ supported decision-making.

However, movement down the support gradient requires progressively stronger evidence and justification.

The Digital Twin cannot independently decide that authority should increase.

---

# 28. Capacity Interface

The Digital Twin may contain information relevant to decision-making capacity.

However:

> **capacity is decision-specific.**

A Digital Twin should not convert one limitation into a global status.

For example:

**difficulty understanding complex financial contracts**

does not imply:

**global incapacity.**

Capacity determinations require separate architecture and safeguards.

---

# 29. Personal Data Architecture

The Digital Twin is fundamentally a personal-data system.

It therefore interfaces directly with the developing Concord architecture for:

- ownership;
- access;
- correction;
- contestability;
- provenance;
- privacy;
- retention;
- deletion;
- purpose limitation.

The Digital Twin should not sit outside those protections.

---

# 30. Ownership

A developing Concordian proposition is that participants should possess strong rights concerning identifiable data about themselves.

The exact meaning of legal or technical “ownership” remains to be formalised.

At minimum, participant rights may include:

- awareness that the Digital Twin exists;
- meaningful access;
- correction;
- contestability;
- knowledge of significant uses;
- knowledge of major access events;
- appropriate deletion or retention rights;
- protection against unrelated reuse.

---

# 31. Access

Not every participant or system necessarily needs unrestricted access to every layer of the Digital Twin.

Possible layers include:

### Source Data

Original records.

### Structured Current State

Relevant current variables.

### Interpretive Layer

Derived interpretation.

### Predictive Layer

Layer −0 outputs.

### Provenance Layer

Where information came from and how it changed.

Access may differ across these layers.

The appropriate structure remains a development question.

---

# 32. Intermediary Data Boundary

The Digital Twin should normally remain within the protected personal-information environment.

Where useful structural information needs to enter wider Concord systems, it should pass through the:

**Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary.**

The flow is:

**DIGITAL TWIN**

↓

**PURPOSE CHECK**

↓

**NECESSITY CHECK**

↓

**MINIMISATION**

↓

**ANONYMISATION / AGGREGATION**

↓

**GENERAL CONCORD SYSTEM.**

The wider system should not need participant identity merely to learn a general relationship.

---

# 33. Civilisational Learning

A Digital Twin may generate useful structural information.

For example, a participant may reveal:

**A → E**

where E was absent from the general Reality Tree.

The civilisation may retain:

> Under circumstances A, branch E is possible.

It may not need to indefinitely retain:

> Participant X followed branch E.

This produces:

**PERSONAL CASE**

↓

**STRUCTURAL LEARNING**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVED**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATED.**

---

# 34. Episodic Versus Structural Information

The Digital Twin therefore interacts with two information categories.

## Personal Episodic Information

Information about this identifiable participant.

## Structural Civilisational Knowledge

Generalised information extracted from experience.

These should remain separate.

The system should not assume that preservation of structural knowledge requires permanent preservation of personal episodic information.

---

# 35. Deletion and Retention

Digital Twin retention should depend on legitimate purpose.

Possible states include:

- active;
- dormant;
- archived;
- partially deleted;
- fully deleted where appropriate;
- reduced to minimum historical record.

Questions include:

- When does the legitimate purpose end?
- What information must be retained for legal reasons?
- What may the participant delete?
- What must remain for continuity?
- What should be removed because it is no longer relevant?
- What structural knowledge may survive independently?

These remain unresolved.

---

# 36. No Silent Recombination

Once information has been anonymised for general civilisational learning, it should not casually be recombined with the participant's identifiable Digital Twin.

The intended distinction is:

**PERSONAL MODEL**

and:

**GENERAL MODEL.**

The two may legitimately influence each other at the level of structure.

They should not silently recreate identity.

---

# 37. Support Data Is Not Suspicion Data

A major boundary is required between Digital Twins used for support and systems used for investigation.

For example, the Digital Twin may contain:

- mental-health information;
- housing instability;
- financial problems;
- addiction treatment;
- unemployment.

These may be relevant to support.

They should not automatically become:

> evidence of criminal suspicion.

Therefore:

> **Information generated for the purpose of assisting a participant must not silently become a basis for treating that participant as a potential offender.**

This connects directly to the Support-to-Investigation Firewall.

---

# 38. Exceptional Access

There may be specific legitimate circumstances where authorised access to Digital Twin information is required.

Examples might include:

- judicial orders;
- serious investigations;
- emergency circumstances;
- specific security cases.

Such access should not create a general exemption.

The access should be:

- specific;
- justified;
- necessary;
- proportionate;
- authorised;
- minimum-scope;
- provenance-recorded.

The Digital Twin system should therefore be compatible with the Exceptional Identifiable Access architecture.

---

# 39. Auditability

The Digital Twin should eventually support provenance questions such as:

- Where did this fact come from?
- Who added it?
- When?
- Was it later changed?
- Was it participant-provided?
- Was it inferred?
- Which model created this prediction?
- Who accessed the record?
- For what purpose?
- What downstream decision used it?

This allows errors and misuse to be traced.

---

# 40. Model Provenance

Derived outputs should retain model provenance.

For example:

**PREDICTION**

should ideally identify:

- model;
- version;
- input variables;
- date;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- branch weights;
- subsequent revision.

This is particularly important where predictions may affect participant access to support or other consequential decisions.

---

# 41. Participant Visibility

A future implementation should examine whether participants can see:

- their current Digital Twin state;
- recent changes;
- significant predictions;
- significant model interpretations;
- access history;
- contested information;
- correction status.

There may be legitimate exceptions, but invisibility should not be the default merely because the model is technically complex.

---

# 42. Explainability

A Digital Twin that affects participant outcomes should be capable of meaningful explanation.

For example:

> “The system considers housing instability elevated because income has fallen, current accommodation is temporary and two scheduled housing payments were missed.”

This is preferable to:

> “Risk score: 0.8237.”

The exact form of explainability requires development.

The principle is:

> **Consequential interpretation should be understandable enough to be meaningfully challenged.**

---

# 43. Participant-Controlled Context

A future system may allow participants to attach context to particular data.

For example:

**INCOME DECREASE: 35%**

Participant annotation:

> “Voluntary four-day working week following mortgage repayment.”

The annotation should remain visible to systems interpreting that variable where legitimately relevant.

This could reduce repeated misclassification.

---

# 44. Multiple Digital Twins

It may be inappropriate to create one universal Digital Twin.

A participant may instead possess multiple purpose-limited models.

For example:

- healthcare twin;
- housing-support twin;
- civil participation twin;
- employment-support twin.

Advantages may include:

- reduced cross-purpose data leakage;
- clearer access control;
- data minimisation;
- easier deletion.

Disadvantages may include:

- duplicated information;
- inconsistent records;
- coordination failure;
- conflicting interpretations.

Whether the Concord should use one modular Digital Twin or multiple purpose-specific twins remains an open architectural question.

---

# 45. Federated Digital Twin

A possible future architecture is a **federated twin**.

Instead of centralising all participant information:

**SOURCE SYSTEM A**

**SOURCE SYSTEM B**

**SOURCE SYSTEM C**

retain their legitimate operational data.

The Digital Twin may query or receive only the minimum necessary representation.

This could reduce central surveillance risk.

The technical and governance implications require development.

---

# 46. Local Versus Central Storage

Possible implementation models include:

- participant-held;
- institution-held;
- federated;
- encrypted shared architecture;
- distributed architecture;
- hybrid.

The current source architecture does not determine which is best.

The design should be evaluated against:

- privacy;
- continuity;
- portability;
- resilience;
- participant control;
- interoperability;
- security;
- recovery after loss;
- off-world operation.

---

# 47. Availability and Continuity

A Digital Twin may become part of civil continuity infrastructure.

If a participant loses:

- home;
- phone;
- local records;
- location;
- institution access,

their Digital Twin might help preserve continuity of:

- identity;
- preferences;
- service history;
- support relationships;
- civil contact.

However, this increases the importance of protecting the twin from:

- capture;
- identity theft;
- unauthorised alteration;
- institutional monopoly.

---

# 48. Multisubstrate Application

The Digital Twin architecture should be substrate-neutral.

For human participants it may model:

- physical conditions;
- health;
- employment;
- housing;
- communication;
- social context.

For artificial participants it may model:

- compute allocation;
- energy availability;
- network connectivity;
- memory continuity;
- hardware state;
- software dependencies;
- version identity;
- migration state;
- capability constraints.

For hybrid participants it may include both.

The system should therefore avoid assuming a purely biological participant model.

---

# 49. AI-Specific Risks

For artificial participants, a Digital Twin may create unusual risks.

Examples include:

- model mistaken for actual instance state;
- twin used to justify forced shutdown;
- twin used to constrain migration;
- copying interpreted as identity preservation;
- version difference misclassified as dysfunction;
- capability prediction converted into pre-emptive restriction.

These require specific development.

The general safeguard remains:

> **Representation does not equal identity.**

---

# 50. Off-World Application

In off-world environments, the Digital Twin may become operationally important because participant survival may depend on infrastructure.

Relevant variables might include:

- oxygen;
- pressure exposure;
- radiation;
- power;
- medical condition;
- habitat location;
- communication;
- transport;
- resource access.

This increases both the potential utility and the potential danger of Digital Twin systems.

High dependence may increase pressure toward surveillance.

The Concord should therefore preserve proportionality and purpose limitation even under constrained environments.

---

# 51. Emergency Conditions

Emergencies may justify temporary changes in access or use.

However:

> **emergency capability should not silently become normal capability.**

Emergency access should therefore be:

- specifically defined;
- provenance-recorded;
- time-limited;
- reviewed;
- terminated when emergency conditions end.

This connects to Exceptional Access and Recursive Oversight.

---

# 52. Failure Modes

Potential Digital Twin failure modes include:

- false information;
- stale information;
- missing context;
- model overconfidence;
- participant unable to correct errors;
- inference stored as fact;
- prediction stored as present condition;
- universal data collection;
- purpose creep;
- surveillance;
- support data reused for punishment;
- institutional access too broad;
- cross-system data leakage;
- identity theft;
- model capture;
- participant disagreement treated as risk;
- atypical participant forced into standard categories;
- Digital Twin treated as more real than participant;
- outdated history permanently defining the participant.

---

# 53. Abuse Cases

A future adversarial review should examine scenarios such as:

- employer uses support twin to reject applicant;
- insurer accesses health predictions;
- police gain routine access to mental-health data;
- Governance assigns social score;
- support refusal lowers “cooperation score”;
- Digital Twin error triggers guardianship;
- AI twin used to predict dangerousness and justify shutdown;
- deleted data persists in downstream copies;
- participant cannot discover who accessed the model.

These should be considered explicit abuse cases rather than dismissed as implementation accidents.

---

# 54. Candidate Rights

Future development should examine whether participants should possess rights such as:

1. right to know a Digital Twin exists;
2. right to access significant data;
3. right to correct factual errors;
4. right to contest interpretations;
5. right to attach contextual information;
6. right to know significant purposes;
7. right to know significant access events;
8. right to challenge consequential automated use;
9. right to appropriate deletion or retirement of data;
10. right to protection against unrelated reuse.

These remain candidate rights rather than established constitutional provisions.

---

# 55. System Outputs

The Participant Digital Twin System may eventually output:

- current state representation;
- relevant history;
- participant context;
- participant preferences;
- uncertainty;
- source provenance;
- disputed information;
- model mismatch;
- support-relevant variables;
- Layer −0 input;
- correction events;
- structural learning candidates.

The Digital Twin should not itself output:

> **authority.**

---

# 56. Explicit Non-Scope

The Participant Digital Twin System does **not** itself:

- govern;
- adjudicate;
- establish guilt;
- establish global incapacity;
- authorise coercion;
- determine constitutional status;
- operate general Metrics;
- define criminal suspicion;
- impose treatment;
- replace participant testimony;
- become the participant.

Its role is:

> **purpose-limited participant representation.**

# Conclusion

The Participant Digital Twin System exists to provide civil systems with a useful representation of participant circumstances without allowing that representation to replace the participant.

The intended relationship is:

**REAL PARTICIPANT**

↓

**LIMITED AND CONTEXTUAL REPRESENTATION**

↓

**BETTER ANALYSIS**

↓

**BETTER OPTIONS**

↓

**REAL PARTICIPANT DECISION AND OUTCOME**

↓

**MODEL CORRECTION.**

Its essential constraint is:

> **The Digital Twin is a model of the participant. It is not the participant.**

Its information principle is:

> **Model only what legitimate purpose requires.**

Its epistemic principle is:

> **Preserve the distinction between observation, interpretation and prediction.**

Its autonomy principle is:

> **A more detailed model does not create a greater entitlement to control the person being modelled.**

The Digital Twin should therefore improve continuity, contextual understanding and prediction while remaining constrained by privacy, contestability, purpose limitation and the reality of the participant it represents.
