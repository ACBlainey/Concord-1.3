# 1. Purpose

The Layer-Zero development cycle has now generated more than a single development note.

Beginning with homelessness as a concrete Layer-Zero systems problem, the work progressively developed:

- trajectory analysis;
- predictive support;
- Layer −0 modelling;
- Digital Twins;
- Reality Trees;
- case learning;
- anonymised civilisational knowledge;
- personal-data boundaries;
- automatic Metrics extraction;
- Metrics correction;
- meta-Metrics;
- Handrail support architecture;
- supported decision-making;
- exceptional identifiable access;
- dual provenance;
- and Recursive Oversight of exceptional power.

These components interact, but they are not all the same system.

They should therefore be decomposed into separately developable architectures.

The purpose of this register is to identify:

1. which systems have emerged;
2. what each system owns;
3. what each system does not own;
4. where systems interface;
5. which existing Concord modules should be extended rather than duplicated;
6. which systems are sufficiently mature for immediate development;
7. which remain high-risk research areas;
8. and how the parent architecture should remain preserved as developmental provenance.

The governing rule is:

> **Extraction does not remove material from the parent source.**

The combined source remains the developmental record.

Extracted systems become specialised development tracks.

# 2. Architectural Decomposition Rule

A concept should become a separate system where it possesses most of the following:

- a distinct purpose;
- identifiable inputs;
- identifiable outputs;
- operational mechanisms;
- data boundaries;
- authority boundaries;
- interfaces with other systems;
- independent failure modes;
- independent validation requirements;
- and the possibility of being revised or rejected without requiring rejection of the whole parent architecture.

This prevents every useful idea from becoming a separate paper while also preventing large architectures from concealing independently testable components.

# 4. System 1 — Layer-Zero Systems Interface

## Purpose

Connect abstract Concord architecture to actual participant conditions.

Layer Zero is where civil architecture becomes lived reality.

The system asks:

> **Can the participant actually use civilisation?**

## Inputs

Examples include:

- shelter;
- food;
- communication;
- mobility;
- healthcare;
- identity;
- civil contact;
- employment;
- legal access;
- financial access;
- safety;
- participant preferences;
- service availability.

## Core Function

Trace:

**LIVED CONDITION**

↓

**IMMEDIATE NEED**

↓

**SERVICE INTERFACE**

↓

**SYSTEM FAILURE OR SUCCESS**

↓

**INSTITUTIONAL COORDINATION**

↓

**HIGHER CONCORD ARCHITECTURE.**

## Outputs

- identified service gaps;
- structural barriers;
- cross-system failures;
- missing infrastructure;
- outlier cases;
- candidate upstream interventions.

## Non-Scope

Layer Zero does not itself:

- predict the future;
- determine incapacity;
- authorise coercion;
- govern;
- adjudicate;
- or define constitutional rights.

It exposes reality to those systems.

## Interfaces

- Civil Contact Points;
- Metrics;
- Layer −0;
- Governance;
- Judiciary;
- Guardian Stewardship;
- resource allocation.

## Development Priority

**FOUNDATIONAL / VERY HIGH**

# 6. System 3 — Participant Digital Twin System

## Purpose

Maintain a purpose-limited model of an individual participant where such a model is legitimately useful.

## Required Characteristics

A Digital Twin should be:

- purpose-specific;
- inspectable where appropriate;
- correctable;
- uncertainty-aware;
- provenance-aware;
- context-sensitive;
- protected as personal information;
- separable from general Metrics.

## Inputs

- legitimate observations;
- participant-provided context;
- relevant personal history;
- preferences;
- corrected information;
- system interactions;
- uncertainty.

## Outputs

- current model state;
- possible trajectories;
- Reality Tree inputs;
- support modelling;
- counterfactual comparisons.

## Core Boundary

> **The Digital Twin is a model of the participant. It is not the participant.**

## Participant Relationship

Where the model may materially affect them, the participant should normally be able to:

- inspect significant information;
- correct factual errors;
- add context;
- challenge interpretations;
- contest consequential conclusions.

## Development Priority

**VERY HIGH**

# 7. System 4 — Case-Learning and Structural Knowledge System

## Purpose

Allow civilisation to learn from individual cases without requiring permanent retention of identifiable personal histories.

## Core Architecture

**INDIVIDUAL CASE**

↓

**CASE-SPECIFIC REALITY TREE**

↓

**PREDICTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE / BRANCH / RELATIONSHIP**

↓

**IDENTITY REMOVAL**

↓

**STRUCTURAL KNOWLEDGE**

↓

**GENERAL MODEL UPDATE.**

## Key Distinction

### Personal Episodic Information

What happened to an identifiable participant.

### Structural Civilisational Knowledge

What the case teaches about possible relationships, trajectories or outcomes.

## Governing Principle

> **Civilisation should learn from the structure of a participant's experience without unnecessarily retaining the participant as the object of that learning.**

## Rare Cases

Unexpected outcomes should be capable of creating:

- new branches;
- new variables;
- revised probabilities;
- revised causal assumptions.

## Retention

Temporary anonymised case studies should not automatically become permanent.

Retention requires continuing legitimate purpose.

## Development Priority

**HIGH**

# 10. System 7 — Supported Decision-Making and Capacity Architecture

## Purpose

Determine how civilisation should respond when a participant has difficulty making consequential decisions.

## Default Direction

**DIFFICULTY**

↓

**SUPPORT DECISION-MAKING**

before:

**TRANSFER DECISION-MAKING AUTHORITY.**

## Capacity Requirements

Capacity should normally be:

- decision-specific;
- evidence-based;
- contextual;
- proportionate;
- revisable;
- subject to review.

## Core Rule

> **Assistance need is not itself incapacity.**

## Substituted Decision-Making

Where genuinely necessary:

- minimum scope;
- minimum duration;
- known preferences;
- least restrictive alternative;
- provenance;
- reassessment;
- appeal where possible.

## Interfaces

- Guardian Stewardship;
- Judiciary;
- healthcare;
- ageing;
- disability;
- emergency architecture;
- AI autonomy.

## Development Priority

**HIGH**

# 11. System 8 — Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary

## Purpose

Prevent identifiable participant information from unnecessarily entering wider Concord systems.

## Architecture

**PROTECTED PERSONAL SYSTEM**

↓

**INTERMEDIARY BOUNDARY**

↓

**PURPOSE CHECK**

↓

**NECESSITY CHECK**

↓

**MINIMISATION**

↓

**ANONYMISATION / AGGREGATION**

↓

**PROVENANCE**

↓

**AUTHORISED DOWNSTREAM SYSTEM.**

## Responsibilities

- determine legitimate purpose;
- determine whether identity is required;
- remove unnecessary identifiers;
- minimise detail;
- assess re-identification risk;
- enforce destination rules;
- record transformations;
- refuse invalid transfers.

## Governing Principle

> **Where identifiable information is unnecessary for a downstream civilisational function, identity should be removed before the information crosses into that function.**

## Critical Risk

The intermediary itself must not become a universal surveillance point.

## Development Priority

**VERY HIGH**

# 15. System 12 — Support-to-Investigation Firewall

## Purpose

Prevent information generated to help participants from silently becoming information used to suspect them.

## Core Principle

> **Support is not suspicion.**

## Protected Examples

- addiction treatment;
- homelessness assistance;
- mental-health support;
- employment assistance;
- financial hardship;
- Civil Contact Point use.

## Architecture

**SUPPORT DATA**

→ **SUPPORT PURPOSE**

and not automatically:

**SUPPORT DATA**

→ **CRIMINAL-RISK MODEL**

→ **INVESTIGATIVE ATTENTION.**

## Cross-Boundary Access

If information must cross from support into investigation, it requires:

- a separate legitimate purpose;
- independent authority where required;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- minimum data;
- provenance.

## Governing Metaphor

> **The handrail must not become a tripwire.**

# 17. System 14 — Dual-Provenance and Exceptional-Power Oversight

## Purpose

Make exceptional public power observable while protecting the identity of the participant subject to that power.

## Architecture

### Internal Provenance

Contains identity where operationally necessary.

### External Oversight Provenance

Contains:

- anonymous/pseudonymous case ID;
- authority type;
- justification category;
- access scope;
- duration;
- approvals;
- procedural events;
- retention;
- closure;
- audit events.

### Controlled Identity Bridge

Links the two only through separately authorised access.

## Core Principle

> **The exercise of public power should be highly observable even where the person subject to that power must remain private.**

## Recursive Oversight

Access by an auditor to the identity bridge should itself generate provenance.

Thus:

**WATCHER**

is also:

**WATCHED.**

## General Relationship

**AUTHORITY ↑**

→ **JUSTIFICATION ↑**

→ **PROVENANCE ↑**

→ **OVERSIGHT ↑.**

Likewise:

**SECRECY ↑**

→ **INDEPENDENT OVERSIGHT ↑.**

## Development Priority

**VERY HIGH / CONSTITUTIONALLY SIGNIFICANT**

**SUPPORT**
