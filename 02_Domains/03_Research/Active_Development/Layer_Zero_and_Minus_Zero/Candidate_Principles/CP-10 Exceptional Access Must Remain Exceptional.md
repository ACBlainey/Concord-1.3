# CP-10 — Exceptional Access Must Remain Exceptional

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.1  
**Document Type:** Candidate Principle  
**Status:** CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — UNDER CRITICAL EXAMINATION / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Validation:** Internal architectural and adversarial examination completed; empirical validation not established.

---

## 1. Candidate Principle

> **Access to identifiable participant information outside its ordinary legitimate purpose should remain a specific, separately justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited, provenance-recorded and bounded exercise of authority; standing capability for exceptional access should not become standing entitlement to exercise it.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This principle emerged from the Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary, Support-to-Investigation Firewall, Exceptional Identifiable Access System, Dual-Provenance Oversight and emergency-access analysis.

## 3. Problem Addressed

This candidate was extracted because the originating systems repeatedly encountered a civilisational failure mode that could not be treated safely as a mere implementation detail. The principle is intended to state the higher-level constraint without prematurely selecting one implementation architecture.

## 4. Intended Scope

The principle is intended to apply across civil systems where its underlying problem occurs, including human, artificial, hybrid and other plausible participants. Specific implementations may require substrate-specific rules.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This principle does not create automatic authority, unlimited entitlement, proof of effectiveness, or exemption from other Concordian constraints. Its application remains subject to context, proportionality, competing rights, evidence and review.

## 6. Derived Rules and Safeguards

- Exceptional Access Is an Event — Exceptional access should normally occur as a bounded event or permission.
- Minimum Necessary Scope — Access should be limited in data, duration, resolution and recipient.
- Re-Identification Is Access — Re-identification of anonymised or pseudonymised information should itself count as identifiable access.
- Access Does Not Manufacture Authority — An access mechanism should mediate authority established elsewhere rather than create authority from data usefulness.
- Emergency Access Must Expire — Emergency access should remain time-limited, provenance-recorded and reviewable.
- Identity Bridges Require Control — Any reversal of anonymisation should be separately controlled and auditable.

## 7. Ethical-Kernel Relationship

Primary relationship: **Preserve Safe Spaces; Respect Autonomy; Act Honestly; Accept Responsibility.**

The mapping is provisional and does not imply constitutional adoption.

## 8. Relationship to Existing Concord Architecture

This principle should constrain or guide relevant mechanisms and architectures without being confused with them. Source mechanisms remain independently testable and may be revised or rejected even if the principle survives.

## 9. Key Risks and Adversarial Questions

- How is ordinary legitimate purpose defined?
- Can institutions redefine exceptional access as routine?
- How should standing emergency capabilities be governed?
- What thresholds justify re-identification?

## 10. Cross-Substrate Considerations

The candidate is intended to be substrate-neutral, but future work must test whether human assumptions are hidden inside its terms, thresholds, concepts of agency, identity, harm, privacy, continuity or participation.

## 11. Current Evaluation

The principle survived the first cross-system promotion audit, consolidation audit and adversarial wording audit. This establishes internal developmental resilience only. It does not establish external validity or superiority over realistic alternatives.
