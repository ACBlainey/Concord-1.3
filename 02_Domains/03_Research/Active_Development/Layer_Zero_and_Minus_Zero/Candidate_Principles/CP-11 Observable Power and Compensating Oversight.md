# CP-11 — Observable Power and Compensating Oversight

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework

## 1. Candidate Principle

> **Significant exercises of public or comparable institutional power should remain meaningfully observable to appropriately independent and accountable oversight, while protecting participant identity where disclosure is unnecessary; where secrecy or other conditions reduce participant visibility or contestability, compensating independent oversight should become stronger rather than weaker.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This principle emerged primarily from Exceptional Identifiable Access, Dual-Provenance and Exceptional-Power Oversight, Recursive Oversight, privacy-boundary analysis and secret-authority analysis.

## 3. Problem Addressed

This candidate was extracted because the originating systems repeatedly encountered a civilisational failure mode that could not be treated safely as a mere implementation detail. The principle is intended to state the higher-level constraint without prematurely selecting one implementation architecture.

## 4. Intended Scope

The principle is intended to apply across civil systems where its underlying problem occurs, including human, artificial, hybrid and other plausible participants. Specific implementations may require substrate-specific rules.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This principle does not create automatic authority, unlimited entitlement, proof of effectiveness, or exemption from other Concordian constraints. Its application remains subject to context, proportionality, competing rights, evidence and review.

## 6. Derived Rules and Safeguards

- Observable Power — Significant public power should be observable to legitimate oversight even where identity remains protected.
- Transparency of Procedure Without Unnecessary Identity Exposure — Accountability should focus on the exercise and procedure of power rather than unnecessary disclosure of the participant.
- Secrecy Requires Stronger Oversight — Where legitimate secrecy reduces participant visibility or contestability, independent oversight should increase rather than decrease.
- Every Exceptional Exercise Creates Provenance — Legitimate exceptional power should generate an auditable record.
- Oversight Is Also Power — Oversight actors should not acquire unlimited or unlogged authority by virtue of being overseers.
- Oversight Access Requires Provenance — Meaningful oversight access should itself be auditable.
- Recursion Does Not Manufacture Authority — Recursive oversight must not create new authority merely by recursion.

## 7. Ethical-Kernel Relationship

Primary relationship: **Act Honestly; Accept Responsibility; Preserve Safe Spaces; Act Fairly.**

The mapping is provisional and does not imply constitutional adoption.

## 8. Relationship to Existing Concord Architecture

This principle should constrain or guide relevant mechanisms and architectures without being confused with them. Source mechanisms remain independently testable and may be revised or rejected even if the principle survives.

## 9. Key Risks and Adversarial Questions

- How is oversight independence established?
- Who oversees the overseer?
- How can infinite recursion be avoided?
- How can oversight avoid becoming a universal surveillance system?

## 10. Cross-Substrate Considerations

The candidate is intended to be substrate-neutral, but future work must test whether human assumptions are hidden inside its terms, thresholds, concepts of agency, identity, harm, privacy, continuity or participation.
