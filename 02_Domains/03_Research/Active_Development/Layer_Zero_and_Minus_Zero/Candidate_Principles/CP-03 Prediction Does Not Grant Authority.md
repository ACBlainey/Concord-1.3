# CP-03 — Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.1  
**Document Type:** Candidate Principle  
**Status:** CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — UNDER CRITICAL EXAMINATION / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Validation:** Internal architectural and adversarial examination completed; empirical validation not established.

---

## 1. Candidate Principle

> **Prediction may supply evidence for attention, preparation, contextual examination or the expansion of available options, but prediction alone should neither create nor automatically escalate legitimate authority over a participant; consequential authority must rest upon an independently justified civil basis.**

## 2. Origin and Provenance

This principle emerged repeatedly across Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling, Participant Digital Twins, Predictive Support, Case Learning, civil Metrics, System-Steering and Predictive Resource Allocation, Support-to-Investigation Firewall, and Evidence-Conditioned Investigative Modelling.

## 3. Problem Addressed

This candidate was extracted because the originating systems repeatedly encountered a civilisational failure mode that could not be treated safely as a mere implementation detail. The principle is intended to state the higher-level constraint without prematurely selecting one implementation architecture.

## 4. Intended Scope

The principle is intended to apply across civil systems where its underlying problem occurs, including human, artificial, hybrid and other plausible participants. Specific implementations may require substrate-specific rules.

## 5. Explicit Non-Scope

This principle does not create automatic authority, unlimited entitlement, proof of effectiveness, or exemption from other Concordian constraints. Its application remains subject to context, proportionality, competing rights, evidence and review.

## 6. Derived Rules and Safeguards

- Population Pattern Is Not Individual Proof — Aggregate or statistical patterns should not by themselves establish the state, intention, capacity, guilt or future behaviour of a particular participant.
- Indicator Is Not Conclusion — An indicator creates grounds for inquiry, not automatic judgement.
- Unrealised Futures Are Not Completed Acts — A simulated or predicted future should not be treated as though the participant has already chosen, caused or committed the acts represented within it.
- No Punishment for Predicted Futures — Participants should not lose rights merely because an adverse future branch has been modelled.
- Models Generate Questions, Not Guilt — Predictive systems may generate hypotheses; guilt or liability requires legitimate evidence and process.
- Prediction Should Expand Options — Where practical, predictive systems should use foresight to increase useful options rather than reduce participant autonomy.
- Evidence Before Predictive Investigation — Predictive investigative modelling should begin from independently legitimate evidence and authority rather than manufacture its own investigative basis.

## 7. Ethical-Kernel Relationship

Primary relationship: **Respect Autonomy; Avoid Unnecessary Harm; Act Fairly; Accept Responsibility.**

The mapping is provisional and does not imply constitutional adoption.

## 8. Relationship to Existing Concord Architecture

This principle should constrain or guide relevant mechanisms and architectures without being confused with them. Source mechanisms remain independently testable and may be revised or rejected even if the principle survives.

## 9. Key Risks and Adversarial Questions

- Can legislation evade the principle by automatically granting authority above a prediction threshold?
- When is pre-authorised emergency action legitimate?
- How should extremely high-confidence predictions be treated?
- How should false positives be handled where consequences are irreversible?

## 10. Cross-Substrate Considerations

The candidate is intended to be substrate-neutral, but future work must test whether human assumptions are hidden inside its terms, thresholds, concepts of agency, identity, harm, privacy, continuity or participation.

## 11. Current Evaluation
