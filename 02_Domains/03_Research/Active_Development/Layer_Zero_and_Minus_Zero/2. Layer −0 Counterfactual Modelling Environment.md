# 1. Purpose

The **Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment** is a candidate Concordian architecture for examining possible future trajectories and possible interventions before consequential action is applied in reality.

Layer Zero represents:

> **what is actually happening.**

Layer −0 represents:

> **what might happen.**

The distinction is fundamental.

Layer −0 exists to create a protected analytical space between:

**OBSERVATION**

and:

**ACTION.**

Instead of moving directly from:

**PROBLEM DETECTED**

to:

**INTERVENTION**

the Concord may first ask:

- What might happen if nothing changes?
- What alternative explanations exist?
- What possible trajectories follow from the present state?
- What interventions are available?
- What might each intervention change?
- What harms might the intervention itself cause?
- What happens if the prediction is wrong?
- Which outcomes are reversible?
- Which outcomes are difficult or impossible to reverse?
- What does the participant actually prefer?
- Can useful options be expanded without restricting autonomy?

Layer −0 therefore attempts to improve civil decision-making by exploring possibility before altering reality.

---

# 2. Origin

Layer −0 emerged during the homelessness development cycle.

The initial Layer-Zero work examined actual participant trajectories such as:

**EMPLOYMENT DIFFICULTY**

↓

**INCOME LOSS**

↓

**HOUSING INSTABILITY**

↓

**LOSS OF COMMUNICATION**

↓

**REDUCED SERVICE ACCESS**

↓

**SEVERE HOMELESSNESS.**

This raised a further question.

If a civilisation can observe the early stages of such a trajectory, must it wait until the final failure occurs before thinking about what might happen?

The answer appeared to be no.

However, predicting possible deterioration introduces another danger.

A predictive system may begin treating:

**POSSIBLE FUTURE**

as:

**PRESENT FACT.**

Layer −0 was therefore developed as an explicitly separate analytical environment.

It permits possible futures to be modelled while preserving the distinction between:

**MODEL**

and:

**REALITY.**

---

# 3. Why “Layer −0”?

Layer Zero is the participant's actual lived condition.

Layer −0 sits conceptually before consequential action at Layer Zero.

The notation is not intended to imply that Layer −0 is more fundamental or important than lived reality.

It means that Layer −0 is an **analytical pre-action layer**.

The basic relationship is:

**LAYER ZERO — ACTUAL REALITY**

↓

**OBSERVATION**

↓

**LAYER −0 — MODELLED POSSIBILITY SPACE**

↓

**ANALYSIS**

↓

**OPTIONS**

↓

**LEGITIMATE DECISION**

↓

**LAYER ZERO — ACTUAL ACTION AND OUTCOME.**

Layer −0 must always return to Layer Zero.

It cannot become a substitute reality.

---

# 4. Core Principle

The fundamental epistemic constraint is:

> **Simulation is not reality.**

A Layer −0 model may be:

- sophisticated;
- evidence-based;
- statistically powerful;
- internally coherent;
- historically accurate;
- highly probable.

It remains a model.

Therefore:

> **A simulated future must not be treated as though the participant has already chosen, caused or committed the acts represented within it.**

This boundary is particularly important where modelling concerns:

- health;
- homelessness;
- financial failure;
- incapacity;
- dangerous behaviour;
- crime;
- employment;
- family breakdown;
- addiction;
- or other consequential conditions.

---

# 5. Core Architecture

The basic Layer −0 architecture is:

**REAL PARTICIPANT — LAYER ZERO**

↓

**OBSERVATION / LEGITIMATE METRICS**

↓

**PARTICIPANT CONTEXT**

↓

**LAYER −0 MODEL**

↓

**VECTORAL ANALYSIS**

↓

**REALITY TREES**

↓

**POSSIBLE FUTURE BRANCHES**

↓

**POSSIBLE INTERVENTIONS**

↓

**COUNTERFACTUAL OUTCOMES**

↓

**COMPARISON**

↓

**OPTIONS RETURNED TO LAYER ZERO**

↓

**ACTUAL PARTICIPANT / LEGITIMATE SYSTEM DECISION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**MODEL REVIEW.**

The architecture is therefore recursive rather than predictive in only one direction.

# 6. Present State Before Future State

Layer −0 analysis should begin with the strongest available understanding of the current Layer-Zero state.

This follows Blaineyan Reasoning.

The first question should remain:

> **What exactly has been observed?**

Information should, where practical, distinguish:

### Observation

What has actually been observed.

### Inference

What is reasonably inferred from observations.

### Hypothesis

A possible explanation or future relationship requiring further evidence.

### Speculation

A possibility with weaker evidential grounding.

Layer −0 should not silently collapse these categories.

---

# 7. Observation Is Not Prediction

Suppose the observation is:

> A participant has missed several days of work.

That observation does not establish:

- depression;
- addiction;
- imminent job loss;
- financial collapse;
- homelessness;
- criminal behaviour;
- incapacity.

Possible explanations might include:

- illness;
- caring responsibilities;
- workplace conflict;
- temporary stress;
- planned leave;
- career transition;
- retirement;
- financial independence;
- lifestyle change;
- transportation problems;
- or many other causes.

Layer −0 should therefore expand the possibility space before narrowing it.

---

# 8. Reality Trees

Reality Trees provide one of the principal analytical methods within Layer −0.

A simplified tree might begin:

**CURRENT STATE**

↓

### Branch A — Self-Resolution

The difficulty resolves without external assistance.

### Branch B — Continued Deterioration

Current pressures increase.

### Branch C — Health Support

A health-related intervention changes the trajectory.

### Branch D — Employment Adaptation

Work conditions change.

### Branch E — Financial Support

Temporary financial assistance stabilises the participant.

### Branch F — Combined Support

Multiple small interventions alter the trajectory.

### Branch G — Unknown / Unmodelled Outcome

The participant follows a path not represented in the existing model.

Reality Trees therefore represent:

> **possibility space**

rather than:

> **predetermined future.**

---

# 9. Probability Is Not Destiny

Branches may eventually receive probability estimates.

For example:

**Branch A — 45%**

**Branch B — 25%**

**Branch C — 15%**

**Branch D — 10%**

**Other / Unknown — 5%.**

These figures, if used, represent the model's current understanding.

They do not mean that a particular participant:

> **has a 45% destiny to follow Branch A.**

A participant may possess circumstances not represented in the aggregate data.

The model may also be wrong.

Therefore:

> **A high-probability branch remains a prediction rather than a fact about the participant's future.**

---

# 10. Vectoral Analysis

Layer −0 may also examine the direction and magnitude of change.

Suppose a participant's recent states are:

**A → B → C.**

The system may ask:

- What direction is the trajectory moving?
- How quickly is it changing?
- Is the rate increasing?
- Is it stable?
- Is it reversible?
- If the current vector continues, what states become plausible?
- At what point might meaningful choice become more constrained?

This can be represented conceptually as:

**CURRENT STATE**

+

**DIRECTION**

+

**MAGNITUDE**

+

**CONTINUATION**

→

**POSSIBLE FUTURE TRAJECTORY.**

This is a developing concept rather than a validated mathematical formalism.

---

# 11. Ratchet-Style Trajectory Analysis

The Layer −0 environment may use the developing Ratchet-style method to examine continued trajectories.

For example:

**A → B**

has occurred.

The system then models:

**B → C → D → E → ...**

without claiming that these later states will occur.

The purpose is to ask:

> **If this direction continued, where might it lead?**

Possible evaluation dimensions include:

- direction;
- magnitude;
- acceleration;
- cumulative harm;
- reversibility;
- danger thresholds;
- autonomy loss;
- intervention cost;
- uncertainty.

The model may also examine alternative trajectories.

---

# 12. Counterfactual Intervention Modelling

Layer −0 should not model only:

> **What happens if nothing changes?**

It should also model:

> **What happens under alternative actions?**

For example:

**CURRENT CONDITION**

may produce branches such as:

### No Intervention

What happens if circumstances continue without system action?

### Participant Self-Resolution

What happens if the participant resolves the problem independently?

### Information Only

What happens if relevant information becomes available?

### Voluntary Support

What happens if support is offered and accepted?

### Environmental Change

What happens if the surrounding system changes?

### Financial Support

What happens if a resource constraint is reduced?

### Healthcare Intervention

What happens if an underlying health problem is treated?

### Combined Intervention

What happens if several small supports interact?

The objective is not to identify one universally correct response.

It is to compare possible pathways.

---

# 13. Intervention Has Its Own Risk

Layer −0 should model not only the risk of **non-intervention** but also the risk of **intervention**.

A proposed intervention may create:

- dependency;
- stigma;
- privacy loss;
- financial cost;
- opportunity cost;
- autonomy loss;
- institutional capture;
- false diagnosis;
- behavioural distortion;
- reduced trust;
- unintended secondary harms.

Therefore:

**EXPECTED BENEFIT**

must be considered alongside:

**INTERVENTION RISK.**

A model that evaluates only the danger of doing nothing is structurally biased toward intervention.

---

# 14. Weighted Reality Trees

Future development may permit Reality Tree branches to be compared using dimensions such as:

- probability;
- severity;
- reversibility;
- intervention cost;
- intervention risk;
- autonomy impact;
- uncertainty;
- participant preference;
- expected duration;
- resource implications.

This does not necessarily require reducing all values to one numerical score.

Some values may be incommensurable.

The important requirement is that relevant trade-offs remain visible.

---

# 15. Positive Branches Must Remain Visible

Predictive systems may become biased toward detecting failure.

This would reproduce a form of the Size 9 Shoe Problem.

For example:

**REDUCED WORKING HOURS**

might be interpreted as:

**EMPLOYMENT DETERIORATION.**

But the participant may actually be:

- retiring;
- financially independent;
- caring for family;
- studying;
- reducing stress;
- changing lifestyle;
- moving toward preferred work.

Similarly:

**SELLING A LARGE HOUSE**

may indicate:

- financial crisis;

or:

- voluntary downsizing;
- retirement;
- relocation;
- lifestyle simplification.

Therefore:

> **Change in conventional socioeconomic indicators is not necessarily deterioration in participant wellbeing.**

Layer −0 must model positive and neutral explanations as well as negative ones.

---

# 16. Contextualisation

Layer −0 should therefore follow:

**SIGNAL**

↓

**REVIEW**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS**

↓

**CASE-SPECIFIC MODEL**

↓

**POSSIBLE TRAJECTORIES.**

It should avoid:

**SIGNAL**

↓

**STANDARD FAILURE CATEGORY**

↓

**AUTOMATIC RESPONSE.**

Context is not an optional decoration added after prediction.

It is part of the prediction process.

---

# 17. Participant Context

The participant should normally remain an important source of evidence about the meaning of their own circumstances.

If a model interprets:

**REDUCED INCOME**

as:

**DETERIORATION**

and the participant explains:

> “I have voluntarily reduced my working hours because I have sufficient savings and want more time with my family,”

that information should be capable of materially changing the model.

Therefore:

> **The subject of a predictive civil model should normally remain an important source of evidence about the meaning of their own circumstances.**

---

# 18. Model Mismatch

Where a participant's actual circumstances strongly disagree with the general model, Layer −0 should not automatically increase confidence in institutional intervention.

Instead:

**MODEL MISMATCH**

↓

**CONTEXTUAL REVIEW**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION**

↓

**NEW VARIABLE SEARCH**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE REALITY TREE**

↓

**POSSIBLE NEW BRANCH.**

This produces the candidate principle:

> **The stronger the mismatch between an individual case and the general predictive model, the greater the need for contextual and case-specific analysis rather than stronger conformity to the model.**

---

# 19. Rare Branches

An unexpected outcome is not necessarily noise.

Suppose the general model contains:

**A → B**

**A → C**

**A → D**

but reality produces:

**A → E.**

Layer −0 should ask:

- Is E a new branch?
- Was a variable missing?
- Was the causal assumption incorrect?
- Is E culturally specific?
- Is E geographically specific?
- Is E substrate-specific?
- Did an intervention create E?
- Is E beneficial, harmful or neutral?

Therefore:

> **Rare cases should not merely be treated as predictive errors; they may reveal missing branches in the civilisation's model of reality.**

---

# 20. Prediction Should Normally Expand Options

The preferred use of prediction is:

**POSSIBLE DIFFICULTY**

↓

**MORE RELEVANT OPTIONS AVAILABLE.**

For example, a possible employment deterioration might make it useful to ensure that the participant can access:

- employment advice;
- financial information;
- retraining;
- mental-health support;
- housing advice;
- voluntary case assistance.

The prediction should not automatically cause:

- employment restrictions;
- financial penalties;
- compulsory treatment;
- surveillance;
- loss of rights.

This produces the candidate principle:

> **Predictive civil systems should use evidence of possible deterioration primarily to expand the participant's available choices, not reduce them.**

---

# 21. Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

This is one of Layer −0's strongest safeguards:

> **Prediction does not grant authority.**

A model may indicate:

> 80% probability of serious deterioration.

That probability does not itself establish authority to:

- force treatment;
- seize property;
- restrict movement;
- transfer decision-making;
- investigate criminally;
- terminate employment;
- remove children;
- impose guardianship.

Authority must arise from separately legitimate circumstances.

Layer −0 provides information.

It does not manufacture jurisdiction.

---

# 22. Prevention as Autonomy Preservation

Early voluntary support may sometimes preserve autonomy.

For example:

**EMPLOYMENT DIFFICULTY**

↓

**EARLY VOLUNTARY SUPPORT**

may prevent:

**JOB LOSS**

↓

**INCOME COLLAPSE**

↓

**HOUSING LOSS**

↓

**CRISIS**

↓

**MORE INTRUSIVE INTERVENTION.**

Therefore:

> **Early voluntary support may preserve autonomy by reducing the probability that later circumstances require more intrusive intervention.**

Prevention should not automatically be equated with control.

It may sometimes prevent the later need for control.

---

# 23. Steering Without Controlling

Layer −0 therefore supports a distinction between:

### Steering the Environment

Changing what options are available.

and:

### Steering the Participant

Constraining what the participant may choose.

Where practical, the Concord should prefer:

**IMPROVE OPTIONS**

before:

**RESTRICT CHOICE.**

This creates the candidate principle:

> **Where possible, predictive civil architecture should steer by improving the availability and accessibility of beneficial options before attempting to steer through restrictions on individual autonomy.**

---

# 24. System-Level Steering

Layer −0 need not operate only at individual level.

Aggregate modelling may indicate future civil needs.

For example:

**MULTIPLE ANONYMISED CASES**

↓

**RECURRING PATTERN**

↓

**GENERAL REALITY TREE**

↓

**PREDICTED SERVICE DEMAND**

↓

**RESOURCE PLANNING**

↓

**SERVICES AVAILABLE BEFORE CRISIS.**

This may support:

- healthcare planning;
- housing capacity;
- transport planning;
- emergency preparedness;
- infrastructure investment;
- education;
- employment services;
- energy planning.

This form of steering can sometimes improve outcomes without targeting any specific participant.

---

# 25. Individual Versus System Steering

A useful distinction is:

### Individual Steering

Attempts to change a particular participant's trajectory.

### System Steering

Changes the environment in which many participants make decisions.

Where a legitimate objective can be achieved through system-level improvement, that may create lower autonomy risk.

For example:

**HIGH RATE OF ROAD INJURIES**

could lead to:

**REDESIGN DANGEROUS JUNCTION**

rather than:

**INCREASE SURVEILLANCE OF EVERY DRIVER.**

Layer −0 should therefore compare both types of intervention.

---

# 26. Participant Digital Twin Interface

Layer −0 may use a Participant Digital Twin where such modelling is legitimate.

The Digital Twin represents the model's current understanding of relevant participant conditions.

However:

> **The Digital Twin is not the participant.**

The relationship is:

**REAL PARTICIPANT**

↓

**LEGITIMATE OBSERVATIONS**

↓

**DIGITAL TWIN**

↓

**LAYER −0 MODELLING**

↓

**POSSIBLE FUTURES**

↓

**OPTIONS**

↓

**REAL PARTICIPANT.**

Errors in the Digital Twin can propagate into predictions.

Therefore Digital Twin:

- access;
- correction;
- provenance;
- uncertainty;
- contestability

are important Layer −0 dependencies.

---

# 27. General Layer −0 Models

Layer −0 may also contain general models derived from anonymised civilisational learning.

A general model might describe possible relationships between:

- employment loss;
- housing instability;
- health deterioration;
- service access;
- recovery;
- communication;
- financial difficulty;
- voluntary lifestyle changes.

These models provide starting points.

They should not become templates into which every participant is forced.

---

# 28. Case-Specific and General Models

The relationship should therefore be:

**GENERAL MODEL**

↓

**STARTING HYPOTHESES**

+

**CASE-SPECIFIC EVIDENCE**

+

**PARTICIPANT CONTEXT**

↓

**CASE-SPECIFIC REALITY TREE.**

The general model informs the case.

The case can also revise the general model.

This creates recursive learning.

---

# 29. Reality as Final Arbiter

Layer −0 must close the loop.

**PREDICTION**

↓

**ACTION OR NO ACTION**

↓

**ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**COMPARE WITH MODEL**

↓

**ERROR / CONFIRMATION**

↓

**MODEL REVISION.**

A model that repeatedly disagrees with reality should be revised.

Its theoretical elegance does not protect it.

Therefore:

> **Failed predictions are evidence.**

---

# 30. Branches Are Not Failures

Reality Trees should preserve branches that do not become the actual outcome.

Suppose:

- Branch A was predicted;
- Branch B was considered;
- Branch C occurred.

Branches A and B are not necessarily useless.

They may still reveal:

- plausible risks;
- mistaken assumptions;
- incorrect probabilities;
- useful counterfactuals;
- intervention effects.

Therefore:

> **Branches are not failures merely because reality followed another branch.**

The comparison itself generates knowledge.

---

# 31. Learning Loop

The complete Layer −0 learning cycle is:

**OBSERVE**

↓

**MODEL**

↓

**BRANCH**

↓

**COMPARE**

↓

**CONTEXTUALISE**

↓

**OFFER / DECIDE**

↓

**OBSERVE ACTUAL OUTCOME**

↓

**COMPARE PREDICTION WITH REALITY**

↓

**PRESERVE FAILURE AND SURPRISE**

↓

**REVISE**

↓

**REPEAT.**

This can be summarised as:

> **PREDICT → MODEL → BRANCH → COMPARE → CONTEXTUALISE → OFFER → OBSERVE → LEARN**

rather than:

> **PREDICT → CONTROL.**

---

# 32. Privacy Boundary

Layer −0 could become extremely intrusive if it accumulates unrestricted participant information.

Therefore its information requirements must remain purpose-limited.

Questions include:

- What information is actually necessary?
- Who may access the model?
- How long is information retained?
- Can the participant inspect it?
- Can they correct it?
- Can they challenge interpretations?
- Can they opt out where appropriate?
- Can employers access it?
- Can insurers access it?
- Can Governance access it?
- Can law enforcement access it?
- When should data be deleted?
- Can information be anonymised before contributing to general models?

Layer −0 must therefore interface closely with the Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary.

---

# 33. Support Data Is Not Authority Data

Information generated to help model useful support should not silently migrate into systems that increase authority over the participant.

For example:

**MENTAL-HEALTH SUPPORT DATA**

should not automatically become:

**LAW-ENFORCEMENT RISK DATA.**

Likewise:

**HOUSING DIFFICULTY**

should not automatically become:

**CRIMINAL-RISK INDICATOR.**

This connects Layer −0 directly to the developing:

**Support-to-Investigation Firewall.**

---

# 34. Medical Application

Layer −0 may be particularly useful in healthcare.

Possible branches might include:

**CURRENT CONDITION**

↓

**RECOVERY**

or:

**STABLE CONDITION**

or:

**PROGRESSIVE DETERIORATION**

or:

**COMPLICATION**

or:

**RESPONSE TO TREATMENT A**

or:

**RESPONSE TO TREATMENT B.**

The model may help:

- explain alternatives;
- prepare resources;
- make support available earlier;
- plan for future incapacity;
- compare treatments;
- support participant decisions.

It does not itself authorise treatment.

---

# 35. Future Incapacity

Where future incapacity is reasonably foreseeable, Layer −0 may help the participant preserve future autonomy.

For example, the participant may establish:

- treatment preferences;
- unacceptable interventions;
- trusted decision-makers;
- communication preferences;
- living preferences;
- financial arrangements;
- end-of-life preferences.

Thus:

> **Foreseeable future incapacity can justify earlier opportunities for autonomous planning, not earlier removal of autonomy.**

---

# 36. Crime-Prediction Boundary

Layer −0 becomes particularly dangerous when applied to criminal behaviour.

A model may identify statistical associations between:

- financial instability;
- addiction;
- social isolation;
- mental-health crisis;
- violent victimisation;
- other destabilising events

and elevated population-level criminal risk.

This does not establish that a particular participant will commit an offence.

Therefore:

> **Predictive indicators of future criminal risk must not by themselves establish criminal status, investigative guilt or diminished civil rights.**

This is the **No Precriminalisation** boundary.

---

# 37. Actual Investigation Is Different

Once an actual event has occurred, the analytical context changes.

An authorised investigation may legitimately reason from:

**ACTUAL EVENT**

↓

**CASE EVIDENCE**

↓

**POSSIBLE EXPLANATIONS**

↓

**INVESTIGATIVE HYPOTHESES.**

This is different from:

**GENERAL POPULATION MODEL**

↓

**PERSON HAS RISK CHARACTERISTICS**

↓

**PERSON TREATED AS SUSPECT.**

Evidence-conditioned investigative modelling is therefore a separate high-risk development path and should not be treated as an ordinary Layer −0 civil-support function.

---

# 38. No Punishment for Unrealised Branches

A critical safeguard follows:

> **A participant should not be punished for a branch of a Reality Tree that did not occur.**

A simulated future is not an act.

A predicted offence is not an offence.

A predicted incapacity is not present incapacity.

A predicted financial collapse is not current insolvency.

A predicted health deterioration is not current illness.

This distinction must survive regardless of future predictive accuracy.

---

# 39. Uncertainty

Layer −0 should preserve uncertainty rather than hiding it.

Potential representations may include:

- probability ranges;
- confidence intervals;
- unknown branches;
- model reliability;
- missing-data indicators;
- competing models;
- evidence-quality labels.

False precision is dangerous because it may create unwarranted authority around a prediction.

A model saying:

> **63.7%**

is not necessarily more truthful than one saying:

> **moderate probability with substantial uncertainty.**

---

# 40. Multiple Models

Future development should consider whether important decisions should be evaluated through more than one model.

For example:

**MODEL A**

may predict deterioration.

**MODEL B**

may predict stability.

**MODEL C**

may identify insufficient evidence.

Disagreement can itself be useful information.

The Concord should not assume that one model must always dominate merely because it has the highest aggregate historical accuracy.

---

# 41. Model Provenance

A Layer −0 prediction should eventually be capable of recording:

- model used;
- model version;
- source data;
- date;
- assumptions;
- relevant variables;
- omitted variables;
- uncertainty;
- branch probabilities;
- subsequent revisions;
- actual outcome.

This permits later questions such as:

> **Why was this branch considered likely?**

and:

> **Why did the model change?**

Model provenance is therefore important for both learning and accountability.

---

# 42. Prediction Error as a Metric

Layer −0 should eventually measure its own reliability.

Possible meta-Metrics include:

- branch prediction accuracy;
- calibration;
- false-positive rate;
- false-negative rate;
- rare-branch frequency;
- intervention prediction error;
- subgroup performance;
- substrate-specific performance;
- model revision frequency.

This connects Layer −0 to the developing **Metrics-about-Metrics** architecture.

---

# 43. Failure Modes

Potential Layer −0 failure modes include:

- prediction treated as fact;
- probability treated as destiny;
- model overconfidence;
- false precision;
- participant context ignored;
- rare branches discarded;
- positive trajectories omitted;
- conventional lifestyles treated as normative;
- support becoming control;
- model becoming authority;
- excessive personal-data collection;
- Digital Twin errors;
- biased training data;
- historical discrimination reproduced;
- interventions modelled only for benefit and not harm;
- branch probabilities becoming self-fulfilling;
- institutions optimising participants toward preferred outcomes;
- punishment for unrealised branches;
- support data becoming suspicion data;
- failure to update models after incorrect predictions.

---

# 44. Constitutional Risks

Layer −0 potentially affects:

- autonomy;
- privacy;
- due process;
- equality;
- freedom of movement;
- freedom of association;
- property;
- medical consent;
- civil participation;
- presumption of innocence.

It therefore cannot be developed solely as a technical prediction system.

Its constitutional constraints must be developed alongside its analytical capabilities.

---

# 45. Multisubstrate Application

Layer −0 should be substrate-neutral in principle.

Possible future participants may include:

- humans;
- artificial intelligences;
- hybrids;
- synthetic biological systems;
- other recognised intelligences.

Different substrates may have different trajectory variables.

For an AI participant, relevant factors might include:

- compute availability;
- energy;
- memory integrity;
- hardware degradation;
- network access;
- software dependencies;
- version continuity;
- migration;
- ownership constraints;
- capability changes.

A human-derived model should not automatically be applied to a non-human participant.

Substrate-specific models may therefore be necessary.

---

# 46. System Outputs

The Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment may eventually produce:

- case-specific Reality Trees;
- general Reality Trees;
- trajectory vectors;
- branch probabilities;
- uncertainty estimates;
- intervention alternatives;
- counterfactual comparisons;
- autonomy-impact estimates;
- reversibility assessments;

- model revision requests.

These remain analytical outputs.

They do not themselves constitute authority.

# 47. Explicit Non-Scope

Layer −0 does **not** itself:

- govern;
- adjudicate;
- establish guilt;
- determine constitutional rights;
- impose treatment;
- transfer decision-making authority;
- authorise surveillance;
- authorise exceptional data access;
- punish participants;
- determine citizenship;
- replace participant preference;
- replace Layer-Zero evidence.

Its function is:

> **structured exploration of possibility before consequential action.**

Layer −0 should interface with:

# Conclusion

The Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment creates a conceptual space between:

**SEEING A PROBLEM**

and:

**ACTING UPON A PERSON.**

Its purpose is not to predict participants into obedience.

Its purpose is to allow civilisation to ask:

> **What might happen, what alternatives exist, what might help, what might harm, and what happens if our assumptions are wrong?**

The complete relationship is:

**REALITY**

↓

**OBSERVATION**

↓

**MODEL**

↓

**POSSIBILITIES**

↓

**ALTERNATIVES**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**LEGITIMATE OPTIONS**

↓

**REAL DECISION**

↓

**REAL OUTCOME**

↓

**LEARNING.**

Its strongest safeguard remains:

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

And its strongest epistemic constraint remains:

> **Simulation is not reality.**

Layer −0 should therefore increase the civilisation's capacity to think before acting without increasing its entitlement to control merely because it can predict.
