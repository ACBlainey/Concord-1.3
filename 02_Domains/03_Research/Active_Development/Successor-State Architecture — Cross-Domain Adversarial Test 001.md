# Successor-State Architecture — Cross-Domain Adversarial Test 001

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.3  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / ADVERSARIAL ABSTRACTION TEST / NON-CANONICAL  
**Date:** September 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

Test the candidate Successor-State architecture derived from lifecycle recurrence and Infrastructure adversarial testing.

Candidate proposition under test:

> When a consequential bounded function or object changes active state, determine the legitimate successor state or state-vector for each material dimension; preserve surviving value, obligations and provenance; terminate authority that has lost its function; release or recover resources that no longer need to remain bound; and preserve reversibility until justified transition confidence makes irreversibility appropriate.

The goal is to discover where this abstraction fails, especially where physical/product metaphors become dangerous.

---

# 2. Test rule

The abstraction passes only if it can:
- describe materially different domains without erasing their domain-specific rules;
- preserve non-transferable identity and rights;
- distinguish authority from obligation;
- allow genuine termination;
- avoid forcing physical-resource logic onto persons/knowledge;
- add useful structure rather than merely rename existing state machines.

---

# 3. Authority — strong match

Bounded Contextual Authority already distinguishes:
- standing eligibility;
- activated authority;
- authority envelope;
- functional sunset;
- successor/follow-up function;
- duties surviving authority.

Example:

`AuthorityStateVector = <EligibilityState, ActivationState, FunctionState, PermissionState, DutyState, ProvenanceState>`

An emergency responder's entry authority can end while confidentiality/evidence-preservation duties survive.

**Result: STRONG MATCH.**

The state-vector model is superior to a single ACTIVE/EXPIRED flag.

---

# 4. Authority — consumable act

BCA further establishes that a specific exercise of authority can be consumed while the broader function continues.

Therefore the successor state of an **authority act** is not necessarily another authority.

It may be:
- consumed;
- provenance retained;
- consequence recorded;
- new authority requiring fresh justification.

**Result: IMPORTANT BOUNDARY.**

> **Past Legitimate Authority Is Not a Reusable Successor Token.**

Successor-state architecture must not imply automatic transfer/reproduction of authority.

---

# 5. Personal identity — abstraction boundary

A participant dies, is destroyed, disappears or otherwise leaves ordinary active civil life.

Historical may transition the participant record from ACTIVE to TRANSITIONAL/RETIRED.

But the participant's identity is not a resource to be reassigned to a successor.

**Result: HARD BOUNDARY.**

> **Record Succession != Identity Succession.**

The state-vector can describe records, roles, claims and obligations surrounding a participant, but must not imply that personal identity itself transfers.

---

# 6. Fundamental rights — abstraction boundary

A participant changes job, residence, participation band or civil status.

Contextual permissions may change.

Fundamental standing/rights must not be treated as disposable permissions merely because a surrounding function ends.

**Result: HARD BOUNDARY.**

> **Functional Sunset Must Not Be Applied to Inherent or Independently Grounded Standing.**

The model must first identify whether the property is:
- function-derived;
- independently grounded;
- personal/non-transferable;
- legally persistent.

---

# 7. Participant record — strong match

Historical already distinguishes:
- ACTIVE;
- TRANSITIONAL;
- RETIRED;
- compartment-specific retention/access.

One record can simultaneously be:
- historically preserved;
- operationally retired;
- restricted;
- available for bounded succession/legal purposes;
- anonymised for legitimate research.

**Result: STRONG MULTIDIMENSIONAL MATCH.**

---

# 8. Knowledge claim — strong match with epistemic semantics

A research claim moves from:
- provisional;
- validated/useful;
- superseded;
- rejected;
- archived.

Superseded knowledge can retain:
- provenance;
- historical value;
- failed-path value;
- dependencies.

But “successor” here may be a better-supported claim, not transfer of identity.

**Result: STRONG MATCH IF DOMAIN LANGUAGE IS PRESERVED.**

> **Supersession != Erasure.**

---

# 9. Dormant capability — strong match

A specialised tool/model/codebase is no longer worth continuous activation but remains useful enough to preserve.

Possible vector:
`<Operational=DORMANT, Recoverability=PRESERVED, Authority=NONE, Historical=PRESERVED>`.

**Result: STRONG MATCH.**

---

# 10. False knowledge — anti-preservation test

A dangerous or incorrect method is rejected.

Historical value may justify preserving evidence of the failure, but not maintaining it as an operationally available capability.

**Result: PASS.**

The model must distinguish:
- informational provenance;
- executable capability;
- operational authority.

> **Preserve the Evidence of Failure Without Necessarily Preserving the Ability to Repeat It.**

---

# 11. Civil Attention issue — strong process match

Civil Attention distinguishes:
- submission;
- Issue;
- Domain Action Objects;
- responsibility resolution;
- processing;
- disposition;
- closure;
- legitimate reopening.

Closure preserves provenance.

**Result: STRONG PROCESS-LIFECYCLE MATCH.**

But “retirement” is inappropriate vocabulary.

---

# 12. Civil Attention — unresolved issue

An Issue has no legitimate owner.

The architecture explicitly refuses to invent authority merely because responsibility is missing.

Successor-state analysis must therefore permit:
`UNRESOLVED_NO_LEGITIMATE_OWNER`

rather than forcing progression.

**Result: IMPORTANT PASS CONDITION.**

> **A Successor State May Legitimately Be Unresolved.**

---

# 13. Civil Attention — reopened issue

A closed Issue receives materially new information.

It may reopen.

Therefore successor-state architecture must not assume all transitions are one-way.

**Result: PASS.**

> **Closure != Irreversibility.**

---

# 14. Product — strong match

A product may transition through:
- active use;
- repair;
- reuse;
- repurpose;
- component recovery;
- material recovery;
- residual disposal.

Different dimensions can diverge:
- original product function ends;
- component function survives;
- materials remain valuable.

**Result: STRONG MATCH.**

---

# 15. Product — destructive recovery

A reusable component is shredded for material recycling.

The state transition is valid but destroys higher-order function.

Successor-state architecture alone cannot decide whether this is good stewardship.

It requires domain-specific value/safety/resource assessment.

**Result: LIMITATION.**

> **State Transition Description != Transition Quality Judgement.**

---

# 16. Enterprise — strong match

Enterprise:
- operates;
- adapts;
- transforms;
- succeeds;
- retires;
- winds down;
- closes.

Meanwhile:
- liabilities may survive;
- records persist;
- participants move;
- assets recover;
- identity becomes historical.

**Result: STRONG MATCH.**

---

# 17. Enterprise — successor company

A successor acquires assets and brand but not necessarily every liability, identity relation or legal obligation.

Successor-state architecture must not infer legal inheritance merely from continuity.

**Result: HARD LEGAL BOUNDARY.**

> **Functional Succession != Automatic Legal Succession.**

---

# 18. Responsibility role — strong match

Fault-Tolerant Accountability distinguishes:
- primary;
- concurrent;
- successor-ready;
- transfer-pending;
- successor-active;
- no-valid-successor;
- safe-state.

It explicitly rejects succession of:
- personhood;
- identity;
- constitutional standing;
- inherently personal functions.

**Result: STRONG MATCH WITH EXISTING BOUNDARY SUPPORT.**

---

# 19. Responsibility — function should end

A role becomes unnecessary.

A naive continuity model searches for a successor.
Correct outcome is RETIRE.

**Result: PASS ONLY IF TERMINATION IS FIRST-CLASS.**

> **No Successor May Be the Correct Successor-State Outcome.**

# 20. Infrastructure — multidimensional match

Prior adversarial test established:
`<Operational=RETIRED, Stewardship=ACTIVE, Physical=IN_PLACE, LogicalAccess=REVOKED, Historical=PRESERVED>`.

**Result: STRONG MATCH.**

# 21. Infrastructure — failed transition

Replacement fails after partial migration.

Rollback is required.

**Result: PASS ONLY IF REVERSIBILITY IS EXPLICIT.**

# 22. Contextual Wrapper — boundary persistence

A participant/object crosses from one legitimate contextual space into another.

Rules/permissions change because context changes.

This is not necessarily lifecycle retirement at all.

It is contextual state transition.

**Result: PARTIAL MATCH.**

The abstraction may describe state change, but adds little unless transition has surviving duties/provenance/resources.

This prevents over-generalisation.

---

# 23. Education qualification — expiry and renewal

A qualification may:
- remain valid;
- expire;
- require refresh;
- be superseded;
- be revoked under legitimate process.

But participant knowledge does not vanish because a credential expires.

**Result: STRONG DISTINCTION.**

> **Credential State != Capability State.**

Again, dimensions must remain separate.

---

# 24. Temporary emergency permission — ends without successor

An emergency permission terminates.

No successor permission is needed.

Confidentiality/accountability duties survive.

**Result: STRONG PASS.**

This confirms that “successor state” means **next legitimate state**, not necessarily a successor holder/object.

---

# 25. Destroyed hazardous object

A hazardous object is intentionally destroyed because preservation itself creates unacceptable risk.

Historical provenance may survive while physical/resource value is deliberately lost.

**Result: PASS.**

> **Preservation Is Not an Absolute Requirement.**

---

# 26. Irreversible personal event

Death/destruction may be irreversible, but civil records/claims remain transitionable.

The reversibility principle from Infrastructure cannot be universalised.

**Result: MAJOR REFINEMENT.**

Replace:
“preserve reversibility until justified transition confidence…”

with:

> **Where transition is practically reversible and premature irreversibility would create material risk, preserve rollback until justified confidence exists.**

Reversibility is conditional, not universal.

---

# 27. Rights relinquishment/consent

A participant may withdraw consent or relinquish a permission/claim where legitimately alienable.

But the architecture cannot infer which rights are alienable.

**Result: CONSTITUTIONAL/LEGAL BOUNDARY.**

Successor-state architecture describes the transition only after legitimate authority/standing rules establish that transition is possible.

---

# 28. Fork/branch

An AI, software system, institution or knowledge line may branch rather than transition into one successor.

Possible:
`ONE → MANY`.

Likewise multiple systems may merge:
`MANY → ONE`.

**Result: DEFECT IN SIMPLE SUCCESSOR MODEL.**

The architecture must support:
- continuation;
- transformation;
- succession;
- split/branch;
- merge;
- retirement;
- termination.

> **Successor Topology May Branch or Merge; It Is Not Necessarily a Chain.**

---

# 29. Simultaneous overlapping states

A system may be:
- active for one function;
- retired for another;
- under transition for a third.

**Result: STRONG VECTOR REQUIREMENT.**

State must be scoped to function/context.

> **State Without Scope Can Be Misleading.**

# 30. Cross-domain findings

The abstraction survives, but only after five major restrictions.

## Restriction 1 — no identity/rights transfer inference
Personal identity, personhood, constitutional standing and independently grounded rights cannot be treated as ordinary successor-state resources.

## Restriction 2 — no automatic legal inheritance
Functional continuity does not determine liability/property/legal succession.

## Restriction 3 — no universal reversibility
Rollback applies only where practical and materially useful.

## Restriction 4 — no forced successor
Termination, unresolved state, safe state or no-successor can be legitimate.

## Restriction 5 — no linear topology assumption
Transitions can branch, merge, overlap, reopen and operate across multiple state dimensions.

# 31. Refined architecture

The stronger abstraction is not a lifecycle chain.

It is a **bounded transition topology**.

Candidate representation:

`Transition = <ObjectOrFunctionRef, Scope, PriorStateVector, Trigger, LegitimateTransitionAuthority, TransitionConstraints, NextStateVectorRefs, SurvivingDuties, SurvivingValue, ReleasedResources, TerminatedAuthority, Provenance, ReversibilityState>`

Possible transition cardinality:
- 1→1;
- 1→many;
- many→1;
- many→many;
- 1→none;
- unresolved.

# 34. Falsification result

**NOT FALSIFIED, BUT SUBSTANTIALLY NARROWED AND FORMALISED.**

The original lifecycle intuition is too broad if interpreted literally.

The defensible recurring architecture is:

> consequential bounded objects/functions often require explicit transition architecture when their operational state changes, with scoped multidimensional states, surviving duties/value/provenance, bounded authority termination, legitimate release/recovery, and domain-specific rules governing what may transfer, terminate, persist, branch, merge or remain unresolved.

This does not replace:
- Law;
- rights;
- identity;
- domain-specific lifecycle rules;
- stewardship judgement;
- substantive authority.

It supplies a reusable transition grammar.

# 36. Utility mesh evidence — transition grammar beyond lifecycle

Subsequent development of:

`02_Domains/12_Infrastructure_and_Essential_Systems/Local-First Bidirectional Utility Mesh Architecture 001.md`

provides an important additional test.

A utility node may simultaneously:
- import one resource;
- export another;
- store a third;
- require treatment before exporting a fourth;
- remain connected to one network while isolated from another.

This is not primarily retirement or succession.

It is continuous bounded transition across resource-specific interfaces.

The case therefore supports the proposed name refinement toward **Bounded Transition Architecture**.

Candidate objects:

`UtilityResourceState = <ResourceClass, QuantityState, QualityState, Location, OwnershipOrStewardshipState, AvailabilityTime, StorageState, TreatmentState, DistributionEligibility, HazardState, Provenance>`

`UtilityFlow = <ResourceClass, SourceNodeRef, DestinationOrNetworkRef, Quantity, QualityState, InterfaceValidationRef, TimeWindow, TreatmentDependencyRef, RoutingState, Provenance>`

The transition grammar appears useful here because:
- state is multidimensional;
- state must be scoped;
- transitions cross bounded interfaces;
- interface validation matters;
- provenance matters;
- the transition itself does not grant authority or determine substantive engineering safety.

This extends the evidence beyond lifecycle/sunset cases:

> **Bounded Transition Architecture May Describe Both Successor-State Change and Continuous Operational Flow Without Owning the Domain-Specific Rules That Make the Transition Legitimate.**

This remains a research finding.
