# Judicial Evidence and Standards of Proof — Research Interface 001

## Function

Research is responsible for investigating and evaluating the knowledge base for evidential reliability, admissibility methods, burdens and standards of proof, uncertainty, false-positive/false-negative consequences, AI and human evidence evaluation, and case-type differences. It should identify limitations, competing approaches and empirical or theoretical support rather than declare a single unsupported numerical probability threshold.

## Domain handoff

**Research → Education → Judiciary**, with separate lawful roles:

- Research supplies evidence, comparative analysis, failure modes and proposed standards, with provenance and revisions.
- Education teaches and assesses competence against **lawfully adopted** judicial standards, including patch education and reassessment.
- Judiciary adopts and procedurises operative burdens, standards, admissibility rules, explanation requirements and case-type decision gates through its authorised processes.

Research does not create binding judicial law by publication; Education does not confer case authority by qualification. Judiciary may request further research where available knowledge is inadequate. Applicable rule versions and changes must be traceable through official Concord communications and records.

## Error feedback and independence

Material procedural or evidential-standard failures discovered in cases, appeals or Steward appraisals should be referred promptly to Judiciary for lawful case review and to Education for competence assessment, and to Research when the underlying method itself requires re-evaluation. The independent process distinguishes bad procedure or misconduct from good-faith disagreement, unknown evidence at the original hearing and defects in the standard itself. Suspension or removal requires a separate lawful process; research conclusions alone do not remove Stewards or alter judgments.

## To be designed/finalised

Research methodology and comparative standards for criminal conviction, civil findings, interim protection, evidential admissibility, uncertainty and emerging AI-generated evidence; the formal adoption interface and standard-version registry; the case feedback and research revision cycle. No universal proof threshold is invented in this interface document.
