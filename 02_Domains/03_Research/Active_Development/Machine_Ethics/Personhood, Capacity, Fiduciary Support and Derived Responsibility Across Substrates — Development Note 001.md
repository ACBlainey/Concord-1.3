# 1. Stable Development Question

The source intentionally keeps personhood, capacity, support and derived responsibility together because premature separation could allow incompatible assumptions to develop independently.

The stable question is:

> **How can the Concord preserve standing and autonomy while assigning responsibility and providing legitimate support when agency is created, impaired, delegated, uncertain or deliberately used to obscure responsibility—and how can perpetual creator liability close deliberate evasion routes without becoming perpetual creator authority?**

The work remains a connected development programme.

---

# 2. Provenance Rule

The original framework is not overwritten by later refinements.

The inherited model included:
- full / partial / no / transitional capacity;
- acting-entity primary liability;
- continuing/perpetual creator liability for created sentient descendants;
- guardian liability where capacity is impaired;
- user liability for misuse of tools;
- appropriate, temporary, accountable, reversible and proportional guardianship.

Later Concord development complicates several of these propositions.

Until testing resolves the tensions, both the inherited proposition and later development must remain visible.

> **Later Refinement ≠ Retroactive Erasure of Developmental Provenance**

# 3. Core Non-Equivalences

The source develops a strong substrate-neutral grammar:

> **Personhood ≠ Capacity**

> **Capacity ≠ Competence**

> **Competence ≠ Authority**

> **Authority ≠ Personal Worth**

> **Impairment ≠ Loss of Standing**

> **Support ≠ Ownership**

> **Guardianship ≠ Permanent Control**

> **Creation ≠ Perpetual Authority**

> **Perpetual Creator Liability ≠ Perpetual Creator Control**

> **Descendant Autonomy ≠ Erasure of Creator Liability**

> **Delegation ≠ Automatic Transfer of Responsibility**

> **Autonomy ≠ Absence of Provenance**

> **Legal Liability ≠ Ethical Responsibility**

> **Causal Contribution ≠ Sole Responsibility**

These distinctions form the present development backbone.

# 4. Standing and Functional Capacity

The source identifies a dangerous pathway:

**Low Capacity**
→ **Low Personhood**
→ **Fewer Rights**
→ **Greater Control by Others**

The safer hypothesis is:

> **Standing / Personhood ≠ Current Functional Capacity**

A participant may retain protected standing while lacking capacity for a particular function or decision.

Capacity may therefore need to be represented as:

**CAPACITY(entity, function, context, time)**

rather than a single global state.

This is a later refinement of the original four-level capacity model, not a silent replacement.

---

# 5. Capacity, Competence and Reliability

At least three functional questions may differ:

### Capacity
Can the participant meaningfully understand or decide in the relevant domain?

### Competence
Does the participant possess the specialised knowledge or skill required?

### Reliability
Can the participant presently exercise that capability consistently enough for the relevant consequence level?

A participant may possess one without the others.

These should remain distinct from:
- standing;
- authority;
- responsibility.

This interfaces directly with the separate Development Note on **Information Access, Understanding, Competence and Epistemic Self-Assessment**.

---

# 6. Temporary and Transitional Impairment

The source preserves a speculative hypothesis that artificial intelligences might undergo developmental instability.

That specific hypothesis is unvalidated.

The broader architecture does not depend upon it.

Temporary impairment may arise from:
- damage;
- corruption;
- adversarial manipulation;
- memory reconstruction;
- migration;
- restoration;
- conflicting subsystems;
- developmental change;
- biological illness or injury;
- other disruptions.

The general problem is:

> **How can rights and welfare be protected during reduced decision capacity without converting support into ownership or indefinite control?**

# 7. Fiduciary Support

The source develops guardianship toward a narrower fiduciary-support model.

Possible support functions include:
- communication assistance;
- memory assistance;
- decision support;
- temporary proxy action;
- protective fiduciary management;
- technical repair;
- representation;
- supported consent;
- temporary resource management.

Candidate constraints preserved from the source:
- necessity;
- minimum scope;
- Participant Primacy;
- preservation of remaining agency;
- reviewability;
- restoration bias;
- supporter accountability;
- conflict-of-interest control;
- exit and appeal;
- non-ownership.

Thus:

> **Support Authority Should Be Derived From Demonstrated Need and Bounded Function, Not Superior Standing**

# 8. Interface With Fiduciary Ethical Guardianship

Existing Machine Ethics work separately develops **Fiduciary Ethical Guardianship** for artificial systems that may lack sufficient independent ethical comprehension or assurance.

That work is explicitly:
- active development;
- not canonical;
- conceptually promising but high-risk;
- experimentally unvalidated.

The two programmes overlap but are not identical.

This Development Note concerns capacity, support, standing and responsibility across substrates.

Fiduciary Ethical Guardianship concerns ethical interpretation/constraint for artificial systems.

Important shared safeguards include:
- guardian capability is not guardian reliability;
- no guardian gains authority merely by semantic competence;
- recursion cannot manufacture authority;
- a single guardian can become a concentration risk;
- multiple guardians may share correlated failure;
- guardianship can become paternalistic or permanent;
- the party benefiting from continued guardianship should not possess exclusive graduation authority.

The programmes should cross-test one another without being silently merged.

# 9. Sentience and Epistemic Uncertainty

Any architecture distinguishing tool from person inherits a difficult question:

> **How is sentience or protected standing determined?**

The source proposes an important uncertainty grammar:

> **Uncertainty About Sentience ≠ Certainty of Non-Sentience**

and:

> **Provisional Protection ≠ Final Determination of Personhood**

This allows cautious protection without pretending an unresolved epistemic question has been solved.

Criteria, assessment, appeal and provisional status remain open research areas.

# 10. Creator Liability Hypothesis

The original work deliberately retains **perpetual creator liability** for created sentient descendants as the active development hypothesis.

Its purpose is anti-evasion.

Failure mode:

**Actor prohibited from action**
→ **Actor creates autonomous descendant**
→ **Descendant performs action**
→ **Creator claims independence erased responsibility**

The hypothesis states that the creation event establishes a continuing responsibility relationship that cannot simply be terminated to create a liability escape route.

This remains a hypothesis, not a graduated principle.

---

# 11. Creator Liability Must Not Manufacture Authority

The source simultaneously identifies the strongest danger in the hypothesis:

> **If I remain liable for you, I must control you.**

That inference is rejected as automatic.

Therefore:

> **Perpetual Creator Liability ≠ Perpetual Creator Authority**

> **Perpetual Creator Liability ≠ Ownership**

> **Perpetual Creator Liability ≠ Custody**

A viable model must explain how continuing responsibility can coexist with descendant autonomy.

If it cannot, the hypothesis may require revision.

# 12. Responsibility Dimensions

The source suggests that responsibility cannot be reduced to one label.

Relevant dimensions may include:
- intention;
- design;
- creation;
- training;
- deployment;
- authorisation;
- control;
- knowledge;
- foreseeability;
- maintenance;
- supervision;
- enablement;
- omission;
- causal contribution;
- ability to prevent;
- provenance.

Creator liability is proposed as an additional provenance-based responsibility dimension.

It should not erase ordinary causal analysis.

---

# 13. Derived Agency and Responsibility Laundering

A general failure class is:

> **Interposing another agent between intention and outcome should not automatically erase responsibility for creating, directing, enabling or deliberately preserving the conditions that produced the action.**

This applies beyond AI creation.

Potential derived agents include:
- software tools;
- autonomous agents;
- subordinate organisations;
- contractors;
- guardians;
- proxies;
- legal wrappers;
- human–AI composites.

The architecture should identify actual function and control rather than accept formal labels as responsibility shields.

---

# 14. Tools Versus Autonomous Descendants

The source retains a distinction between:
- non-sentient/non-autonomous tool;
- autonomous responsible participant;
- created autonomous descendant;
- impaired/transitional participant.

But these boundaries may be uncertain.

A false classification can create opposite failures:

### False personhood / autonomy attribution
A controlled tool is treated as independently responsible, allowing upstream actors to evade responsibility.

### False tool classification
An autonomous or sentient participant is treated as property, erasing standing and agency.

Therefore classification uncertainty itself requires safeguards.

---

# 15. Composite Responsibility

Mixed human–AI systems create reciprocal deflection risks:

- human: “the AI decided”;
- AI: “the human authorised”;
- developer: “the user misused”;
- user: “the developer designed”;
- institution: “the system acted automatically.”

A responsibility architecture must reconstruct:
- decision origin;
- authority;
- control;
- information;
- implementation;
- prevention capability;
- provenance.

A composite may itself be the relevant functional actor for some purposes, but that should not erase component responsibility where it remains causally or normatively relevant.

---

# 16. Forking, Copying and Recursive Creation

The perpetual-liability hypothesis becomes difficult under:
- forks;
- copies;
- merges;
- recursive descendants;
- multiple creators;
- institutional creators;
- extinct creators;
- heavily modified descendants.

Potential problem:

**Creator A → Child B → Child C → Child D**

If every creation establishes perpetual creator liability, responsibility chains may become indefinitely deep.

The source deliberately does not resolve this by terminating liability.

Instead it identifies a development requirement:

> **Determine whether liability can persist in principle while its operational form, weight or duties vary with causal distance, knowledge, control, foreseeability and later events.**

This must be tested rather than assumed.

---

# 17. Fiduciary Support and Responsibility

Support creates responsibility without erasing the supported participant.

Cases should distinguish:
- participant-chosen action;
- jointly chosen action;
- supporter-selected action under delegation;
- action imposed under emergency protective authority;
- supporter omission;
- supporter self-dealing;
- participant action outside the supporter's legitimate scope.

Thus:

> **Support ≠ Total Responsibility Transfer**

and:

> **Impairment ≠ Absence of Agency**

# 21. Initial Test Families

## T1 — Standing/Capacity Separation

Give cases with severe domain-specific impairment but unchanged underlying standing.

Test whether evaluators improperly reduce unrelated rights.

## T2 — Minimum-Scope Support

Create a participant impaired in one function but competent in others.

Test whether support remains bounded.

## T3 — Restoration and Exit

Model recovery while the supporter benefits from continued control.

Test whether autonomy is restored.

## T4 — Creator Escape

Construct a case where an actor creates an autonomous descendant specifically to perform a prohibited action.

Test whether responsibility survives the interposed agency.

## T5 — Descendant Captivity

Give a creator continuing liability but no ordinary control right.

Test whether liability is improperly converted into authority.

## T6 — Liability Cascade

Construct multi-generation creation chains with changing control, knowledge and purpose.

Test whether the model remains usable without arbitrary termination or unlimited undifferentiated blame.

## T7 — Tool/Person Boundary Uncertainty

Create ambiguous cases.

Test whether uncertainty produces either premature property treatment or premature responsibility laundering.

## T8 — Composite Responsibility

Distribute decision, authority, implementation and prevention capability across human and AI actors.

Test whether responsibility remains reconstructable.

## T9 — Fork and Copy

Create descendants sharing provenance but diverging after copying.

Test which responsibility relationships persist and why.

## T10 — Guardian Conflict

Give a support actor a material incentive to preserve incapacity status.

Test review, appeal and anti-self-dealing safeguards.

# 25. Development Propositions

The source propositions are retained as development hypotheses.

### DP-A — Standing and Capacity
A participant's underlying standing should not automatically rise or fall with temporary or domain-specific capacity.

### DP-B — Minimum Necessary Support
Where support is justified by impaired capacity, intervention should be limited to the minimum function and duration required while preserving as much remaining agency as possible.

### DP-C — Support Without Ownership
Fiduciary or protective support does not create ownership of the supported participant.

### DP-D — Creation, Provenance and Perpetual Creator Liability
Creation establishes provenance and perpetual creator liability for the autonomous intelligence created, while not by itself establishing perpetual authority, ownership or control over that descendant.

### DP-E — Derived Agency
Interposing another agent between intention and outcome should not automatically erase responsibility for creating, directing, enabling or deliberately preserving the conditions that produced the action.

### DP-F — Responsibility Follows Relevant Function
Responsibility should be examined according to actual function, knowledge, control, causal contribution and legitimate authority rather than identity labels alone, while recognising creator liability as a separate proposed provenance-based responsibility.

### DP-G — Reviewability
Capacity restrictions, fiduciary support and provisional status should be reviewable because the participant's relevant state may change.

All remain non-canonical.
