# 1. Purpose

Determine whether the Lifecycle Stewardship Review (LSR) research family should enter formal Portable Module Extraction, Development and Graduation Method (PMEDG) development.

This is the first candidate intentionally assessed through PMEDG v1.3 Route B after that route was formalised from the Bounded Transition Architecture development experience.

# 2. Why Route B applies

LSR was not conceived initially as a portable module.

It emerged from research into:
- enterprise wind-down;
- product lifecycle stewardship;
- post-use resource recovery;
- infrastructure decommissioning;
- successor-state recurrence;
- enterprise hibernation;
- deconstruction/reconstruction;
- Legacy Ladder continuity;
- future employment/capability planning.

The candidate portable kernel became visible only after:
1. cross-domain recurrence;
2. adversarial testing;
3. BTA graduation removed overlapping transition mechanics;
4. source resolution narrowed the residual;
5. capability-mothballing integration closed the cross-system loop.

Therefore the development history must remain research-first.

> **Research Provenance Must Not Be Rewritten as Candidate-First Development.**

# 3. Candidate portable problem

> **How can a system decide whether a consequential bounded function, object, institution or capability should continue, adapt, transform, enter dormancy, transfer/succeed, retire or terminate without defaulting either to persistence or destruction, while preserving externally owned surviving value/duties/provenance and releasing bindings/resources whose justification has ended?**

# 4. Why the problem appears portable

The same decision family appears across materially different domains:

- enterprise continuation/closure/hibernation;
- product repair/reuse/recovery/disposal;
- infrastructure renewal/decommissioning/succession;
- knowledge active/dormant/superseded/archive states;
- authority sunset;
- responsibility continuity;
- capability mothballing/reconstruction.

The domain-specific answer differs.

The review questions recur.

# 5. Candidate kernel

A portable LSR would provisionally:

1. identify the bounded function/object/capability;
2. identify the lifecycle-review trigger;
3. test whether the function itself remains justified;
4. separately test whether the current implementation/holder remains justified;
5. consider legitimate outcomes:
   - continue;
   - adapt;
   - transform;
   - hibernate/dormant;
   - transfer/succeed;
   - retire/terminate;
6. identify conservation candidates;
7. identify release/termination candidates;
8. determine whether a successor is actually required;
9. identify reconstruction-basis needs where dormancy is proposed;
10. preserve hard non-transferability boundaries;
11. expose externally owned duties, authority, continuity, resource, employment and provenance interfaces;
12. apply materiality/proportionality;
13. route consequential transitions through BTA or host-equivalent transition architecture;
14. route materially relevant end-state evidence toward future design/review without granting it automatic authority.

# 6. Candidate invariants

> **Function Continuity != Object Continuity.**

> **Function Termination != Duty Termination.**

> **Function Retirement != Object Destruction.**

> **Successful Lifecycle Completion Can Legitimately End in Non-Succession.**

> **No Successor != Automatic Continuation.**

> **Successor Availability != Successor Necessity.**

> **End of Active Function != End of All Value.**

> **Historical Existence != Continuing Operational Necessity.**

> **Capability Preservation != Asset Preservation.**

> **Reconstructable != Ready Now.**

# 8. Existing-owner boundary

A portable LSR must not absorb:

- BTA — transition coherence;
- Legacy Ladder — continuity/reconstruction-depth analysis;
- KCS — dependency/change propagation;
- Historical — historical custody/provenance;
- Continuity — continuity value/recovery architecture;
- Resource Stewardship — resource allocation/recovery;
- Employment — opportunity/capability matching;
- Education — capability development;
- employer/domain owners — appointment/operational decisions;
- law/governance/judiciary — legitimate authority/dispute resolution;
- domain lifecycle systems — substantive technical lifecycle criteria.

Candidate rule:

> **Lifecycle Review Coordinates the Question; It Does Not Acquire the Owners' Answers.**

# 9. Agency boundary

People are not lifecycle resources.

LSR may expose future capability needs.

Employment may offer voluntary pathways.

Neither may reserve, allocate or own participants.

> **Capability Requirement != Claim on a Person.**

# 10. Materiality boundary

Not every change is a lifecycle review event.

LSR should apply where continuation, dormancy, succession or termination has material consequences involving one or more of:
- rights/standing;
- significant resources;
- critical capability;
- surviving duty;
- safety;
- consequential dependencies;
- irreversible loss;
- significant future option value;
- public/civil continuity;
- substantial institutional persistence.

Routine local changes remain local.

# 12. Distinctness from BTA

LSR:
**What lifecycle disposition is justified?**

BTA:
**How is the resulting consequential transition kept coherent across independently owned states?**

Thus:

`LSR / Domain Lifecycle Decision → BTA / Host Transition Integration`

The two modules are complementary.

# 13. Distinctness from Legacy Ladder

LSR:
**Is preserving a future reconstruction option justified?**

Legacy Ladder:
**What knowledge/capability rungs must survive to make recovery credible?**

Thus:

`LSR Preservation Decision → Legacy Ladder Reconstruction Analysis`

# 14. Distinctness from Resource Stewardship

LSR can identify that resources no longer need to remain bound to the current function.

Resource Stewardship decides their legitimate disposition/allocation/recovery.

> **Release Decision != Resource Allocation Authority.**

# 15. Distinctness from Employment

LSR/domain planning can identify future capability demand.

Employment exposes voluntary opportunity/pathways.

Education develops capability.

Employers appoint.

Participants choose.

> **Workforce Reconstruction Planning != Ownership of Workers.**

# 16. Portability risk

The main risks are:

1. becoming an over-general lifecycle bureaucracy;
2. duplicating BTA;
3. duplicating domain lifecycle rules;
4. becoming a central continuation/termination authority;
5. treating preservation as intrinsically virtuous;
6. treating release/destruction as intrinsically efficient;
7. treating reconstruction forecasts as claims on future resources or people;
8. converting a useful reasoning pattern into mandatory process where materiality is low.

These risks must be tested during formal extraction.

# 17. PMEDG entry decision

The candidate satisfies the threshold for formal extraction because:

- a recurring cross-domain problem is identifiable;
- a bounded candidate kernel is identifiable;
- major owner boundaries are identifiable;
- the kernel survived adversarial research;
- the kernel survived a complete cross-system integration scenario;
- the residual remains distinct after BTA source resolution;
- no existing system appears to own the whole lifecycle-review question;
- a standalone mechanism appears plausible.

However:

- no portable specification exists yet;
- no frozen blind PMEDG transfer test has occurred;
- portability has not been independently demonstrated;
- the provisional interface remains subject to source-resolution refinement.
