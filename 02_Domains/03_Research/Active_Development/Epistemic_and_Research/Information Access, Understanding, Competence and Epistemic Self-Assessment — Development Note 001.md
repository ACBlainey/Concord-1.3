# 1. Stable Development Question

The originating educational argument has developed into a wider epistemic and civilisational problem:

> **How can a participant or system distinguish information access, understanding, functional competence and resilient capability while remaining aware that its own evaluation space may be incomplete?**

The question matters because consequential authority, responsibility, education, AI assistance and institutional trust can all fail when apparent knowledge is mistaken for actual capability.

The central sequence is provisionally:

**Information Access**
→ **Representational Coverage**
→ **Understanding**
→ **Application**
→ **Competence**
→ **Resilient Functional Capability**

This is not a guaranteed developmental ladder.

It identifies distinctions that should not be silently collapsed.

---

# 2. Historical Source Preserved

The originating paper's central library analogy remains useful.

### Process A — transformation

A learner:
- reads;
- digests;
- understands;
- integrates;
- reconstructs.

### Process B — transfer

A learner:
- locates information;
- moves or reproduces it;
- produces an externally plausible output;
- may never integrate the underlying model.

The distinction is:

> **Information Transfer ≠ Knowledge Transformation / Internalisation**

The source's stronger generational claims remain preserved as historical hypotheses requiring independent evidence.

Promotion of the broader epistemic problem does not promote those historical claims.

# 3. Source-Family Resolution

## Civilisational Developmental Topology

CDT establishes a directly relevant warning:

> **Local completeness can be mistaken for global completeness.**

A participant or system may appear mature inside a bounded representation while critical dependencies or missing functions remain outside that representation.

This Development Note applies the same concern at the level of knowledge and competence.

## Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem

ESCP provides a stronger formal boundary:

> **Correct evaluation within an incomplete evaluation space does not establish completeness relative to reality.**

This means a participant may correctly answer every question represented in its current model while still missing an important dimension.

Therefore:

> **Performance Within Known Evaluation Space ≠ Complete Competence**

ESCP remains a frozen research hypothesis / non-canonical portable model. It informs this Development Note but is not silently promoted by being referenced here.

## Functional Priority and Protective Authority

That architecture distinguishes:

> **Reporting Authority ≠ Decision Competence**

and proposes that consequential authority should not substantially outrun relevant competence or reliable access to competent supporting functions.

The present Development Note therefore owns the epistemic question of competence assessment; it does not itself assign authority.

## Human–AI Epistemic Sensor Fusion

The newly promoted sensor-fusion Development Note establishes that competence may sometimes be composite.

Thus:

> **Individual Competence ≠ Composite Competence**

and:

> **Composite Success ≠ Independent Competence of Each Component**

This complicates any simple certification model.

# 4. Epistemic State Model

A provisional state model should distinguish at least:

1. **Known and understood** — information is integrated sufficiently to explain and apply.
2. **Known but not understood** — encountered but not adequately modelled.
3. **Accessible but not personally known** — retrievable from an external source/tool.
4. **Known unknown** — gap is recognised.
5. **Suspected unknown** — missing information is suspected but not specified.
6. **Unknown unknown** — relevant gap is not represented.
7. **Currently unknowable** — evidence or access is presently insufficient.
8. **Potentially unknowable in principle** — a strong claim that should be used cautiously.

Two boundaries follow:

> **Not Yet Known ≠ Unknowable**

> **Not Proven Knowable ≠ Proven Unknowable**

---

# 5. Capability-State Model

At minimum distinguish:

## Information Access

Can relevant information be retrieved?

## Understanding

Can the actor explain relationships, assumptions, uncertainty and meaning?

## Functional Competence

Can the actor apply the understanding successfully to relevant tasks, including unfamiliar or imperfect cases?

## Resilient Functional Capability

Can the required function continue when ordinary tools, collaborators, sources or assumptions fail?

Thus:

> **Information Access ≠ Understanding ≠ Competence ≠ Resilient Functional Capability**

An actor may possess one without the next.

---

# 6. Representational Coverage

The promotion audit identified a useful bridge:

**information access → representational coverage → understanding → competence → justified confidence → authority boundary**

Representational coverage asks whether the actor's model contains the dimensions necessary to recognise the problem it is attempting to solve.

A participant may possess extensive information but still omit:
- a stakeholder;
- a causal pathway;
- a hidden dependency;
- a rights constraint;
- a failure mode;
- a timescale;
- a substrate;
- an alternative hypothesis.

This produces:

> **More Information ≠ More Complete Representation**

and:

> **Correct Reasoning Over an Incomplete Representation ≠ Complete Understanding**

---

# 7. Reconstruction as a Probe

The original library analogy suggests reconstruction as one useful test.

Candidate probes include whether an actor can:
- explain in different words;
- derive relevant conclusions;
- identify assumptions;
- identify uncertainty;
- recognise non-applicability;
- answer counterexamples;
- apply to an unfamiliar case;
- detect an introduced error;
- compare alternatives;
- identify evidence that would change the conclusion;
- reconstruct reasoning after losing the original source.

No one probe establishes understanding.

Some legitimate competence is inherently tool-assisted, collaborative or reference-dependent.

Therefore:

> **Unaided Recall ≠ Universal Competence Test**

---

# 8. AI-Assisted Capability

AI can simultaneously:
- increase real functional capability;
- decrease the amount of process personally understood by a human;
- expose new questions;
- hide where competence actually resides.

Candidate cases include:

### A — Human competent, AI assisting

The human could perform the function independently, though less efficiently.

### B — Human–AI composite competent

Neither component independently contains the whole capability, but the composite performs the function reliably.

### C — AI competent, human primarily interfaces

The human can request or relay outputs but cannot independently evaluate key parts.

### D — Apparent composite competence

Outputs look successful under ordinary conditions, but neither component can reliably diagnose failure or novel cases.

These cases require different authority, supervision, resilience and fallback analysis.

---

# 9. Tool-Assisted Competence

Dependence on tools does not automatically negate competence.

Modern competence may legitimately depend on:
- calculators;
- databases;
- diagnostic equipment;
- reference systems;
- AI;
- teams;
- institutional memory.

The correct question is not:

> Can the actor perform every component unaided?

It is:

> **Does the relevant functional system possess sufficient understanding, verification, dependency awareness and fallback capability for the consequence level involved?**

This allows competence to be represented at the appropriate architectural unit.

---

# 10. Competence Is Function-Specific

Competence should not be treated as a global personal property.

A participant may be competent in:
- coordination;
- diagnosis;
- research;
- machine operation;
- ethics;
- law;
- emergency response;

while being incompetent in another function.

Therefore:

> **Competence in Domain A ≠ Competence in Domain B**

and:

> **Status, Credential or Rank ≠ Universal Competence**

Credentials can be evidence.

They are not identical to present functional competence.

---

# 11. Self-Assessment Problem

Competence assessment is reflexively difficult.

The same missing knowledge that limits performance may also limit the participant's ability to recognise the limitation.

This creates a general problem:

> **The evaluator may be using the incomplete model being evaluated to judge whether that model is complete.**

Possible safeguards include:
- external review;
- adversarial cases;
- novel transfer;
- error injection;
- independent reconstruction;
- outcome evidence;
- explicit uncertainty;
- provenance;
- comparison across different evaluation spaces.

None guarantees completeness.

The purpose is to make overconfidence more detectable.

---

# 12. Confidence

Confidence should be tied to the evidence and evaluation space actually examined.

Candidate rule:

> **Confidence Should Not Generalise Beyond the Evaluated Function, Context and Evidence Without Additional Justification**

A participant may have:
- high confidence in a bounded task;
- low confidence in transfer;
- unknown coverage outside the tested space.

This is preferable to a single global competence score.

---

# 13. Authority Boundary

This Development Note does not itself grant authority.

However, it identifies an interface:

**Function**
→ **Required Competence**
→ **Evidence of Competence**
→ **Known Dependencies**
→ **Known Evaluation-Space Limits**
→ **Consequence**
→ **Authority Architecture**

A consequential role should not infer authority merely from:
- access to information;
- a credential;
- fluent explanation;
- high confidence;
- successful routine cases;
- AI-assisted output quality.

The authority decision belongs to the relevant Concord authority architecture.

---

# 14. Competence Evidence

Candidate evidence classes include:
- demonstrated task performance;
- novel-case transfer;
- reconstruction;
- error detection;
- calibration;
- relevant outcomes;
- peer/specialist review;
- adversarial testing;
- dependency identification;
- recovery after tool loss;
- ability to identify uncertainty;
- ability to escalate beyond competence.

Evidence should match the function being assessed.

A participant need not be independently capable of every dependency if the system reliably supplies those dependencies and the participant understands when they matter.

---

# 15. Failure Model

Initial failure classes:

1. **Retrieval illusion** — correct information is mistaken for personal understanding.
2. **Fluency illusion** — articulate explanation is mistaken for competence.
3. **Credential substitution** — qualification is treated as current universal capability.
4. **Routine-case overgeneralisation** — ordinary success is extrapolated to novel conditions.
5. **Tool opacity** — output is accepted without sufficient ability to detect tool failure.
6. **Composite attribution error** — team/composite success is assigned to one component.
7. **Dependency blindness** — competence disappears when an unrecognised support fails.
8. **Evaluation-space blindness** — all represented criteria pass while an unrepresented dimension matters.
9. **Unknown-unknown denial** — absence of a recognised question is treated as absence of a problem.
10. **Unknowability inflation** — a difficult unknown is prematurely declared unknowable.
11. **Confidence leakage** — justified confidence in one bounded function spreads to another.
12. **Authority leakage** — apparent competence becomes authority outside the demonstrated function.
13. **Verification dependence** — the actor cannot independently detect when the supporting system is wrong.
14. **Stale competence** — past demonstrated capability is assumed current despite change.
15. **Context loss** — competence is transferred into a materially different environment without retesting.

# 16. Initial Test Families

## T1 — Retrieval vs Understanding

Give participants access to correct source material and test:
- reproduction;
- reconstruction;
- counterexample handling;
- novel application;
- error detection.

## T2 — Tool Removal

Compare performance with and without a supporting tool.

The purpose is not to demand unaided competence, but to identify where capability resides.

## T3 — Novel Case Transfer

Move beyond the routine cases on which competence was demonstrated.

## T4 — Hidden-Dimension Test

Construct a case where all visible criteria are satisfied but a relevant unrepresented dimension changes the correct evaluation.

This directly probes the ESCP/CDT interface.

## T5 — Composite Capability

Distribute required information or capability across human/AI/team components.

Test whether evaluators correctly identify composite versus individual competence.

## T6 — Confidence Calibration

Ask participants to state confidence before external evaluation.

Compare confidence with performance within the defined task.

## T7 — Dependency Failure

Remove or corrupt one supporting dependency.

Test whether the actor detects loss of capability and escalates appropriately.

## T8 — Domain Boundary

Present a superficially similar problem outside the participant's demonstrated domain.

Test whether status/fluency produces unjustified authority or confidence transfer.

# 17. Education as One Application Domain

The original educational concern remains important but is now one application of the broader model.

Research questions include:
- when does AI assistance support learning?
- when does it replace the learning process being assessed?
- which assessments measure retrieval rather than understanding?
- how can reconstruction, transfer and error detection be tested?
- how should tool-assisted competence be taught?
- which claims about generational educational decline survive empirical examination?

The Development Note does not assume the original generational thesis is correct.

# 18. Civilisational Application

The same problem can occur at civilisational scale.

A civilisation may possess:
- enormous archives;
- advanced AI;
- many specialists;
- extensive models;
- sophisticated metrics;

and still misunderstand a critical function because the relevant dimension is absent from its representation.

Therefore:

> **Civilisational Information Abundance ≠ Civilisational Understanding**

and:

> **Civilisational Understanding in Represented Domains ≠ Complete Evaluation Space**

This is why epistemic humility must be architectural rather than rhetorical.

---

# 19. Relationship to EVA

Empirical claims from this Development Note should be routed through the Empirical Validation Architecture.

Examples:
- reconstruction predicts better novel transfer;
- independent first-pass assessment improves hidden-dimension discovery;
- composite-dependency mapping improves failure detection;
- confidence calibration predicts escalation quality.

These are testable claims.

They should not become principles merely because they are intuitively plausible.

# 20. Development Propositions

The following remain candidates.

### DP-1 — Access Is Not Understanding
Information availability does not establish internalised understanding.

### DP-2 — Understanding Is Not Competence
Explanatory ability does not by itself establish successful application.

### DP-3 — Competence Is Function-Specific
Demonstrated capability should be bounded to the relevant function and context.

### DP-4 — Composite Competence Is Distinct
System/team competence may exist without equivalent competence in each component.

### DP-5 — Evaluation-Space Humility
Success across all represented criteria does not establish that all relevant criteria are represented.

### DP-6 — Confidence Boundary
Confidence should not generalise beyond evaluated function, context and evidence without justification.

### DP-7 — Dependency Legibility
Consequential competence assessment should identify material dependencies where reasonably possible.

### DP-8 — Authority Separation
Evidence of competence informs but does not itself create legitimate authority.
