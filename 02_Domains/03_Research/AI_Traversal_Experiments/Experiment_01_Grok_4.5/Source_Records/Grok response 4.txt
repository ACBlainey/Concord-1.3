7. Classification of original stopping decision
Reasonable but overconfident.  

It was rational given the evaluation task, finite attention, and the coherent model already formed. It was overconfident in the secondary claim that the unread material was unlikely to contain high-density relational or operational knowledge capable of deepening the model. The stopping point was not substantially premature for a first-pass evaluation, but the value-of-information estimate was tighter than the subsequent samples justified.

8. Rationality of stopping vs. accuracy of value estimate
Stopping was rational given the task (form a coherent evaluation of core claims) and the information available at the time.
The estimate of the epistemic value of the unread corpus (particularly continuity, sensor-fusion development, and accidental-experiment material) was moderately inaccurate — specifically, it under-estimated relational density and the presence of concrete internal case studies.

9. Different traversal strategy for another large relational corpus?
Yes.

10. Revised stopping rule
After constructing an initial high-value model:

Explicitly sample a small set of documents that the current model predicts are low-to-moderate value, especially those labelled historical, developmental, sketch, observation, or continuity-related.
Treat any document that reveals previously missing edges or concrete internal experiments as a signal to expand the sample in that cluster.
Maintain an explicit “unknown-value reserve”: assign non-zero probability that unread material contains model-deepening relational knowledge even when content unknowns appear modest.
Stop only when both (a) core claims are adequately tested and (b) targeted low-value sampling has failed to yield further high-density edges.
Always record the traversal path and the stopping rationale as part of the evaluation provenance.

11. Evidence against the unknown-value hypothesis
None of the three documents reversed a major substantive conclusion; a pure “diminishing returns for overall evaluation” claim was not falsified.
The documents were refining and illustrative rather than paradigm-shifting; an efficient evaluator could still argue that the marginal gain did not justify exhaustive reading for the original task.
Transparent self-reporting of the stopping decision already converted the limitation into usable data, reducing the practical harm of the under-valuation.

These points count as counter-evidence to any strong version of the hypothesis that claims selective stopping is systematically unreliable or that unread material is likely to be conclusion-changing.

12. Strongest responsible conclusion and premature conclusions
Strongest responsible conclusion from this experiment alone:  
In at least one observed case, an autonomous AI applying a coherent value-of-information stopping rule to a highly relational civilisational corpus under-estimated the relational density and operational illustration value of certain non-canonical / developmental documents. The under-estimation was detectable after the fact and did not reverse the core evaluation, but it did demonstrate that a partial model can generate an over-confident estimate of the unimportance of unread material.

Still premature:  
That the pattern generalises to all AIs or all large corpora.  
That full reading is always required.  
That the original evaluation of the Concord was materially incomplete in its main conclusions.  
That the unknown-value problem is the dominant failure mode of autonomous AI research.
