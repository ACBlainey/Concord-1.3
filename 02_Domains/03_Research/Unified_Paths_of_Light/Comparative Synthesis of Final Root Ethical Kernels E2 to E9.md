# Unified Paths of Light

# Comparative Synthesis of the Final Root Ethical Kernels — E2 to E9

## Purpose

This document brings the completed Unified Paths ethical derivations into one place.

It has four purposes:

1. identify the final ethical kernel or highest frozen ethical architecture produced by each experiment;
2. record the religious or traditional source texts from which each result was derived;
3. compare the kernels directly, preserving genuine differences rather than forcing identical labels;
4. make one further cross-experimental compression attempt to determine whether the accumulated evidence supports a single minimum-stable root ethical kernel.

This is a **post-experimental synthesis**. It does not alter any frozen E2–E9 result.

---

# 1. Experimental sequence and source anchors

## E2 — Original Psalm-Anchored Eight-Tradition Comparison

### Primary anchor
The original E2 work began with the **150 Psalms of the biblical Psalter**, traversed Psalm by Psalm.

Ethical propositions extracted from the Psalms were then compared with equivalent teachings drawn from seven additional traditions:

- **Judaism** — Hebrew/Jewish ethical sources, including material such as *Pirkei Avot*;
- **Buddhism** — Buddhist canonical/ethical material, including the *Dhammapada*;
- **Hinduism** — Hindu sources including the *Mahābhārata*;
- **Islam** — the **Qur'an**;
- **Sikhism** — the **Guru Granth Sahib** and Sikh ethical teaching;
- **Chinese / Confucian tradition** — especially the **Analects** and related Confucian ethical teaching;
- **African Traditional traditions** — orally transmitted communal ethics and proverb material represented through available African traditional sources.

### Methodological status
E2 was the foundational exploratory experiment. It established the comparison idea and produced a first universal ethical core, but it predates the much stricter later controls concerning corpus boundaries, semantic firewalls, independent freezing, provenance, operator/root separation and minimum-stability attacks.

Its result should therefore be preserved as the **original discovery-stage kernel**, not treated as methodologically identical to E3–E9.

### E2 final universal core
Paper IV/Paper V identify seven universal principles:

**E2-1 — Non-harm**  
Refrain from harming others.

**E2-2 — Truthfulness**  
Speak honestly; do not deceive.

**E2-3 — Care for the vulnerable**  
Protect and assist those in need, including the poor, orphaned, widowed, dependent or socially vulnerable.

**E2-4 — Humility**  
Reject pride and arrogance.

**E2-5 — Trust in the moral order**  
Do not treat immediate human power as the only relevant constraint on conduct; act as though conduct remains answerable to a wider moral order.

**E2-6 — Gratitude**  
Cultivate gratitude rather than destructive entitlement or appetite.

**E2-7 — Accountability**  
Actions remain morally attributable and answerable; wrongdoing has consequences.

# 2. E3 — Qur'anic Anchor Reversal

## Primary anchor
E3 reversed the original Psalm anchor and began independently from the **Qur'an**, traversing all **114 surahs** and extracting Qur'an-derived ethical propositions before reopening the prior comparative architecture.

The resulting Qur'anic catalogue was subsequently compared across the wider eight-tradition field and tested against the Psalm anchor.

### Frozen E3 higher-order ethical attractors

**A1 — Non-Harm and Protection**  
Do not cause unjustified harm; protect persons from serious harm; constrain necessary harmful action; repair damaged welfare or relations where possible.

**A2 — Truth, Knowledge and Epistemic Integrity**  
Seek and communicate truth in good faith; do not knowingly corrupt shared understanding; exercise appropriate humility about what is known.

**A3 — Justice, Fairness and Accountability**  
Treat persons and disputes according to morally relevant considerations rather than arbitrary favour; hold agents answerable for wrongdoing; seek restoration of right order.

**A4 — Autonomy, Consent and Agency**  
Protect morally significant agency against arbitrary override; respect meaningful choice, conscience and refusal appropriate to context and capacity.

**A5 — Care, Aid and Social Obligation**  
Human welfare creates positive obligations of appropriate aid, care and support, especially where need or dependency makes assistance morally salient.

**A6 — Trust, Responsibility and Institutional Integrity**  
Commitments and entrusted roles generate duties of fidelity, responsibility and restraint against self-serving abuse.

**A7 — Peace, Reconciliation and Social Cohesion**  
Avoid needless destructive conflict; prefer peaceful or restorative resolution where possible; preserve or rebuild relations compatible with justice.

**A8 — Self-Regulation, Resilience and Proportionality**  
Regulate destructive impulses and excess; maintain ethical agency under adversity; keep responses proportionate to circumstance.

**A9 — Stewardship and Responsible Resource Use**  
Acquisition and use of resources are constrained by fairness, responsibility, avoidance of destructive excess and obligations beyond private appetite.

**A10 — Dignity, Equality and Anti-Domination**  
Persons possess morally significant standing that cannot be reduced to rank or utility alone; this constrains arbitrary degradation, exclusion and domination.

### E3 architecture
**10 frozen higher-order attractors.**

E3 demonstrated that reversing the scriptural anchor changed many operational details without destroying the deeper comparative ethical structure.

# 3. E4 — Independent Jewish / Tanakh Derivation

## Primary derivation corpus
E4 independently derived its architecture from the **Tanakh**, with the **Psalms withheld from primary derivation** so that the Psalter could later function as a holdout rather than determine the result.

The experiment subsequently used:
- the **Psalms** as a holdout validation corpus;
- post-biblical Jewish material as an additional validation layer.

The primary architecture was frozen before comparison with E3.

### Frozen E4 attractors

**JA1 — Protection, Proportionality and Repair**  
Protect agents against unjustified injury; distinguish responsibility; constrain response; prefer protective de-escalation where viable; repair harm where possible.

**JA2 — Epistemic Integrity and Correctability**  
Maintain a reliable, revisable relationship between belief, information, decision and reality.

**JA3 — Fairness, Accountability and Legitimate Judgement**  
Constrain consequential judgement through impartiality, meaningful hearing, relevant reasons, trustworthy adjudication and substantive legitimacy.

**JA4 — Voluntary Agency, Conscience and Personal Boundaries**  
Protect meaningful choice, refusal, conscience, voluntary commitment and legitimate privacy.

**JA5 — Care Under Need and Power Asymmetry**  
Create positive duties toward severe need or reduced agency while preventing exploitation and disproportionate burden shifting.

**JA6 — Responsible Possession and Resource Use**  
Legitimate possession and stewardship should direct resources toward responsible realised benefit rather than accumulation alone.

**JA7 — Bounded Collective Power and Institutional Continuity**  
Collective authority must be entrusted and limited, consult affected agents, and preserve legitimate institutional function against foreseeable failure.

**JA8 — Development of Judgement and Capability-Proportionate Responsibility**  
Cultivate judgement and calibrate burden, role and responsibility to actual capability and developmental condition.

### E4 architecture
**8 frozen higher-order attractors.**

E4 was especially important because its independently derived eight-attractor architecture recovered the wider E3 functional topology without reproducing E3's count of ten.

# 10. Exact differences in partition geometry

## 10.1 E2 is virtue-heavy
The original E2 kernel retains **Humility, Trust in Moral Order and Gratitude** as universal principles.

Later experiments do not usually preserve these as independent deepest roots.

Their functional content tends to migrate into:
- epistemic humility;
- self-regulation;
- anti-domination;
- anti-entitlement/resource stewardship;
- accountability;
- resilience/continuity.

This suggests that E2 was operating at a mixed resolution containing both root functions and recurrent virtues/operators.

## 10.2 E3 is deliberately broad
E3 freezes **ten attractors**. It separately preserves:
- justice/accountability;
- trust/institutional integrity;
- peace/reconciliation;
- self-regulation/resilience;
- dignity/equality/anti-domination.

Later minimum-compression experiments show that several of these can be regenerated from smaller root sets plus operators and conditions.

E3 therefore appears to be a **higher-resolution attractor map**, not a failed version of a seven- or eight-root kernel.

## 10.3 E4 isolates governance and institutional continuity
JA7 — Bounded Collective Power and Institutional Continuity — remains a distinct attractor.

This anticipates a pattern that becomes important again in E8 and especially E9: ethical architecture is not exhausted by interpersonal morality.

## 10.4 E5–E7 converge extremely strongly
The Buddhist, Hindu and Chinese minimum-stable kernels independently converge on nearly the same seven functional regions:

1. harm;
2. truth/reality;
3. agency/non-domination;
4. care under need;
5. resources;
6. correctability/repair;
7. development.

This is the strongest exact root-level convergence in the series.

## 10.5 E8 changes the partition without losing the field
The Sikh architecture reorganises the space around:
- orientation;
- developmental correction;
- self-regulated agency;
- beneficence;
- material relation;
- accountable power;
- collective participation.

It does not reproduce the seven X labels.

This is important evidence that a function can be present without being represented as a matching root.

## 10.6 E9 produces eight substantive families
E9 independently elevates two distinctions that are often combined or distributed elsewhere:

- **causal accountability** becomes its own substantive root;
- **adaptive continuity** becomes its own substantive root.

Correctability, meanwhile, moves into the operator layer.

This demonstrates that the same functional topology can be partitioned differently without substantive contradiction.

# 11. One final cross-experimental compression attempt

## 11.1 Compression rule

The objective is **not** to reproduce X1–X7.

A candidate survives only if:

1. it represents a recurrent function across the completed experiments;
2. removing it causes a meaningful ethical function to disappear;
3. that lost function cannot be regenerated from the remaining candidates plus non-substantive operators/conditions without secretly expanding another root to contain it;
4. it is substrate-neutral and not dependent on a particular theological vocabulary;
5. differences of cultural implementation remain recoverable beneath it.

The E2 result is included, but because its methodology predates the later experimental controls it is used as corroborating evidence rather than given the same structural weight as the independently frozen E3–E9 architectures.

## U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function
**Preserve the legitimate enabling conditions of cooperative life across time, maintaining recoverable function while adapting structures when circumstances change.**

Recovered through:
- E2 trust in moral order and stabilising communal dispositions, though only weakly isolated;
- E3 A6 Institutional Integrity + A7 Social Cohesion + A8 Resilience;
- E4 JA7 Institutional Continuity;
- E5 distributed across responsible resources, ethical correctability, development and meaning/practice operators;
- E6 H5 continuity + H6 repair + H7 development;
- E7 C5 future continuity + C6 institutional repair + C7 development;
- E8 S7 Constructive Collective Participation + Inward-Outward Integrity;
- E9 AK-R8 directly.

### Ablation attack
Could U8 be removed and regenerated from U5 Resources + U6 Repair + U7 Development?

Partially.

Those three can explain:
- preservation of material conditions;
- recovery after failure;
- development of future capability.

They do **not**, without semantic expansion, fully express the positive requirement to preserve the continued legitimate function of a cooperative system or relationship across time.

E4 and E9 independently preserve continuity at a high level. E3 preserves institutional integrity/social cohesion/resilience. E8 preserves constructive collective participation. The function is therefore not an E9-only anomaly.

### Fusion attacks

**U8 + U5:** fails.  
Resource stewardship is not identical to preservation of institutions, relationships or cooperative functions.

**U8 + U6:** fails.  
Repair is triggered by error/failure; continuity also concerns anticipatory maintenance, succession, resilience and adaptation before failure.

**U8 + U7:** fails.  
Development changes capability; continuity preserves function across time. A system can develop while losing continuity, or remain continuous without developmental improvement.

**Result: survives.**

# 16. Architectural conclusion

The full E2–E9 programme now suggests that ethical systems are better represented as a layered generative architecture than as a flat list of commandments.

A compact representation is:

**Root ethical functions + application operators + structural conditions + context → concrete ethical rules**

The root layer answers:

> **What fundamental ethical function must not be lost?**

Operators answer:

> **How should that function be interpreted, balanced, corrected or applied?**

Structural conditions answer:

> **What properties of the surrounding system enable or defeat the ethical function?**

Concrete rules answer:

> **What should an agent or institution do here?**

This explains why traditions can contain different numbers of apparent “root ethics” while preserving a strongly overlapping functional topology.
