The formal question is not whether the architecture evokes a fractal image. It is whether a meaningful structural object remains similar under a transformation of scale.

The result proves something narrower:

# 5. Three Meanings of “Fractal”

The work now allows three different claims to be separated.

## F-A — Visual Fractality

The architecture looks tree-like, organic, recursively branching or aesthetically self-similar.

**Status:** observed in the first conceptual visualisation, but evidentially weak.

## F-B — Architectural / Relational Self-Similarity

Comparable relational operators recur under transformation of scale even though concrete entities and mechanisms change.

**Status:** supported in the current source sample.

## F-C — Mathematical Fractality

The system exhibits a formal fractal property such as statistically demonstrable scale invariance, fractal dimension, recursive graph-generating law, or related quantitative property.

**Status:** not established.

This distinction resolves much of the ambiguity around the historical Concord use of the word “fractal”.
