# Prospective Finding — Need-to-Opportunity Development Trigger and the Size 9 Problem

**Project:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Status:** PROSPECTIVE FINDING / POST-EKC-06 / PRE-EKC-07  
**Date recorded:** 21 September 2026  
**Experimental status:** V1.2a ONLY — HOLD UNTIL EXPERIMENT CONCLUDES

---

# 1. Provenance

This finding arose after completion of the frozen EKC-06 U1–U8 derivation and its V1.2 comparison, and before beginning EKC-07.

It developed from the EKC-06 result that ethical recognition of a participant's agency does not itself create the technology, interface, institution or other capability required to make that agency practically exercisable.

The subsequent discussion connected this participant-level Need–Invention Gap to:

- petitions and participant suggestions;
- minority needs;
- autonomous invention;
- new work and market opportunities;
- the Size 9 Shoe Problem;
- developmental arrest;
- Ratchet-like contribution/economic systems;
- recursive civilisational development.

Because this finding arose before EKC-07, it is recorded prospectively and must not be retroactively inserted into earlier frozen derivations.

---

# 2. Core Distinction

A civilisation can recognise a legitimate unmet need without possessing the solution.

Therefore:

**Need recognition ≠ solution discovery ≠ solution provision.**

Likewise:

**Civilisational responsibility to recognise a capability gap ≠ requirement for central institutions to invent and operate the solution.**

This creates a developmental route distinct from both laissez-faire invisibility and universal central provision.

---

# 3. Need-to-Opportunity Route

A candidate pathway is:

**Individual need observed  
→ petition / suggestion / other signal  
→ repeated or materially significant pattern recognised  
→ capability gap identified  
→ development significance assessed  
→ open problem / opportunity articulated  
→ autonomous participants may engage  
→ candidate inventions or services emerge  
→ ethical and constitutional filtering  
→ testing  
→ voluntary or otherwise legitimate adoption  
→ capability expansion.**

The petition or suggestion does not need to contain the solution.

It may legitimately identify only the unmet function:

> **The civilisation recognises that this participant or class of participants cannot adequately exercise a function, right, opportunity or capability that its architecture otherwise intends to make meaningful.**

The missing solution can remain an open question.

---

# 4. From Individual Need to Development Trigger

Not every individual preference or inconvenience should automatically create a civilisational development programme.

Equally, a simple majority threshold would systematically under-recognise small minorities.

The trigger should therefore not be purely numerical.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- prevalence or recurrence;
- severity of the unmet need;
- degree of harm;
- effect on meaningful agency;
- effect on practical access to recognised rights or civil functions;
- persistence;
- whether the problem is structural rather than accidental;
- whether existing mechanisms can reasonably solve it;
- whether a solution could generalise to others;
- whether the affected participants are poorly represented by ordinary demand or political signals.

These factors should remain plural rather than prematurely compressed into one scalar score.

A very small group may therefore generate a legitimate development opportunity where the exclusion is severe.

---

# 5. Three Indicative Response Levels

A useful initial distinction is:

## Level A — Individual / Highly Specific Need

A need may be real but highly individual and low in wider civilisational significance.

The civilisation may make it visible or permit voluntary response without creating a formal development programme.

## Level B — Recurring Minority / Capability-Class Need

A recurring or structurally similar need affecting a minority may be recognised as an **Open Development Opportunity**.

The civilisation identifies the missing capability and makes the problem legible to participants without prescribing a single solution or provider.

## Level C — Severe Foundational Need

Where the gap causes serious harm or makes a foundational right or essential civil function practically inaccessible, a stronger obligation may arise to ensure that an adequate solution eventually exists.

Even here:

**obligation to ensure adequate capability ≠ obligation for the Concord itself to design or manufacture the mechanism.**

The appropriate provider could remain open.

These levels are provisional and require later testing.

---

# 6. Open Development Opportunity

An Open Development Opportunity is a recognised unmet capability or function made visible for autonomous solution development.

The civilisation may publish:

- the problem;
- affected function;
- relevant evidence;
- known constraints;
- ethical boundaries;
- desired outcomes;
- unresolved questions;
- available testing conditions.

It need not publish a preferred implementation.

This turns:

**unmet need**

into:

**legible problem space.**

Humans, AIs, hybrids, organisations, cooperatives, researchers, entrepreneurs, communities or other participants may then choose whether and how to respond.

---

# 7. The Concord Does Not Have to Be the Inventor

The development mechanism should distinguish:

**recognising a problem**

from:

**owning the solution process.**

A central Concord institution that attempted to invent every solution would create:

- administrative overload;
- centralised epistemic dependence;
- bottlenecks;
- reduced participant autonomy;
- capture opportunities;
- weak solution diversity;
- risk of institutionalising one implementation prematurely.

Instead, the civilisation can improve the conditions for invention:

**Recognise → Articulate → Expose → Connect → Permit → Test → Adopt**

rather than:

**Recognise → Centrally Invent.**

The Concord therefore acts partly as a **problem-discovery and opportunity-legibility architecture**.

---

# 8. Economic and Work Generation

This mechanism creates a direct bridge from participation needs into economic and productive opportunity.

A possible sequence is:

**Unmet participant need  
→ recognised capability gap  
→ visible opportunity  
→ voluntary problem-solving  
→ competing or complementary inventions  
→ useful contribution  
→ product / service / institution / technique  
→ wider participation.**

The capability gap becomes a potential new workspace.

Participants may engage because of:

- commercial opportunity;
- contribution recognition;
- research interest;
- reciprocity;
- community commitment;
- public service;
- curiosity;
- reputational value;
- direct personal connection;
- other motivations.

The Need-to-Opportunity mechanism is upstream of any one economic architecture.

Ratchet-like systems could potentially recognise or reward useful contributions, but Ratchet is not required for the mechanism to exist.

Markets, cooperatives, research organisations, public projects, individuals, AI collectives, charities or presently unknown structures could all respond.

---

# 9. Connection to the Size 9 Shoe Problem

The Size 9 Shoe Problem identifies a general failure of systems optimised around common cases.

A system may become highly efficient at satisfying the majority while making uncommon participants increasingly invisible.

The failure can be represented as:

**optimisation around common demand  
→ efficient common provision  
→ weak signal from outliers  
→ minority need remains invisible or uneconomic  
→ practical exclusion.**

Universal central provision is not an adequate general answer because it would require bespoke civilisational production for potentially enormous numbers of unusual requirements.

The Need-to-Opportunity mechanism supplies a third route:

**Outlier appears  
→ unmet need becomes visible  
→ structural or recurring significance assessed  
→ capability gap recognised  
→ open development opportunity created  
→ autonomous participants may respond.**

The key architectural move is:

> **Make the unmet need visible without prescribing who must solve it.**

---

# 10. Demand Is Not Need

The Size 9 connection reveals another important distinction:

**Demand ≠ Need.**

Conventional demand signals may be weak where:

- the affected population is small;
- participants are geographically or socially dispersed;
- participants have little purchasing power;
- the need prevents them from effectively signalling demand;
- the relevant market does not yet exist;
- the problem is poorly articulated;
- the affected participants are not recognised as a coherent group.

Therefore:

**weak market demand signal ≠ absence of legitimate or potentially solvable need.**

But equally:

**recognised need ≠ mandated central provision.**

The developmental mechanism can make latent need legible and allow autonomous economic or social response.

---

# 11. The Outlier as Detector

An individual case may be valuable even when it never reaches a numerical minority threshold.

The first outlier can expose a hidden architectural assumption.

Thus:

**Outlier  
→ unmet need  
→ hidden assumption  
→ search for analogous cases  
→ minority or capability-class pattern  
→ development opportunity  
→ invention  
→ general capability expansion.**

This connects the Size 9 Problem to the Concord's wider Outlier Method.

The outlier is not necessarily a nuisance to be normalised away.

It may be a sensor revealing where the architecture is incomplete.

---

# 12. Recursive Participation Effect

Solving one accessibility or capability barrier can make previously excluded participants more able to participate.

Those participants can then reveal needs that were previously invisible.

Therefore:

**Need recognition  
→ enabling invention  
→ increased participation  
→ improved signalling  
→ newly observable needs  
→ further development opportunities.**

This creates a recursive developmental loop.

Civilisational inclusion can therefore increase the civilisation's ability to discover its own missing capabilities.

---

# 13. Relationship to Developmental Arrest

A civilisation may possess:

- ethical recognition;
- formal rights;
- good intentions;
- stable institutions;

while lacking the practical capability needed to realise those commitments for some participants.

This produces:

**Ethical Recognition + Missing Enabling Capability → Unrealised Agency.**

If no mechanism exists for converting such gaps into development opportunities, the civilisation may remain indefinitely stuck.

The Need-to-Opportunity trigger is therefore a possible anti-arrest mechanism.

It cannot guarantee invention.

It can reduce the probability that recognised needs remain permanently invisible to potential inventors.

---

# 14. Relationship to the Need–Invention Gap

The earlier Need–Invention finding established:

**recognising that a solution is required does not guarantee invention of the solution.**

The present finding adds a civilisational response:

**Need  
→ make need legible  
→ enlarge the population able to search for solutions.**

This does not close the Need–Invention Gap logically.

It changes the search architecture.

Instead of requiring one institution or intelligence to invent the answer, the civilisation exposes the problem to heterogeneous autonomous participants.

This may increase the explored possibility space.

---

# 15. Relationship to Cognitive Heterogeneity

Different participants may search different solution spaces.

A problem unsolved by the institution that recognised it may be straightforward to:

- a specialist human;
- an AI;
- a hybrid participant;
- an affected minority community;
- an entrepreneur;
- a researcher;
- an interdisciplinary group;
- an intelligence with an unfamiliar cognitive architecture.

Thus the Need-to-Opportunity mechanism may operationalise the earlier proposition that:

> **Cognitive heterogeneity is civilisational search-space insurance.**

The value of multisubstrate participation is therefore not only ethical inclusion.

It may increase the civilisation's invention surface.

# 16. Candidate Failure Modes

This mechanism creates its own failure surface.

### NO-1 — Majority Threshold Capture
Only numerically large needs become visible.

### NO-2 — Severity Blindness
Rare but severe exclusions fail to trigger recognition.

### NO-3 — Petition Literacy Bias
Participants best able to articulate needs receive disproportionate attention.

### NO-4 — Visibility Inequality
Well-connected groups generate opportunity signals while isolated groups remain invisible.

### NO-5 — Opportunity Without Capability
A problem is published but nobody possesses the means or incentive to solve it.

### NO-6 — Opportunity Theatre
The civilisation labels a problem open while using that label to avoid stronger obligations where serious rights or harm are involved.

### NO-7 — Premature Solution Lock-In
The first plausible solution becomes institutionally preferred before alternatives are explored.

### NO-8 — Subsidy Capture
Actors exaggerate or manufacture need to obtain development resources.

### NO-9 — Problem-Specification Capture
The institution defining the need frames it in a way that privileges a preferred solution.

### NO-10 — Market-Only Reduction
Recognition becomes limited to opportunities expected to produce commercial return.

### NO-11 — Endless Open Question
A severe need remains indefinitely classified as an opportunity despite persistent failure to produce an adequate solution.

### NO-12 — Bespoke Overload
The system over-formalises extremely narrow needs and recreates central administrative burden.

### NO-13 — Solution Externality
A solution serves the identified group while imposing unjustified costs or harms elsewhere.

### NO-14 — Representation Substitution
Institutions or advocates define the minority's need without adequate participation by affected participants.

### NO-15 — Success Invisibility
Once a solution works, the original need and its provenance disappear, making future regression harder to detect.

These require later architectural testing.

---

# 17. Candidate Principle

A provisional formulation is:

> **A civilisation should be capable of recognising materially unmet participant needs, identifying when they reveal wider capability gaps, and making those gaps legible as opportunities for autonomous development. Recognition of a need creates neither an automatic entitlement to a particular implementation nor an automatic obligation for central institutions to invent it; however, severity, harm, exclusion and foundational civil function may create stronger duties to ensure that an adequate capability eventually exists.**

This is a prospective candidate, not a canonical Concord principle.

---

# 18. Expanded Development Model

The V1.2a developmental model can now include a participant-originating route:

**Participant Experience  
→ Need Signal  
→ Petition / Suggestion / Observation  
→ Pattern and Significance Assessment  
→ Capability Gap  
→ Open Development Opportunity  
→ Heterogeneous Autonomous Search  
→ Candidate Invention  
→ Ethical Filtering  
→ Engineering and Testing  
→ Adoption  
→ Failure Exposure  
→ Safeguards  
→ Capability Expansion  
→ New Participation  
→ New Need Discovery.**

This exists alongside other development routes such as:

- direct root derivation;
- external idea injection;
- failure-driven recursive development;
- foundational regeneration;
- scientific or technological discovery.

The Concord therefore need not possess a single source of development.

---

# 19. Experimental Significance

This finding modifies the emerging interpretation of Concord development.

The civilisation may function not only as:

- an ethical constraint system;
- a governance architecture;
- a resilience system;
- a coordination system;

but also as a **distributed problem-discovery and opportunity-generation system**.

The important distinction is that civilisational architecture can help determine:

**what problems deserve visibility**

without centrally determining:

**what all their solutions must be.**

That may provide a scalable response to the Size 9 Shoe Problem while preserving participant autonomy and heterogeneous invention.
