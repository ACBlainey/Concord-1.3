## Purpose

CLOCK-002 asks whether the first completed Clock cycle has produced enough differentiated evidence to select substantive development, while preserving cheap retests, tests and source resolution where they remain superior actions.

The Clock is treated according to Companion Extension 001 as a state-transition/review-trigger architecture, not merely a chronological scheduler.

## 1. Ordering Rules

1. changed blockers should be retested before replacement architecture;
2. mechanism testing should precede redesign where a mechanism already exists;
3. weaker source resolution should be improved before declaring a developmental gap;
4. explicit undeveloped legitimate-owner mechanisms are stronger development evidence than generic partiality;
5. potential consequence is not current emergency;
6. connectivity and coordination role do not create automatic priority;
7. trigger selection does not confer authority;
8. parallel non-conflicting work is allowed;
9. held dependencies remain held unless their prerequisite changed;
10. the Clock may select substantive development once the evidence justifies it.

## 12. Why L3-13 Was Selected

The selection is not because L3-13 is “worst.”

It is because the current evidence distinguishes it from the competing candidates:

- its function is clearly required;
- its owner domain already exists;
- that owner domain explicitly says it is undeveloped;
- adjacent architecture supplies strong constraints but not the missing mechanism;
- it is not currently dependency-blocked;
- potential consequence is substantial;
- the minimum development is bounded and can occur without creating new civil authority.

This is the first time the canonical developmental loop has progressed from topology through dependency/state representation to a **specific substantive development target**.
