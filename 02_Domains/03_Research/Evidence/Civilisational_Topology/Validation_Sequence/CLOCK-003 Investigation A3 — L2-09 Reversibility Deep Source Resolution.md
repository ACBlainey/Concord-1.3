## Question

Does Concord lack a reversibility/safe-experimentation mechanism, or is the function distributed across existing architecture?

## Source resolution

The earlier E1 search was too shallow. Deeper inspection identifies substantial canonical mechanisms.

Constitutional Emergency V2 contains:
- minimum constitutional deviation;
- purpose-specific temporary authority;
- automatic sunset;
- renewed justification for continuation;
- restoration of ordinary governance and rights;
- recovery and post-event review;
- explicit resistance to permanent emergency inheritance.

Continuity Protocol V2 contains:
- restoration and recovery;
- preservation of prior knowledge and failed work;
- succession and continuity;
- preservation of capability through change.

The E3 cold-storage refinement adds reversible demotion of information without destructive deletion.

## Finding

Reversibility is not absent.

A strong distributed grammar exists:

**Provisional Change → Bounded Scope → Preserve Prior State/Provenance → Test/Observe → Review → Retain / Modify / Revert / Supersede.**

What is not yet demonstrated is one universal cross-domain “reversibility gate.” The evidence instead suggests that reversibility is legitimately domain-sensitive: emergency authority, research candidates, knowledge state, governance decisions and participant continuity have different rollback semantics.

Therefore forcing one universal mechanism may be incorrect.

### Revised classification

**L2-09: PRESENT-DISTRIBUTED / MECHANISMS DOMAIN-SPECIFIC / CROSS-DOMAIN INTEGRATION DEVELOPING.**

The previous candidate gap is downgraded from likely mechanism deficit to **INTEGRATION / REPRESENTATION GAP**.

For L3-15, irreversible deletion should use a consequence-sensitive interface to this distributed reversibility grammar rather than await invention of a universal reversibility authority.
