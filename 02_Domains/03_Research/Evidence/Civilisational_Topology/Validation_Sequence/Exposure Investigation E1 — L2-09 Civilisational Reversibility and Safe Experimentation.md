# 3. Irreversibility Classes

The exposure question becomes clearer if irreversible change is separated into classes.

## IR-1 Informational irreversibility

Examples include disclosure of private information, destruction of provenance, or loss of knowledge.

Once information is disclosed, simple rollback may be impossible.

**Potential consequence: HIGH.**

Existing relevant owners include bounded information/privacy, KCS and continuity.

## IR-2 Authority/institutional lock-in

A temporary mechanism can become difficult to remove through capture, dependency or accumulated legitimacy.

**Potential consequence: HIGH.**

Existing relevant owners include bounded authority, emergency limits, recursive oversight and anti-capture.

## IR-3 Infrastructure/resource irreversibility

Resource depletion, physical construction, environmental change or destruction may not be cheaply reversible.

**Potential consequence: HIGH to CRITICAL depending on context.**

Existing relevant owners include stewardship, sufficiency and continuity architecture.

## IR-4 Identity/rights-state irreversibility

Changes to membership, standing, rights recognition, succession or exclusion may create durable consequences even if formally reversed later.

**Potential consequence: HIGH.**

Relevant owners include participation, constitutional rights, identity/membership and intercivilisational architecture.

## IR-5 Knowledge/model lock-in

A civil self-model, metric or classification can become path-dependent and suppress alternatives.

**Potential consequence: MODERATE to HIGH.**

Relevant owners include epistemic pluralism, KCS, contestability and CDT self-model correction.

## IR-6 Developmental/experimental lock-in

A candidate system may create dependencies before its adequacy is established.

**Potential consequence: context-dependent, potentially HIGH.**

Relevant owners include Active Development, provenance, evaluation and Clock sequencing.

## IR-7 Civilisational fork/trajectory irreversibility

Some changes may alter long-term identity, coordination or external relationships in ways that cannot simply be restored.

**Potential consequence: potentially CRITICAL.**

Relevant owners include identity, fork/succession, continuity and option preservation.
