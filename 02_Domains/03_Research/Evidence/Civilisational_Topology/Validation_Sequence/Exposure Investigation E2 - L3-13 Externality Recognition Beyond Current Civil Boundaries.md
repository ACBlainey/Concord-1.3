## Purpose

Civilisation Clock Queue Test 001 selected L3-13 for exposure investigation before development.

The question is whether Concord already possesses sufficient architecture to recognise materially affected parties and harms beyond its current civil boundaries, or whether the concept exists while the mechanism remains undeveloped.

## 1. Source Resolution

The strongest direct canonical evidence is the structure of the Intercivilisational Architecture.

Its root README explicitly assigns disputes, jurisdiction, responsibility, shared resources, external harms and dispute resolution to:

**07_Disputes_Jurisdiction_and_Externalities**

The designated folder itself currently contains only a development placeholder with status:

**UNDEVELOPED / NOT CANONICAL.**

This is strong evidence because Concord itself identifies the required function, allocates a domain for it, and records that the domain has not yet been developed.

## 2. Adjacent Existing Architecture

The surrounding architecture is not empty. Existing material supplies:

- civilisational wrappers;
- interoperability without forced internal uniformity;
- peaceful distance;
- rights translation and recognition;
- emergent-polity recognition;
- hostile and low-trust interoperability;
- functionally bounded summits;
- non-inference between technical interoperability and political authority;
- bounded scope expansion where genuine dependencies exist.

Emergent-polity work explicitly asks whether a polity can manage consequences extending beyond its claimed domain.

Functionally Bounded Summits identifies shared environmental hazards, water, infrastructure, disease, humanitarian transit and digital incidents as cross-boundary functional problems.

Therefore the civilisational grammar already recognises that consequences cross political and civil boundaries.

## 3. Concept Versus Mechanism

**Conceptual recognition: PRESENT.**

Concord recognises external harms, shared resources, cross-boundary consequences, jurisdictional disputes and responsibilities extending beyond claimed domains.

**Operational mechanism: UNDEVELOPED / RESERVED.**

No developed canonical mechanism was found in the directly designated owner domain for:

1. detecting a material externality;
2. identifying affected external parties;
3. establishing standing;
4. tracing causal responsibility;
5. distinguishing direct from indirect effects;
6. resolving overlapping jurisdiction;
7. handling uncertainty;
8. selecting legitimate mitigation or repair channels;
9. preventing externality claims from manufacturing unlimited jurisdiction;
10. propagating correction across civil boundaries.

## 4. Exposure Classes

Relevant classes include environmental/resource, infrastructure, information/digital, economic/coordination, rights/personhood, security/emergency and civilisational-development externalities.

Potential consequence ranges from moderate to critical depending on context.

## 5. Current Exposure

**Architectural exposure: YES — DEFINITE.**

The Intercivilisational Architecture explicitly reserves this problem domain.

**Verified current deployed externality event: NOT ESTABLISHED.**

The current Concord is primarily a framework and research architecture rather than an operating sovereign civilisation executing cross-boundary policy.

No emergency exposure override is therefore justified by this audit.

## 6. Gap Classification

Best classification:

**DEVELOPMENTAL GAP — EXPLICITLY RESERVED / MECHANISM UNDEVELOPED.**

This is not a true architectural gap because the function has already been identified and allocated a development domain.

It is not merely an integration gap because the designated mechanism itself has not yet been developed.

## 7. Development Consequence

L3-13 is a strong future substantive development candidate because:

- the need is explicit;
- the owner/domain is explicit;
- the mechanism is explicitly undeveloped;
- adjacent architecture already supplies constraints;
- development can occur without inventing a new civilisational domain.

Potential consequence is high, but current active exposure is not verified. It therefore does not bypass the Clock.

## 8. Minimum Necessary Development

The appropriate development belongs inside the existing Disputes, Jurisdiction and Externalities domain.

A minimum mechanism would need to represent:

**Action/Event → Potential Cross-Boundary Consequence → Affected External Parties/Systems → Standing → Evidence/Causal Confidence → Materiality → Jurisdiction/Responsibility → Mitigation/Repair → Legitimate Coordination Channel → Review/Correction/Provenance**

## 9. Anti-Expansion Constraint

Externality recognition must not create universal jurisdiction.

**External Consequence ≠ Unlimited External Authority.**

**Recognition of Harm ≠ Automatic Jurisdiction.**

**Need for Coordination ≠ Right to Govern the Other Party.**

These constraints follow directly from the wrapper architecture.

## 12. Mechanism-Maturity Distinction

This investigation reinforces an important distinction also raised by the L2-09 reversibility investigation:

**Concept Present ≠ Mechanism Present ≠ Mechanism Tested ≠ Mechanism Integrated.**

A civilisational function can appear throughout Concord as a principle or constraint while its reusable operational mechanism remains absent or immature.

Future State Map refinement should represent these states explicitly rather than treating conceptual presence as equivalent to operational maturity.

# Conclusion

L3-13 is a clear example of a required civilisational function whose concept and architectural position exist but whose mechanism remains undeveloped.

The designated canonical domain explicitly reserves disputes, jurisdiction and externalities while marking itself undeveloped.

The correct future response is development inside the already identified owner domain, constrained by non-sovereignty, standing, evidence, bounded jurisdiction, repair and peaceful interoperability.

No current exposure justifies bypassing the Clock, but L3-13 is now a well-supported substantive candidate for the next development cycle.
