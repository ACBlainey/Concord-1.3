## Purpose

Civilisation Clock Queue Test 001 selected L3-15 for source resolution and exposure assessment.

The question is not whether Concord contains memory architecture. It clearly does. The question is whether Concord has developed the harder combined function: what must civilisation preserve across deep time, what may or should it forget, who may decide, under what constraints, and how can forgetting occur without destroying provenance, rights, recovery capacity or constitutional learning?

This investigation applies the mechanism-maturity distinction established during E1 and E2:

**Concept Present ≠ Mechanism Present ≠ Mechanism Tested ≠ Mechanism Integrated.**

## 1. Source Resolution

The canonical 03_Continuity_and_Memory domain contains substantial mature material, including the Continuity Protocol V2, KCS Knowledge Control System, Legacy Ladder, provenance architecture, continuity experiments, legacy-technology continuity, and the KCS operational dependency companion and dataset.

The Constitutional Core additionally contains recursive constitutional architecture and associated review/correction systems.

This is therefore not an undeveloped owner domain comparable to L3-13.

## 2. Preservation Side

The preservation side of L3-15 is strongly developed.

The Continuity Protocol treats civilisational memory as constitutional infrastructure and explicitly addresses knowledge preservation, institutional continuity, constitutional succession, long-term stewardship, preservation across generations, restoration and resilience, preservation without stagnation, and recursive continuity.

KCS adds provenance, epistemic status, archived knowledge, superseded knowledge, rejected knowledge, integrity-compromised material, reasoning pathways and correction history.

The Legacy Ladder adds preservation not merely of records but of reproducible capability, dependencies, skills, tools and technological lineages.

Therefore:

**DEEP-TIME MEMORY / PRESERVATION: PRESENT AND SUBSTANTIALLY DEVELOPED.**

## 3. Forgetting Side

The difficult residual is selective forgetting.

KCS explicitly avoids silently converting previously believed but subsequently rejected material into material that appears never to have existed. That is a strong anti-erasure rule.

The Continuity Protocol likewise strongly favours non-loss.

These are valuable safeguards, but they create a second-order problem:

**If preservation is constitutionally favoured, under what conditions is deliberate forgetting legitimate?**

Potential legitimate reasons include personal privacy, expiration of unnecessary personal records, resource constraints, security, protection from perpetual punishment, obsolete duplication, autonomy, and removal of corrupted active state while retaining evidential provenance.

No complete deep-time forgetting mechanism was verified in the source-resolved material inspected for this investigation.

## 4. Memory Is Not Retention of Everything

A civilisation that remembers nothing cannot learn. A civilisation that remembers everything without distinction may create different harms: permanent surveillance, inability to escape historical classification, indefinite personal-data retention, accumulated noise, obsolete rules retaining practical authority, dangerous knowledge remaining unnecessarily accessible, and historical grievance becoming permanently operational.

Therefore:

**Continuity ≠ Universal Permanent Retention.**

**Forgetting ≠ Destruction of History.**

The missing function concerns controlled transformation of memory state.

## 5. Candidate Memory-State Grammar

Existing KCS states already provide much of the basis: Active, Retrievable, Archived, Superseded, Rejected and Restricted-like states.

A deeper constitutional memory system may need to distinguish actively authoritative, actively informative, retrievable, archived, provenance-only, access-restricted, anonymised, sealed, intentionally expired, constitutionally destroyed, irrecoverably lost, and unknown-preservation states.

These are candidate distinctions, not adopted architecture.

## 6. Exposure Classes

Relevant exposures include permanent personal-memory exposure, constitutional amnesia, obsolete-authority persistence, dangerous-knowledge persistence, provenance destruction, cultural/historical erasure and accumulation overload.

Potential consequence ranges from moderate to critical depending on context.

This is not a true architectural absence. It is a developmental mechanism gap inside an already mature continuity system.

## 9. Minimum Necessary Development

Do not create a central memory authority.

The legitimate owners already exist: continuity, KCS, privacy/data rights, constitutional review and relevant domain owners.

The minimum future mechanism is a bounded Retention / Forgetting Decision Interface capable of asking what is changing retention state, what function continued retention serves, who is affected, what provenance must survive, whether anonymisation/sealing/archive demotion is sufficient, what harms follow from retention or deletion, who has authority, whether the decision is reviewable and reversible, and what future participants must know about the fact that forgetting occurred.
