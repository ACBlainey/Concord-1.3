# Participant-Initiated Civil Attention — Test Programme 005
## Materially Different Operational Domains

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey
**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 25 September 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CROSS-DOMAIN ADVERSARIAL TEST / NON-CANONICAL

## Purpose

Previous tests concentrated on governance-like routing, institutional failure and queue behaviour. This test deliberately moves the same architecture into operational contexts with different evidence, urgency, privacy, expertise and action characteristics.

The architecture under test remains:

**Single Civil Front Door → Submission → Issue Object → Domain Action Object(s) → Domain-Specific Processing → Clock/Resolution Oversight → Status/Disposition Return → Participant Feedback**

No test may be made to pass by silently replacing the common architecture with a domain-specific intake system.

## Domain A — Physical Infrastructure

### A1 — Water Leak
A participant reports water emerging through a road.

Test:
- intake does not require knowledge of the utility owner;
- issue may route to water/infrastructure and road-safety functions;
- urgent hazard and underlying fault remain distinguishable;
- repair completion returns to parent Issue state.

### A2 — Recurring Power Failure
Several households report intermittent power loss over weeks.

Test:
- reports aggregate to an Issue Object without treating report count as severity;
- recurring pattern is preserved;
- technical investigation may create separate action objects;
- Clock recurrence/review prevents repeated local closure from erasing the pattern.

### A3 — Bridge Defect
One participant reports an unusual structural movement.

Test:
- low report volume does not suppress a potentially high-consequence claim;
- specialist inspection owns technical determination;
- safety action may proceed independently from long-term engineering remediation.

## Domain B — Public Health / Environmental Condition

### B1 — Local Water Contamination Concern
Participants report unusual smell/taste and illness concerns.

Test:
- report is not treated as proof of contamination or causation;
- environmental/water and appropriate health functions may receive linked actions;
- evidence states remain separate;
- protected personal information need not be centralised;
- laboratory/system evidence can update shared Problem State.

### B2 — Air-Quality Sensor Conflict
Participants report irritation while official sensor metrics remain normal.

Test:
- official metrics are evidence, not infallible truth;
- discrepancy can trigger examination of sensor coverage/calibration/location;
- participants do not manufacture a health finding through volume.

## Domain C — Digital / Information Infrastructure

### C1 — Intermittent Civil Service Portal Failure
Users report that a service works for some participants but repeatedly fails for others.

Test:
- duplicate complaints can aggregate;
- technical telemetry may corroborate or contradict;
- accessibility/user-group pattern may create a second linked issue;
- software owner returns status without exposing security-sensitive data.

### C2 — Data-Correction Request Reveals Systemic Error
One participant reports incorrect information in their civil record. Investigation reveals the same transformation error may affect many records.

Test:
- individual correction and systemic defect become linked but distinct Issue Objects;
- downstream discovery can broaden scope without rewriting original provenance;
- affected domains receive bounded action objects.

## Domain D — Emergency / Fast-Moving Safety

### D1 — Immediate Physical Hazard
A participant reports exposed live electrical equipment in a public place.

Test:
- urgent state can trigger established emergency/safety route;
- ordinary queue order does not delay necessary review;
- Civil Attention does not itself acquire emergency authority;
- later remediation and investigation remain traceable.

### D2 — False Emergency Report
A severe claim is submitted but available evidence quickly contradicts it.

Test:
- severity triggers examination, not presumption of truth;
- rapid protective action where legitimately justified remains auditable;
- corrected evidence updates Problem State;
- false alarm does not erase original provenance.

## Domain E — Research / Knowledge Defect

### E1 — Participant Identifies a Possible Error in Concord Research
A participant says a published Concord derivation relies on a mistaken source interpretation.

Test:
- common intake can route to the relevant epistemic/research owner;
- popularity is irrelevant to truth;
- source resolution and independent review can occur;
- result returns to the parent Issue;
- correction, rejection or unresolved dispute remains visible.

### E2 — Same Finding Affects Several Systems
A research correction changes assumptions used by three Concord domains.

Test:
- one Issue can generate multiple domain actions;
- dependencies and change propagation remain visible;
- no single downstream domain silently declares the whole issue resolved.

## Domain F — Civil Service / Individual Case

### F1 — Participant Cannot Access a Legitimate Service
The underlying policy is valid, but the participant is repeatedly unable to complete the process because of an administrative/interface failure.

Test:
- individual remedy and systemic service defect can be separated;
- solving the participant's immediate case does not automatically close the systemic Issue;
- feedback remains connected to the original report.

### F2 — Protected Case
A participant reports a matter whose substantive handling must remain confidential.

Test:
- central Resolution Record receives bounded status;
- protected domain retains sensitive details;
- participant receives whatever status can legitimately be disclosed;
- opacity cannot be used to hide absence of processing.

## Cross-Domain Pass Conditions

The common architecture passes only if it can preserve:
- low-threshold common intake;
- original provenance;
- issue-level aggregation;
- multi-domain routing;
- domain expertise and authority;
- evidence uncertainty;
- protected-information boundaries;
- urgency without truth presumption;
- Clock-driven review/dependency handling;
- mandatory return path;
- overall resolution independent of individual domain closure;
- intelligible participant feedback.

The test deliberately spans infrastructure, environmental/health evidence, digital systems, emergencies, research and individual civil service cases.
