# 1. Purpose

The first four cases of the Ethical Kernel Counterfactual Generativity Experiment suggest that the Concord's development cannot be adequately described as a simple deductive expansion from root ethics into an ever-larger ethical topology.

This observation is consistent with two earlier sketches by Alexander C. Blainey that predate the present V1.2a experiment:

1. a sketch concerning **ideas, sketches and participant petitions as inputs into Concord development**; and
2. a sketch concerning **“the map is not the land,” provenance, successive reinterpretation and the importance of returning to source material and fundamental derivation**.

Because those sketches predate the present experimental finding, they are relevant provenance. They indicate that important components of the developmental model were independently articulated before the EKC-01–04 results made the pattern experimentally salient.

This document records the resulting hypothesis prospectively before EKC-05.

It does not alter the frozen U1–U8 kernel, the experiment protocol, the frozen sample, or any completed EKC result.

# 2. Earlier Sketch A — External Problem and Idea Injection

The earlier sketches-and-petitions note states in substance that the Concord did not grow solely from the ethical kernel.

Ideas were also introduced through sketches and discussion.

Those ideas nevertheless passed through the ethical kernel to maintain constitutional conformity.

The sketch further proposed an analogous route for participant petitions or suggestions:

- a participant may identify a problem, issue or tension;
- a proposed solution may accompany it;
- if no solution is supplied, candidate solutions may be developed;
- proposed solutions are evaluated for constitutional conformity;
- clearly incompatible solutions are rejected;
- compatible proposals may become candidate ideas for further development.

The ethical kernel therefore has at least two possible developmental roles:

1. **Generator** — producing constraints, functions or candidate responses from root principles.
2. **Filter** — evaluating ideas generated by observation, participants, experimentation or other external problem discovery.

This distinction is important.

A small ethical kernel need not independently enumerate every future civilisational problem in order to constrain how those problems may legitimately be solved.

# 6. Five Distinct Developmental Processes

The current evidence suggests at least five analytically distinct processes.

## D1 — Root-Ethical Generativity

Root ethics combine to generate:

- constraints;
- legitimate functions;
- protected relationships;
- prohibitions;
- obligations;
- and broad architectural requirements.

This is the process most directly tested by the U1–U8 experiment.

## D2 — Exogenous Problem and Idea Injection

New developmental material can enter because:

- reality presents an unanticipated problem;
- a participant identifies a tension;
- a sketch proposes an idea;
- an experiment fails;
- technology changes;
- a new substrate appears;
- an environmental condition changes;
- an external civilisation creates a novel interaction;
- or a participant proposes a candidate solution.

These inputs need not themselves be derivable as predictions from the kernel.

They can still be constitutionally evaluated by it.

## D3 — Architecture Selection and Engineering

Ethical requirements usually leave multiple permissible implementations.

Further work must determine:

- mechanism;
- institutional form;
- technical design;
- allocation of responsibility;
- interfaces;
- thresholds;
- procedures;
- and implementation.

This stage depends upon empirical and engineering information in addition to ethics.

## D4 — Failure-Driven Recursive Development

Once an architecture exists, it can reveal failures that were not obvious from the original ethical derivation.

The cycle becomes:

[
Architecture
ightarrow

ightarrow

ightarrow

ightarrow

]

EKC-03 provided particularly strong evidence for this process.

## D5 — Foundational Regeneration and Drift Correction

Mature architecture can accumulate:

- assumptions;
- inherited mechanisms;
- complexity;
- historical contingency;
- safeguard burden;
- and conceptual drift.

A fresh derivation from:

- the original problem;
- the root ethics;
- and current relevant facts

can therefore function as an anti-drift test.

V1.2a itself is an example of this process.

# 7. Generator Versus Filter

One of the most important distinctions produced by this synthesis is that an ethical kernel need not be only a generative engine.

It may also function as a **constitutional filter**.

These roles should not be conflated.

## Generative Role

Question:

> What follows from these roots when applied to this problem?

This is the primary V1.2a counterfactual question.

## Filtering Role

Question:

> Given this externally generated idea or candidate solution, is it compatible with the ethical constraints, and if so what modifications are required?

This is the process anticipated by the earlier sketches-and-petitions note.

The two processes can interact recursively.

An externally injected idea may survive ethical filtering and then create new problems whose solutions are subsequently generated from the roots.

Likewise, root-derived architecture may create an implementation problem solved by an externally discovered technical mechanism.

# 10. Stored Failure Knowledge

A significant portion of mature Concord complexity may therefore be interpretable as:

> **stored failure knowledge**

A safeguard encodes information about a failure that the civilisation has learned to anticipate.

A meta-safeguard encodes information about how an earlier safeguard can itself fail.

Provenance records why the safeguard exists.

Removing a mechanism without preserving that provenance may therefore recreate a previously solved failure.

This gives provenance a functional resilience role beyond historical documentation.

# 12. Foundational Re-Derivation as Anti-Drift Mechanism

V1.2a suggests a possible formal anti-drift procedure.

Periodically:

1. freeze the mature architecture;
2. restate the underlying problem neutrally;
3. return to the foundational kernel;
4. derive a fresh response without consulting the mature solution;
5. freeze the new derivation;
6. compare it with current architecture;
7. identify:
   - convergence;
   - historical contingency;
   - missing safeguards;
   - accumulated complexity;
   - possible drift;
   - newly exposed blind spots;
8. retain, revise, prune or investigate accordingly.

This does not assume that the fresh derivation is superior.

Mature architecture may contain indispensable failure knowledge absent from the fresh derivation.

Conversely, the fresh derivation may expose assumptions or failure modes that mature architecture has normalised.

The value lies in the **comparison**.

# 15. Falsifiable Implications

This model generates several testable expectations.

## P-D1

Later EKC cases should continue to recover substantial functional architecture from U1–U8 while failing to reproduce all mature V1.2 mechanisms.

## P-D2

Some V1.2 mechanisms should be traceable to specific recognised failure modes rather than direct root-level derivation.

## P-D3

Fresh U1–U8 derivations should occasionally identify plausible failure modes or constraints not prominent in mature V1.2 comparison material.

## P-D4

The greatest divergence should occur in domains strongly dependent upon contingent environment, engineering, technology or historical failure exposure.

## P-D5

If a first-pass U-derived architecture is exposed to repeated adversarial development, it should acquire additional safeguards.

## P-D6

Those later safeguards need not duplicate V1.2 mechanisms, but may converge on similar resilience functions.

## P-D7

Some mature V1.2 safeguards may prove unnecessary, historically contingent or excessively complex when re-examined through fresh derivation and failure testing.

## P-D8

Loss of provenance should make it harder to distinguish unnecessary complexity from stored failure knowledge.

These predictions should be tested rather than assumed.

# 16. Alternative Explanations

Several alternatives remain possible.

### A. Familiarity contamination

The apparent regeneration may partly reflect prior knowledge of Concord architecture.

### B. Generic systems reasoning

Many recovered functions may arise from competent systems reasoning rather than U1–U8 specifically.

### C. Selection effects

The chosen cases may disproportionately favour areas where convergence is expected.

### D. Retrospective narrative fitting

The developmental model may impose coherence on a historically messier process.

### E. Corpus incompleteness

The mature V1.2 corpus may not preserve every historical reason a mechanism was introduced.

### F. Redundant complexity

Some apparent stored failure knowledge may simply be unnecessary accumulated complexity.

These alternatives are reasons to continue the experiment rather than treat the model as established fact.

# 19. Provisional Conclusion

The current evidence suggests that the Concord is better understood not as a static ethical tree but as a **recursive ethically constrained learning architecture**.

Its growth appears to involve:

> **a small ethical foundation constraining an open stream of problems, ideas, experiments, failures and corrections.**

Root ethics provide orientation and limits.

Reality supplies novelty.

Participants and reasoning processes generate candidate responses.

Failure reveals missing dimensions.

Safeguards encode lessons.

Further failures expose weaknesses in those safeguards.

Provenance preserves why the architecture changed.

Fresh foundational derivation provides a possible mechanism for detecting drift and accumulated blind spots.

The resulting architecture may therefore be large not because the ethical kernel contains millions of words of hidden deduction, but because:

> **civilisational reality continuously supplies new things for a stable ethical foundation to evaluate, constrain, learn from and adapt to.**

That proposition is now registered prospectively before EKC-05.
