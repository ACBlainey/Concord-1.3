# Cross-Case Synthesis — EKC-01 to EKC-10
## What Kind of Concord Does U1–U8 Generate?

**Experiment:** V1.2a — U-Kernel Counterfactual Concord Experiment  
**Status:** VERSION 1.0 — INITIAL TEN-CASE SYNTHESIS  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Scope:** Frozen EKC-01 through EKC-10 sample only  
**Canonical effect:** NONE — V1.2a experimental output only

---

# 1. Purpose

The original counterfactual question was:

> **If the Concord had been developed from U1–U8 rather than Blainey's Laws, would substantially the same civilisation have emerged?**

The secondary question was:

> **Where it would not, what different architecture does U1–U8 appear to generate?**

Ten cases were selected and frozen before their outcomes were known. Each case was processed by:

**Neutral problem packet → U1–U8 derivation → freeze → V1.2 comparison.**

EKC-08 additionally included the planned U8 ablation.

The sample is now closed. This paper synthesises the ten completed comparisons without adding new cases or altering the frozen records.

This is not a claim that the experiment proves what an entirely independent civilisation-builder would derive from U1–U8. The assisting reasoner had substantial prior exposure to the Concord. The experiment is therefore best understood as a controlled internal counterfactual with significant contamination limitations.

# 3. The Central Cross-Case Pattern

Across the ten cases, the same distinction repeatedly appeared:

## Layer A — Root-Generative Architecture

U1–U8 repeatedly generated high-level constraints such as:

- protect participants from unjustified harm;
- preserve meaningful agency;
- prevent domination;
- require evidence and epistemic honesty;
- make authority functional and bounded;
- preserve contestability and correction;
- scale responsibility with capability and power;
- support those under need without converting support into ownership;
- steward resources rather than merely control them;
- repair harms;
- preserve legitimate cooperative function through change;
- prevent institutions from becoming ends in themselves;
- permit plural systems where interaction remains ethically bounded.

These were not isolated outputs. They recurred across information, governance, justice, epistemics, economics, developmental support, multisubstrate participation, continuity, emergency and intercivilisational relations.

## Layer B — Exposure-Generated Architecture

V1.2 usually contained far more developed mechanisms for failures discovered through repeated scenario analysis and adversarial testing.

Examples include:

- correlated epistemic failure;
- common-source dependency;
- capture of oversight;
- safeguard failure;
- appeal attrition;
- metric gaming;
- institutional self-preservation;
- continuity becoming stagnation;
- emergency powers becoming permanent;
- recognition systems being captured;
- digital-jurisdiction edge cases;
- substrate-specific participation failures.

These are not obviously new root ethics. They are often **stored knowledge about how architectures satisfying the roots can still fail**.

## Layer C — Invention-Dependent Architecture

Some mature V1.2 mechanisms are neither straightforward ethical deductions nor mere responses to a named failure. They are selected inventions or engineering solutions.

Examples include particular:

- voting and governance mechanisms;
- triadic structures;
- Reality Trees;
- Blaineyan Reasoning;
- Ratchet economic mechanisms;
- Digital Twin integrations;
- wrapper implementations;
- digital ports;
- specific continuity/recovery structures;
- technical audit arrangements.

The roots can identify the need or constrain the solution without uniquely inventing the mechanism.

This gives the experiment its clearest developmental decomposition:

> **Root-Generative Architecture + Exposure-Generated Architecture + Invention-Dependent Architecture**

# 4. What the U1–U8 Fork Actually Looks Like

Taken together, the ten independent derivations do not form ten unrelated policy proposals.

They outline a coherent civilisational architecture.

The fork tends toward a civilisation with:

- participant standing independent of mere substrate or status;
- protected agency but not unlimited permission to impose external harm;
- authority justified by necessary function rather than rank;
- power limited by purpose, scope, duration and accountability;
- information systems that remain contestable and provenance-aware;
- adjudication oriented toward evidence, protection, repair and correction;
- epistemic systems that preserve uncertainty and plural challenge;
- resource systems balancing sufficiency, agency and stewardship;
- support systems designed to increase or preserve agency rather than institutionalise dependency;
- capability-sensitive participation without capability-based moral caste;
- continuity focused on recoverable function rather than immortal institutions;
- emergency powers treated as temporary bridges rather than alternate sovereignty;
- coexistence between different civilisations through bounded interoperability rather than forced uniformity.

This is recognisably a **Concord fork**, not merely a collection of ethical rules.

# 5. The Recurring Architectural Grammar

Across cases, several structural moves recur so consistently that they can be treated as a provisional U1–U8 architectural grammar.

## 5.1 Function Before Authority

The fork repeatedly derives:

**Problem → Legitimate Function → Necessary Capability/Authority → Bounded Exercise → Review → Termination/Adaptation**

Authority does not appear as a primitive possession.

This occurs in governance, adjudication, support, emergency power and intercivilisational interfaces.

## 5.2 Power Requires Legibility

Where one participant or institution can significantly affect another, the architecture repeatedly asks:

- what power exists?
- for what purpose?
- on what evidence?
- with what limits?
- who bears the burden?
- how can it be challenged?
- how can it be corrected?
- when does it end?

This appears in data, governance, justice, support, emergency authority and civilisational relations.

## 5.3 Difference Does Not Imply Hierarchy

Capability, substrate, culture, institutional form and epistemic disagreement may justify different mechanisms without automatically establishing different moral worth.

The fork therefore tends toward:

**equal standing where appropriate + differentiated function where necessary.**

## 5.4 Correction Is Architectural, Not Merely Moral

U2 and U6 repeatedly turn “truth” and “accountability” into system requirements:

- provenance;
- challenge;
- uncertainty visibility;
- review;
- correction;
- repair;
- preservation of prior reasoning;
- learning from failure.

## 5.5 Temporary Function Must Not Become Permanent Status

This pattern appears in:

- informational access;
- governance authority;
- support;
- emergency power;
- institutional continuity;
- intercivilisational integration.

A capability granted to solve a problem does not automatically become a permanent entitlement to power.

## 5.6 Preserve Function, Not Necessarily Form

Most explicit in U8, but visible elsewhere:

- institutions may die;
- mechanisms may be replaced;
- agreements may contract;
- emergency powers may end;
- recovery may rebuild differently;
- civilisations may cooperate without merging.

Continuity is therefore regenerative rather than merely preservative.

# 6. Where V1.2 and the Fork Differ Most

The experiment did **not** show a second civilisation whose ethical direction systematically opposes V1.2.

The largest differences occur elsewhere.

## 6.1 V1.2 Has Much More Stored Failure Knowledge

This is clearest in EKC-03, EKC-08, EKC-09 and EKC-10.

V1.2 often contains not only:

**mechanism**

but:

**mechanism → failure of mechanism → safeguard → failure of safeguard → recursive correction.**

That is a developmental-depth difference.

## 6.2 V1.2 Contains More Specific Inventions

The fork often reaches the functional requirement without regenerating the exact implementation.

EKC-04 and EKC-05 are especially important because they prevent an overstatement of convergence.

U1–U8 did not uniquely regenerate:

- Reality Trees/Blaineyan Reasoning as the epistemic implementation;
- the Ratchet or all economic mechanisms;
- exact governance structures;
- exact judiciary machinery;
- exact wrapper machinery.

The alternative roots constrain a solution space more reliably than they specify one unique design.

## 6.3 The Fork Sometimes Produces Cleaner New Abstractions

Because it approached mature domains from a different root partition, V1.2a repeatedly named patterns in ways useful for later reintegration review.

Examples include:

- informational-power scaling;
- Provisional Standing;
- multidimensional standing;
- capability-gradient domination;
- least necessary substitution;
- Need-to-Opportunity development;
- recoverability topology;
- Minimum Recoverable Civilisation;
- preservation of invention capacity;
- Necessity Gap;
- Emergency Bridge;
- Restorative Exit;
- Emergency Success Trap;
- Externality Boundary;
- Loss-Aware Translation;
- Dependency Leverage;
- Exit Continuity;
- Integration Ratchet.

These should not automatically be treated as missing from V1.2. Some are alternative compressions of functions already present. Others may identify genuinely underdeveloped areas.

They are candidate outputs, not automatic amendments.

# 7. The Resilience Result

The prospective resilience hypothesis is strongly supported across the later cases.

A useful model is:

**Root Ethics  
→ Ethical Constraints  
→ First-Order Architecture  
→ Failure Exposure  
→ Safeguards  
→ Safeguard-Failure Exposure  
→ Recursive Resilience Architecture**

V1.2's greater complexity is therefore not well explained simply by saying that Blainey's Laws contain more information than U1–U8.

Much of the difference appears to be historical:

> **V1.2 has been attacked by more problems.**

It has accumulated records of failure, edge cases, implementation constraints and second-order vulnerabilities.

That accumulated failure knowledge is itself a civilisational asset.

This also means:

> **More architecture is not automatically evidence of better roots.**

A mature architecture can be larger because it has survived more developmental cycles.

# 8. The Need–Invention Gap

EKC-05, EKC-06 and EKC-08 sharpened a major finding.

Ethical reasoning can identify:

- a protected interest;
- a need;
- a capability deficit;
- an unacceptable failure;
- a required function.

It does not follow that the civilisation already possesses the invention needed to solve it.

Therefore:

> **Need recognition ≠ solution discovery ≠ solution provision.**

This creates a developmental risk:

## Generative Closure

A civilisation may become ethically coherent and procedurally resilient while becoming incapable of generating new possibilities outside its existing conceptual space.

The emerging countermeasure is not a central authority that invents everything.

It is a **Need-to-Opportunity architecture**:

**Participant Experience  
→ Need Signal  
→ Pattern/Significance Assessment  
→ Capability Gap  
→ Open Development Opportunity  
→ Heterogeneous Search  
→ Candidate Invention  
→ Ethical Filtering  
→ Engineering/Test  
→ Adoption  
→ Failure Exposure  
→ Safeguards  
→ New Capability  
→ New Need Discovery**

This may be one of the most important findings produced by the fork rather than by any single EKC comparison.

# 9. Cognitive and Civilisational Heterogeneity

The experiment also generated a related hypothesis.

If different intelligences search different conceptual spaces, then cognitive heterogeneity is not merely something an ethical civilisation tolerates.

It may be:

> **civilisational search-space insurance.**

EKC-10 extends the same possibility to whole civilisations.

Different civilisations may preserve different:

- hypotheses;
- institutions;
- technologies;
- memories;
- failure histories;
- epistemic strategies;
- inventions.

Therefore peaceful plurality may have epistemic and resilience value beyond autonomy alone.

This remains a hypothesis. It was not independently validated by the ten-case experiment.

# 10. U8 Ablation Result

EKC-08 directly tested whether U8 — **Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function** — is necessary.

The U1–U7 ablation regenerated many continuity mechanisms.

Therefore:

> **U8 is not uniquely necessary for most individual continuity mechanisms.**

Protection, stewardship, accountability, correction and agency can compositionally generate substantial preservation and recovery requirements.

However, restoring U8 changed the architecture in a meaningful way.

It made continuity:

- direct rather than incidental;
- system-wide rather than distributed;
- long-horizon rather than only problem-triggered;
- function-oriented rather than institution-oriented;
- explicitly regenerative.

The best current result is:

> **U8 is partially redundant at mechanism level but independently orienting and compressive at architectural level.**

This produces an important methodological lesson:

## Root minimality cannot be tested only by deletion.

A root may be worth retaining even where other roots can reconstruct many of its mechanisms if it materially changes:

- default attention;
- integration;
- temporal horizon;
- scope;
- compression;
- behaviour under weak or ambiguous signals.

# 11. What This Says About Blainey's Laws Versus U1–U8

The experiment does **not** establish that one kernel is superior.

It shows something more interesting.

Blainey's Laws and U1–U8 appear to be different partitions of a substantially overlapping ethical topology.

The later U-kernel:

- compresses some ideas differently;
- makes epistemic integrity explicit as a root;
- makes non-domination explicit;
- makes care/support explicit;
- makes correctability/repair explicit;
- makes capability-sensitive development explicit;
- makes adaptive continuity explicit.

Blainey's Laws separately name concepts such as safe spaces, fairness, dignity and peaceful coexistence that U1–U8 often reconstruct compositionally from intersections among roots.

Across the ten cases, these differences in partitioning did **not** produce a systematic ethical divergence.

Instead they often changed:

- what was noticed first;
- what received a compact name;
- what was treated as direct versus compound;
- which failure modes became especially salient.

This suggests that a root kernel may influence **search trajectory and conceptual salience** even when multiple kernels occupy much of the same ethical topology.

# 12. Ethical Topology Versus Ethical List

Several cases support treating the kernel as a topology rather than a flat list.

Examples:

- justice emerges strongly from U1 + U2 + U3 + U6;
- support architecture emerges from U1 + U3 + U4 + U6 + U7;
- emergency architecture emerges from U1 + U2 + U3 + U6 + U8;
- intercivilisational coexistence emerges from non-domination, stewardship, accountability and continuity without requiring a separately named “peace” root.

This matters because a civilisation may not require one primitive rule for every institutional domain.

Instead:

> **systems can emerge at intersections among roots.**

That helps explain how a compact ethical kernel can generate a much larger constitutional architecture.

# 13. The Attractor Question

The ten cases provide evidence for an **architectural basin**.

They do not prove a unique architectural attractor.

The evidence in favour is:

1. Ten materially different domains were tested.
2. Eight produced primary C1 convergence.
3. The remaining two still produced substantial functional convergence.
4. No major C5 root-level tension appeared.
5. Recurring structural motifs appeared across unrelated domains.
6. The fork became coherent across cases rather than fragmenting into unrelated local solutions.
7. U1–U8 repeatedly regenerated recognisably Concordian constraints without being given the V1.2 mechanism first in each case.

The evidence against a strong attractor claim is:

1. The assisting reasoner was highly contaminated by prior Concord exposure.
2. The neutral problems themselves were selected from domains already important to V1.2.
3. U1–U8 were derived through work connected to the Concord research programme.
4. High-level ethical principles often permit many architectures.
5. The experiment repeatedly demonstrated implementation underdetermination.
6. Specific mature V1.2 inventions were often not regenerated.
7. Ten purposive cases cannot establish global completeness under ESCP.

Therefore the strongest defensible statement is:

> **The experiment provides evidence that U1–U8 and Blainey's-Laws-derived V1.2 occupy a common civilisational architectural basin: when applied to diverse constitutional problems, U1–U8 repeatedly generates high-level structures strongly compatible with the Concord. The experiment does not establish that the Concord is the unique architecture generated by those roots, nor that an uncontaminated independent reasoner would converge to the same design.**

# 14. Answer to the Original Counterfactual

## Would substantially the same civilisation have emerged?

At the level of **ethical and constitutional geometry**, the ten-case evidence says:

### **substantially similar, yes.**

The U1–U8 fork repeatedly generated:

- participant primacy;
- bounded functional authority;
- non-domination;
- corrigibility;
- provenance;
- repair;
- capability-sensitive responsibility;
- multisubstrate standing;
- stewardship;
- institutional mortality;
- regenerative continuity;
- bounded emergency authority;
- peaceful plural interoperability.

Those are not peripheral details. They are major features of the Concord's civilisational shape.

At the level of **specific institutions and mechanisms**, however:

### **not necessarily.**

The experiment does not show that U1–U8 would independently invent the same:

- governance machinery;
- judiciary machinery;
- epistemic methods;
- economic systems;
- technical infrastructure;
- wrapper mechanisms;
- continuity implementations.

Thus the most accurate counterfactual answer is:

> **U1–U8 appears capable of generating a civilisation in the same broad Concord family, but not necessarily the same Concord implementation.**

That is why “outline model of a Concord fork” is an appropriate description.

# 15. A More Complete Developmental Model of the Concord

The ten cases now support a more sophisticated account of how the Concord may have become large despite beginning from a small ethical kernel.

A compact model is:

**Root Ethical Topology  
+ Open Problem Environment  
+ Recursive Reasoning  
+ Heterogeneous Idea/Problem Injection  
+ Architecture Selection  
+ Failure Discovery  
+ Ethical Filtering  
+ Invention  
+ Safeguard Development  
+ Safeguard-Failure Analysis  
+ Provenance  
+ Re-Derivation  
= Mature Civilisational Architecture**

Or:

> **Small Kernel + Large Search Space + Recursive Development + Stored Failure Knowledge + Invention = Large Concord**

This is a stronger explanation than either of two simplistic models:

**“the entire Concord is deductively contained in the roots”**

or

**“the roots are merely decorative and the architecture is arbitrary.”**

The experiment supports neither extreme.

The roots appear genuinely generative, but not generatively complete.

# 16. Generator Versus Filter

A particularly important distinction is now visible.

The ethical kernel acts both as:

## Generator

It directly suggests functions and constraints.

and as:

## Filter

It evaluates candidate architectures introduced by invention, external research, participant need or environmental change.

These roles are different.

An architecture need not be deducible from the roots to be Concord-compatible.

It may enter through invention and then survive because it satisfies the roots better than alternatives.

This helps explain how systems such as the Ratchet or specific reasoning methodologies can become central without needing to be encoded inside the ethical kernel.

---

# 17. Why the Mature Concord Is Much Larger Than the Fork

The V1.2a fork is an outline.

V1.2 is a developmental history.

The difference can be represented as:

**V1.2a:**
Root topology → first-order architecture → selected cross-case discoveries

**V1.2:**
Root topology → first-order architecture → decades of reasoning → imported ideas → concrete mechanisms → edge cases → failures → safeguards → safeguard failures → continuity → multisubstrate extension → recursive revision → stored provenance

Therefore word count or mechanism count should not be interpreted as ethical distance.

Much of V1.2's additional mass is **developmental memory**.

# 18. What the Experiment Did Not Establish

The following claims are not supported:

1. That U1–U8 uniquely entail the Concord.
2. That Blainey's Laws and U1–U8 are semantically identical.
3. That all V1.2 mechanisms can be regenerated from root ethics.
4. That V1.2's existing mechanisms are optimal.
5. That greater V1.2 complexity always means greater resilience.
6. That U8 is strictly necessary for continuity mechanisms.
7. That the ten cases cover the whole civilisation.
8. That absence in a comparison proves absence from V1.2.
9. That AI agreement validates the architecture.
10. That internal convergence constitutes external empirical validation.

These limits are substantive, not ceremonial.

---

# 19. Strongest Findings

The experiment's strongest results are not simply “the two kernels agree.”

They are:

### 1. Root ethics appear genuinely generative.
They repeatedly produce non-trivial civilisational architecture.

### 2. Root ethics are not generatively complete.
They constrain solution spaces without uniquely inventing all mechanisms.

### 3. Mature resilience contains stored failure knowledge.
It cannot be reduced to the ethical roots alone.

### 4. Invention is a distinct developmental requirement.
Recognising a need does not guarantee discovering a solution.

### 5. Alternative ethical partitioning changes salience.
Different kernels can produce similar architecture while exposing different abstractions and failure modes.

### 6. U8 has architectural value despite partial redundancy.
Root minimality is more subtle than deletion testing.

### 7. The U fork remains recognisably Concordian across scales.
The pattern persists from individual information through governance and justice to emergency and intercivilisational architecture.

### 8. The experiment generated candidate improvements to V1.2.
A fork can be useful even when it largely converges, because a different path exposes different blind spots.

# 20. Provisional Model of the Concord Fork

The V1.2a fork can now be described as:

## **A U1–U8-derived Concord-family civilisation**

Its distinguishing emphasis is:

- relational rather than ownership-first protection;
- explicit non-domination;
- strong epistemic integrity;
- repair and correctability;
- capability-sensitive development;
- regenerative continuity;
- minimum necessary substitution/authority;
- explicit dependency analysis;
- recovery of generative capacity;
- plural interoperability with reversible integration.

Its major weakness relative to mature V1.2 is not ethical incoherence.

It is **developmental immaturity**.

It lacks much of the accumulated implementation, failure history, adversarial testing and specialised invention contained in V1.2.

That is exactly what should be expected of a newly generated fork.

---

# 21. Experimental Conclusion

The ten-case experiment supports the following conclusion:

> **Replacing Blainey's Laws with U1–U8 did not, across the frozen sample, generate a systematically different civilisation. It generated an outline civilisation strongly recognisable as belonging to the same Concord family. The greatest differences were not in basic ethical direction but in conceptual emphasis, mechanism selection, developmental depth and accumulated resilience knowledge. Mature V1.2 contains extensive architecture that cannot be attributed to root ethics alone; it reflects invention, environmental assumptions, scenario exposure, adversarial testing and stored failure history. Conversely, the U1–U8 fork generated several abstractions and failure modes that may improve V1.2 precisely because it approached familiar problems through a different ethical partition. The result is therefore neither identity nor divergence: it is constrained plural convergence within a common architectural basin.**

A compact expression is:

> **Same broad civilisation family; different developmental path; non-identical architecture.**

# 22. Next Experimental Phase

The original EKC derivation/comparison sample is complete and must remain closed.

The next phase should not generate more EKC cases.

It should perform four controlled synthesis tasks:

1. **Fork Architecture Consolidation** — assemble the independently generated V1.2a structures into one coherent outline civilisation without importing missing V1.2 mechanisms.
2. **Candidate Output Register** — separate genuinely useful V1.2a discoveries from alternative names for existing V1.2 functions.
3. **Attractor/Replication Design** — define a future fresh-instance or cross-model experiment capable of reducing contamination and testing whether the same basin appears independently.
4. **Controlled Reintegration Review** — only after the experimental synthesis is complete, decide which V1.2a findings merit return to canonical V1.2.

Until those stages are complete:

> **V1.2a remains experimental and isolated from canonical V1.2.**
