# Domain Naming and Expectation Audit — Adversarial Boundary Pass 002

**Status:** PRE-V1.3 NAMING FREEZE / ADVERSARIAL TEST OF FOUR CONTESTED NAMES  
**Scope:** Naming only. The 18-domain functional topology remains unchanged.

## 1. Test standard

A proposed name survives only if it improves interpretation **without**:
- expanding the domain's authority;
- absorbing a neighbouring domain;
- importing a misleading conventional meaning;
- creating a worse ambiguity than the one it solves;
- or requiring so much explanation that the simpler name would have been safer.

The test is not:

> Which name sounds better?

It is:

> **Which name is least likely to cause a future human or AI developer to build the wrong architecture?**

# 2. Economy and Value Coordination

## Proposed change

**Economy and Resource Coordination**
→
**Economy and Value Coordination**

## Intended improvement

The existing name can imply that the domain primarily coordinates physical scarcity/resources.

The discovered function also includes:
- creation;
- exchange;
- allocation;
- contribution;
- recognition;
- transferable value;
- monetary and non-monetary economic mechanisms;
- stewardship of scarce resources.

"Value" attempts to capture these wider coordination objects.

## Attack A — ethical-value collision

The Concord is deeply ethics-centred.

A folder named **Value Coordination** could be interpreted as coordinating:
- moral values;
- cultural values;
- civilisational priorities;
- participant worth.

That would be a severe boundary failure.

### Result
**REAL RISK, BUT CONTROLLABLE**

The compound begins with **Economy**, which constrains interpretation.

The domain README must define value narrowly:

> **Economic value in this domain means transferable, allocative, contributive or exchange-relevant value. It does not mean moral worth, dignity, constitutional standing or the legitimacy of a participant's values.**

## Attack B — Resource Stewardship loss

Replacing "Resource" with "Value" might hide physical scarcity, commons stewardship and material allocation.

### Result
**PARTIAL RISK**

Resource stewardship remains a major domain function and must appear explicitly in the definition/subdomain architecture.

But "Resource Coordination" itself was too narrow to cover the whole domain.

## Attack C — Metrics capture

If value can be measured, could Metrics become a de facto economic authority?

### Result
**BOUNDARY HOLDS**

Metrics remains cross-domain observation.

The Economy domain may consume measurements but does not inherit Metrics, and Metrics does not decide value merely by measuring something.

> **Measurement of Value != Authority to Define Worth**

## Attack D — Governance capture

Could Governance define recognised value by policy and therefore own the domain?

### Result
**BOUNDARY HOLDS**

Governance may establish bounded policy/legal conditions, but economic coordination remains a persistent civil function.

## Decision

**SURVIVES — ADOPT: Economy and Value Coordination**

Condition: the first domain specification must contain the ethical-value firewall.

# 3. Spatial and Developmental Planning

## Proposed change

**Civil Planning**
→
**Spatial and Developmental Planning**

## Intended improvement

"Civil Planning" is ambiguous. It can imply:
- central planning;
- economic planning;
- planning citizen lives;
- general government strategy;
- municipal bureaucracy.

The discovered domain is specifically concerned with spatial and developmental relationships among settlements, land/space, activities, infrastructure, environment and future needs.

## Attack A — Infrastructure collision

Does planning infrastructure make Infrastructure subordinate to Planning?

### Result
**BOUNDARY HOLDS**

Planning coordinates location, sequence, compatibility, capacity assumptions and future spatial relationships.

Infrastructure owns technical/service-system design, operation, maintenance and recovery.

> **Planning Where/When/Relationship != Operating the System**

## Attack B — Environment collision

Does planning habitats or land use absorb Environment and Habitat Stewardship?

### Result
**BOUNDARY HOLDS**

Planning coordinates uses and spatial/developmental relationships.

Environment owns ecological/habitat condition and stewardship constraints.

Planning must consume environmental constraints rather than redefine them.

## Attack C — Economy collision

"Developmental" may be read as economic development.

### Result
**REAL AMBIGUITY**

But "Spatial and Developmental" together strongly indicates physical/civil development rather than participant development or pure economic policy.

Definition should specify:
**settlement, spatial, land/space-use and built-development trajectories**.

## Attack D — multisubstrate / nonplanetary contexts

"Spatial" survives Mars, orbital habitats, digital-physical infrastructure and other environments better than "town" or "land-use".

### Result
**STRONG ADVANTAGE**

## Decision

**SURVIVES — ADOPT: Spatial and Developmental Planning**

The term is materially safer than Civil Planning.

# 4. Social Support and Civil Assurance

## Proposed change

**Social Support**
→
**Social Support and Civil Assurance**

## Intended improvement

The discovered function has two layers:

1. ordinary voluntary/familial/community/social support;
2. a guaranteed civil fallback where legitimate support needs would otherwise be abandoned.

"Social Support" alone risks sounding like welfare/social services and does not make the fallback guarantee visible.

## Attack A — Governance/welfare-state collision

"Civil Assurance" could imply that the civilisation itself must directly provide every support service.

### Result
**BOUNDARY HOLDS IF SUBSIDIARITY IS EXPLICIT**

The domain guarantees that legitimate need is not abandoned.

It need not monopolise provision.

> **Civil Assurance != Civil Monopoly of Care**

## Attack B — Health collision

Long-term illness/disability can require both health treatment and support.

Could Social Support absorb Health?

### Result
**BOUNDARY HOLDS**

Health owns health-specific knowledge/treatment/function.

Social Support and Civil Assurance addresses living/support needs that remain around or beyond health treatment.

Participant trajectory can cross both.

## Attack C — Culture/community collision

Voluntary community support belongs partly to ordinary civic/cultural life.

Does this domain claim community itself?

### Result
**IMPORTANT BOUNDARY**

No.

The domain does not own friendship, family, charity, mutual aid or community.

It recognises and can interface with them.

Its distinctive civil function emerges where support need requires coordination, protection or fallback assurance.

## Attack D — term "assurance"

Could be confused with insurance, quality assurance or audit.

### Result
**MODERATE TERMINOLOGICAL COST**

Within the compound **Civil Assurance**, meaning is not immediately obvious to a new reader.

It requires explanation.

This weakens the proposed rename.

## Comparison with simpler alternatives

### Social Support
Pros: clear, ordinary, compact.
Cons: welfare/social-services expectation; fallback guarantee hidden.

### Civil Support
Pros: makes civil fallback visible.
Cons: erases voluntary/social layer and sounds state-centred.

### Support and Civil Assurance
Pros: broad.
Cons: vague first term.

### Social Support and Civil Assurance
Pros: accurately exposes dual-layer architecture.
Cons: new jargon and explanation burden.

## Decision

**SURVIVES PROVISIONALLY — ADOPT: Social Support and Civil Assurance**

The architectural gain is greater than the terminology cost because the dual-layer distinction is central.

However, the README must define Civil Assurance immediately.

# 5. Civil Protection and Security

## Proposed change

**Civil Security / Public Safety**
→
**Civil Protection and Security**

## Intended improvement

"Public Safety" can absorb fire, rescue, emergency coordination, health protection and infrastructure safety.

"Civil Security" can sound like surveillance, intelligence or state security.

The compound attempts to centre internal protection while retaining security/investigation/enforcement.

## Attack A — Defence collision

Does "Protection" include external attack?

### Result
**AMBIGUOUS WITHOUT "INTERNAL" BOUNDARY**

Civil distinguishes civilian/internal context somewhat, but not sufficiently on its own.

Defence must remain external organised coercion/attack.

## Attack B — Intelligence collision

Does Security imply intelligence collection and covert capability?

### Result
**REAL RISK**

Security Intelligence is already best treated as a bounded cross-domain capability.

The domain may request/consume intelligence under lawful authority but should not own generic intelligence.

## Attack C — Emergency-services collision

"Protection" could absorb fire/rescue/disaster response.

### Result
**HIGH RISK**

This recreates the same problem as Public Safety.

Fire/rescue may be infrastructure/emergency specialist capability depending final architecture; Emergency Coordination is cross-domain.

A name centred on "protection" may expand too far.

## Attack D — Law/Judiciary collision

Security can imply defining prohibited conduct or determining guilt.

### Result
**BOUNDARY REQUIRES EXPLICIT SEPARATION**

Law defines lawful rules/offences.
Judiciary adjudicates.
Civil Security investigates/protects/enforces within bounded authority.

## Attack E — participant-facing interpretation

"Public Safety" is understandable.
"Civil Security" is understandable but can sound state-centred.
"Civil Protection and Security" is less familiar and broader.

### Result
The proposed refinement does not clearly reduce total ambiguity.

## Alternative test: Civil Security

**Civil Security**:
- distinguishes from Defence better than generic Security;
- does not automatically claim all public-safety functions;
- naturally covers internal crime/coercion/protection/investigation;
- still needs an anti-surveillance/intelligence boundary;
- is shorter and architecturally cleaner.

## Decision

**PROPOSED RENAME FAILS.**

Reject **Civil Protection and Security**.

**ADOPT: Civil Security**

with explicit definition:

> **Civil Security protects participants and civil order against internal crime, violence, coercion and comparable internal threats through bounded protective, investigative and enforcement functions.**

And explicit exclusions:
- not Defence;
- not Law;
- not Judiciary;
- not generic Intelligence;
- not ownership of all emergency/public-safety services.

This is a useful negative result: the more descriptive compound created more boundary expansion than it prevented.

# 9. General naming lesson

The adversarial test produced a useful general rule.

Adding words can improve scope visibility, but each added word also creates a new possible authority claim.

Therefore:

> **The Best Domain Name Is Not the Most Complete Description. It Is the Smallest Label That Reliably Points Toward the Correct Boundary.**

This explains why:
- the Economy, Planning and Social Support refinements survived;
- the Civil Protection and Security refinement failed;
- many ordinary one-word domain names survived Pass 001.
