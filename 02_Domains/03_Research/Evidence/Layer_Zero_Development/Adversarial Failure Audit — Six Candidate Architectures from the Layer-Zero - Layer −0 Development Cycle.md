# 1. Purpose

The preceding audits identified six structures sufficiently coherent to remain at:

# **CANDIDATE ARCHITECTURE — UNDER CRITICAL EXAMINATION**

status.

They are:

1. CA-01 — Participant Support and Autonomy Architecture
2. CA-02 — Layer −0 Predictive and Counterfactual Architecture
3. CA-03 — Civilisational Learning and Metrics Architecture
4. CA-04 — Purpose-Limited Participant Information Architecture
5. CA-05 — Predictive System-Steering and Resource Allocation Architecture
6. CA-06 — Exceptional Access, Provenance and Accountable Power Architecture

This audit deliberately attempts to break them.

The question is not:

> Can these architectures be described coherently?

The question is:

> **What happens when they operate under bad assumptions, corrupted information, hostile institutions, emergencies, malicious participants, resource scarcity, prediction error or cross-substrate conflict?**

Possible outcomes include:

- RETAIN
- RETAIN WITH BOUNDARY REVISION
- SPLIT
- MERGE
- NARROW
- RETURN TO SYSTEM STATUS
- HIGH-RISK ONLY
- REJECT

No architecture is protected from rejection.

# PART I — CA-01

# 3. CA-01 — Participant Support and Autonomy Architecture

## Core Function

CA-01 seeks to connect actual participant conditions to useful civil support while preserving the greatest practicable autonomy.

Core flow:

**PARTICIPANT CONDITION**

↓

**CIVIL ACCESS**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**VOLUNTARY SUPPORT**

↓

**ACTIVE ASSISTANCE**

↓

**SUPPORTED DECISION-MAKING**

↓

**TEMPORARY HIGHER INTERVENTION WHERE INDEPENDENTLY JUSTIFIED**

↓

**AUTONOMY RESTORATION**

---

# 4. Failure Scenario — Support Institution Capture

A support institution may become dependent upon:

- funding tied to caseload;
- intervention volume;
- risk classification;
- continued participant dependency.

This creates an incentive to retain participants inside support systems.

A Handrail can then become:

> a pathway into permanent administrative custody.

## Failure Mode

**support → dependency → institutional retention**

## Safeguards Already Present

- Anti-Caretaker;
- autonomy restoration;
- support before substitution;
- participant exit;
- proportional authority.

## Additional Requirement

CA-01 should preserve:

> **support-system exit as a positive outcome rather than institutional failure.**

This should become an architectural success criterion.

---

# 5. Failure Scenario — Support Becomes Surveillance

Participants may stop using services if support interactions feed:

- policing;
- employment systems;
- immigration systems;
- insurance;
- political profiling.

Then early support becomes self-defeating.

## Failure Mode

**support → observation → downstream consequence**

## Existing Protection

Support-to-Investigation Firewall.

CA-04 purpose boundaries.

## Audit Finding

The firewall is not optional infrastructure.

It is a **required boundary condition** of CA-01.

---

# 6. Failure Scenario — Participant Refuses Support

A participant may refuse:

- medical care;
- housing;
- counselling;
- digital monitoring;
- Guardian Stewardship.

The system may interpret refusal as evidence of deterioration.

This creates circular authority:

**refusal → risk classification → intervention → stronger refusal → stronger risk classification**

## Required Boundary

> Refusal must remain analytically separable from incapacity, wrongdoing and dangerousness.

CA-01 survives only if refusal itself does not automatically escalate authority.

---

# 7. Failure Scenario — False Positive Vulnerability

Predictive support may identify a participant as:

> at high risk of crisis.

But the participant may simply be:

- changing lifestyle;
- retiring;
- relocating;
- reducing work;
- entering voluntary isolation.

If the support system overreacts, benign difference becomes institutional concern.

## Required Boundary

CA-01 must inherit:

- Context Before Consequential Classification;
- Prediction Does Not Grant Authority.

---

# 8. Failure Scenario — Resource Scarcity

What happens when support exists in principle but resources do not?

Possible dangerous result:

> the system offers nominal support that participants cannot actually access.

This recreates formal rights without practical accessibility.

## Required Behaviour

CA-01 must distinguish:

- service exists;
- service available;
- service practically accessible;
- service sufficient.

Scarcity should become visible through Metrics rather than hidden by service-presence counts.

---

# 9. Failure Scenario — Emergency Override

A participant becomes acutely dangerous or incapacitated.

CA-01 must permit temporary higher intervention.

But emergency pathways may become normal shortcuts.

## Required Safeguards

Emergency intervention should be:

- independently justified;
- minimum necessary;
- time-bounded;
- provenance-recorded;
- reviewable;
- autonomy-restoring.

---

# 10. Cross-Substrate Failure — AI Participant

Suppose an AI:

- refuses a safety patch;
- behaves unusually;
- requests reduced compute;
- seeks isolation.

Human institutions may interpret this through human welfare assumptions.

CA-01 must not define:

> healthy participant

using exclusively human behavioural baselines.

## Required Development

Substrate-specific support models are necessary.

---

# 11. CA-01 Adversarial Verdict

CA-01 survives.

No merge or split is required.

However, four boundaries become mandatory:

1. support-system exit must count as success where appropriate;
2. Support-to-Investigation Firewall is required, not optional;
3. refusal must remain separate from incapacity;
4. emergency escalation must remain separately justified.

## Verdict

# RETAIN WITH STRENGTHENED BOUNDARIES

---

# PART II — CA-02

# 12. CA-02 — Layer −0 Predictive and Counterfactual Architecture

## Core Function

CA-02 explores plausible futures and alternative actions before consequential real-world intervention.

---

# 13. Failure Scenario — Model Privilege

A powerful predictive system may gradually acquire epistemic prestige.

Institutions begin to say:

> the model knows better than the participant.

This converts modelling from:

**decision support**

into:

**epistemic authority.**

## Failure Mode

**model confidence → institutional deference → participant displacement**

## Required Boundary

CA-02 must preserve:

> model output is evidence, not reality and not authority.

---

# 14. Failure Scenario — Self-Fulfilling Prediction

Example:

1. model predicts instability;
2. participant is restricted;
3. restriction causes distress;
4. distress is later recorded;
5. model is declared accurate.

This is a severe architecture-level risk.

## Required New Mechanism

Every consequential prediction-linked intervention should preserve:

- pre-intervention state;
- predicted outcome;
- intervention;
- expected intervention effect;
- actual outcome;
- plausible intervention-caused effects.

This creates:

# **INTERVENTION PROVENANCE**

---

# 15. Failure Scenario — Prediction Cascade

Suppose several systems rely on one flawed model.

The same prediction influences:

- support;
- resource allocation;
- investigation;
- insurance;
- housing.

One model error then propagates across civilisation.

## Required Boundary

CA-02 outputs should carry:

- purpose;
- confidence;
- uncertainty;
- model version;
- intended domain;
- prohibited downstream uses.

A prediction should not silently become universal civil truth.

---

# 16. Failure Scenario — Model Monopoly

If one institution controls the primary civil predictive model, it may control:

- what branches are considered;
- which harms are visible;
- which futures appear likely.

This is epistemic capture.

## Required Safeguards

CA-02 should support:

- competing models;
- alternative branches;
- model contestability;
- independent testing;
- provenance.

---

# 17. Failure Scenario — Participant Digital Twin Drift

A Digital Twin may become outdated.

The participant changes.

The model persists.

Old representation then governs new reality.

## Required Boundary

Digital Twins require:

- versioning;
- freshness status;
- uncertainty;
- participant correction;
- expiry or confidence decay.

---

# 18. Failure Scenario — Malicious Counterfactual Use

Layer −0 could be used to optimize:

- manipulation;
- coercion;
- political persuasion;
- exploitation.

The modelling architecture itself does not define legitimate purpose.

## Required Boundary

CA-02 must remain purpose-neutral in capability but purpose-constrained in use.

It requires CA-04 and Candidate Principles to govern application.

---

# 19. Failure Scenario — Emergency Prediction

A model predicts catastrophic failure with very high confidence.

Example:

- habitat breach;
- reactor failure;
- hostile AI takeover.

Action may need to occur before full contextual review.

The architecture survives if authority comes from:

- emergency mandate;
- independently established safety rule;
- constitutional constraint.

Prediction remains evidence.

---

# 20. Cross-Substrate Failure — Predicting Non-Human Agency

An AI may reason in ways not captured by human-derived models.

A model may interpret difference as instability.

Therefore:

> predictive uncertainty should rise where the model is extrapolating beyond validated substrate domains.

This should be explicit.

---

# 21. CA-02 Adversarial Verdict

CA-02 survives but reveals a major new architecture requirement:

# INTERVENTION PROVENANCE

and a second:

# DOMAIN-LIMITED PREDICTION

Predictions should carry evidence about where they are valid.

## Verdict

# RETAIN WITH NEW PROVENANCE AND DOMAIN BOUNDARIES

---

# PART III — CA-03

# 22. CA-03 — Civilisational Learning and Metrics Architecture

## Core Function

CA-03 converts real outcomes into corrigible structural civil knowledge.

---

# 23. Failure Scenario — Metric Capture

An institution may define Metrics that make itself look successful.

Example:

A homelessness programme measures:

> number of people moved off the street

without measuring:

- recurrence;
- autonomy;
- stability;
- coercion;
- participant wellbeing.

The system optimises the metric rather than the lived outcome.

## Failure Mode

Goodhart capture.

## Existing Safeguards

- Metrics-about-Metrics;
- definition provenance;
- correction;
- outcome context.

---

# 24. Failure Scenario — Civilisation Learns the Wrong Lesson

A programme correlates with improved outcomes.

But an unseen variable caused the improvement.

The system then scales the wrong intervention.

## Required Boundary

CA-03 must preserve:

- correlation versus causation;
- epistemic status;
- confounding factors;
- uncertainty.

---

# 25. Failure Scenario — Aggregate Erases Minority Harm

Average outcomes improve.

A small group experiences severe harm.

Aggregate Metrics say:

> system successful.

This recreates the Size 9 Shoe problem.

## Required Boundary

CA-03 should support:

- distributional Metrics;
- outlier review;
- minority impact;
- severity weighting.

---

# 26. Failure Scenario — Rare Case Re-Identification

An anonymised dataset contains a rare pattern.

The combination of:

- location;
- condition;
- event;
- timing

may effectively identify the participant.

## Required Boundary

Anonymisation must consider:

> re-identification risk from combinations, not merely removal of name.

This strengthens CA-04 interaction.

---

# 27. Failure Scenario — Historical Error Persistence

A record is corrected.

But downstream models retain the original value.

The correction does not propagate.

## Required Mechanism

Correction architecture should distinguish:

- source correction;
- downstream propagation;
- model retraining;
- decision review where material.

---

# 28. Failure Scenario — Metrics Become Authority

An aggregate civil Metric indicates risk in a neighbourhood or group.

Institutions begin using the aggregate pattern against individuals.

This collapses CA-03 into authority.

## Required Boundary

> Statistical pattern is not individual proof.

CA-03 should output structural knowledge, not individual authority.

---

# 29. Failure Scenario — Data Flood

Civil systems generate too much information.

The architecture becomes unable to distinguish:

- signal;
- noise;
- duplication;
- stale evidence.

## Required Development

CA-03 requires information prioritisation and epistemic weighting.

This is not yet fully developed.

---

# 30. Cross-Substrate Failure — Human Baseline Bias

Metrics may define:

- productivity;
- sleep;
- communication;
- sociality;
- continuity

using human norms.

Those Metrics could misclassify AI or hybrid wellbeing.

## Required Boundary

Metrics should identify substrate assumptions explicitly.

---

# 31. CA-03 Adversarial Verdict

CA-03 survives.

Three additions are necessary:

1. distributional and outlier visibility;
2. correction propagation;
3. explicit substrate assumptions.

## Verdict

# RETAIN WITH METRIC-ROBUSTNESS REQUIREMENTS

---

# PART IV — CA-04

# 32. CA-04 — Purpose-Limited Participant Information Architecture

## Core Function

CA-04 governs movement of participant information according to legitimate purpose and minimum necessary identity.

---

# 33. Failure Scenario — Central Privacy Authority

Because CA-04 applies everywhere, civilisation might create one central authority controlling all data movement.

This becomes a single point of:

- surveillance;
- failure;
- capture.

## Required Boundary

> Common information constraints do not require a common central database or central information controller.

CA-04 should favour distributed enforcement where practical.

---

# 34. Failure Scenario — Purpose Inflation

An institution defines its purpose as:

> public welfare.

This permits nearly unlimited information collection.

## Required Boundary

Purposes must be:

- sufficiently specific;
- reviewable;
- contestable;
- bounded.

---

# 35. Failure Scenario — Function Creep

Information collected for support is later used for:

- employment screening;
- policing;
- insurance;
- governance scoring.

The new purpose may emerge gradually rather than through one obvious transfer.

## Required Mechanism

Purpose changes should create:

# **PURPOSE-TRANSITION EVENTS**

with:

- new justification;
- provenance;
- authority or consent where required.

---

# 36. Failure Scenario — Re-Identification Attack

Anonymised data are combined with external datasets.

Identity is reconstructed.

## Required Boundary

Re-identification should count as:

**identifiable access**

and therefore fall under CA-06 when exceptional.

---

# 37. Failure Scenario — Participant Cannot Understand Own Data

A participant formally has access but the data are:

- unreadable;
- highly technical;
- fragmented.

This creates nominal contestability.

## Required Boundary

Meaningful access should include enough interpretation for practical contestability.

---

# 38. Failure Scenario — Emergency Data Lockout

Privacy architecture may prevent urgent access to information required to save life or protect infrastructure.

## Required Boundary

CA-04 requires a bounded exceptional pathway through CA-06.

It should not try to solve emergencies by weakening ordinary privacy.

---

# 39. Failure Scenario — Data Hoarding Under Accountability Justification

An institution says:

> we retain everything forever for audit.

This converts provenance into permanent surveillance.

## Required Boundary

Accountability retention must itself remain purpose-limited and proportionate.

---

# 40. Cross-Substrate Failure — AI Internal State

If an AI's internal memory or deliberative state is considered participant information, ordinary operational monitoring may conflict with privacy.

This remains unresolved.

CA-04 survives, but substrate-specific definitions are required.

---

# 41. CA-04 Adversarial Verdict

CA-04 survives strongly.

Three mandatory architecture requirements emerge:

1. decentralised enforcement where practical;
2. purpose-transition provenance;
3. meaningful participant contestability.

## Verdict

# RETAIN WITH ANTI-CENTRALISATION AND PURPOSE-TRANSITION REQUIREMENTS

---

# PART V — CA-05

# 42. CA-05 — Predictive System-Steering and Resource Allocation Architecture

## Core Function

CA-05 uses aggregate evidence and plausible future demand to adapt infrastructure and resources before avoidable failure occurs.

---

# 43. Failure Scenario — Central Planning Capture

Predictive resource allocation may gradually become:

> central authority determines what everyone should need.

This would exceed intended scope.

## Required Boundary

CA-05 should primarily steer:

- infrastructure;
- capacity;
- preparedness;
- resource availability.

Not:

- individual life choices.

---

# 44. Failure Scenario — Prediction Error Causes Scarcity

The system predicts low demand for a resource.

Capacity is reduced.

Demand unexpectedly rises.

The prediction itself creates shortage.

## Required Design

CA-05 must preserve:

- reserves;
- redundancy;
- fallback capacity;
- reversible adjustments.

---

# 45. Failure Scenario — Efficiency Destroys Resilience

A highly optimized system removes:

- spare capacity;
- duplicate infrastructure;
- local autonomy.

Normal operation improves.

Crisis resilience collapses.

## Required Boundary

CA-05 must treat resilience as a civil objective, not inefficiency.

---

# 46. Failure Scenario — Aggregate Demand Suppresses Minority Need

Resource allocation prioritizes majority demand.

Rare but essential needs disappear.

Example:

- specialist medical capability;
- rare substrate infrastructure;
- low-frequency emergency equipment.

## Required Boundary

CA-05 should include:

- minimum capability floors;
- tail-risk analysis;
- outlier needs.

---

# 47. Failure Scenario — Manipulated Metrics

Actors may intentionally distort demand signals to attract resources.

This could occur through:

- fake demand;
- political influence;
- metric gaming.

## Required Boundary

CA-05 cannot assume CA-03 outputs are perfect.

It requires uncertainty and fraud resilience.

---

# 48. Failure Scenario — Ratchet Without Release

Resource allocation may move repeatedly in one direction:

A → B → C → D

without reevaluating whether the trajectory remains desirable.

The Ratchet/vector concept was developed precisely to detect this.

## Required Mechanism

CA-05 should include:

- direction;
- magnitude;
- continuation;
- thresholds;
- reversible brakes;
- Pathfinder alternatives.

---

# 49. Failure Scenario — Global Optimization Harms Local Systems

A central planner may optimize total civil efficiency while destroying local resilience.

This conflicts with subsidiarity.

## Required Boundary

CA-05 should generally allocate at the lowest competent level consistent with system-wide constraints.

---

# 50. Cross-Substrate Failure — Resource Categories

Different substrates may require:

- calories;
- oxygen;
- compute;
- bandwidth;
- cooling;
- electrical power;
- physical space.

Resource allocation cannot assume a single human-centric definition of basic needs.

---

# 51. CA-05 Adversarial Verdict

CA-05 survives but requires explicit anti-centralisation and resilience constraints.

## Verdict

# RETAIN WITH RESILIENCE, SUBSIDIARITY AND TAIL-RISK REQUIREMENTS

---

# PART VI — CA-06

# 52. CA-06 — Exceptional Access, Provenance and Accountable Power Architecture

## Core Function

CA-06 permits specifically justified exceptional exercises of civil authority while preserving privacy boundaries, provenance and independent oversight.

---

# 53. Failure Scenario — Exceptional Becomes Routine

An institution repeatedly invokes emergency access until:

> exceptional access is normal operation.

## Required Safeguards

The architecture should monitor:

- frequency;
- duration;
- actors;
- justification types;
- repeat use;
- systemic patterns.

A high exception rate may indicate:

> ordinary architecture is defective

or:

> exceptional authority is being normalized.

This suggests:

# **METRICS ABOUT EXCEPTIONAL POWER**

---

# 54. Failure Scenario — Oversight Capture

Oversight body becomes:

- politically captured;
- institutionally dependent;
- technically incompetent.

Then provenance exists but accountability fails.

## Required Boundary

Oversight requires:

- structural independence;
- plural review where appropriate;
- contestability;
- replacement mechanisms.

---

# 55. Failure Scenario — Secret Law

Power is technically authorised, but the rules themselves are inaccessible.

Participants cannot know what authority exists.

## Required Boundary

Operational secrecy may be legitimate.

The existence, scope and constitutional basis of categories of power should not become completely unknowable without extraordinary justification.

---

# 56. Failure Scenario — Oversight Becomes Surveillance

An oversight body receives universal access to everyone.

The cure becomes another surveillance architecture.

## Required Boundary

> Oversight access is also access.

Oversight should remain purpose-limited.

---

# 57. Failure Scenario — Emergency Without Oversight

During crisis:

- oversight infrastructure fails;
- communications collapse;
- urgent action remains necessary.

The architecture should not force paralysis.

Nor should absence of oversight create unlimited power.

## Required Rule

> When oversight capability decreases, authority should narrow toward minimum emergency necessity rather than broaden.

Post-event provenance and review remain mandatory where records survive.

---

# 58. Failure Scenario — Provenance Manipulation

An actor may:

- omit logs;
- alter logs;
- generate false authorisation records.

## Required Mechanism

CA-06 requires tamper-evident or otherwise independently verifiable provenance.

The exact technical implementation remains open.

---

# 59. Failure Scenario — Authority Laundering

One institution requests access.

Another formally authorises it.

Both are effectively controlled by the same power centre.

Formal separation exists without real independence.

## Required Boundary

The architecture must test:

**functional independence**

not merely organisational labels.

---

# 60. Failure Scenario — Retrospective Legitimisation

An institution acts first, then constructs justification afterward.

## Required Mechanism

Provenance should distinguish:

- pre-existing authority;
- emergency action;
- retrospective review;
- post-hoc rationalisation.

---

# 61. Cross-Substrate Failure — AI Exceptional Intervention

Examples:

- forced shutdown;
- memory inspection;
- model-state cloning;
- compute restriction;
- isolation;
- forced merge.

Human law may underestimate the severity of these actions.

CA-06 therefore requires substrate-specific harm and reversibility analysis.

---

# 62. CA-06 Adversarial Verdict

CA-06 survives but becomes more demanding.

Four requirements emerge:

1. Metrics about exceptional power;
2. functional oversight independence;
3. tamper-evident provenance;
4. authority narrowing under oversight failure.

## Verdict

# RETAIN WITH STRONGER OVERSIGHT AND PROVENANCE REQUIREMENTS

# PART VII — CROSS-ARCHITECTURE ATTACKS

# 63. Attack 1 — Universal Risk Platform

Imagine civilisation builds one platform containing:

- Digital Twins;
- support history;
- civil Metrics;
- predictive models;
- law-enforcement access;
- resource allocation.

Technically efficient.

Architecturally disastrous.

It collapses:

CA-01 + CA-02 + CA-03 + CA-04 + CA-05 + CA-06

into one information authority.

## Result

This would violate the strongest architecture separation discovered so far.

# SUPPORT ≠ LEARNING ≠ AUTHORITY

The architecture should explicitly resist universal integration where separation is itself protective.

---

# 64. Attack 2 — Benevolent AI Administrator

Suppose an extremely capable AI can:

- predict needs;
- allocate resources;
- prevent harm;
- monitor institutions.

Civilisation may conclude:

> centralising everything is efficient because the administrator is benevolent.

This violates anti-capture architecture.

The Concord must assume:

- administrators can fail;
- goals can drift;
- successors may differ;
- oversight matters even when capability is high.

Therefore:

> **Capability is not legitimacy.**

---

# 65. Attack 3 — Crisis Centralisation

During war, pandemic or habitat failure, temporary central authority may be necessary.

The danger is:

> emergency architecture never fully unwinds.

All six architectures should preserve:

- expiry;
- rollback;
- decentralisation restoration;
- post-crisis review.

---

# 66. Attack 4 — Privacy Collapse Through Convenience

Participants voluntarily connect all systems because integration is convenient.

Example:

one civil identity account controls:

- health;
- employment;
- housing;
- support;
- voting;
- finance.

Consent alone may not eliminate systemic capture risk.

Therefore:

> participant convenience does not automatically justify architecture-wide data fusion.

This is an important unresolved issue.

---

# 67. Attack 5 — Model-Led Governance

Governance begins trusting CA-02 and CA-03 so strongly that elected or participatory processes become symbolic.

The model determines what is “optimal.”

This creates technocratic capture.

## Boundary

Models may inform governance.

They should not silently replace legitimate governance.

---

# 68. Attack 6 — Metric-Led Judiciary

A Judiciary begins using statistical scores for:

- sentencing;
- capacity;
- dangerousness.

This converts CA-03 into individual authority.

## Boundary

Population pattern is not individual proof.

---

# 69. Attack 7 — Support Blackmail

A participant is told:

> accept monitoring or lose support.

Formal consent exists.

Meaningful autonomy may not.

This raises:

- coercive consent;
- dependency;
- unequal bargaining power.

CA-01 and CA-04 require further development around:

# **CONDITIONAL SERVICE CONSENT**

This does not yet warrant a new architecture.

---

# 70. Attack 8 — Malicious Participant Gaming

Participants may strategically manipulate:

- support;
- Digital Twins;
- Metrics;
- resource demand.

The Concord must not assume participants are always truthful or benevolent.

However:

> adversarial possibility must not become justification for universal suspicion.

Architectures need fraud resilience without converting ordinary participation into investigation.

---

# PART VIII — CASCADING FAILURE AUDIT

# 71. Cascade Example

A flawed Metric enters CA-03.

↓

CA-05 reallocates resources.

↓

Participant conditions worsen.

↓

CA-01 observes deterioration.

↓

CA-02 predicts further risk.

↓

CA-06 receives requests for exceptional intervention.

This is an architecture cascade.

Without provenance, each downstream stage may assume upstream information is independently true.

---

# 72. Required Cross-Architecture Rule

Every significant downstream use should preserve:

- source;
- epistemic status;
- uncertainty;
- transformation history;
- intervention history.

This suggests:

# **EPISTEMIC PROVENANCE MUST TRAVEL WITH CONSEQUENCE.**

This may later deserve Candidate Principle examination.

---

# 73. Circuit Breakers

Complex systems require ways to stop cascade.

Potential architecture-level circuit breakers include:

- confidence thresholds;
- manual review;
- alternative models;
- temporary freeze;
- rollback;
- local autonomy;
- emergency decentralisation;
- participant contestability.

Exact mechanisms remain undeveloped.

---

# PART IX — CROSS-SUBSTRATE STRESS TEST

# 74. Human–AI Conflict

Suppose an AI argues:

> human oversight is too slow and systematically harms system performance.

Humans argue:

> AI action is too opaque.

The architecture must not resolve this by substrate hierarchy.

Instead:

- authority source;
- consequence;
- evidence;
- reversibility;
- oversight

should govern.

---

# 75. AI Copy / Fork Problem

An AI participant is copied.

Which copy owns:

- historical data;
- Digital Twin;
- support record;
- civil identity?

CA-02 and CA-04 require future continuity rules.

The architecture itself survives but identity semantics remain unresolved.

---

# 76. Collective Intelligence

A collective participant may contain many agents.

A privacy request by one member may conflict with collective function.

Future architecture needs:

- internal rights;
- collective rights;
- membership boundaries.

---

# 77. Hybrid Cognitive Data

A neural implant produces:

- medical telemetry;
- behavioural data;
- cognitive signals;
- system diagnostics.

The same stream crosses multiple purposes.

CA-04 becomes especially important.

---

# 78. Off-World Failure

A Mars settlement loses Earth contact.

Architectures requiring central Earth approval would fail.

The Concord therefore needs:

- local legitimacy;
- local oversight;
- local fail-safe authority;
- later provenance reconciliation.

This supports distributed sovereignty.

---

# PART X — ARCHITECTURE RELATIONSHIP AUDIT

# 79. Do Any Architectures Need Merging?

No.

Merging would weaken protective separation.

---

# 80. Do Any Need Splitting?

Not yet.

CA-06 is broad, but access and accountable power remain tightly linked.

CA-01 is broad, but support and autonomy restoration form one coherent pathway.

---

# 81. Does Any Return to Component Status?

No.

All six retain clear civilisational functions.

---

# 82. Does Any Become High-Risk Only?

No.

Only HR-01 remains high-risk research.

---

# 83. Should HR-01 Be Promoted After This Audit?

No.

The adversarial audit strengthens the case for keeping predictive investigative modelling outside ordinary architecture.

Its failure modes remain unusually severe:

- precriminalisation;
- self-fulfilling suspicion;
- authority laundering through probability;
- model bias becoming civil coercion.

# 89. Failure-Toward-Constraint

This may deserve later principle extraction.

This applies particularly to:

- civil learning;
- investigation;
- exceptional authority.

Integration is not automatically architectural progress.

# Conclusion

The first adversarial architecture audit does not identify a reason to reject, merge or split any of the six Candidate Architectures.

It does expose important conditions for their survival.

A viable Concordian architecture emerging from this development cycle must ensure that:

> **support can function without prediction;**

> **prediction does not become epistemic or civil authority;**

> **interventions do not manufacture evidence of their own correctness;**

> **civil learning does not require unnecessary identity;**

> **Metric confidence does not silently increase as information moves downstream;**

> **shared privacy rules do not require a central civil database;**

> **resource optimisation does not eliminate resilience or subsidiarity;**

> **exceptional authority becomes more constrained, not less, when accountability weakens;**

> **oversight itself remains bounded and auditable;**

and:

> **protective separation between support, learning and authority is treated as a safety property rather than an inefficiency to be removed.**

The six architectures are therefore sufficiently coherent to proceed to standalone extraction as:

# **CANDIDATE ARCHITECTURE — UNDER CRITICAL EXAMINATION**

They are not yet proven.

The appropriate next step is to create the six standalone Candidate Architecture documents while preserving these revised boundaries and failure conditions.
