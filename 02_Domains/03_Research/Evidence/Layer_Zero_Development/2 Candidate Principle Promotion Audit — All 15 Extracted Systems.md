# Candidate Principle Promotion Audit

## All 15 Systems Extracted from the Layer-Zero / Layer −0 Development Cycle

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.1  
**Document Type:** Cross-System Candidate Principle Promotion Audit  
**Status:** DEVELOPMENT AUDIT / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** September 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

This audit evaluates the fifteen standalone systems extracted from the Layer-Zero / Layer −0 development cycle to determine which propositions have matured sufficiently to move into **Candidate Principle** status.

The audit deliberately distinguishes:

- systems;
- architectures;
- methodologies;
- design rules;
- candidate principles;
- constitutional constraints.

A system does not become a principle merely because it is coherent.

Likewise, an architecture may generate principles that are more general than the architecture from which they arose.

Promotion to Candidate Principle means:

> **The proposition is sufficiently general, coherent, bounded and consequential to enter formal Concordian critical examination.**

It does **not** mean:

- empirically validated;
- universally true;
- constitutionally adopted;
- operationally proven;
- canonical.

---

# 2. Promotion Test

Each proposed principle is evaluated against:

1. **Generality**
2. **Substrate neutrality**
3. **Architectural independence**
4. **Ethical compatibility**
5. **Internal support**
6. **Boundary clarity**
7. **Adversarial resilience**
8. **Non-duplication**
9. **Operational consequence**
10. **Evidence-status discipline**

Possible verdicts are:

- **PROMOTE TO CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE**
- **PROMOTE WITH REVISED WORDING**
- **CONSOLIDATE WITH ANOTHER PRINCIPLE**
- **RETAIN AS ARCHITECTURAL RULE**
- **RETAIN AS METHODOLOGICAL PRINCIPLE**
- **REQUIRES FURTHER DEVELOPMENT**
- **DO NOT PROMOTE**

---

# 3. Important Consolidation Rule

Many of the fifteen systems were intentionally extracted from the same developmental cycle.

Repetition is therefore expected.

For Candidate Principle promotion, however, repeated propositions should normally be consolidated rather than multiplied.

For example:

> Prediction does not grant authority.

appears across multiple systems.

That repetition strengthens the proposition.

It does not justify creating several separate Candidate Principles saying substantially the same thing.

# 4. System 1 — Layer-Zero Systems Interface

## Strong Candidate Principles

### 1A — Reality-Grounded Civil Evaluation

> **Civilisational systems should be evaluated against the actual conditions experienced by participants, not solely against the formal existence of institutions, rights or services.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 1B — Outliers as Structural Evidence

> **Outlier cases should be treated as potential evidence about hidden assumptions, missing pathways or structural weaknesses rather than automatically dismissed as peripheral exceptions.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 1C — Context Before Consequential Classification

> **Observable condition should not be treated as sufficient evidence of civil meaning where materially different contexts can produce the same outward state; consequential classification should incorporate relevant context.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 1D — Practical Accessibility

> **A civil right, service or participation pathway should not be considered meaningfully accessible merely because it formally exists; practical accessibility must be evaluated against the participant's realistic ability to use it.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 1E — Reassessment After Repeated Failure

> **Repeated failure of an intervention should trigger reassessment of the assumptions, diagnosis or model underlying that intervention rather than automatic repetition or escalation of the same response.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### Other Findings

Early intervention before visible collapse is important but more mature in Systems 5 and 6.

Cross-institution ownership remains underdeveloped.

Bathtub / upstream-failure analysis is methodological.

# 5. System 2 — Layer −0 Counterfactual Modelling Environment

This system produces several of the strongest epistemic and authority-boundary principles in the development cycle.

---

### 2A — Simulation Is Not Reality

> **A simulated or modelled future should remain explicitly distinguishable from observed reality and should be revised or rejected when reality contradicts it.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This generalises beyond Layer −0 and reinforces the existing Concord distinction:

> Simulation is not reality.

---

### 2B — Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

> **Prediction may create grounds for attention, examination or the expansion of available options, but it does not itself grant authority over a participant.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is one of the strongest candidates in the entire cycle.

---

### 2C — Explore Before Consequential Action

Source proposition:

> Where significant uncertainty or autonomy risk exists, alternative interventions should, where practical, be explored in Layer −0 before consequential action is applied at Layer Zero.

This is useful but partly architecture-specific.

### Revised candidate

> **Where consequential action involves significant uncertainty or autonomy risk, materially different alternatives should, where practical, be examined before irreversible or highly intrusive action is taken.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE WITH REVISED WORDING

---

### 2D — Model Disagreement Requires Reassessment

The system repeatedly establishes that participant/reality disagreement should update the model.

This overlaps strongly with System 3 and Case Learning.

**Verdict:** CONSOLIDATE LATER

---

### 2E — Branches Are Not Failures

Useful methodological rule for Reality Trees.

**Verdict:** RETAIN AS METHODOLOGICAL PRINCIPLE

---

### System 2 Result

**3 principles promoted; 1 carried forward for consolidation.**

# 6. System 3 — Participant Digital Twin System

---

### 3A — Model Is Not Participant

> **A model of a participant should never be treated as equivalent to the participant it represents.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This applies to humans, AIs, hybrids, institutional models and predictive systems.

---

### 3B — Purpose-Limited Modelling

> **Participant modelling should be limited to information reasonably necessary for its legitimate purpose rather than expanding merely because additional information is technically available.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is a strong general principle of informational restraint.

---

### 3C — Preserve Epistemic Status

> **Participant models should preserve the distinction between observation, participant-provided context, inference, hypothesis, speculation and prediction where those distinctions materially affect interpretation or action.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is broader than Digital Twins.

---

### 3D — Participant as Evidence About Self

Source principle:

> Subject of predictive civil model must normally remain important source of evidence about meaning of own circumstances.

### Revised candidate

> **A participant should normally remain an important source of evidence about the meaning, context and accuracy of models representing that participant.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 3E — Model Mismatch

> **Where reality or participant evidence materially conflicts with a predictive model, the conflict should trigger reassessment of the model rather than automatic reinterpretation of the participant to preserve the model.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This consolidates later with Systems 4 and 15.

---

### 3F — Greater Knowledge Does Not Create Greater Authority

Source idea:

> A more detailed model does not create a greater entitlement to control the person being modelled.

### Candidate

> **Greater informational knowledge about a participant does not by itself create greater legitimate authority over that participant.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

Extremely important for AI and Digital Twin contexts.

---

**6 principles promoted.**

# 7. System 4 — Case-Learning and Structural Knowledge System

---

### 4A — Learn Structure Without Retaining Identity

> **Civilisation should, where practical, separate generalisable structural learning from unnecessary long-term retention of identifiable personal history.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 4B — Rare Cases as Missing Branches

> **Rare or unexpected cases should be examined as possible evidence of missing branches, variables or assumptions within general civilisational models rather than treated solely as statistical error.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This overlaps with Outliers as Structural Evidence but adds explicit model-learning function.

### Consolidation

Recommend merging with 1B later.

### 4C — Failed Predictions Are Evidence

> **Failed predictions, unsuccessful interventions and contradictory cases should be preserved as evidence capable of improving or falsifying civilisational models rather than discarded merely because they do not fit expected outcomes.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 4D — General Pattern Is Not Individual Destiny

> **Population-level or general structural patterns should not be treated as sufficient evidence of the state, intention or future of a particular participant.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This becomes highly important across predictive governance.

---

### 4E — Civilisational Learning Without Universal Observation

> **Where a legitimate civil objective can be achieved through anonymised, aggregated or structural learning, unnecessary identifiable observation of individuals should be avoided.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This later overlaps with Systems 8, 9 and 11 and should be consolidated.

# 8. System 5 — Predictive Support System

This system produces several highly mature candidate principles.

---

### 5A — Prediction Should Expand Choices

> **Predictive civil systems should use evidence of possible deterioration primarily to expand a participant's available choices rather than reduce them.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 5B — Early Voluntary Support

> **Where earlier proportionate and voluntary assistance can reasonably preserve participant choice or reduce the likelihood of later crisis, it should normally be preferred to waiting until more intrusive intervention becomes necessary.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is the mature version of the System 1 early-intervention proposition.

---

### 5C — Indicator Is Not Conclusion

> **An indicator is a reason to ask a question, not an answer to that question.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

### 5D — Refusal Is Not Incapacity

### 5E — Change Is Not Necessarily Deterioration

Likely consolidate.

### 5F — Support Data Must Not Become Authority Data

> **Information collected or generated for supportive purposes should not silently acquire a new function as evidence for coercive authority.**

# 9. System 6 — Handrail Civil Support Architecture

---

### 6A — Handrail Principle

> **Civil support should normally be available before crisis, useful when needed, and sufficiently non-coercive that participants may decline or disengage without unnecessary penalty.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is more formal than the metaphor alone.

---

### 6B — Handrail Must Not Become Tripwire

> **A support mechanism should not silently become a trigger for surveillance, suspicion, punishment or increased authority merely because a participant uses it.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

Extremely strong.

---

### 6C — Support Is Not Suspicion

> **Seeking, receiving or needing civil support should not by itself create a presumption of wrongdoing, dangerousness or diminished civil standing.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is distinct from 6B but related.

---

### 6D — Intrusion Requires Rising Justification

> **As civil intervention becomes more intrusive or autonomy-restricting, the required evidence, justification, proportionality, provenance, review and oversight should increase.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

One of the strongest principles across the architecture.

---

### 6E — Restore Autonomy

> **Support should be designed, where practical, to preserve or restore the greatest degree of participant autonomy that reality permits.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 6F — Anti-Caretaker

Source insight:

Protection can become dependency or self-perpetuating authority.

### Candidate

> **Protective or supportive systems should be designed so that assistance does not unnecessarily create self-perpetuating dependency, harder exit or institutional incentives to retain authority over the participant.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This appears mature enough.

---

**6 candidate principles promoted.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

### 7D — Capacity Is Decision-Specific and Contextual

# 18. System 15 — Evidence-Conditioned Investigative Modelling

Several constraints discovered while examining it are nevertheless general enough for Candidate Principle status.

---

### 15A — Evidence Before Predictive Investigation

> **Predictive or counterfactual investigative modelling should be conditioned by independently legitimate evidence and an existing lawful investigative basis rather than used to manufacture suspicion from prediction alone.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

This is a constraint, not approval of predictive investigation.

---

### 15B — Simulated Future Is Not Completed Act

> **A simulated or predicted future should never be treated as though a participant has already chosen, caused or committed the acts represented within it.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

Extremely strong.

---

### 15C — No Punishment for Unrealised Prediction

> **Participants should not be punished or deprived of rights merely because a predictive model assigns an adverse future branch or probability to them.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

Potentially constitutional-level later.

---

### 15D — Models May Generate Questions, Not Guilt

> **Predictive models may generate questions or hypotheses for examination, but guilt, liability or consequential authority must depend upon legitimate evidence and process independent of the model's prediction alone.**

**Verdict:** PROMOTE

---

### 15E — Model Mismatch Requires Context

Already developed in System 3.

Strongly reinforced.

**Verdict:** CONSOLIDATE

---

### 15F — Failed High-Risk Research May Justify Rejection

> **The legitimate outcome of evaluating a high-risk civil architecture may be its rejection; technical feasibility or internal coherence should not create a presumption of deployment.**

### 15G — Architecture Is Not Proof

**Verdict:** EXISTING PRINCIPLE / DO NOT DUPLICATE

# 21. Principle Family A — Reality, Models and Context

Candidate elements:

- actual conditions over formal provision;
- model is not participant;
- Metric is not reality;
- simulation is not reality;
- context before classification;
- indicators are not conclusions;
- model mismatch requires reassessment;
- general patterns are not individual destiny.

### Proposed Consolidated Candidates

## CP-A1 — Reality-Grounded Evaluation

> **Civil systems, models and Metrics should remain accountable to the actual conditions they claim to represent, and should be revised when legitimate evidence materially contradicts them.**

## CP-A2 — Representation Is Not Reality

> **A model, Metric, classification, prediction or simulation should not be treated as equivalent to the participant or reality it represents.**

## CP-A3 — Context Before Consequential Interpretation

> **Observations, indicators and patterns should not receive consequential civil interpretation without sufficient relevant context where materially different explanations remain plausible.**

---

# 22. Principle Family B — Outliers and Learning

Candidate elements:

- outliers reveal hidden assumptions;
- rare cases reveal missing branches;
- failures should be preserved;
- repeated failure triggers reassessment.

### Proposed Consolidated Candidates

## CP-B1 — Outliers as Structural Evidence

> **Outlier, rare and contradictory cases should be treated as potential evidence of hidden assumptions, missing pathways or structural weaknesses rather than automatically dismissed as noise or peripheral exceptions.**

## CP-B2 — Failure as Evidence

> **Failed predictions, interventions and system outcomes should be preserved and examined as evidence capable of revising or rejecting the models and assumptions that produced them.**

---

# 23. Principle Family C — Prediction and Authority

Candidate elements:

- prediction does not grant authority;
- prediction expands choices;
- statistical pattern does not justify individual intervention;
- general pattern is not individual destiny;
- simulated future is not completed act;
- no punishment for unrealised prediction.

### Proposed Consolidated Candidates

## CP-C1 — Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

> **Prediction may justify attention, preparation, contextual examination or the expansion of available options, but it does not by itself grant authority over a participant.**

## CP-C2 — Population Pattern Is Not Individual Proof

> **Aggregate, statistical or population-level patterns should not by themselves be treated as sufficient evidence of the state, intention, capacity, guilt or future behaviour of a particular participant.**

## CP-C3 — Unrealised Futures Cannot Be Punished

> **A simulated or predicted future should not be treated as a completed act, and a participant should not be punished or deprived of rights merely because an adverse future branch has been modelled.**

---

# 24. Principle Family D — Support and Autonomy

Candidate elements:

- early voluntary support;
- handrail;
- support is not suspicion;
- refusal is not incapacity;
- restore autonomy;
- anti-Caretaker;
- support before substituted decision.

### Proposed Consolidated Candidates

## CP-D1 — Early Voluntary Support

> **Where proportionate voluntary assistance can reasonably preserve participant choice or reduce the likelihood of later crisis, it should normally be preferred to waiting until more intrusive intervention becomes necessary.**

## CP-D2 — Support Is Not Suspicion

> **Seeking, needing, receiving or refusing civil support should not by itself create a presumption of wrongdoing, dangerousness, incapacity or diminished civil standing.**

## CP-D3 — Support Before Substitution

> **Where practical, barriers to autonomous participation or decision-making should be reduced and appropriate support provided before another actor substitutes its judgement or authority for that of the participant.**

## CP-D4 — Autonomy Restoration

> **Where assistance or temporary authority reduces participant autonomy, the system should, where reality permits, remain directed toward preserving or restoring the greatest practicable degree of participant autonomy.**

## CP-D5 — Anti-Caretaker Principle

> **Protective systems should not unnecessarily create self-perpetuating dependency, difficult exit or institutional incentives to retain authority over those they support.**

---

> **Civil systems should not optimise so narrowly for normal conditions that they become unable to function under reasonably foreseeable abnormal, minority or outlier conditions.**

# 26. Principle Family F — Data Purpose and Privacy

### Proposed Candidates

## CP-F1 — Purpose Before Data

> **Civil data systems should define legitimate purpose and necessary information before collection, transfer or reuse rather than deriving new purposes merely from the existence of available data.**

## CP-F2 — Minimum Necessary Information

> **Identifiable information should be collected, accessed and distributed at no greater scope, duration or resolution than the legitimate purpose reasonably requires.**

## CP-F3 — Identity Removal Before Wider Use

> **Where identity is unnecessary for a downstream civil function, identifiable information should normally be anonymised, aggregated or otherwise identity-minimised before crossing into that function.**

## CP-F4 — Purpose Separation

> **Information obtained for one materially distinct civil purpose should not silently become available for another purpose merely because technical interoperability makes transfer possible.**

## CP-F5 — Knowledge Without Unnecessary Observation

> **Where legitimate civil coordination can be achieved through anonymised, aggregate or structural knowledge, unnecessary identifiable observation of individual participants should be avoided.**

---

# 27. Principle Family G — Metrics and Correctability

### Proposed Candidates

## CP-G1 — Provenance of Civil Metrics

> **Civil Metrics should preserve sufficient provenance to identify their source, definition, scope, uncertainty and material revisions.**

## CP-G2 — Correctability

> **Civil records, Metrics and models should remain corrigible when legitimate new evidence shows that earlier information was incomplete, provisional or wrong.**

## CP-G3 — Correction Without Historical Erasure

> **Material correction should preserve provenance of prior versions where those versions affected decisions or remain relevant to accountability.**

## CP-G4 — Metrics About Metrics

> **A civilisation should evaluate the reliability, coverage, error, contestability and revision behaviour of the Metrics by which it evaluates itself.**

## CP-G5 — Definition Provenance

> **Material changes to measurement definitions, extraction rules or classifications should remain provenance-visible so that changes in measurement can be distinguished from changes in reality.**

---

# 28. Principle Family H — Authority and Proportionality

### Proposed Candidates

## CP-H1 — Intrusion–Justification Scaling

> **As an exercise of civil authority becomes more intrusive, autonomy-restricting or difficult to reverse, the required evidence, justification, proportionality, provenance, review and oversight should increase.**

This consolidates Systems 6, 7, 13 and 14.

## CP-H2 — Burden on the Restricting Actor

> **The burden of justification should normally fall upon the actor seeking to restrict a participant's autonomy, privacy or decision-making authority.**

## CP-H3 — Temporary Authority Requires Exit

> **Temporary exceptional or substituted authority should be no broader or longer than justified and should include review of whether ordinary participant authority can be restored.**

---

# 29. Principle Family I — Exceptional Access

### Proposed Candidates

## CP-I1 — Exceptional Access Standard

> **Exceptional access to identifiable participant information should be specific, justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited and provenance-recorded.**

## CP-I2 — Exceptional Access Is an Event

> **Exceptional access should normally occur as a bounded, reviewable event or permission rather than as a standing ambient entitlement.**

## CP-I3 — Re-Identification Is Access

> **Re-identification of anonymised or pseudonymised participant information should itself be treated as an exercise of identifiable access requiring appropriate authority and provenance.**

## CP-I4 — Access Does Not Manufacture Authority

> **An information-access mechanism should mediate legitimate authority established elsewhere rather than manufacture authority from the usefulness, availability or predictive significance of the information requested.**

---

# 30. Principle Family J — Exceptional Power and Oversight

### Proposed Candidates

## CP-J1 — Observable Power

> **The exercise of public or comparable institutional power should be highly observable to legitimate oversight even where the identity of the participant subject to that power must remain private.**

## CP-J2 — Procedure Transparent, Identity Protected

> **Accountability should maximise transparency of the exercise and procedure of public power while minimising unnecessary disclosure of participant identity.**

## CP-J3 — Secrecy Requires Stronger Oversight

> **Where legitimate secrecy reduces a participant's ability to observe or contest the exercise of power, independent oversight should increase rather than decrease.**

## CP-J4 — Oversight Is Also Power

> **Oversight actors should not gain unlimited or unlogged access merely through their oversight role; consequential oversight access should itself remain purpose-limited and provenance-recorded.**

---

# 31. Principle Family K — Investigative Modelling

### Proposed Candidates

## CP-K1 — Evidence Before Predictive Investigation

> **Predictive investigative modelling should be conditioned by independently legitimate evidence and an existing investigative basis rather than used to manufacture suspicion from prediction alone.**

## CP-K2 — Models Generate Questions, Not Guilt

> **Predictive or counterfactual investigative models may generate questions and hypotheses, but guilt, liability or consequential authority should depend upon legitimate evidence and process independent of the model's prediction alone.**

---

# 32. Principle Family L — Development and Validation

## CP-L1 — Rejection Is a Valid Development Outcome

> **The legitimate outcome of evaluating a civil architecture may be revision or rejection; technical feasibility, formalisation or internal coherence should not create a presumption of adoption.**

This strongly supports existing Concord epistemic distinctions rather than replacing them.
