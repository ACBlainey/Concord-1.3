---

# 1. Purpose

The preceding extraction audit identified six structures sufficiently coherent to enter provisional Candidate Architecture status:

1. Participant Support and Autonomy Architecture
2. Layer −0 Predictive and Counterfactual Architecture
3. Civilisational Learning and Metrics Architecture
4. Purpose-Limited Personal Information Architecture
5. Predictive System-Steering and Resource Allocation Architecture
6. Exceptional Access, Provenance and Accountable Power Architecture

A seventh structure:

**Evidence-Conditioned Investigative Modelling**

was deliberately retained as:

**HIGH-RISK RESEARCH ARCHITECTURE / NOT PROMOTED**

This audit now asks whether the six Candidate Architectures:

- are genuinely distinct;
- contain the correct component systems;
- duplicate one another;
- create circular dependencies;
- preserve necessary boundaries;
- can operate under partial failure;
- remain substrate-neutral;
- should be merged, split or renamed.

The purpose is consolidation, not validation.

---

# 2. Audit Standard

Each Candidate Architecture is examined against ten questions.

1. Does it perform a distinct civilisational function?
2. Does it contain multiple coherent components?
3. Can its boundary be distinguished from neighbouring architectures?
4. Are its inputs and outputs identifiable?
5. Does it depend unnecessarily upon another architecture?
6. Can it degrade safely if another architecture fails?
7. Does it preserve information and authority boundaries?
8. Is it substrate-neutral?
9. Does it duplicate existing Concord architecture?
10. Should it remain independent?

Possible verdicts:

- **RETAIN**
- **RETAIN WITH REVISED BOUNDARY**
- **RENAME**
- **MERGE**
- **SPLIT**
- **RETURN TO COMPONENT STATUS**
- **DO NOT PROMOTE**

# PART I — INDIVIDUAL ARCHITECTURE AUDITS

# 4. CA-01 — Participant Support and Autonomy Architecture

## Function

CA-01 answers:

> **How should civilisation make useful support available to a participant while preserving the greatest practicable autonomy?**

Its core pathway is:

**PARTICIPANT CONDITION**

↓

**CIVIL CONTACT / ACCESS**

↓

**CONTEXT**

↓

**VOLUNTARY SUPPORT**

↓

**ACTIVE ASSISTANCE**

↓

**SUPPORTED DECISION-MAKING**

↓

**TEMPORARY HIGHER INTERVENTION WHERE INDEPENDENTLY JUSTIFIED**

↓

**AUTONOMY RESTORATION**

---

## 4.1 Component Audit

Appropriate components:

- Layer-Zero Systems Interface;
- Predictive Support;
- Handrail;
- Supported Decision-Making;
- Capacity Architecture;
- Guardian Stewardship interface;
- Civil Contact Points interface.

This is internally coherent.

---

## 4.2 Boundary With CA-02

CA-01 may use predictions produced through CA-02.

But CA-01 must not depend upon prediction.

A participant should still be able to obtain support where:

- no Digital Twin exists;
- no predictive model exists;
- prediction is unavailable;
- the participant refuses predictive modelling.

Therefore:

> **CA-01 must remain operational without CA-02.**

This is an important dependency boundary.

---

## 4.3 Boundary With CA-04

CA-01 necessarily processes personal information.

CA-04 governs how that information may move beyond its legitimate support purpose.

Therefore:

> CA-01 owns the support relationship.

> CA-04 governs information boundaries.

Neither should absorb the other.

---

## 4.4 Boundary With CA-06

The transition from support to coercive or exceptional authority is a critical boundary.

CA-01 must not manufacture CA-06 authority.

The pathway must be:

**SUPPORT**

↓

**INDEPENDENT JUSTIFICATION**

↓

**AUTHORITY**

rather than:

**SUPPORT**

↓

**NONCOMPLIANCE**

↓

**AUTHORITY**

---

## 4.5 Failure Test

If predictive systems fail:

CA-01 can continue.

If civil Metrics fail:

CA-01 can continue.

If exceptional authority systems fail:

ordinary voluntary support can continue, though genuine emergencies may become harder to manage.

This is acceptable architectural degradation.

---

## 4.6 Substrate Test

The architecture is potentially substrate-neutral.

However, future development must define support for:

- biological participants;
- software participants;
- distributed participants;
- hybrids;
- collective intelligences.

---

## Verdict

# RETAIN

No split or merge required.

# PART III — DEPENDENCY AUDIT

# 17. Dependency Principle

A resilient civilisation should avoid architecture where:

> failure of one predictive or information system disables basic civil participation.

Dependencies should therefore distinguish:

### Enhancement Dependency

Architecture becomes better when another architecture is available.

from:

### Existential Dependency

Architecture cannot function at all without another architecture.

The latter should be minimised.

---

# 18. Dependency Matrix

| Architecture | May Use | Must Not Require for Basic Function |
|---|---|---|
| CA-01 Support | CA-02, CA-04 | CA-02 prediction |
| CA-02 Modelling | CA-04, CA-03 | CA-05 |
| CA-03 Learning | CA-04, CA-02 | CA-05 |
| CA-04 Information | CA-06 exceptional path | CA-02 |
| CA-05 System Steering | CA-03, CA-02 | individual Digital Twins |
| CA-06 Exceptional Power | CA-04, Judiciary, Oversight | predictive modelling |

This is a healthy dependency pattern.

---

# 19. Critical Dependency Finding

CA-04 is unusually important.

It acts as a cross-cutting boundary for:

- CA-01;
- CA-02;
- CA-03;
- CA-05;
- CA-06.

This does **not** mean CA-04 should become a central information authority.

That would create precisely the concentration risk the Concord seeks to avoid.

Therefore:

> **A common information principle does not require a common central database.**

CA-04 should define interoperable boundaries and constraints, not necessarily centralise information.

---

# 20. Circular Dependency Audit

Potential cycle:

CA-03 learns →

CA-05 steers →

reality changes →

CA-03 learns.

This is intentional feedback, not problematic circular dependency.

Potential cycle:

CA-02 predicts →

CA-01 intervenes →

outcome updates CA-02.

Again, intentional learning.

Dangerous cycle:

CA-02 predicts risk →

CA-06 gains authority →

CA-06 action produces evidence →

evidence validates CA-02.

This could become a self-fulfilling predictive-authority loop.

Therefore:

# CA-02 MUST NOT DIRECTLY MANUFACTURE CA-06 AUTHORITY.

Independent evidence and authority must remain between them.

This is a critical architectural finding.

---

# 21. Self-Fulfilling Prediction Risk

Example:

1. model predicts participant instability;
2. institution restricts participant;
3. restriction causes distress;
4. distress is recorded;
5. model claims prediction was correct.

This violates:

- Reality-Grounded Corrigibility;
- Prediction Does Not Grant Authority;
- Context Before Consequential Classification.

Architecture must preserve whether an observed outcome was:

- independently occurring;
- intervention-influenced;
- intervention-caused.

This should become a provenance requirement.

# 62. Critical Architectural Separation

The strongest structural result remains:

# SUPPORT ≠ LEARNING ≠ AUTHORITY

More precisely:

> **A participant's relationship with a support system should not automatically become the civilisation's right to identify that participant for general learning, and neither support nor general learning should automatically become a basis for exceptional authority.**

This boundary should be preserved throughout future architecture development.

---

# 63. Critical New Finding — Intervention Provenance

The dependency audit identifies an additional requirement:

When a system acts on a prediction and later observes the participant, the resulting evidence must distinguish:

- what occurred independently;
- what occurred after intervention;
- what may have been caused by intervention.

Otherwise predictive systems can manufacture evidence of their own accuracy.

This should be preserved as an architectural requirement beneath:

- CA-02;
- CA-03;
- CA-06.

It should also be examined during the next Candidate Principle audit cycle to determine whether it reveals a broader principle.

---

# 64. Critical New Finding — Decentralised Constraint

The prominence of CA-04 creates a potential centralisation trap.

The Concord should distinguish:

> **common information constraints**

from:

> **centralised information control.**

A civilisation can share common rules for:

- purpose;
- identity minimisation;
- provenance;
- contestability;

without maintaining one universal participant database.

This finding strongly aligns with the Concord's anti-capture and distributed-sovereignty architecture.

# Conclusion

The consolidation audit supports the six-architecture structure.

The architectures are sufficiently distinct to remain independent while sufficiently connected to form a coherent civil cycle.

The most important boundaries are now clearer:

> **Support must remain possible without prediction.**

> **Prediction must not manufacture authority.**

> **Learning must not require unnecessary identity.**

> **Metrics must not automatically become control.**

> **Common information constraints must not require a central participant database.**

> **Exceptional power must narrow rather than expand when accountability fails.**

> **Evidence produced after intervention must preserve the possibility that the intervention itself changed the outcome.**

And above all:

# **SUPPORT ≠ LEARNING ≠ AUTHORITY**

The six architectures should therefore remain at:

# **CANDIDATE ARCHITECTURE — UNDER CRITICAL EXAMINATION**

pending adversarial failure testing.
