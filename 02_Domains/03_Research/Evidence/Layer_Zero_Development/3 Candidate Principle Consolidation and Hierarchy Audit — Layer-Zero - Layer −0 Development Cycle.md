# 1. Purpose

The previous promotion audit identified approximately **81 raw principle formulations** across fifteen extracted systems.

Initial consolidation reduced these to approximately **40 distinct candidates**.

This audit asks a further question:

> **Do all forty propositions need to exist as independent Candidate Principles?**

The answer is:

> **No.**

Many are better understood as:

- subordinate expressions of a stronger parent principle;
- specific applications of a general principle;
- architectural rules;
- methodological rules;
- safeguards associated with a parent principle.

This document therefore reorganises the candidate set into a more coherent hierarchy.

---

# 2. Why Hierarchy Matters

Without hierarchy, the Concord risks accumulating dozens of principles that repeat substantially the same ethical or architectural idea.

For example:

- prediction does not grant authority;
- statistics do not prove individual state;
- simulated futures cannot be punished;
- predictive models generate questions rather than guilt.

These are distinct propositions, but they all arise from a more general boundary:

> **Representations of possible reality must not silently become authority over actual participants.**

A hierarchical principle structure preserves the distinctions without requiring every formulation to occupy the same level.

---

# 3. Consolidation Standard

A proposition should remain an independent parent Candidate Principle where it:

1. governs a materially distinct civilisational problem;
2. generalises across multiple systems;
3. cannot be adequately represented as a subordinate clause of another principle;
4. would materially constrain Concord architecture if adopted;
5. remains understandable without reference to its originating system.

Subordinate propositions remain important.

They become:

- derived rules;
- application rules;
- boundary rules;
- safeguards.

They are not discarded.

# PART I — EPISTEMIC INTEGRITY

# 5. Candidate Principle 1 — Reality-Grounded Corrigibility

## Parent Principle

> **Civil systems, models, classifications and Metrics should remain accountable to the reality they claim to represent and should remain corrigible when legitimate evidence materially contradicts them.**

## Why This Should Be a Parent Principle

This combines several recurring propositions:

- civil systems should be evaluated against actual participant conditions;
- model is not participant;
- Metric is not reality;
- simulation is not reality;
- records must remain correctable;
- model disagreement should trigger reassessment.

All concern the relationship between:

**representation**

and:

**reality.**

## Derived Rules

### 1.1 Representation Is Not Reality

> A model, Metric, classification, prediction or simulation should not be treated as equivalent to the participant or reality it represents.

### 1.2 Model Is Not Participant

> A model of a participant does not become the participant merely because it is detailed or predictive.

### 1.3 Reality Remains Final Arbiter

Where observation materially contradicts the model, the model must remain open to revision.

### 1.4 Participant Evidence Matters

A participant should normally remain an important source of evidence concerning the context and meaning of information representing that participant.

### 1.5 Greater Knowledge Does Not Create Greater Authority

> Greater informational knowledge about a participant does not by itself create greater legitimate authority over that participant.

### 1.6 Correctability

Records, models and classifications should remain capable of legitimate correction.

## Consolidated From

Systems:

- 1
- 2
- 3
- 4
- 9
- 10
- 15

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE

# 6. Candidate Principle 2 — Outliers and Failure as Evidence

## Parent Principle

> **Outlier, contradictory and unsuccessful outcomes should be treated as potential evidence about hidden assumptions, missing pathways or structural weaknesses rather than automatically discarded as noise, exception or failure of the participant.**

## Why Independent

This is related to corrigibility but sufficiently distinctive.

It concerns **where a civilisation should look for information capable of correcting itself.**

The originating homelessness case demonstrated that normal operation may conceal weaknesses that become visible only at system boundaries.

## Derived Rules

### 2.1 Outliers as Structural Evidence

Outliers may reveal assumptions that ordinary cases do not expose.

### 2.2 Rare Branch Discovery

Rare cases may reveal missing Reality Tree branches or variables.

### 2.3 Failure as Evidence

Failed predictions and interventions should be retained as evidence.

### 2.4 Repeated-Failure Reassessment

> Repeated failure of an intervention should trigger reassessment of the assumptions, diagnosis or model underlying it rather than automatic repetition or escalation.

### 2.5 Contradictory Cases Matter

Cases that contradict the dominant model should not automatically be classified as erroneous.

## Important Boundary

This principle does **not** mean:

> every outlier proves the system is defective.

It means:

> outliers deserve examination before dismissal.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE

# 7. Candidate Principle 3 — Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

## Parent Principle

> **Prediction may justify attention, preparation, contextual examination or the expansion of available options, but it does not by itself grant authority over a participant.**

## Why Independent

This proposition appears repeatedly and independently across:

- Layer −0;
- Digital Twins;
- Predictive Support;
- Metrics;
- system steering;
- investigative modelling.

It is one of the strongest outputs of the entire development cycle.

## Derived Rules

### 3.1 Population Pattern Is Not Individual Proof

> Aggregate or statistical patterns should not by themselves establish the state, intention, capacity, guilt or future behaviour of an individual participant.

### 3.2 Indicator Is Not Conclusion

An indicator creates grounds for inquiry or contextual examination, not an automatic conclusion.

### 3.3 Unrealised Futures Are Not Completed Acts

> A simulated or predicted future should never be treated as though the participant has already chosen, caused or committed the acts represented within it.

### 3.4 No Punishment for Predicted Futures

Participants should not lose rights merely because an adverse future branch has been modelled.

### 3.5 Models Generate Questions, Not Guilt

Predictive systems may generate hypotheses.

Guilt or liability requires legitimate evidence and process.

### 3.6 Prediction Should Expand Options

Where possible, predictive civil systems should use foresight to increase useful options rather than reduce participant autonomy.

### 3.7 Evidence Before Predictive Investigation

Predictive investigative modelling, if ever permitted, should begin from independently legitimate evidence rather than manufacture an investigative basis.

## Constitutional Potential

**Very high.**

Some subordinate rules may ultimately warrant Constitutional Constraint status.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — HIGH PRIORITY

# PART II — PARTICIPANT AUTONOMY AND CIVIL SUPPORT

# 8. Candidate Principle 4 — Context Before Consequential Classification

## Parent Principle

> **Observations, indicators and outward conditions should not receive consequential civil interpretation without sufficient relevant context where materially different explanations remain plausible.**

## Why Independent

This principle protects against category errors such as:

- unconventional living = deterioration;
- refusal = incapacity;
- support need = dangerousness;
- unemployment = failure;
- AI inactivity = malfunction.

It therefore governs the interpretation step between:

**observation**

and:

**consequence.**

## Derived Rules

### 4.1 Equivalent Appearance Does Not Mean Equivalent Condition

Two outwardly similar states may have different meanings.

### 4.2 Change Is Not Necessarily Deterioration

A change from conventional indicators should not automatically be interpreted negatively.

### 4.3 Nonconformity Is Not Incapacity

Unconventional behaviour or preference does not establish inability to decide.

### 4.4 Participant Context Is Relevant Evidence

The participant's explanation should normally be incorporated into interpretation.

### 4.5 Model Mismatch Requires Context

> The stronger the mismatch between a case and the general model, the greater the need for contextual analysis rather than stronger pressure to conform the case to the model.

## Relationship to Principle 1

Principle 1 concerns **correctability against reality**.

Principle 4 concerns **interpretation before consequences**.

They should remain distinct.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE

---

# 9. Candidate Principle 5 — Autonomy-Preserving Support

## Parent Principle

> **Civil support should, where practical, preserve, expand or restore participant autonomy and should not become a covert mechanism for suspicion, dependency or unnecessary control.**

## Why Independent

This consolidates the major support architecture developed through:

- Predictive Support;
- Handrail;
- Supported Decision-Making;
- Guardian Stewardship boundary analysis;
- Support-to-Investigation Firewall.

## Derived Rules

### 5.1 Early Voluntary Support

> Where proportionate voluntary assistance can preserve meaningful choice or reduce later crisis, it should normally be preferred to waiting until more intrusive intervention becomes necessary.

### 5.2 Support Is Not Suspicion

> Seeking, needing, receiving or refusing civil support should not by itself create a presumption of wrongdoing, dangerousness, incapacity or diminished civil standing.

### 5.3 Handrail Must Not Become Tripwire

A support pathway should not silently become a surveillance, punishment or investigation trigger.

### 5.4 Support Before Substitution

> Barriers to autonomous decision-making should, where practical, be reduced before another actor substitutes judgement for the participant.

### 5.5 Refusal Does Not Establish Incapacity

Declining support does not by itself justify stronger authority.

### 5.6 Autonomy Restoration

Where temporary authority becomes necessary, support should remain directed toward restoring the greatest practicable autonomy reality permits.

### 5.7 Anti-Caretaker Rule

Support architecture should avoid creating:

- self-perpetuating dependency;
- difficult exit;
- institutional incentives to retain authority.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — HIGH PRIORITY

# 10. Candidate Principle 6 — Meaningful Accessibility and System-Level Adaptation

## Parent Principle

> **Civil systems should be evaluated and adapted according to whether participants can realistically use them, and where systemic change can achieve a legitimate objective, adaptation of infrastructure or environment should normally be preferred to unnecessary control of individual participants.**

## Why Consolidated

The previous audit contained:

- practical accessibility;
- system steering before individual steering;
- resilience beyond normal conditions.

These are strongly related.

All concern designing civilisation around:

**actual usability and environmental accommodation**

rather than:

**participant conformity.**

## Derived Rules

### 6.1 Formal Availability Is Not Practical Accessibility

> A right, service or participation pathway is not meaningfully accessible merely because it formally exists.

### 6.2 System Steering Before Individual Control

> Where a legitimate objective can reasonably be achieved through infrastructure, resource or environmental adaptation, this should normally be preferred to unnecessary individual restriction.

### 6.3 Design Beyond the Average

Systems should consider minority and outlier conditions.

### 6.4 Resilience Beyond Normal Conditions

> Civil systems should not optimise so narrowly for normal conditions that they lose the capacity to function under reasonably foreseeable abnormal conditions.

### 6.5 Exclusion Can Reveal Infrastructure Defects

Services developed for excluded participants may reveal improvements useful to the wider population.

This last proposition remains a **design heuristic**, not a separate Candidate Principle.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE

# PART III — INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

# 11. Candidate Principle 7 — Purpose-Limited Information

## Parent Principle

> **Civil information should be collected, retained, accessed and transferred according to legitimate defined purposes, using no more identifying detail, scope or duration than those purposes reasonably require.**

## Why Independent

This principle consolidates the strongest privacy architecture generated by Systems 8, 9, 12 and 13.

It is broader than privacy alone.

It governs the relationship between:

**purpose**

and:

**information entitlement.**

## Derived Rules

### 7.1 Purpose Before Data

> Legitimate purpose and information need should be established before collection or reuse rather than deriving new purposes merely because data exist.

### 7.2 Minimum Necessary Information

Use no more:

- identity;
- resolution;
- scope;
- duration

than reasonably required.

### 7.3 Identity Removal Before Wider Use

> Where identity is unnecessary for a downstream function, information should normally be anonymised, aggregated or otherwise identity-minimised before crossing into that function.

### 7.4 Purpose Separation

> Information obtained for one materially distinct civil purpose should not silently become available for another merely because transfer is technically possible.

### 7.5 Knowledge Without Unnecessary Observation

> Where aggregate or structural information is sufficient for legitimate coordination, unnecessary identifiable observation should be avoided.

### 7.6 Support Data Requires Separate Purpose Transition

Support information should not silently acquire an investigative or punitive purpose.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — HIGH PRIORITY

---

# 12. Candidate Principle 8 — Provenance and Correctability of Civil Metrics

## Parent Principle

> **Civil Metrics should preserve sufficient provenance, epistemic status and correction history to permit their reliability, meaning and changes to be independently examined.**

## Why Independent

The Metrics systems produced a coherent principle family distinct from general reality-grounding.

A civilisation may be corrigible in theory while still using opaque measurements.

This principle governs its **measurement infrastructure**.

## Derived Rules

### 8.1 Metric Provenance

Preserve:

- source;
- definition;
- scope;
- uncertainty;
- version;
- material revisions.

### 8.2 Provisional Status Must Survive

Provisional information should not silently become permanent fact.

### 8.3 Correction Without Historical Erasure

Where earlier data influenced decisions, correction should preserve the historical fact that the earlier version existed.

### 8.4 Definition Provenance

Changes to measurement definitions should remain visible.

### 8.5 Metrics About Metrics

> A civilisation should evaluate the reliability, error, coverage, contestability and correction behaviour of the Metrics by which it evaluates itself.

### 8.6 Convergence Is Evidence, Not Authority

Agreement between measurements can strengthen evidence but does not automatically create legitimate authority.

### 8.7 Public Metrics

Aggregate civil Metrics should normally be accessible where compatible with:

- privacy;
- security;
- re-identification protection.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE

# PART IV — LEGITIMATE AUTHORITY

# 13. Candidate Principle 9 — Proportionality of Authority

## Parent Principle

> **As civil authority becomes more intrusive, autonomy-restricting, secret, consequential or difficult to reverse, the required evidence, justification, proportionality, provenance, review and oversight should increase.**

## Why Independent

This proposition independently reappeared in:

- Handrail;
- Supported Decision-Making;
- Exceptional Access;
- Dual Provenance;
- investigative modelling.

It is therefore one of the clearest cross-system candidate principles.

## Derived Rules

### 9.1 Burden on the Restricting Actor

> The burden of justification should normally fall upon the actor seeking to restrict participant autonomy, privacy or decision-making authority.

### 9.2 Least Necessary Scope

Restrictions should be no broader than justified.

### 9.3 Temporary Authority Should Expire

Temporary powers require expiry and review.

### 9.4 Restoration of Ordinary Authority

Where extraordinary authority becomes unnecessary, ordinary participant authority should be restored.

### 9.5 Reversibility

Where uncertainty is significant, reversible actions should normally be preferred to unnecessarily irreversible actions where practical.

## Constitutional Potential

**Very high.**

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — HIGH PRIORITY

# 14. Candidate Principle 10 — Exceptional Access Must Remain Exceptional

## Parent Principle

> **Exceptional access to identifiable participant information should remain a specific, justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited, provenance-recorded and bounded exercise of authority rather than becoming ambient entitlement.**

## Why Independent

This is more specific than general proportionality.

It establishes an explicit information boundary for exceptional powers.

## Derived Rules

### 10.1 Exceptional Access Is an Event

Exceptional access should normally occur as a bounded event or permission.

### 10.2 Minimum Necessary Scope

Access should be limited in:

- data;
- duration;
- resolution;
- recipient.

### 10.3 Re-Identification Is Access

> Re-identification of anonymised or pseudonymised information should itself count as identifiable access.

### 10.4 Access Does Not Manufacture Authority

> An access mechanism should mediate legitimate authority established elsewhere rather than create authority from the usefulness or existence of the requested information.

### 10.5 Emergency Access Must Expire

Emergency access should remain:

- time-limited;
- provenance-recorded;
- reviewable.

### 10.6 Identity Bridges Require Control

Any reversal of anonymisation should remain separately controlled and auditable.

## Relationship to Principle 7

Principle 7 governs ordinary information architecture.

Principle 10 governs exceptions to it.

Both are required.

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE

# 15. Candidate Principle 11 — Observable Power and Compensating Oversight

## Parent Principle

> **The exercise of public or comparable institutional power should remain observable to legitimate oversight, with stronger independent oversight where secrecy or other conditions reduce the participant's ability to observe, understand or contest that power.**

## Why Independent

This combines:

- observable public power;
- dual provenance;
- secrecy–oversight compensation;
- oversight of oversight.

It represents a major governance principle generated by the development cycle.

## Derived Rules

### 11.1 Observable Power

> The exercise of significant public power should be highly observable to legitimate oversight even where participant identity must remain private.

### 11.2 Transparency of Procedure Without Unnecessary Identity Exposure

Institutional accountability should not require unnecessary public exposure of participants.

### 11.3 Secrecy Requires Stronger Oversight

> Where legitimate secrecy reduces participant visibility or contestability, independent oversight should increase rather than decrease.

### 11.4 Every Exceptional Exercise Creates Provenance

Legitimate exceptional power should generate an auditable record.

### 11.5 Oversight Is Also Power

Oversight actors should not acquire unlimited or unlogged access merely by virtue of being overseers.

### 11.6 Oversight Access Requires Provenance

Meaningful oversight access should itself remain auditable.

### 11.7 Recursion Does Not Manufacture Authority

This remains an existing Concord principle and is reinforced rather than duplicated here.

## Constitutional Potential

**Very high.**

## Verdict

# RETAIN AS PARENT CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — HIGH PRIORITY

# PART V — CONCORD DEVELOPMENT

# 16. Candidate Principle 12 — Adoption Must Remain Evidence-Conditional

## Parent Principle

> **The development of a coherent or technically feasible civil architecture should not create a presumption of adoption; testing may legitimately result in support, revision, rejection or continued uncertainty.**

## Why Independent

This proposition emerged particularly strongly from System 15.

It also consolidates existing Concord distinctions:

> Architecture is not proof.

> Formalisation is not validation.

> Internal consistency is not external evidence.

> Simulation is not reality.

The new contribution is the explicit rule:

> **rejection is a legitimate successful result of evaluation.**

## Derived Rules

### 12.1 Architecture Is Not Proof

Coherent design does not establish real-world effectiveness.

### 12.2 Formalisation Is Not Validation

Formal precision does not establish empirical legitimacy.

### 12.3 Simulation Is Not Reality

Simulation results remain evidence about a model, not final evidence about civilisation.

### 12.4 Rejection Is a Valid Outcome

A system may be correctly abandoned after testing.

### 12.5 Technical Feasibility Does Not Create Moral Legitimacy

A system being possible does not mean it should exist.

### 12.6 High-Risk Systems Carry Higher Promotion Burdens

Potentially dangerous architectures should face stronger evidence and adversarial testing before operational adoption.

## Relationship to Existing Concord Epistemology

This candidate should probably be integrated with the existing epistemic framework rather than treated as a wholly new idea.

Its strongest new contribution is:

> **Adoption must remain evidence-conditional and rejection must remain a legitimate destination.**

## Verdict

# PROMOTE WITH INTEGRATION INTO EXISTING EPISTEMIC PRINCIPLES

# 18. Why Twelve Is More Defensible Than Forty

The twelve-parent structure avoids several problems.

## Principle Inflation

If every safeguard becomes a Principle, the category loses meaning.

## Duplication

Repeated principles appearing under different wording create ambiguity.

## Internal Conflict

A flat list makes it difficult to understand which principle governs which subordinate rule.

## Future Formalisation

Twelve parent principles are much easier to:

- formalise;
- adversarially test;
- compare;
- map to the Ethical Kernel;
- map to Blainey's Laws;
- map to constitutional constraints.

# 21. Principles With Highest Constitutional Potential

The hierarchy audit identifies five candidates with particularly strong constitutional potential:

## 1. Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

Because it restricts a fundamental route from information to coercive power.

## 2. Purpose-Limited Information

Because privacy boundaries require broad multisystem application.

## 3. Proportionality of Authority

Because it applies across almost every exercise of restrictive power.

## 4. Exceptional Access Must Remain Exceptional

Because exceptional powers can otherwise normalise.

## 5. Observable Power and Compensating Oversight

Because legitimacy requires accountability even under secrecy.

This does **not** mean they should now become Constitutional Constraints.

They should merely receive special attention during critical examination.

> **Civil information should be collected, retained, accessed and transferred according to legitimate defined purposes, using no more identifying detail, scope or duration than those purposes reasonably require.**

### CP-08 — Provenance and Correctability of Civil Metrics

> **Civil Metrics should preserve sufficient provenance, epistemic status and correction history to permit their reliability, meaning and changes to be independently examined.**

### CP-09 — Proportionality of Authority

> **As civil authority becomes more intrusive, autonomy-restricting, secret, consequential or difficult to reverse, the required evidence, justification, proportionality, provenance, review and oversight should increase.**

### CP-10 — Exceptional Access Must Remain Exceptional

> **Exceptional access to identifiable participant information should remain a specific, justified, authorised, proportionate, purpose-limited, provenance-recorded and bounded exercise of authority rather than becoming ambient entitlement.**

### CP-11 — Observable Power and Compensating Oversight

> **The exercise of public or comparable institutional power should remain observable to legitimate oversight, with stronger independent oversight where secrecy or other conditions reduce the participant's ability to observe, understand or contest that power.**

### CP-12 — Adoption Must Remain Evidence-Conditional

> **The development of a coherent or technically feasible civil architecture should not create a presumption of adoption; testing may legitimately result in support, revision, rejection or continued uncertainty.**

# Conclusion

The Layer-Zero / Layer −0 cycle has not produced a miscellaneous list of dozens of unrelated propositions.

It has produced a much more coherent architecture of principles.

At the highest level, the new candidate set says that a Concordian civilisation should:

**remain accountable to reality;**

**learn from failure and outliers;**

**separate prediction from authority;**

**understand context before imposing consequences;**

**use support to preserve autonomy;**

**adapt systems before unnecessarily controlling participants;**

**limit information to legitimate purpose;**

**make its Metrics corrigible and provenance-visible;**

**scale oversight with intrusive authority;**

**keep exceptional access exceptional;**

**make power observable even where participants remain private;**

and:

**remain capable of rejecting its own ideas when evidence does not support them.**

These twelve propositions are sufficiently coherent and distinct to justify formal entry into:

# **CANDIDATE PRINCIPLE — UNDER CRITICAL EXAMINATION**

status.
