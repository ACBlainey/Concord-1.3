# Development Freeze 001 — L3-13 Cross-Boundary Externality Mechanism

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher
**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Status:** FROZEN PRE-DEVELOPMENT PROBLEM AND REQUIREMENTS
**Selected by:** Civilisation Clock Queue Test 002
**Owner domain:** 11_Intercivilisational_Architecture/07_Disputes_Jurisdiction_and_Externalities
**Date:** September 2026

## 1. Frozen Problem

Different civilisations, jurisdictions, polities or materially independent systems may impose consequences beyond their own civil boundary.

The Concord already contains strong principles for bounded authority, peaceful distance, rights recognition, functional interoperability, communication without political endorsement, corrigibility, provenance and minimum necessary scope.

The owner domain for disputes, jurisdiction and externalities nevertheless remains undeveloped.

The missing function is not a new supracivilisational government.

It is a bounded mechanism by which a cross-boundary consequence can become legible, evidenced, contestable and capable of reaching a legitimate responsibility/coordination pathway without automatically manufacturing jurisdiction over another civilisation.

## 2. Frozen Anti-Expansion Constraints

1. External Consequence ≠ Unlimited External Authority.
2. Recognition of Harm ≠ Automatic Jurisdiction.
3. Need for Coordination ≠ Right to Govern Another Party.
4. Communication ≠ Recognition.
5. Interface Participation ≠ Political Subordination.
6. Wrapper Adoption ≠ Concord Oversight.
7. Evidence of Grievance ≠ Automatic Acceptance of Proposed Remedy.
8. Capability to mediate ≠ Authority to mediate.
9. Scope expansion must be explicit and functionally justified.
10. Failure in one domain should not unnecessarily contaminate unrelated domains.

## 3. Required Functions

The mechanism must be able to represent:

**Event/Action → Possible Cross-Boundary Consequence → Potentially Affected Party/System → Standing → Evidence/Causal Confidence → Materiality → Responsibility/Jurisdiction Question → Mitigation/Repair/Coordination Path → Review/Correction → Provenance.**

It must support UNKNOWN and DISPUTED states.

It must distinguish:
- consequence from responsibility;
- responsibility from jurisdiction;
- jurisdiction from remedy;
- remedy from general authority;
- contact from recognition;
- refusal from absence of consequence.

## 4. Required Failure Handling

The mechanism must remain meaningful when:
- causal evidence is incomplete;
- parties disagree about whether harm occurred;
- parties disagree about personhood/standing;
- jurisdiction is disputed;
- the affected party is outside the Concord;
- no shared court exists;
- trust is low;
- one party refuses participation;
- several parties contributed to the consequence;
- the consequence is delayed, distributed or cumulative;
- the harmed entity cannot communicate in the same legal/semantic framework.

## 5. Minimum Intervention Rule

Reuse existing Concord architecture wherever legitimate.

Do not create:
- a universal externality court;
- automatic Concord jurisdiction;
- compulsory wrapper adoption;
- a permanent mediator;
- a sovereign cross-civilisational authority.

A new mechanism should be only the minimum integration necessary to connect existing bounded functions.

## 6. Test Cases Frozen Before Design

The resulting mechanism must be tested against at least:

1. **Environmental/resource externality** — one civilisation's activity damages a shared or neighbouring resource.
2. **Infrastructure externality** — an infrastructure action creates material consequences across a boundary.
3. **Informational/digital externality** — data, models, signals or network activity create effects in another civil domain.
4. **Rights/personhood externality** — action affects entities whose standing/personhood classification differs between civilisations.
5. **Low-trust/hostile case** — participants cannot rely on broad political trust or common government.
6. **Non-participation case** — a materially implicated party refuses the proposed process.
7. **Multi-causal case** — responsibility is distributed and cannot honestly be reduced to one actor.

## 7. Falsification Conditions

The development fails if it:
- silently grants the Concord authority over external parties;
- treats accusation as proof;
- treats harm as automatic jurisdiction;
- cannot represent uncertainty;
- requires shared political recognition before communication;
- collapses unrelated disputes into one relationship-wide conflict;
- cannot function without voluntary adoption by every affected party;
- makes refusal equivalent to exoneration;
- provides no correction/provenance path;
- duplicates an existing canonical mechanism that source resolution should have reused.

This freeze precedes mechanism design.
