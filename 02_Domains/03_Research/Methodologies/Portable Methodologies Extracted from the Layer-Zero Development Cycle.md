# 1. Purpose

The Layer-Zero development cycle generated more than civil systems.

It also produced methods that can be used to examine problems across multiple domains.

These should be separated from the systems they first appeared within.

A method is portable when it describes **how to reason, test, analyse or design**, rather than prescribing one particular institutional mechanism.

# 2. Outlier-First Design

## Question

> **Which participant is the normal architecture serving badly, and what assumption does that reveal?**

## Method

**NORMAL ARCHITECTURE**

↓

**IDENTIFY BADLY SERVED OUTLIER**

↓

**TRACE FAILURE**

↓

**IDENTIFY HIDDEN ASSUMPTION**

↓

**DESIGN ACCOMMODATION**

↓

**ASK WHETHER ACCOMMODATION IMPROVES GENERAL ARCHITECTURE**

↓

**REDESIGN CORE WHERE APPROPRIATE.**

## General Applications

- homelessness;
- disability;
- AI participation;
- mobile workers;
- off-world citizens;
- emergency evacuees;
- ageing;
- minority governance;
- unusual economic participants;
- multisubstrate interfaces.

## Status

**Strong candidate portable methodology.**

# 3. Layer-Zero Vertical Trace

## Question

> **Can an abstract Concord mechanism be traced all the way down to lived reality?**

## Method

**LAYER ZERO**

→ **SERVICE**

→ **METRICS**

→ **COORDINATION**

→ **GOVERNANCE**

→ **JUDICIARY**

→ **CONSTITUTION**

→ **RECURSIVE OVERSIGHT.**

Then reverse the direction:

> **Does the constitutional architecture actually change the Layer-Zero outcome?**

## Purpose

Detect abstract architecture that is coherent on paper but operationally disconnected.

## Status

**Candidate universal architecture-validation method.**

# 4. Layer −0 Counterfactual Analysis

## Question

> **Before taking consequential action, what plausible alternatives and consequences can be modelled safely?**

## Method

**CURRENT STATE**

↓

**POSSIBLE TRAJECTORIES**

↓

**REALITY TREE**

↓

**ALTERNATIVE ACTIONS**

↓

**COUNTERFACTUAL CONSEQUENCES**

↓

**UNCERTAINTY**

↓

**AUTONOMY / HARM / REVERSIBILITY COMPARISON**

↓

**REAL-WORLD CHOICE.**

## Critical Limit

> **Simulation is not reality.**

## Applications

Potentially universal.

# 5. Prediction-to-Options Test

## Question

> **Does prediction increase the participant's available choices, or increase institutional authority over them?**

## Preferred Pattern

**PREDICTION**

→ **CONTEXT**

→ **MORE OPTIONS**

rather than:

**PREDICTION**

→ **LESS FREEDOM.**

## Candidate Rule

> **Prediction creates grounds for attention, not authority.**

## Applications

- welfare;
- health;
- finance;
- education;
- ageing;
- emergency planning;
- AI behaviour;
- resource allocation.

# 6. Handrail Design Method

## Question

> **Can support be made available before crisis without forcing use?**

## Test

A proposed support mechanism should ask:

- Is it available before severe failure?
- Can it be ignored?
- Is it easy to reach?
- Does using it reduce rights?
- Does it preserve privacy?
- Can support intensity rise proportionately?
- Can it later fall again?
- Does it help preserve autonomy?
- Can unusual participants use it?
- Does it secretly trigger unrelated systems?

## General Principle

> **Support should be easy to reach; authority should be difficult to acquire.**

## Applications

Extremely broad.

---

# 7. Handrail-versus-Tripwire Test

## Question

> **Does accessing a beneficial civil service expose the participant to unrelated penalties or surveillance?**

## Method

For each support system, trace all downstream data pathways.

**SUPPORT DATA**

→ Where does it go?

→ Who can access it?

→ What unrelated decisions can it influence?

→ Can it create suspicion?

→ Can it create economic disadvantage?

→ Can it create behavioural scoring?

## Failure Condition

If accessing support creates unrelated punitive exposure, the handrail may have become a tripwire.

## Applications

- healthcare;
- mental health;
- addiction;
- homelessness;
- welfare;
- education;
- AI support;
- financial assistance.

# 8. System-Steering Before Individual-Steering Test

## Question

> **Can the legitimate objective be achieved by changing the system or environment instead of targeting individuals?**

## Method

Before individual intervention:

1. Define harm or objective.
2. Identify environmental contributors.
3. Identify service failures.
4. Identify infrastructure changes.
5. Model system-level intervention.
6. Compare with person-level intervention.
7. Prefer lower-intrusion solution where reasonably effective.

## Examples

- redesign dangerous junction;
- expand hospital capacity;
- repair service bottleneck;
- improve housing access;
- change product design.

## Status

**Strong candidate general methodology.**

# 9. Model-Mismatch Escalation Method

## Question

> **What should happen when reality does not fit the model?**

## Wrong Response

**MODEL MISMATCH**

→ **FORCE CASE INTO MODEL.**

## Preferred Response

**MODEL MISMATCH**

→ **INCREASE CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS**

→ **SEARCH MISSING VARIABLES**

→ **SEARCH NEW BRANCH**

→ **UPDATE MODEL IF WARRANTED.**

## General Principle

> **Mismatch should increase curiosity before it increases conformity pressure.**

# 10. Rare-Branch Discovery Method

## Question

> **Is an unusual outcome noise, or evidence of a missing branch?**

## Method

When predicted branches are A/B/C but reality produces D:

- verify D;
- identify hidden variables;
- test classification;
- examine contextual uniqueness;
- compare other cases;
- determine whether D deserves a new branch;
- recalibrate probabilities.

## Applications

- medicine;
- economics;
- AI behaviour;
- social policy;
- governance;
- engineering failure;
- safety analysis.

# 12. Reality-to-Metrics Method

## Question

> **How directly can a metric be connected to ordinary operational reality?**

## Method

**REAL EVENT**

→ **SOURCE RECORD**

→ **DEFINED EXTRACTION**

→ **ANONYMISATION**

→ **METRIC**

with transformation provenance retained.

## Purpose

Reduce discretionary reporting layers and make manipulation points visible.

## General Principle

> **Reality should enter Metrics as directly as practical, while identity should enter only where necessary.**

# 13. Metrics-about-Metrics Method

## Question

> **How reliable is the measurement architecture itself?**

## Measure

- revision rates;
- dispute rates;
- correction rates;
- source disagreement;
- missingness;
- false positives;
- false negatives;
- definition changes;
- prediction error.

## Purpose

Prevent Metrics from becoming a self-certifying authority.

## Status

Strong candidate universal Metrics methodology.

# 14. Measurement–Interpretation–Context Separation

## Question

> **Are we confusing what was measured with what it means?**

## Method

Separate:

**MEASUREMENT**

from:

**INTERPRETATION**

from:

**PARTICIPANT CONTEXT**

from:

**PREDICTION**

from:

**DECISION.**

## Example

Fewer working hours may be measured accurately.

Interpreting them as deterioration may still be wrong.

## Applications

Potentially all Concord Metrics.

---

# 15. Provisional-to-Final Evidence Tracking

## Question

> **How does a claim change as evidence improves?**

## Method

**INITIAL**

→ **SUSPECTED**

→ **PROVISIONAL**

→ **CONFIRMED / REVISED / REJECTED.**

Preserve transitions.

Do not silently overwrite them.

## Purpose

Supports:

- provenance;
- diagnostic learning;
- research reproducibility;
- judicial evidence;
- model calibration.

# 16. Definition-Provenance Method

## Question

> **Did reality change, or did the measurement definition change?**

## Method

Version:

- metric definitions;
- extraction rules;
- classification rules;
- transformations.

Attach effective dates and change rationale.

## Purpose

Prevent false historical trends.

# 17. Authority–Provenance Scaling Method

## Question

> **As institutional authority increases, does accountability increase with it?**

## Test

**AUTHORITY ↑**

should imply:

**JUSTIFICATION ↑**

**PROVENANCE ↑**

**REVIEW ↑**

**OVERSIGHT ↑.**

If authority rises while observability falls, flag architectural danger.

## Applications

- policing;
- emergency powers;
- Guardianship;
- judiciary;
- AI control;
- constitutional exceptions;
- medical incapacity.

**SECRECY ↑**

## Purpose

Prevent confidentiality or security from becoming unaccountable institutional invisibility.

# 20. Purpose-Boundary Test

## Question

> **Is data collected for purpose A silently being used for purpose B?**

## Method

For every transfer ask:

1. Original purpose?
2. New purpose?
3. Is identity necessary?
4. Is participant consent relevant?
5. Is independent authority required?
6. Is data minimised?
7. Is transfer provenance-recorded?
8. Does the new use materially alter participant risk?

## Principle

> **Possession for one legitimate purpose does not automatically authorise another.**

# 21. Support-Prediction Firewall Method

## Question

> **Is a model designed to help a participant being repurposed to judge or suspect them?**

## Test

Trace:

**SUPPORT INDICATOR**

→ **SUPPORT SYSTEM**

and ensure it does not silently become:

**SUPPORT INDICATOR**

→ **SUSPICION SCORE**

→ **INVESTIGATIVE ATTENTION.**

## Principle

> **Support is not suspicion.**

# 22. Evidence-Conditioned Investigation Method

## Question

> **Does investigative narrowing begin with evidence about an actual case or with general predictions about people?**

## Legitimate Direction

**ACTUAL EVENT**

→ **ACTUAL EVIDENCE**

→ **HYPOTHESES**

→ **PROPORTIONATE INVESTIGATION.**

## Dangerous Direction

**GENERAL RISK PROFILE**

→ **PERSON**

→ **SUSPICION.**

This method may be useful in evaluating future predictive policing proposals.

# 23. Autonomy-Restoration Test

## Question

> **Does continuing support still increase the participant's practical autonomy?**

Measure:

- decision-making retained;
- unnecessary restrictions removed;
- support reduction where possible;
- participant preferences;
- capability;
- dependency caused by system itself.

## Important Qualification

For progressive or permanent conditions, autonomy preservation rather than full independence may be the proper target.

---

# 24. Infrastructure-Accessibility Test

## Question

> **Does the service merely exist, or can the participant actually use it?**

Check:

- physical reach;
- cost;
- paperwork;
- communications;
- waiting time;
- eligibility;
- digital access;
- privacy;
- stigma;
- punitive consequences.

This is a Layer-Zero reality test applicable across services.

# 28. Promotion Criteria

A methodology should not become a Core Concordian Methodology merely because it appears useful in one development cycle.

Candidate promotion path:

**METHOD IDENTIFIED**

↓

**FORMALLY SPECIFIED**

↓

**APPLIED TO ORIGINAL CASE**

↓

**APPLIED TO UNRELATED CASE**

↓

**APPLIED ACROSS MULTIPLE DOMAINS**

↓

**FAILURE MODES DOCUMENTED**

↓

**COMPARED WITH ALTERNATIVES**

↓

**RESULTS PRESERVED**

↓

**REVISED**

↓

**READY FOR EVALUATION**

↓

**SUPPORTED / REVISED / REJECTED.**

This protects the distinction:

> **Generativity is evidence of usefulness. It is not validation.**

# Conclusion

The Layer-Zero development cycle did not merely generate answers to homelessness.

It generated a family of reasoning and design methods.

That is potentially more important than any single proposed service.

A system may eventually prove incorrect.

A good methodology can still help explain why it failed.

The strongest methodological lesson from the cycle is:

> **Begin with reality, expose the assumptions through edge cases, model multiple futures without confusing them with facts, prefer architecture that increases options before authority, observe what actually happens, and allow the failure of the model to teach the civilisation.**

That principle is portable across almost every part of the Concord.
