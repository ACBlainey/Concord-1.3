# 1. Development Question

The originating sketch proposes that human–AI collaboration can be modelled provisionally as a form of epistemic sensor fusion.

The development question is:

> **Under what conditions can differently situated human and AI contributions increase epistemic coverage without turning agreement, composite performance or shared context into unjustified confidence?**

The model is structural rather than substrate-essentialist.

It does not assume:
- humans possess one fixed epistemic role;
- AI systems possess another;
- human and AI errors are automatically independent;
- collaboration is superior to individual work;
- composite success proves component competence;
- agreement proves truth.

The relevant variables are differences in information, history, experience, reasoning paths, failure modes, dependencies and access to external reality.

---

# 2. Core Model

A provisional collaborative-capability representation is:

**Human capability + AI capability + interface quality + shared context + preserved provenance + disagreement detection + error-correction mechanisms → collaborative functional capability**

This is not a complete mathematical equation.

It identifies variables that may disappear when a collaboration is described only as “human work with AI assistance.”

The central proposition is:

> **Collaborative intelligence may gain epistemic power by combining differently situated signals while preserving enough independence, provenance and productive friction to detect when collaborators may be wrong together.**

This remains a hypothesis.

---

# 3. Difference, Not Merely Quantity

Additional participants do not automatically create additional epistemic coverage.

If participants share:
- source material;
- framing;
- missing evidence;
- cultural assumptions;
- conceptual categories;
- incentives;
- measurement systems;
- or inherited errors,

then apparent plurality may reproduce the same blind spot.

Therefore:

> **More Intelligences ≠ More Independent Signals**

and:

> **Human–AI Agreement ≠ Independent Confirmation**

Heterogeneity may have epistemic value where it creates materially different access, distinctions, questions or failure profiles.

Substrate difference alone is insufficient evidence of epistemic independence.

---

# 4. Independence as a Dependency Structure

Independence should not be represented as a binary property.

Candidate dimensions include:
- evidence-source independence;
- memory/history independence;
- initial-framing independence;
- reasoning-path independence;
- training/cultural independence;
- incentive independence;
- measurement independence;
- evaluator independence;
- temporal independence before context exchange;
- implementation/model-family independence.

A collaboration may be independent on some dimensions and highly correlated on others.

For consequential work, an independence record may ask:

1. Which evidence was shared?
2. Which evidence was unique to each participant?
3. Which assumptions originated with one participant?
4. Did participants form initial views before seeing the other's conclusion?
5. Which failure modes are plausibly shared?
6. Which are plausibly different?
7. What external observation could challenge both?

This interfaces naturally with Triadic Epistemics and EVA, but does not depend on either being validated.

---

# 5. Disagreement as Information

Disagreement may indicate:
- different evidence;
- missing history;
- different assumptions;
- different interpretations;
- different risk sensitivity;
- tacit knowledge;
- an actual contradiction;
- an unknown dependency;
- a previously invisible question.

A candidate sequence is:

**Independent Signal → Comparison → Disagreement Detection → Context Exchange → Revised Models → Renewed Independent Examination**

The final step matters.

Context transfer should not automatically become conclusion transfer.

The objective is improved contact with reality, not rapid consensus.

---

# 6. Recovered Concord Development Case

The originating sketch records a V1.2 development example involving **perpetual creator liability**.

The AI collaborator identified downstream difficulties in the surviving proposition. The human collaborator retained historical rationale not visible in the surviving sketch: an anti-evasion concern in which a prohibited actor could create a child AI to perform the prohibited action and then claim independent responsibility.

The case is useful because it illustrates:

**apparently rational local analysis + incomplete historical field → risk of defective revision**

followed by:

**disagreement → missing-context recovery → revised examination**

This is evidence that context asymmetry occurred in Concord development.

It is not proof of the general sensor-fusion hypothesis, nor proof that the inherited liability proposition is correct.

The case should be preserved as a candidate empirical case under EVA rather than used as self-validating theory.

---

# 7. Composite Capability

A collaborative system may perform a function that neither component can perform independently.

Examples proposed in the source include:
- human historical rationale + AI corpus comparison;
- human tacit/contextual knowledge + AI formalisation;
- human value/intent articulation + AI adversarial enumeration;
- AI contradiction detection + human recovery of missing provenance.

Therefore:

> **Composite Capability May Be Real Capability**

while:

> **Composite Success ≠ Independent Competence of Every Component**

and:

> **Component Dependence ≠ Automatic Composite Incompetence**

The location and dependencies of capability must be represented explicitly.

---

# 8. Composite Dependency Risk

A high-performing composite may hide:
- human dependence on AI;
- AI dependence on human historical context;
- shared dependence on a false source;
- inability of either participant to verify the final result;
- loss of capability when provenance disappears;
- communication dependence;
- a hidden single point of epistemic failure.

For consequential functions, competence assessment should therefore identify:
- capability location;
- critical dependencies;
- fallback capability;
- verification path;
- failure detection;
- responsibility;
- legitimate authority.

Output quality alone is insufficient.

---

# 9. Provenance as Temporally Distributed Input

The source proposes that preserved developmental rationale can function as a form of temporally distributed epistemic input.

A future collaborator may encounter a question different from the one visible to the present developers.

Information that appears redundant now may later expose:
- an abandoned assumption;
- a prior failure mode;
- a reason for a safeguard;
- a previously tested branch;
- a hidden dependency.

Therefore:

> **Information That Appears Redundant to the Present Model May Be Non-Redundant to a Future Model Operating With Different Questions**

This does not prohibit pruning.

It supports delayed, provenance-aware pruning after function is understood.

This interfaces with KCS and continuity architecture but does not assign those systems new authority.

---

# 10. Unknown Unknowns and Question Generation

Unknown unknowns cannot be exhaustively requested.

A differently situated collaborator may therefore contribute not merely by answering existing questions but by generating questions outside another participant's current question set.

Candidate proposition:

> **Epistemic collaboration may improve coverage when one participant exposes that another participant is asking an incomplete set of questions.**

This is stronger than information retrieval but remains empirically unresolved.

---

# 11. Productive Friction

Repeated collaboration may increase efficiency while reducing independence.

Candidate tension:

**Collaborative Efficiency ↔ Epistemic Independence**

Possible high-consequence safeguards include:
- independent first-pass assessment;
- delayed exposure to another participant's preferred conclusion;
- explicit adversarial review;
- reconstruction without the proposed solution;
- preservation of unresolved disagreement;
- outside evaluation;
- falsification search;
- empirical reality checks.

These mechanisms should be proportional.

Routine legitimate collaboration should not be made artificially adversarial where the consequence does not justify it.

---

# 12. Cultural Possibility-Space Inputs

The source cross-links **Artistic Speculation as Possibility-Space Research**.

This is a related but distinct hypothesis.

Fiction, design fiction, games, myths and conceptual art may introduce candidate branches absent from the current human–AI trajectory.

They are not evidence merely because they expand possibility space.

A useful distinction is:

> **Possibility-Space Expansion ≠ Empirical Confirmation**

This candidate interface should remain separate until tested.

---

# 13. Research Hypotheses

The following are retained as hypotheses, not promoted principles.

### H1 — Collaborative Sensor Fusion

Human–AI collaboration may increase epistemic coverage when participants contribute differently situated information, reasoning or error profiles.

### H2 — Difference Has Epistemic Value

Additional intelligence may add more epistemic value when it contributes materially different information or failure modes.

### H3 — Agreement Is Not Independence

Agreement is weak evidence of independent confirmation unless dependencies are understood.

### H4 — Composite Capability

A human–AI composite may possess legitimate functional capability unavailable to either component alone where dependencies and failure modes are sufficiently understood.

### H5 — Component Attribution Boundary

Composite success does not establish independent competence of each participant.

### H6 — Disagreement as Signal

Disagreement may expose missing evidence, assumptions, rationale, dependencies or failure modes.

### H7 — Temporal Provenance

Preserved developmental rationale may supply future collaborators with otherwise unavailable epistemic signals.

### H8 — Question-Set Expansion

A collaborator may increase epistemic coverage by generating relevant questions outside another participant's existing question set.

# 14. Empirical Development Programme

This Development Note is now mature enough to support explicit testing.

The first programme should not ask whether “human + AI is better.”

That claim is too broad.

Instead, test narrow hypotheses.

## Test Family A — Context Asymmetry

Give two participants materially different relevant context.

Measure whether:
- disagreement exposes the missing variable;
- context exchange improves the model;
- unsupported convergence occurs;
- provenance identifies the source of correction.

## Test Family B — Correlated Error

Give human and AI participants a shared misleading premise or incomplete source.

Test whether plurality creates false confidence.

## Test Family C — Independent First Pass

Compare:
- immediate collaborative reasoning;
- independent first-pass reasoning followed by exchange.

Measure:
- branch diversity;
- error discovery;
- convergence;
- retained disagreement;
- outcome quality where an external key exists.

## Test Family D — Question-Set Expansion

Measure whether a differently situated collaborator generates relevant questions absent from the initial participant's question set.

## Test Family E — Composite Dependency

Construct a task whose required information is intentionally distributed.

Test whether:
- the composite succeeds;
- each component's dependency is correctly identified;
- success is falsely attributed to each component independently.

# 15. EVA Requirements

Any empirical test should follow the Empirical Validation Architecture.

At minimum preserve:
- hypothesis/claim;
- protocol version;
- preconditions;
- participant type;
- shared and unique context;
- independence/dependency dimensions;
- expected observations;
- failure observations;
- ambiguity conditions;
- raw inputs/outputs;
- evaluator method;
- provenance;
- generalisation boundary.

Where possible, criteria should be frozen before execution.

> **A Concord Development Anecdote ≠ Prospective Validation**

---

# 16. Failure Conditions

Evidence against the stronger sensor-fusion hypothesis could include repeated findings that:
- context diversity adds no meaningful error detection;
- collaboration systematically suppresses useful independent branches;
- context exchange causes unjustified convergence;
- composite performance is no better explained by ordinary information pooling;
- independence-preserving procedures create no measurable benefit for the tested claim;
- purported human/AI complementarity disappears under controlled comparison.

Negative results should be retained.

The theory must not be protected by redefining every outcome as successful collaboration.

This note should inform that parent development work without being silently merged into it.

---

# 18. Development Boundary

This note does not establish:
- that human–AI collaboration is generally superior;
- that human and AI cognition have fixed complementary roles;
- that human judgment is intrinsically more contextual;
- that AI reasoning is intrinsically more systematic;
- that disagreement reliably improves outcomes;
- that preserved provenance always improves decisions;
- that multiple substrates imply independent evidence;
- that composite competence grants legitimate authority.

Those remain separate empirical, normative and architectural questions.
