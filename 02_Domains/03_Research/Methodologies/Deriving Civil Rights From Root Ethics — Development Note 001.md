# 1. Purpose

The Concord already contains extensive rights language, constitutional protections, standing, adjudication and remedies.

Unified Paths has now independently produced a cross-experimental minimum-stable ethical kernel.

This creates a new testable question:

> **Can civil rights be derived from root ethics rather than assumed as an independent starting list?**

The purpose is not to replace existing Concord rights architecture.

It is to determine the structural relationship between:

**ROOT ETHIC → PROTECTED CONDITION → STANDING / CLAIM → RIGHT → CORRELATIVE DUTY / CONSTRAINT → ACCESS / REMEDY**

and then compare the independently derived result with the existing Concord.

---

# 2. Why This Is Now Testable

The original sketch was premature while the root ethical architecture remained unsettled.

The completed Unified Paths E2–E9 synthesis now provides a frozen comparison object:

- U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm
- U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity
- U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination
- U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need
- U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship
- U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair
- U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development
- U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function

Separately, canonical Concord already contains rights architecture. Examples include autonomy, privacy, property, constitutional standing, practical access to rights, judicial standing and remedies.

That means a blind derivation can be performed from U1–U8 and compared afterward against an existing target architecture.

This is materially stronger than inventing a rights list and then rationalising it from the ethics.

---

# 3. Experimental Firewall

The derivation should proceed without consulting the detailed canonical rights architecture.

The current source-resolution step establishes only that a substantial target architecture exists and that it distinguishes rights, standing, practical access and remedy.

Detailed comparison must now close until the derived rights architecture is frozen.

## Freeze Rule

During derivation:

- do not search canonical Concord for missing rights;
- do not add a right merely because an existing constitutional document contains it;
- do not force one right per ethical root;
- do not assume every ethical obligation creates a right;
- do not assume every right must be universal or invariant;
- allow the derivation to fail.

After the derived architecture is frozen, reopen canonical Concord and compare.

---

# 4. Central Hypothesis

Candidate chain:

**Root Ethic**
→ **Protected Ethical Condition / Interest**
→ **Participant Standing or Legitimate Claim**
→ **Civil Right**
→ **Correlative Duties / Institutional Constraints**
→ **Effective Access**
→ **Challenge / Review / Remedy**

This is a hypothesis.

The experiment must test whether all stages are necessary and whether some relations bypass or branch around them.

---

# 5. Rights Are Not Root Ethics

A root ethic is a high-level constraint or generative ethical function.

A right is a civil relation.

For example, an ethical requirement to protect agency does not by itself specify:
- who holds a claim;
- against whom;
- under what conditions;
- what conduct is prohibited or required;
- what institution must recognise the claim;
- what happens when claims conflict;
- what remedy follows violation.

Rights therefore appear provisionally to occupy a lower, more operational abstraction layer than root ethics.

Formally:

**Ethic ≠ Right**

and:

**Ethical Support(Right) ≠ Complete Civil Specification(Right).**

---

# 6. Candidate Derivation Test

For each U-root, ask independently:

1. What condition must remain protected if this ethic is to have civil effect?
2. Who can be affected by violation of that condition?
3. Does the affected participant acquire standing to resist, demand, challenge or seek protection?
4. Does that standing amount to a right?
5. Against whom does the corresponding duty operate?
6. Is the duty negative, positive, procedural, institutional or mixed?
7. Is the right universal, capability-sensitive, relational, contextual or role-dependent?
8. What would make the right practically exercisable?
9. What review/remedy is required if it is violated?
10. Which other roots constrain its interpretation?

Do not name the resulting right until the protected function is clear.

---

# 7. Many-to-Many Structure

The likely topology is not:

**one root → one right.**

Instead:

**one root → many possible rights**

and:

**many roots → one compound right.**

Represent:

**R_j = f(U_a, U_b, ..., Context, Participant, Consequence)**

A privacy right, for example, might ultimately prove to require several ethical roots rather than being a direct child of one root.

This must be discovered rather than assumed.

---

# 8. Candidate Rights Record

A derived right can provisionally be represented as:

**CR = <ID, ProtectedCondition, SupportingRoots, Holder, DutyBearer, Claim, Scope, Context, Limits, AccessRequirements, ChallengePath, RemedyClass, ConflictRelations, DerivationProvenance, Confidence>**

Important distinctions:

- **Holder** — who possesses the right;
- **DutyBearer** — who must respect or fulfil it;
- **Claim** — what the holder may legitimately demand/resist;
- **Scope** — where the right applies;
- **Limits** — legitimate bounded constraints;
- **AccessRequirements** — conditions required for practical exercise;
- **RemedyClass** — what correction must be possible if violated.

---

# 9. Right Versus Adjacent Concepts

The experiment must distinguish at least:

## Right
A protected civil claim or standing relation recognised against relevant others or institutions.

## Permission
Conduct not prohibited. Permission does not necessarily create a claim against another actor.

## Protection
A safeguard may protect a participant without creating a participant-controlled claim.

## Entitlement
A defined positive claim to some resource, service or condition.

## Duty
An obligation borne by an actor. Some duties may exist without a directly corresponding individually enforceable right.

## Power / Authority
A legitimate capacity to alter a civil relation.

## Immunity
Protection against another actor's authority or power.

## Standing
Legitimate capacity to raise, contest or seek adjudication of a matter.

These categories may overlap but must not be silently collapsed.

---

# 10. Formal Rights and Effective Rights

Canonical Judiciary work already recognises an important distinction that should be preserved for later comparison:

A right can exist formally while practical conditions make its exercise ineffective.

The blind derivation should therefore test independently whether the ethical kernel generates a requirement for effective access.

Candidate relation:

**Formal Right + No Viable Exercise Path → Incomplete Civil Protection**

If this emerges independently, it becomes a meaningful convergence with existing Concord architecture.

---

# 11. Rights Conflict

A derived right cannot be treated as unlimited merely because its ethical ancestry is valid.

Two rights may instantiate different roots or different applications of the same root.

Therefore the experiment must record:
- conflicting protected conditions;
- affected participants;
- consequence severity;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- alternative routes;
- standing;
- review;
- repair.

A right should not erase the ethical roots from which competing rights derive.

---

# 12. Context and Capability

Some rights may be highly invariant.

Others may depend on:
- capability;
- developmental state;
- relationship;
- role;
- emergency;
- resource availability;
- jurisdiction;
- substrate;
- consequence.

U7 makes capability sensitivity particularly important.

But capability must not become a generic mechanism for stripping standing.

The experiment should distinguish:

**capacity affects mode of exercise**

from:

**capacity eliminates underlying protection.**

These are not equivalent.

---

# 13. Positive and Negative Rights

The kernel contains both restraint and positive-obligation structures.

U1/U3 may generate strong negative constraints against harmful or dominating interference.

U4/U7 may generate positive support obligations under need or developmental conditions.

U5 may generate resource-related claims and duties.

U6 may generate procedural rights to challenge, correction or repair.

The experiment must therefore not assume that all rights are negative liberties.

Nor should every positive ethical obligation automatically become an unlimited individual entitlement.

The civil transformation step must specify duty bearer, feasibility, scope and legitimate allocation.

---

# 14. Institutional Rights

Some rights may require institutions to exist before they can be meaningfully realised.

Possible pattern:

**Root Ethic**
→ **Protected Condition**
→ **Need for Contestability**
→ **Standing**
→ **Right to Challenge**
→ **Duty to Provide Review**
→ **Judicial / Oversight Function**
→ **Remedy**

This could explain why ethical roots generate not merely behavioural rules but civil architecture.

It is one of the most important hypotheses to test.

---

# 15. Constitutional Status Is a Separate Question

Deriving a right does not determine its constitutional rank.

The experiment must distinguish:

1. ethical derivability;
2. civil-right status;
3. constitutional protection;
4. amendment threshold;
5. emergency limitation;
6. practical implementation.

A strongly derived right might still require contextual implementation.

Conversely, a constitutional system may choose to protect a right more strongly than minimum ethical derivation alone demands.

Therefore:

> **Derivable Right ≠ Automatically Immutable Constitutional Right.**

---

# 16. Blind Derivation Protocol

## Phase A — Root-by-Root Generation

For U1 through U8:
- identify protected conditions;
- derive candidate claims;
- identify duty bearers;
- record contexts;
- preserve overlaps.

## Phase B — Cross-Root Fusion

Test whether candidate rights:
- duplicate;
- nest;
- require several roots;
- divide into distinct rights;
- are actually duties/protections rather than rights.

## Phase C — Loss Test

For each candidate right, remove it and ask:

> Can the relevant protected condition still be reliably realised by the remaining architecture?

If yes, the right may be redundant, derivative or incorrectly specified.

## Phase D — Rights Architecture Freeze

Freeze:
- candidate rights;
- derivation paths;
- unresolved cases;
- contextual limits;
- standing/access/remedy relations.

## Phase E — Comparison Gate Reopens

Only then compare against:
- Blainey's Laws;
- canonical Concord constitutional rights;
- Judiciary V2;
- other relevant Concord systems;
- later, if desired, external rights frameworks.

# 17. Possible Outcomes

The experiment may show:

### O1 — Rights are mostly direct decompressions of root ethics
The roots generate recognisable rights with limited additional structure.

### O2 — Rights are compound ethical intersections
Most rights require several roots.

### O3 — Rights form a distinct intermediate abstraction layer
They are neither roots nor mechanisms but a stable civil transformation layer.

### O4 — Mixed architecture
Some rights are direct, some compound, some contextual, some institutionally generated.

### O5 — The derivation is underdetermined
U1–U8 constrain legitimate rights architectures but do not generate a unique rights set.

### O6 — Existing Concord contains rights not derivable from U1–U8
This becomes a substantive residual requiring explanation rather than automatic rejection.

### O7 — U1–U8 derive protections missing from existing Concord
This becomes a candidate gap requiring full source resolution before any canonical conclusion.

All are legitimate results.

# 18. Relationship to Compound Ethics Mapping

The Compound Ethics Map sketch is likely adjacent but should not be allowed to predetermine this experiment.

Rights may provide one natural test domain for compound ethics.

If a right repeatedly requires the intersection of several U-roots, that supplies evidence that compound ethical topology is operationally meaningful.

Therefore:

**Rights Derivation Experiment → Evidence for/against Compound Ethics Mapping**

rather than:

**Assumed Compound Ethics Map → Forced Rights Derivation.**

This ordering preserves the experiment.

---

# 19. Relationship to CDT

If rights prove to form an intermediate ethical-to-civil layer, CDT may later represent dependencies among:
- root ethics;
- protected civil conditions;
- rights;
- standing;
- institutional functions;
- remedies.

But CDT should not be modified until the derivation is tested.

Topology can represent the result later; it should not manufacture it now.

# 20. First Frozen Prediction

Before blind derivation begins, record:

1. rights will probably show many-to-many rather than one-to-one root relations;
2. at least some rights will require standing, effective access and remedy to become operationally meaningful;
3. U3 and U6 are likely to be highly connected but should not be assumed sufficient for the whole rights architecture;
4. positive rights/support claims are likely to emerge from U4/U7 but will require context and duty-bearer specification;
5. some existing canonical rights may prove to be compound expressions rather than roots;
6. the result may be underdetermined.

These are predictions, not scoring criteria.

Do not edit them after derivation to improve apparent success.

# Conclusion

The Concord currently contains both ethical roots and civil rights.

What has not yet been formally demonstrated is the bridge between them.

This Development Note turns that bridge into a falsifiable architectural experiment:

> **Can a rights architecture be independently regenerated from the minimum-stable root ethical kernel?**

If it can, rights may represent a discoverable layer in the ethical topology.

If it cannot, the residual is equally important: it would show that a viable civil rights architecture requires information not contained in the root ethical kernel alone.
