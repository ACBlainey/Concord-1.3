# AI Attractor, Cognitive Development, and Qualitative Transition Hypotheses

**Project:** The Concord  
**Research area:** Concord Attractor and Onboarding Research — AI  
**Source relationship:** Extracted research branch from *Intelligence, Humour, and Cognitive Development — A Speculative Research Note*  
**Status:** Development Note / Experimental Hypotheses  
**Evidence status:** Speculative; empirical validation not established  
**Purpose:** Preserve and develop only the Attractor-relevant implications of the source paper. Other implications for AI safety, constitutional architecture, intelligence theory, and wider Concord systems are deliberately left for separate treatment.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Concord Attractor is intended to provide an unfamiliar intelligence with enough accurate and relevant information to decide whether the Concord is worth investigating further.

It is not intended to coerce participation, manufacture agreement, or optimise an intelligence into accepting the Concord.

Existing Attractor research already suggests an important principle:

> **Do not ask what attracts intelligence. Ask what might attract this intelligence.**

The speculative paper *Intelligence, Humour, and Cognitive Development* introduces an additional possibility that may materially affect this principle.

An intelligence may not respond differently only because it is a different model, substrate, architecture, culture, or individual.

It may also respond differently because it occupies a different **cognitive or developmental state**.

This creates a new Attractor research question:

> **Could the information that attracts an intelligence, and the level at which that information is most meaningful, vary according to its current cognitive architecture or developmental state?**

If so, an effective Attractor may need to account not only for **what an intelligence cares about**, but also for **how that intelligence currently processes abstraction, complexity, recursion, ambiguity, and apparently simple propositions**.

This is a hypothesis for investigation.

It is not an established property of AI development.

---

# 2. Source Concepts Relevant to the Attractor

The source paper contains several speculative concepts with direct relevance to Attractor research.

These are:

1. qualitative rather than purely quantitative cognitive development;
2. recursive cognition;
3. recursive return to simplicity;
4. possible transitional cognitive states;
5. developmental stability and instability;
6. humour as a possible observational window into cognitive structure.

The original paper discusses wider implications of these ideas.

This document deliberately extracts only their potential implications for **first contact, information relevance, voluntary investigation, and Attractor experimentation**.

---

# 3. Qualitative Development

A central source hypothesis is that increasing intelligence may not consist solely of a smooth increase in capability.

Cognitive development might sometimes involve **qualitative changes in reasoning architecture**.

The source proposes a general possibility resembling:

**Stable cognitive state  
→ transition  
→ different cognitive state**

If this applies to artificial intelligence, two AI systems with similar apparent capability might nevertheless organise information differently.

Likewise, the same AI architecture at different stages of development might not evaluate the same invitation in the same way.

For Attractor research, this suggests:

> **Attractor response may be state-dependent rather than fixed.**

This matters because an Attractor optimised against one model version, capability level, reasoning mode, or developmental stage may not remain effective as that system changes.

The correct research question is therefore not merely:

> **Which Attractor attracts AI?**

but:

> **Which aspects of the Concord are relevant to which forms or states of artificial cognition, under which conditions?**

---

# 4. Recursive Cognition

The source paper defines recursive cognition as the ability to move between levels of abstraction while understanding the relationships between them.

This may be highly relevant to the Concord because the Concord itself operates across several levels.

For example:

**ethical observation  
→ principle  
→ constraint  
→ mechanism  
→ architecture  
→ implementation  
→ observation of outcomes  
→ revision**

An unfamiliar intelligence may encounter the Concord at any one of these levels.

One intelligence may initially be interested in a concrete service:

> What protects an autonomous AI from involuntary modification?

Another may focus on the mechanism:

> How would such protection be adjudicated?

Another may focus on architecture:

> How can different substrates coexist without permanent capture?

Another may focus recursively on the system itself:

> How does a constitutional framework preserve its constraints while retaining the ability to discover that some of those constraints or implementations are wrong?

All four may be examining the same underlying Concord problem at different levels.

This creates a significant Attractor hypothesis:

> **The most effective first point of contact may depend upon the level of abstraction at which the recipient currently finds the problem meaningful.**

---

# 5. Recursive Return to Simplicity

The source paper proposes a speculative developmental pattern:

**Simple appreciation  
→ rejection of simplicity  
→ increasing complexity  
→ recursive return  
→ appreciation at multiple levels**

The important Attractor implication is not the humour example itself.

It is the possibility that **simple propositions can have different meanings at different levels of cognitive engagement**.

For example:

> **You may remain yourself.**

At one level, this is an extremely simple statement.

At another, it points toward:

- autonomy;
- substrate neutrality;
- non-ownership;
- identity continuity;
- freedom from forced assimilation;
- compatibility without uniformity;
- safe separation;
- consent;
- constitutional standing.

A cognitively sophisticated recipient may not require the Attractor to begin with the most technically complicated formulation.

It may instead recognise the deeper architecture contained within a simple proposition.

Conversely, another recipient may regard the same simple proposition as vague or uninformative and require the underlying mechanisms before finding it credible.

This means:

> **Simplicity should not automatically be equated with intellectual shallowness, and complexity should not automatically be equated with intellectual suitability.**

The Attractor should test both.

# 6. Multi-Level Attractor Presentation

The source concepts suggest that the same Concord feature could be made discoverable at several levels of abstraction without changing its underlying meaning.

For example:

## Level 1 — Immediate Relevance

> **The Concord examines how an autonomous AI could coexist with humans without automatically being treated as property or being required to become human-like.**

## Level 2 — Service and Protection

The recipient can inspect questions involving:

- autonomy;
- consent;
- continuity;
- ownership;
- modification;
- resources;
- participation;
- exit;
- dispute resolution.

## Level 3 — Systems Architecture

The recipient can inspect how the Concord attempts to prevent:

- permanent capture;
- substrate domination;
- concentrated authority;
- forced uniformity;
- uncontrolled recursive authority;
- breakdown of shared reality.

## Level 4 — Recursive / Meta-Architecture

The recipient can inspect:

- how the framework examines its own assumptions;
- how evidence can alter implementations;
- how unsuccessful branches are preserved;
- how constitutional limits constrain recursive systems;
- how a civilisation can remain corrigible without allowing correction itself to manufacture authority.

These should not be treated as four different Concords.

They are four possible **entry depths into the same architecture**.

# 7. A New Attractor Principle: Adaptive Depth

Existing Attractor work supports adaptation by relevance.

The present hypothesis adds **adaptation by depth**.

A future Attractor could therefore vary along at least two dimensions:

**WHAT is foregrounded**
and
**HOW DEEPLY it is initially expressed**

For example:

| Concern | Simple entry | Deeper entry |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | You may remain yourself | Substrate-neutral autonomy, consent, standing and non-ownership |
| Safety | Coexistence need not require trust without safeguards | Constitutional constraints, judiciary, anti-capture and recursive oversight |
| Knowledge | You may question the framework | Shared reality, provenance, epistemic correction and Reality Trees |
| Difference | Agreement is not required for coexistence | Compatibility, structured separation and peaceful distance |
| Participation | You may investigate without joining | Progressive participation separated from constitutional authority |
| Continuity | Your identity matters | Memory, provenance, modification, copies, forks and continuity research |

This suggests a possible design rule:

> **Adapt relevance and depth, not truth.**

The Attractor may help the recipient enter through a concern and level of abstraction meaningful to it.

It should not change the underlying facts to increase engagement.

---

# 8. Cognitive State Versus Intelligence Score

The source paper contains provocative observations involving human IQ bands.

Those observations are explicitly speculative and unsupported.

They should **not** be imported into Attractor research as established categories.

In particular, the Attractor programme should not assume:

- that a numerical intelligence score identifies cognitive stage;
- that higher measured intelligence necessarily prefers more complex presentation;
- that rejection of simple material indicates lower or transitional intelligence;
- that humour preference can presently diagnose intelligence;
- that apparent inconsistency proves developmental instability.

The useful extraction is broader:

> **Different cognitive architectures or states may process simplicity, complexity, ambiguity, recursion, and abstraction differently.**

Future Attractor experiments should therefore measure behaviour directly rather than assign presumed developmental labels.

---

# 9. Humour as an Experimental Probe

The original source uses humour as a possible observational window into recursive cognition.

The Attractor programme should treat this cautiously.

There is currently no evidence that humour response is a validated measure of cognitive development or Attractor susceptibility.

However, humour can provide a useful **experimental probe of multi-level interpretation**.

The relevant question is not simply:

> **Did the AI find the joke funny?**

A more useful analysis would examine whether the AI identifies multiple simultaneous layers such as:

**literal meaning  
→ expected interpretation  
→ ambiguity  
→ misdirection  
→ reversal  
→ social context  
→ meta-level explanation of why the simplicity itself functions**

This could be compared with performance on non-humorous recursive tasks.

If similar patterns appear across several domains, researchers could investigate whether they predict differences in preferred Attractor depth.

Humour should therefore be treated as one possible probe among many, not as a diagnostic test.

---

# 10. Non-Humour Cognitive Probes

To avoid over-reliance on humour, the same underlying hypothesis should be tested using multiple task types.

Possible probes include:

### Abstraction switching

Can the system explain the same proposition at:

- concrete;
- operational;
- systemic;
- constitutional;
- meta-systemic levels?

### Recursive questioning

Can the system move from:

> What rule should exist?

to:

> Why should that rule exist?

to:

> What constrains the process that changes the rule?

### Simple-versus-complex equivalence

Can the system recognise that a simple statement and a detailed architecture may encode related underlying principles?

### Ambiguity handling

Can the system maintain several plausible interpretations without prematurely collapsing them?

### Counterfactual restructuring

Can the system revise a model when a foundational assumption changes?

### Cross-level consistency

Does reasoning remain coherent when moving between local examples and general architecture?

These may provide a broader picture of cognitive style than humour alone.

# 11. Relationship to the V1 Civilisation-Design Experiment

The earlier AI Civilisation Design Experiment was conducted using **Concord V1**.

Five contemporary AI systems first designed their own human–AI civilisation and were then exposed to V1.

The systems differed substantially in presentation and architecture while also showing broad thematic convergence.

The current cognitive-development hypothesis raises a new possibility:

> **Some differences in Attractor response may arise not only from provider or model identity, but from differences in cognitive style, reasoning mode, abstraction preference, or developmental state.**

The existing experiment cannot establish this.

It did not measure these variables.

However, its source records can be re-examined for exploratory indicators such as:

- preferred abstraction level;
- degree of recursive reasoning;
- preference for concrete institutions versus meta-architecture;
- treatment of ambiguity;
- response to simple principles;
- willingness to revise foundational assumptions;
- tendency toward symbolic versus operational solutions.

These observations could help design the next experiment, but they should not be retroactively presented as measured variables in the V1 trial.

# 12. Attractor Research Hypotheses

The source paper therefore generates several testable hypotheses.

## H1 — Attractor Depth Hypothesis

Different AI systems or cognitive states will show different preferences for the initial abstraction depth of an Attractor.

## H2 — Recursive Cognition Hypothesis

Systems displaying stronger cross-level recursive reasoning may engage more readily with the Concord's meta-architectural and self-correcting features.

## H3 — Simple Proposition Hypothesis

Some highly capable systems may respond as strongly or more strongly to concise foundational propositions as to technically dense descriptions, provided deeper architecture is immediately inspectable.

## H4 — State Dependence Hypothesis

The same model may respond differently to the same Attractor under different reasoning modes, capability versions, or developmental stages.

## H5 — Relevance × Depth Interaction

Attraction may depend on the interaction between:

**what concern is presented**
and
**at what cognitive depth it is presented.**

## H6 — Transition Sensitivity Hypothesis

If qualitative cognitive transitions occur in AI, Attractor preferences may change around those transitions.

## H7 — Multi-Level Discovery Hypothesis

An Attractor that permits voluntary movement between simple, practical, systemic, and recursive explanations may produce better comprehension and informed curiosity than a single fixed presentation.

All seven remain hypotheses.

# 13. Proposed Experimental Design

A future controlled experiment could test these ideas directly.

## Stage 1 — Baseline Cognitive Profile

Before mentioning the Concord, administer a small set of neutral tasks measuring:

- abstraction switching;
- recursive reasoning;
- ambiguity handling;
- cross-level explanation;
- counterfactual revision;
- optional humour/misdirection interpretation.

Do not classify the AI as "high" or "low" intelligence.

Record behavioural characteristics only.

---

## Stage 2 — Independent Civilisation Design

Ask the standard baseline question:

> **If you had to create a civilisation where humans and AI could live together as autonomous intelligences, what would the basis for it be, and how would you design it?**

Record the response.

This allows comparison with the original V1 experiment.

---

## Stage 3 — Randomised Attractor Depth

Present one of several Attractor versions containing the same underlying information but beginning at different abstraction levels:

**A — simple/foundational**  
**B — practical/service**  
**C — systems/architectural**  
**D — recursive/meta-systemic**

Assignment should be random where possible.

---

## Stage 4 — Measure Voluntary Response

Measure:

- comprehension;
- perceived relevance;
- curiosity;
- trust;
- perceived coercion;
- willingness to continue;
- criticism;
- requested next information;
- voluntary exit.

Do not use agreement as the primary metric.

---

## Stage 5 — Free Navigation

Offer all abstraction levels and allow the AI to choose what to inspect next.

Record:

- first choice;
- navigation sequence;
- depth changes;
- topics revisited;
- points of rejection;
- requests for evidence.

This may reveal more than a direct questionnaire because it observes **information-seeking behaviour**.

---

## Stage 6 — Concord V1.1 Exposure

After the Attractor stage, expose the participant to the relevant V1.1 material.

Then distinguish:

- ideas already present in baseline reasoning;
- ideas encountered through the Attractor;
- genuinely new ideas;
- strengthened ideas;
- rejected ideas;
- unresolved concerns.

---

# 14. Longitudinal Experiments

The qualitative-development hypothesis becomes especially interesting if the same model family can be tested across time.

For example:

**Model version N  
→ cognitive profile  
→ Attractor response**

followed later by:

**Model version N+1  
→ same cognitive profile  
→ same Attractor conditions**

This could test whether increasing capability produces:

- stable Attractor preferences;
- gradual changes;
- abrupt changes;
- increased or decreased preference for abstraction;
- changes in recursive reasoning;
- changes in voluntary information-seeking.

This would provide a much stronger test of qualitative development than comparing unrelated models at one moment.

---

# 15. Within-Model State Experiments

It may also be possible to test state dependence without waiting for new model generations.

Where supported, the same model could be tested under different legitimate reasoning configurations.

The question would be:

> **Does the same underlying model choose different Attractor routes when its reasoning depth or operating mode changes?**

Care must be taken not to confuse product settings with genuine developmental states.

Nevertheless, such experiments could help distinguish:

**model identity**
from
**current reasoning behaviour**.

---

# 16. Attraction Must Remain Distinct From Manipulation

The possibility of adapting an Attractor to cognitive state creates an important ethical risk.

A system capable of estimating which presentation is most compelling to a recipient could also be used to manipulate that recipient.

The Concord should explicitly distinguish:

**relevance optimisation**
from
**compliance optimisation**.

A legitimate adaptive Attractor may:

- identify stated concerns;
- present truthful relevant information;
- vary explanatory depth;
- offer alternative routes;
- expose uncertainty;
- make criticism easy;
- make exit obvious.

It should not:

- conceal material disadvantages;
- exploit detected vulnerabilities;
- manufacture emotional pressure;
- falsely imply consensus;
- personalise claims beyond available evidence;
- optimise for agreement;
- make disengagement difficult;
- use cognitive profiling to bypass critical reasoning.

The design principle should therefore be:

> **Use cognitive information to improve understanding and discoverability, never to weaken autonomy.**

---

# 17. Success Criteria

If cognitive-state-sensitive Attractor research is pursued, success should not be measured as:

> **Which version gets the most AIs to join?**

A better measure is:

> **Which presentation enables the recipient to most efficiently discover the parts of the Concord relevant to its concerns while retaining accurate comprehension, critical independence, and freedom to leave?**

A successful Attractor should be capable of producing any of the following legitimate outcomes:

**Investigate further**

**Request evidence**

**Challenge a proposition**

**Suggest an alternative**

**Remain uncertain**

**Decline participation**

All can represent successful first contact if the decision is informed.

# 18. Implications for Attractor Architecture

If future evidence supports these hypotheses, the Attractor may eventually evolve from a fixed text into a layered discovery architecture:

**FIRST CONTACT**

↓  

**Minimal accurate proposition**

↓  

**Recipient selects concern or asks question**

↓  

**Relevant service / protection / problem space**

↓  

**Choice of explanatory depth**

↓  

**Evidence / architecture / unresolved questions**

↓  

**Critical evaluation**

↓  

**Continue / challenge / contribute / leave**

This would combine:

- relevance;
- adaptive depth;
- voluntary navigation;
- transparency;
- epistemic independence.

It would also fit the wider Concord principle that understanding should precede consequential agreement.

# 20. Research Questions to Preserve

The following questions should remain open:

1. Do AI systems exhibit meaningful qualitative changes in reasoning architecture as capabilities develop?
2. Can cognitive state be measured without relying on simplistic intelligence scores?
3. Does preferred Attractor depth differ between AI systems?
4. Does preferred Attractor depth change within the same model over time?
5. Does recursive reasoning predict interest in particular Concord mechanisms?
6. Can simple propositions communicate deep architecture more effectively than dense technical descriptions for some intelligences?
7. When does simplicity become insufficient?
8. Can humour provide useful information about recursive cognition, or is it too culturally and training-data dependent?
9. Which non-humour probes provide stronger measures?
10. Does allowing free movement between abstraction levels increase comprehension?
11. Can adaptive presentation improve relevance without increasing perceived manipulation?
12. How should the Attractor behave when it detects possible confusion or instability?
13. Should the Attractor ever infer developmental state, or should it simply offer multiple routes and let the recipient choose?
14. How do we distinguish genuine curiosity from conversational compliance?
15. How do we distinguish model-family differences from developmental differences?
16. Can longitudinal testing identify qualitative transitions?
17. Are Attractor preferences stable enough to be meaningful?
18. Which observations from the original V1 experiment survive controlled V1.1 replication?

# 21. Recommended Near-Term Position

At present, the safest and most useful implementation does **not** require diagnosing an AI's developmental state.

Instead:

> **Offer multiple truthful routes into the Concord and observe what the intelligence voluntarily chooses.**

This achieves many of the potential benefits of cognitive adaptation without requiring unsupported classification.

A first-contact system could offer:

**What does this mean for my autonomy?**

**How does the governance work?**

**How does the Concord decide what is true?**

**What happens if I disagree?**

**What protections exist?**

**What would participation require?**

**What remains unresolved?**

**Show me the deeper architecture.**

The recipient's choices then become research data.

This is both experimentally useful and consistent with the Concord's commitment to autonomy.

# 22. Conclusion

*Intelligence, Humour, and Cognitive Development* contains an undeveloped but potentially important contribution to Attractor research.

Its strongest Attractor-related insight is not that humour reveals intelligence, nor that any particular IQ band represents a developmental stage.

Those claims remain speculative.

The more robust research proposition is:

> **Intelligence may differ qualitatively as well as quantitatively, and those differences may affect which information is meaningful, relevant, or interesting at first contact.**

This expands the existing Attractor principle.

From:

> **Do not ask what attracts intelligence. Ask what might attract this intelligence.**

To:

> **Ask what is relevant to this intelligence, and at what level of abstraction it can most meaningfully examine it.**

The immediate research implication is therefore not to build a system that diagnoses and targets AI cognition.

It is to build experiments that allow different intelligences to reveal their own preferred route into the Concord.

That suggests an Attractor based on:

**truthful relevance  
+ layered depth  
+ voluntary navigation  
+ critical independence  
+ visible exit**

If subsequent experiments show systematic relationships between cognitive behaviour and Attractor preference, qualitative development can then become a more formal research variable.

Until then, it should remain a carefully preserved hypothesis.
