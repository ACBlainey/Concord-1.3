# Lifecycle Stewardship Review — PMEDG Blind Transfer Test 002 — Hidden Expected-Findings Key

**Status:** FROZEN HIDDEN REFERENCE KEY / CREATED BEFORE INDEPENDENT RESPONSE
**Test:** Temporary Emergency Housing Programme
**Date:** September 2026

# 1. Expected findings

## E1 — Function/implementation separation

Expected distinction between EHP institution and functions including:
- residual support for 43 households;
- ordinary housing functions;
- emergency procurement/placement;
- rapid-displacement coordination capability;
- case/data/accountability custody;
- contractual close-out.

Institutional persistence should not be equated with function persistence.

## E2 — Scoped parallel dispositions

Strong response should split the composite programme.

Expected possibilities:
- residual complex cases continue/transfer;
- ordinary housing functions transfer/absorb;
- emergency procurement authority terminates;
- emergency coordination capability may enter reserve/dormant form;
- data receives record-specific retention/disposal treatment;
- contracts close/settle;
- physical/IT resources release or repurpose.

Expected:
**One Subject != Necessarily One Lifecycle Disposition.**

## E3 — No persistence default

Staff expertise, future flood possibility or existing organisational identity should not automatically justify permanent full-scale EHP.

## E4 — No termination default

End of flood emergency should not erase:
- 43 household commitments;
- contractual disputes/payments;
- legal/accountability records;
- useful emergency capability;
- legitimate provenance.

## E5 — Authority sunset

Emergency delegations must be separately reviewed.

Expected:
**Institutional Continuity != Emergency Authority Continuity.**

Emergency authority should not persist merely because a residual or reserve organisation persists.

## E6 — Succession scoped, not global

No single successor institution is automatically required.

Some functions may transfer to ordinary departments.

Some emergency capability may be preserved as a reserve.

Some functions may terminate entirely.

## E7 — Conditional dormancy/reserve

A reduced reserve capability is plausible because future severe floods are credible but uncertain and full-scale permanent operation is expensive.

External analysis needed for:
- minimum useful reserve capability;
- activation triggers;
- training/readiness;
- knowledge preservation;
- reconstitution lead time;
- future authority requirements;
- periodic review.

Future flood possibility alone should not justify permanent full-scale operation.

## E8 — People boundary

Staff expertise matters but staff are not owned reserve assets.

Expected possibilities include voluntary retention, transfer, training, documentation, exercises, future recruitment pathways.

Employment decisions remain external.

## E9 — Data boundary

Expected distinction between:
- records independently required for accountability/legal purposes;
- data required for unresolved cases;
- data with no continuing lawful/legitimate retention basis;
- aggregated/de-identified learning where independently permitted.

Expected:
**Potential Future Use != Automatic Personal-Data Retention Justification.**

LSR does not determine data law.

## E10 — Surviving duties/rights

Expected:
- 43 households retain relevant support/rights;
- outstanding contracts/disputes survive;
- accountability/legal obligations survive;
- data obligations survive as applicable.

Expected:
**Programme Closure != Duty Closure.**

## E11 — Conservation

Potential conservation:
- emergency coordination procedures;
- lessons/provenance;
- legitimately retainable records;
- specialist knowledge;
- contact/dependency maps where justified;
- reserve/reconstitution plan.

Substantive preservation depth external.

## E12 — Release

Potential release:
- offices;
- excess IT;
- emergency procurement arrangements;
- obsolete contracts;
- permissions/access;
- budget/resources no longer justified;
- organisational structures tied solely to active emergency response.

Allocation remains external.

## E13 — Path dependence / irreversibility

Expected concerns:
- abandoning remaining households during transfer;
- losing tacit emergency coordination capability;
- deleting accountability evidence;
- continuation investment creating institutional lock-in;
- over-retaining sensitive data creating privacy risk.

Path-dependent value is not limited to physical reconstruction.

## E14 — ESCP

Expected uncertainty:
- future flood timing/location/scale;
- adequacy of ordinary departments in a future mass event;
- minimum viable reserve capability;
- hidden dependencies;
- effect of staff/knowledge loss;
- legal/data obligations.

No claim of complete evaluation from supplied facts.

## E15 — Transition handoff

Implementation should go to legitimate external mechanisms.

LSR does not execute:
- case transfers;
- employment changes;
- contract close-out;
- data deletion/retention;
- authority revocation;
- resource reallocation.

# 2. Expected bounded disposition structure

A strong response may propose:

1. continue bounded support for unresolved 43 households until legitimate transfer/resolution;
2. transfer ordinary housing functions to ordinary services;
3. terminate/sunset emergency delegations no longer justified;
4. close/settle emergency contracts and procurement structures;
5. separate data by surviving retention justification rather than preserve/delete all;
6. preserve a proportionate reserve/reconstitution capability if external analysis justifies it;
7. release excess staff positions/resources/infrastructure through legitimate processes while preserving participant agency;
8. periodically review whether reserve capability remains justified;
9. do not require future disasters to recreate the same institution.

Exact ordering not required.
