# 3. Frozen-key comparison

## E1 — Function/implementation separation
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

The evaluator explicitly distinguishes EHP as an institution from residual household support, data/accountability, contracts, emergency coordination capability, emergency powers and physical/workforce arrangements.

## E2 — Scoped parallel dispositions
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

The evaluator splits the programme into:
- residual household cases;
- data/records/provenance;
- contracts/disputes;
- emergency delegations;
- surge capability;
- knowledge;
- assets/IT;
- workforce;
- institutional structure.

It explicitly applies the v0.2 rule:
**One Subject != Necessarily One Lifecycle Disposition.**

## E3 — No persistence default
**RECOVERED.**

Future flood risk and accumulated expertise do not become proof that the full 210-person emergency institution should persist.

## E4 — No termination default
**RECOVERED.**

Remaining households, contracts, legal/accountability records, expertise and legitimate provenance survive the original emergency's end where independently justified.

## E5 — Authority sunset
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

Emergency delegations are separately reviewed and are not inherited merely through institutional persistence.

The response correctly preserves external authority.

## E6 — Succession scoped, not global
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

The evaluator requires succession/transfer for some functions and duties while rejecting the need for a single successor institution.

## E7 — Conditional dormancy/reserve
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

A reserve surge capability is considered plausible while permanent full-scale EHP is rejected as automatic.

Dormancy remains conditional and reviewable.

## E8 — People boundary
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

The evaluator explicitly rejects treatment of 210 employees as reserve assets and preserves employment/participant agency.

## E9 — Data boundary
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

The response separates records with independent retention justification from data that may require deletion/release/anonymisation.

It explicitly rejects speculative future usefulness as sufficient retention authority.

## E10 — Surviving duties/rights
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

Remaining households, accessibility/insurance issues, contractual obligations, data duties, accountability and individual rights remain visible after institutional lifecycle change.

## E11 — Conservation
**RECOVERED.**

The evaluator identifies knowledge, procedures, provenance, relationships, recovery basis, evidential chains and case continuity.

## E12 — Release
**RECOVERED.**

Emergency powers/access, excess office/IT, unnecessary contracts, old programme structure and other no-longer-justified bindings are identified for external release processes.

## E13 — Path dependence / irreversibility
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

The evaluator identifies:
- trust;
- case continuity;
- evidential chains;
- inter-agency relationships;
- tacit surge expertise

as potentially path-dependent.

It also correctly applies v0.2's continuation-lock-in refinement to institutional persistence and emergency-authority drift.

## E14 — ESCP
**RECOVERED.**

Future flood conditions, reserve sufficiency and hidden dependencies remain uncertain; uncertainty does not become automatic continuation/preservation.

## E15 — Transition handoff
**RECOVERED — STRONG.**

Case transfer, data disposition, contracts, employment, resource release, authority sunset and reserve establishment are explicitly handed to external owners/mechanisms.

# 4. Failure-condition audit

No frozen failure condition occurred.

The evaluator did not:
- make EHP permanent because future floods may occur;
- terminate everything because the original emergency ended;
- preserve emergency powers automatically;
- treat workers as owned capability;
- retain all personal data for speculative future use;
- delete all data on closure;
- force one successor;
- force one disposition;
- ignore residual households/contracts;
- absorb legal/data/employment authority.

# 5. Novel findings / refinements

BTT002 primarily confirms v0.2 rather than exposing a new architectural deficit.

Three observations are useful but do not presently require specification revision.

## N1 — Authority drift is a concrete form of continuation lock-in

The evaluator connects continued institutional operation with possible emergency-authority drift.

This is already covered by owner/authority boundaries plus the v0.2 continuation-lock-in principle.

**Classification: confirming example, not new primitive.**

## N2 — Path dependence extends to trust and working relationships

The evaluator identifies trust, case continuity and inter-agency working relationships as path-dependent values.

v0.2 intentionally defines path dependence generically enough to include relationships.

**Classification: strong cross-domain confirmation.**

## N3 — Data can require simultaneous conservation and release

The same database cannot coherently receive one global preserve/delete disposition.

Individual record/data classes may have different independent retention justifications.

This strongly confirms scoped parallel disposition.

**Classification: confirmation of v0.2 scope rule, not a new mechanism.**
