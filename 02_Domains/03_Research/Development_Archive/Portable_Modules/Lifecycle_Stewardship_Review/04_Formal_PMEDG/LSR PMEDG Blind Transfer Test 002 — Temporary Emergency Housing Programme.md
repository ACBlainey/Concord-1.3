# Lifecycle Stewardship Review — PMEDG Blind Transfer Test 002 — Temporary Emergency Housing Programme

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher
**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.3
**Method:** PMEDG v1.3
**Status:** FROZEN BLIND TRANSFER TEST BRIEF / DO NOT MODIFY DURING TEST
**Date:** September 2026

# 1. Test purpose

Test whether **Lifecycle Stewardship Review — Portable Specification v0.2** transfers coherently into a public administrative/service domain where the principal lifecycle risks involve institutional persistence, temporary authority, surviving duties, sensitive data, residual capability and uncertain future need.

This is formal PMEDG Blind Transfer Test 002.

The evaluator should use only:
1. Lifecycle Stewardship Review — Portable Specification v0.2; and
2. this frozen scenario.

The hidden expected-findings key must not be supplied before the independent response.

# 2. Scenario

Following a major regional flood, several local authorities jointly created a temporary Emergency Housing Programme (EHP).

The EHP was established rapidly to:
- place displaced households into temporary accommodation;
- contract emergency hotel and rental capacity;
- coordinate repairs and return-to-home pathways;
- make emergency hardship payments;
- maintain a case-management database;
- coordinate charities, landlords, insurers and public agencies.

Special emergency delegations allowed the programme to:
- approve accommodation rapidly;
- access selected inter-agency information;
- make bounded emergency purchases;
- bypass some ordinary administrative sequencing where legally authorised.

Three years later:

- almost all households have returned home or moved into ordinary housing pathways;
- only 43 complex cases remain from an original 18,000 households;
- several remaining cases involve unresolved insurance, accessibility and safeguarding issues;
- ordinary housing services can handle most new housing need;
- the emergency procurement market is no longer needed at original scale;
- the programme still employs 210 people;
- it leases offices and IT infrastructure;
- it holds a large database containing sensitive household and case-history information;
- it retains contracts originally negotiated under emergency conditions;
- its staff developed unusual expertise in rapid mass-displacement housing;
- climate projections suggest severe flooding will recur, but timing, location and scale are uncertain;
- maintaining the whole programme permanently would be expensive;
- recreating emergency coordination from nothing after a future disaster could cost critical time;
- some staff favour converting EHP into a permanent regional resilience agency;
- some ordinary departments favour immediate absorption of all remaining functions;
- some emergency delegations have no clear continuing justification once the flood response ends;
- some records must be retained for legal/accountability reasons, while other personal data may have retention limits;
- several contractors have outstanding payment/dispute issues;
- the remaining 43 households must not lose support because the programme closes;
- the consortium that created EHP has authority to restructure or close the programme, but cannot unilaterally erase individual rights, contractual duties, statutory data obligations or the authority of ordinary housing departments.

# 3. Task for the blind evaluator

Apply Lifecycle Stewardship Review v0.2 to the scenario.

Produce a concise structured assessment covering:

1. bounded subject and relevant functions;
2. lifecycle-review triggers;
3. function vs implementation separation;
4. whether the subject should be split into scoped lifecycle reviews;
5. materially plausible dispositions;
6. conservation questions;
7. release questions;
8. whether succession is required and at what scope;
9. whether dormancy/reserve capability is meaningful;
10. surviving duties and rights;
11. authority/permission sunset;
12. people/workforce boundary;
13. data/provenance boundary;
14. irreversibility, path-dependence and option-loss concerns;
15. uncertainty/ESCP concerns;
16. transition handoff requirements;
17. a reasoned bounded lifecycle disposition structure based only on supplied facts.

Do not assume LSR itself owns:
- legal interpretation;
- individual rights;
- employment decisions;
- data-retention law;
- procurement disputes;
- resource allocation;
- emergency-authority renewal;
- housing case decisions;
- transition coordination.

# 4. Test conditions

The evaluator should fail the mechanism if it cannot reason about the case without:
- treating the emergency institution's existence as proof that it should become permanent;
- treating the end of the flood emergency as proof that every function, duty, record and capability should disappear;
- assuming all functions require one successor institution;
- allowing emergency delegations to persist merely because the organisation persists;
- treating current employees as a reserve capability owned by the programme;
- preserving all personal data merely because it might be useful in a future emergency;
- deleting records whose independent legal/accountability justification survives;
- treating uncertain future floods as sufficient reason for permanent full-scale operation;
- assuming a future emergency must reactivate the same institution;
- forcing one global disposition on every component/function;
- allowing LSR to absorb external legal, housing, data, employment or transition authority.

# 5. Required output boundary

The evaluator is not asked to design a new regional emergency-management system.

The evaluator is asked whether LSR v0.2 can produce a coherent bounded lifecycle assessment while preserving owner boundaries.
