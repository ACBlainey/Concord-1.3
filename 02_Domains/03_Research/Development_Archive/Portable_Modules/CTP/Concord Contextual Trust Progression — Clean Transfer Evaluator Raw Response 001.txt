## Concise architecture reconstruction

Concord Contextual Trust Progression uses:

**Evidence -> Evidence Relevance Record (ERR) -> Exposure Transition Record (ETR)**

- **Evidence** is raw or declared material: behavioural, operational, attestation, recommendation, incident, audit, non-event, historical, etc.
- **ERR** contextualizes evidence for a target function, context, and exposure dimension. It records relevance, projection, legitimacy, observation conditions/integrity/coverage, correction, dispute, freshness, identity continuity, causation, and review state.
- **ETR** decides a bounded exposure transition. It records requested/current exposure, dimension transitions, prerequisites, consequence assessment, ERR set, material adverse/high-consequence exceptions, evidence sufficiency, controls, decision state, review, exit, and provenance.

Core separations:
- **Evidence != Permission**
- **Recommendation != Transferred Trust**
- **Provider Federation != Reputation Federation**
- **NOT_APPLICABLE != NOT_CHECKED**
- **Exceptional Authority != CTP Override**
- **Terminal Relationship State != Adverse Trust Finding**

CTP is contextual/relational because relevance and sufficiency are assessed for a specific function, context, exposure dimension, consequence level, and relationship transition. It is not a universal trust score.

## Per-item results

### 1. Evidence, ERR, and ETR distinction
- **Evidence**: raw material, observation, attestation, recommendation, incident, audit, or declared record.
- **ERR**: Evidence Relevance Record. It explains whether evidence is relevant, legitimate, projected, corrected, disputed, fresh, identity-continuous, causally clear, and sufficiently observed for a target use.
- **ETR**: Exposure Transition Record. It decides whether a requested exposure change is approved, partially approved, narrowed, declined, blocked, maintained, suspended, or ended.

Evidence does not grant exposure. ERR does not itself grant exposure. ETR makes a bounded exposure decision using ERR context and separate prerequisites.

### 2. Why CTP is contextual/relational rather than a universal trust score
CTP evaluates evidence for a specific:
- source function and context;
- target function and context;
- target exposure dimension;
- consequence compatibility;
- relationship transition;
- observation coverage and integrity;
- identity continuity and causation;
- freshness and correction/dispute state.

It does not produce a single global reputation or trustworthiness score. A record can be relevant for one dimension and not another, or sufficient for a low-consequence transition but insufficient for a high-consequence one.

### 3. Separation of trust/reliability evidence, authority, resource entitlement, self-stewardship, and civil standing
- **Trust/reliability evidence** appears through ERR relevance and ETR evidence sufficiency.
- **Authority** is a separate prerequisite (`authorityPrerequisite`) with its own applicability, state, basis, check time, and dispute state.
- **Resource entitlement** is a separate prerequisite (`resourcePrerequisite`).
- **Self-stewardship** is a separate prerequisite (`selfStewardshipPrerequisite`).
- **Composition** and **contestability** are separate prerequisites.
- **Civil standing** is not created or replaced by CTP. CTP does not convert evidence into civil status, permission, or entitlement.

Thus evidence of reliability does not automatically grant authority, resources, stewardship standing, or civil standing.

### 4. Why Schema Validation is not Full Semantic Conformance
JSON Schema 001 enforces:
- types;
- required fields;
- enumerations;
- structural conditional requiredness;
- basic if/then relations.

Deterministic Validator 001 enforces cross-field semantic rules such as:
- projection fields when projected;
- correction/supersession references;
- dispute references where required;
- observation coverage consistency;
- review state consistency;
- mixed-dimension decision consistency;
- prerequisite applicability/state consistency;
- control requiredness for conditional approval;
- terminal reason requiredness;
- recommendation type preservation;
- no CTP authority self-reference.

A record can therefore satisfy Schema 001 while failing deterministic semantic conformance.

### 5. How a structurally valid record can fail deterministic semantic validation
The invalid fixtures are intentionally structurally plausible. Their invalidity depends on relationships between fields, not malformed JSON or missing required schema fields.

Examples:
- Fixture 003 is structurally valid but combines ordinary approval with a REQUIRED/UNKNOWN authority prerequisite.
- Fixture 004 declares partial approval but contains only one approved dimension transition.
- Fixture 010 declares control-dependent approval while supplying no controls.
- Fixture 013 is structurally valid but lacks an `endReason` for `ENDED`.

These are semantic failures, not serialization failures.

### 6. Role of DimensionTransitions in mixed exposure decisions
`DimensionTransitions` are authoritative for material requested exposure changes. Each material requested dimension change should appear as a transition with:
- dimension;
- current state;
- requested state;
- disposition;
- ERR refs;
- prerequisite refs;
- added controls;
- basis.

For mixed decisions:
- `PARTIALLY_APPROVED_BOUNDED` requires materially different dispositions.
- It requires at least one approved or approved-with-controls disposition and at least one non-approved disposition.
- Overall decision state must be consistent with dimension dispositions.
- `APPROVED_WITH_CONTROLS` requires non-empty controls at dimension or record level.

### 7. Representation of corrections, disputes, projection, observation coverage, and review state
- **Projection**: `projectionState` is `NOT_PROJECTED`, `PROJECTED`, or `UNKNOWN`. If `PROJECTED`, `sourceProviderRef` and `evidenceProjectedAt` are required.
- **Corrections**: `correctionState` includes `NONE_REQUIRED`, `UNCORRECTED`, `CORRECTED`, `PARTIALLY_CORRECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `DISPUTED`, `UNKNOWN`. Corrected/partially corrected requires `correctionRef`; superseded requires `supersedesRef`.
- **Disputes**: `disputeState` includes `NONE`, `OPEN`, `RESOLVED`, `UNKNOWN`. If open/resolved and material to consequential use, `disputeRef` is required.
- **Observation coverage**: `observationCoverage` includes sufficient, partial, minimal, unknown, not applicable, disputed. `NON_EVENT_OBSERVATION` cannot use `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
- **Review state**: ERR `reviewState` required/not required/unknown. If required, `reviewOrExpiry` is required. ETR review state required/not required/unknown. If required, `reviewTime` and `reviewFailureDisposition` are required and the failure disposition cannot be `NOT_APPLICABLE`. If not required, a present failure disposition must be `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

### 8. Treatment of recommendations and cross-provider evidence
Recommendation evidence remains typed as `RECOMMENDATION` when projected or imported. It is not converted into direct behavioural evidence.

Cross-provider projected evidence must declare:
- `sourceProviderRef`;
- `evidenceProjectedAt`;
- projection provenance.

Recommendation evidence can be partially relevant, but it does not transfer trust. Provider federation does not become reputation federation.

### 9. Why voluntary exit, privacy choice, and absence of history are not adverse trust evidence
- Empty `errSet` is valid and must not be interpreted as adverse evidence.
- `evidenceSufficiencyState = NOT_REQUIRED_FOR_TRANSITION` permits an empty ERR set.
- A voluntary participant-requested narrowing must not require adverse evidence.
- `ENDED` requires an `endReason`, but terminal relationship state is not itself an adverse trust finding.
- Privacy boundary, exit route, and absence of history are not converted into negative trust evidence.

### 10. Prerequisite rule, including NOT_APPLICABLE != NOT_CHECKED
Prerequisite wrapper:
- Applicability: `REQUIRED`, `NOT_REQUIRED_FOR_FUNCTION`, `UNKNOWN`.
- State: `SATISFIED`, `PARTIALLY_SATISFIED`, `NOT_SATISFIED`, `UNKNOWN`, `DISPUTED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Rules:
- If applicability is `NOT_REQUIRED_FOR_FUNCTION`, state must be `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
- If applicability is `REQUIRED`, state must not be `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
- Ordinary approval cannot rely on a REQUIRED prerequisite that is `UNKNOWN`, `NOT_SATISFIED`, or `DISPUTED`.
- `NOT_APPLICABLE` means checked and not required. It is not the same as unchecked, unknown, or omitted.

### 11. Why CTP has no generic emergency/admin override
Model 004 contains no `emergencyOverride`, `adminOverride`, `trustOverride`, or equivalent bypass field.

Emergency or exceptional authority may exist externally, but it must be established independently and referenced as an external basis. CTP itself does not provide a generic override. `EMERGENCY` may be a transition trigger, but it does not become a CTP bypass.
