# Environment and Habitat Stewardship

**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL ARCHITECTURE DISTRIBUTED / DOMAIN COMPOSITION IN DEVELOPMENT / ENVIRONMENT-SPECIFIC SEMANTIC LAYER PARTIALLY ABSENT

## Persistent function

This domain stewards environmental and ecological conditions, habitats and long-term living contexts across places, worlds and generations.

Its first responsibility is environmental legibility: understanding what exists, how it functions, what it depends upon, how well it is understood and how it changes through time before assuming that regulation or intervention is required.

Environmental commons are managed by Concord as steward on behalf of participants rather than treated as property of the administrative system.

## Current development direction

Environment and Habitat Stewardship is a **composition-first domain**.

Substantial generic operating architecture already exists elsewhere in Concord for knowledge/provenance, metrics and state observation, Historical temporal custody, state representation, review triggers, dependency propagation, contextual boundaries, permissions, bounded capability, cross-boundary externalities, transitions, lifecycle review, continuity and participant reporting.

The principal remaining local work is the environmental semantic layer: environmental/habitat subjects, functions and dependencies, state/baseline semantics, evidence and monitoring requirements, impact/materiality, compatibility, restoration/recovery/equivalence and commons-stewardship interfaces.

Environmental observations should bind to the existing Metrics and State Observation architecture for current measurement and to Historical for durable environmental records, baselines and temporal reconstruction. Environment supplies environmental meaning; Metrics supplies observation architecture; KCS preserves current knowledge/provenance; Historical preserves interpretable environmental state across time.

> **Observation != Classification != Understanding != Evaluation != Intervention != Authority**

> **Management of the commons != ownership of the commons**

> **Protected != unused**

> **Development presence != environmental impact**

## Three-dimensional spatial interface

The domain recognises that the Concord is not a two-dimensional plane. One geographic footprint may contain multiple bounded surface, subsurface and above-surface contexts.

Environment owns assessment of material environmental interactions between those layers. Spatial and Developmental Planning owns wider spatial composition, while other domains retain ownership of their substantive functions.

Compatible layers may coexist, and complementary layers may exchange useful resources, provided environmental function, uncertainty, dependencies and cross-layer effects remain represented.
