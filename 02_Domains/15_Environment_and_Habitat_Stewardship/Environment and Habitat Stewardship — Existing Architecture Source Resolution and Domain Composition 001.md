## 1. Purpose

This note resolves what the Environment and Habitat Stewardship domain should legitimately own before new environmental architecture is invented.

The domain was initially established with the honest status **ARCHITECTURE LARGELY UNDEVELOPED**. Subsequent source resolution shows that this is now too coarse. A substantial portion of the required architecture already exists elsewhere in Concord as portable or cross-domain mechanisms.

The correct development task is therefore not to build an environmental governance system from first principles.

It is to:

1. identify the environmental functions that genuinely belong to this domain;
2. bind those functions to existing Concord architectures;
3. expose missing interfaces;
4. apply ESCP to the environmental representation itself; and
5. develop only residual environmental/habitat functions for which no adequate supplier exists.

> **Missing locally != missing architecturally.**

> **Environmental domain composition != environmental reinvention.**

---

## 2. Resolved architectural unit

**Target unit type:** developing domain / candidate future integrated domain architecture.

The Environment and Habitat Stewardship domain is intended to own environmental and habitat subject-matter functions. It is not intended to absorb generic Concord mechanisms merely because they are needed in environmental cases.

The domain should therefore own the meaning of environmental state while consuming generic mechanisms for knowledge, review, permission, authority, transition, continuity, externalities and participant attention.

---

## 3. Foundational development orientation

The domain should begin with understanding rather than regulation.

**Observe -> Identify -> Classify -> Map relationships/dependencies -> Establish baseline -> Monitor -> Detect change -> Investigate/understand -> Evaluate -> Consider response where justified.**

The following separations are foundational:

> **Observation != Classification != Understanding != Evaluation != Intervention != Authority.**

> **Environmental knowledge != environmental judgement != environmental authority.**

Monitoring is primarily a learning and state-detection function. A change in represented environmental state creates a reason for review; it does not by itself establish harm, causation, authority or remedy.

---

## 4. Commons stewardship

Environmental commons are stewarded by Concord on behalf of participants rather than owned by the administrative system.

> **Management of the commons != ownership of the commons.**

The domain should support maintenance and improvement of valuable natural function where reasonably possible while allowing compatible civil and productive use.

> **Protected != unused.**

> **Unused development capacity != wasted commons.**

Environmental stewardship therefore requires environmental understanding, not a presumption either for development or for exclusion.

## 5. AUR functional source-resolution matrix

### 5.1 Environmental subject identification and domain boundary

**Required function:** identify what environmental/habitat subject is being evaluated and distinguish the environmental object from neighbouring civil, spatial, infrastructure or resource objects.

**Local ownership:** LOCAL-OWNER.

**Existing supplier:** AUR supplies the method for resolving architectural units, but it does not define environmental subjects.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C7 candidate residual at the environmental semantic layer**, bounded to the definition/representation of environmental and habitat subjects.

Environment must define what counts as the environmental subject of observation without assuming that a legal parcel, map polygon, building footprint or administrative boundary is the correct ecological unit.

---

### 5.2 Environmental observation, evidence and knowledge provenance

**Required function:** preserve observations, datasets, models, baselines, hypotheses, confidence, uncertainty, provenance and revision history.

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment owns domain-specific evidence meaning and measurement requirements; generic knowledge preservation is non-local.

**Existing suppliers:** Knowledge Control System; Constitutional Metrics and Civilisation Architecture Framework.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION**, subject to a domain interface.

Environment should specify what environmental observations mean and what evidence is relevant. KCS should preserve knowledge state/provenance. Existing metrics architecture supplies the broader measurement-without-automatic-authority philosophy.

**Interface need:** environmental evidence objects and measurements must be representable through KCS/metrics without collapsing observation into judgement.

---

### 5.3 Evaluation-space completeness

**Required function:** test whether environmental evaluation omits material dimensions, relationships or effects.

**Local ownership:** NON-LOCAL method; Environment must apply it.

**Existing supplier:** Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem (ESCP).

**AUR result:** **AUR-C3 EXTERNALLY SATISFIED.**

Environment should not create a parallel completeness method.

---

### 5.4 Environmental state representation

**Required function:** represent current environmental/habitat state without collapsing presence, condition, maturity, sufficiency, uncertainty or dependency into a single score.

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment owns domain state dimensions and semantics; generic state grammar is non-local.

**Existing supplier:** State and Maturity Mapping (SMM).

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION**, with a residual need to define environmental state dimensions.

No universal “habitat quality percentage” should be assumed merely for convenience.

---

### 5.5 Environmental causal investigation

**Required function:** examine possible causes of observed environmental state/change and reconsider whether the correct problem has been represented.

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment supplies environmental evidence and domain hypotheses.

**Existing suppliers:** Reality Trees; Blaineyan Reasoning.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C3/C4 EXTERNALLY/DISTRIBUTED SATISFIED.**

Environment does not need a new generic causal-reasoning method.

---

### 5.6 Monitoring and review triggers

**Required function:** detect material environmental state/evidence changes and route them for review.

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment defines material environmental conditions and thresholds where justified; trigger architecture is non-local.

**Existing supplier:** State Triggered Review Architecture (STRA).

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION.**

Environment supplies domain state/evidence and materiality criteria. STRA supplies trigger representation and routing.

> **Triggering review != authority over outcome.**

---

### 5.7 Knowledge-change propagation

**Required function:** reconsider dependent environmental conclusions, classifications, permissions or systems when upstream evidence/models materially change.

**Local ownership:** NON-LOCAL mechanism; Environment supplies dependency semantics.

**Existing supplier:** KCS Change Propagation.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C3/C4 EXTERNALLY/DISTRIBUTED SATISFIED.**

New environmental evidence should create bounded candidate review, not automatic invalidation.

---

### 5.8 Spatial and habitat context classification

**Required function:** represent public/protected/restricted relationships, nested contexts, temporary protection and environmental compatibility without treating every physical area as one indivisible use.

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment owns environmental/habitat function and protection basis; generic contextual boundary/access grammar is non-local.

**Existing suppliers:** Basic Civil Space Architecture; Contextual Wrapper Architecture (CWA); PH-03.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION**, with domain composition required.

Important separations:

> **Habitat type != access class.**

> **Protected != untouchable.**

> **Primary access class != environmental state.**

> **Temporary environmental protection != permanent reclassification.**

---

### 5.9 Three-dimensional contextual spatial use

**Required function:** represent multiple vertically or otherwise spatially overlapping contexts within the same geographic footprint.

**Local ownership:** DISTRIBUTED between Environment and Spatial/Developmental Planning.

**Existing supplier:** CWA supplies bounded/nested/overlapping context grammar; FPA supplies contextual permission; PH-03 supplies compatibility reasoning.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION**, with a new domain application/interface rather than a new core architecture currently indicated.

Foundational development principle:

> **The Concord is not a two-dimensional plane.**

A geographic footprint may contain surface, subsurface and above-surface contexts with different functions, protections and authorities.

> **Co-location != shared authority.**

> **Physical separation != environmental separation.**

Environment owns assessment of environmental interactions between layers. Spatial Planning owns composition of civil spatial uses. Other domains own their substantive functions.

---

### 5.10 Environmental compatibility and development impact

**Required function:** determine whether a civil activity materially affects environmental/habitat function.

**Local ownership:** LOCAL-OWNER for environmental effect/compatibility semantics.

**Existing suppliers:** PH-03 provides contextual compatibility reasoning; ESCP guards omitted dimensions; CBERR supplies cross-boundary consequence handling.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 with a C7 residual environmental-science layer.**

The residual is not a generic permission system. It is the environmental method/content needed to describe habitat functions, sensitivities, dependencies, impact pathways, evidence and materiality.

> **Development presence != environmental impact.**

Environmental compatibility should be based on material effects on environmental state/function rather than mere spatial overlap.

---

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION.**

> **Need for environmental action != unlimited environmental authority.**

### 5.13 Cross-boundary environmental effects

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment supplies environmental evidence/materiality.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C3/C4 EXTERNALLY/DISTRIBUTED SATISFIED.**

Environment should not allow environmental consequence to manufacture unlimited jurisdiction.

---

### 5.14 Environmental transition and restoration state

**Required function:** coordinate changes such as degradation, contamination, restoration, recovery, protection activation/deactivation, reopening and other transitions across independently owned state systems.

**Local ownership:** PARTIAL. Environment defines environmental transition semantics.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C4 DISTRIBUTED SATISFACTION.**

Examples:

- construction complete != ecological recovery complete;
- access reopened != habitat restored;
- authority ended != residual environmental responsibility ended;
- rollback != restoration unless relevant prior-state equivalence is actually re-established.

### 5.20 Functional nature and environmental replacement

**Required function:** determine what environmental functions are displaced by development and which can genuinely be retained, restored or recreated within the spatial stack.

**Local ownership:** LOCAL-OWNER for environmental equivalence/function assessment.

**Existing suppliers:** ESCP, SMM, KCS, STRA and CWA supply generic safeguards/mechanisms but do not define environmental equivalence.

**AUR result:** **AUR-C7 candidate residual environmental-semantic function.**

Important safeguards:

> **Visual greening != ecological replacement.**

> **Presence of vegetation != environmental function.**

> **Replacement != equivalence.**

The domain needs a corrigible way to describe environmental functions and compare claimed preservation/replacement without reducing ecology to decorative surface coverage.

## 6. Residual functions currently justified for Environment ownership

After bounded source resolution, the strongest genuine Environment-specific development candidates are:

### E-R1 — Environmental/habitat subject model

A way to identify the environmental subject being observed that does not assume administrative parcel boundaries are ecologically correct.

Candidate dimensions may include spatial extent, vertical extent, temporal state and relational/dependency boundaries.

### E-R2 — Environmental function and dependency representation

A domain grammar for representing what an environmental/habitat system does and what materially depends on what.

This may include hydrological, geological, soil, atmospheric, ecological, lifecycle, habitat-connectivity and other functions where relevant.

The grammar must remain extensible rather than assuming present ecological science is complete.

### E-R3 — Environmental state and baseline semantics

Domain-specific state dimensions needed by SMM/KCS/STRA: current condition, historical/baseline context, trend/change, vulnerability/sensitivity, recovery state, uncertainty and other justified dimensions.

No universal scalar score is currently justified.

### E-R4 — Environmental evidence and monitoring requirements

Domain-specific requirements for what should be observed, at what spatial/temporal resolution, with what provenance/confidence, and how observational blind spots are represented.

This is environmental content bound to KCS/metrics/STRA, not a replacement for those architectures.

### E-R5 — Environmental impact/compatibility model

A way to represent material interaction between an activity/context and environmental functions, including direct, indirect, delayed, cumulative and cross-layer effects.

This is necessary for three-dimensional contextual planning.

### E-R6 — Environmental materiality and intervention basis

Domain criteria for when a represented change/effect is sufficiently material to justify review and when evidence may support candidate intervention.

This must remain separate from authority to intervene.

### E-R7 — Restoration/recovery/equivalence semantics

A way to distinguish improvement, restoration, functional replacement, partial recovery and non-equivalent substitution.

This is required to prevent nominal greening or superficial replacement from being treated as ecological equivalence.

### E-R8 — Environmental commons stewardship interface

A domain-level expression of stewardship duties toward participant commons: maintenance, improvement, compatible use, preservation of options, monitoring and accountability.

The legitimate source of stewardship authority remains external to this domain architecture.

### E-R9 — Planetary/off-world environmental applicability

The environmental representation must not assume Earth ecology, atmosphere, gravity, biology or human habitability as universal defaults.

This remains a candidate development requirement rather than a completed architecture.

## 7. ESCP analysis of the environmental representation

Environment is unusually exposed to ESCP because apparently precise environmental models can omit consequential dimensions.

At minimum, every consequential environmental evaluation should consider whether its represented space has omitted relevant dimensions in the following families.

### 7.1 Spatial completeness

Possible omissions:

- surface/subsurface interaction;
- vertical extent;
- upstream/downstream effects;
- groundwater;
- air movement;
- ecological corridors;
- neighbouring habitats;
- distributed resource flows;
- effects outside administrative boundaries.

A two-dimensional map is not a complete environmental model merely because the development footprint is drawn accurately.

---

### 7.2 Temporal completeness

Possible omissions:

- seasonal cycles;
- breeding periods;
- migration;
- dormancy;
- regeneration;
- delayed contamination;
- cumulative exposure;
- succession;
- long recovery periods;
- climate trends;
- rare but consequential events.

A snapshot may correctly describe current state while misrepresenting the system's lifecycle.

---

### 7.3 Relational/dependency completeness

Possible omissions:

- pollination;
- predator/prey relationships;
- soil/microbial relationships;
- water dependencies;
- nutrient cycles;
- migration routes;
- shared resources;
- hidden infrastructure dependencies;
- cross-habitat functions.

Observed objects alone may not reveal the consequential relationships between them.

---

### 7.4 Substrate/participant completeness

Possible omissions:

- environment safe for one substrate but hazardous to another;
- participant activity harmless to one habitat but damaging to another;
- sensing/maintenance needs differing across embodiments;
- artificial/hybrid participants exposing assumptions inherited from human-only planning.

> **Access class != substrate compatibility.**

---

### 7.5 Scale completeness

Possible omissions:

- locally negligible effects that accumulate regionally;
- locally beneficial interventions with wider negative consequences;
- regional processes invisible at parcel scale;
- small critical habitat elements whose function is disproportionate to area.

Area alone is not a reliable proxy for environmental importance.

---

### 7.6 Causal completeness

Possible omissions:

- multiple interacting causes;
- delayed causes;
- feedback loops;
- confounding factors;
- common upstream causes;
- intervention effects mistaken for natural change.

Reality Trees can search the represented causal possibility space; ESCP must still ask whether the possibility space itself is incomplete.

---

### 7.7 Historical/baseline completeness

Possible omissions:

- shifting baselines;
- already degraded reference conditions;
- historical land use;
- previous contamination;
- prior restoration;
- lost seasonal or migratory states;
- historical observation bias.

“Current normal” does not necessarily mean healthy or natural.

---

### 7.8 Lifecycle/transition completeness

Possible omissions:

- construction versus operation effects;
- maintenance;
- decommissioning;
- restoration;
- dormant infrastructure;
- failure states;
- emergency operation;
- long-lived residual effects.

A development can be compatible in normal operation while environmentally harmful during construction or retirement.

---

### 7.9 Reversibility/recoverability completeness

Possible omissions:

- whether removal actually restores prior function;
- recovery time;
- irreversible threshold crossing;
- path dependence;
- legacy contamination;
- lost biodiversity or connectivity.

> **Designed reversibility != demonstrated environmental recoverability.**

---

### 7.10 Knowledge/measurement completeness

Possible omissions:

- unmeasured variables;
- sensor blind spots;
- inaccessible locations;
- proxy measures mistaken for direct measures;
- model assumptions;
- sparse historical data;
- observational bias;
- confidence inflation.

Absence of detected harm is not equivalent to evidence that no harm exists.

## 8. Three-dimensional contextual planning interface

The recent development note establishes a major cross-domain interface.

Environment should not own general spatial planning, but it must be able to describe environmental conditions across spatial layers and the interactions crossing them.

Candidate conceptual stack:

**Above-surface context**  
↓ interactions  
**Surface environmental/civil context**  
↓ interactions  
**Shallow subsurface context**  
↓ interactions  
**Deep engineered/geological context**

The stack is not a universal number of layers. Layers should follow material function.

Examples include:

- habitat above deep computing;
- crops/livestock above processing/storage/infrastructure;
- managed forest above timber processing/logistics;
- housing above compatible infrastructure;
- functional nature on roofs or terraces above buildings.

The environmental question is not simply whether layers overlap. It is whether their interactions preserve, improve or materially degrade environmental function.

---

## 9. Beneficial cross-layer coupling

Environment should be capable of representing beneficial as well as harmful interactions.

Examples include:

- waste heat used for crop growth or residential heat;
- organic residues used as feedstock/energy;
- forestry residues reused materially or energetically;
- water capture/treatment integrated with habitat;
- functional green layers providing water management, food, thermal or biodiversity value.

However:

> **Beneficial output != mandatory dependency.**

Where contexts become dependent upon each other, Continuity/BTA/LSR should represent the dependency and transition consequences.

---

## 10. Functional nature interface

Where infrastructure displaces natural or semi-natural surface, Environment should identify:

1. what environmental functions were present;
2. what functions are lost or altered;
3. which functions can remain;
4. which functions can be recreated elsewhere in the spatial stack;
5. whether claimed replacement is materially equivalent, partial, different or uncertain;
6. how performance will be monitored.

The domain should prefer useful environmental function over decorative substitution where functional integration is reasonably possible, without converting examples such as green roofs into universal prescriptions.

## 11. Updated domain maturity finding

The prior description **ARCHITECTURE LARGELY UNDEVELOPED** is no longer an adequate architectural diagnosis.

A more accurate current finding is:

> **FOUNDATIONAL ARCHITECTURE DISTRIBUTED / DOMAIN COMPOSITION IN DEVELOPMENT / ENVIRONMENT-SPECIFIC SEMANTIC LAYER PARTIALLY ABSENT**

The domain already has substantial architectural suppliers for:

- knowledge/provenance;
- measurement philosophy;
- state representation;

- dependency propagation;
- causal reasoning;
- contextual boundaries;
- permissions;
- bounded capability;
- cross-boundary externalities;

The primary residual work is environmental science/semantics and the interfaces that bind those semantics to the existing Concord architecture.

## 12. Development order

The next development work should proceed in this order:

1. **Environmental/habitat subject model.**
2. **Environmental function and dependency representation.**
3. **Environmental state/baseline/evidence model.**
4. **Monitoring interface to KCS/SMM/STRA.**
5. **Impact and compatibility representation, including three-dimensional cross-layer effects.**
6. **Materiality/review interface.**
7. **Restoration/recovery/equivalence semantics.**
8. **Commons stewardship interface.**
9. **Cross-domain binding to Spatial Planning, Infrastructure, Economy/Resource Stewardship, Health, Research, Historical, Governance/Judiciary/Law and Intercivilisational Relations.**
10. **Planetary/off-world applicability test.**

Only after these functions are source-resolved and represented should detailed regulation or management rules be developed.

## 15. Current conclusion

Environment and Habitat Stewardship should now be treated as a **composition-first domain**.

Its central task is to make environmental reality sufficiently legible that Concord can steward participant commons intelligently while preserving uncertainty, compatible use, environmental function and future options.

The domain should understand before it regulates; classify before it intervenes; monitor before it assumes; and reuse existing Concord architecture before creating new mechanisms.

Its remaining development challenge is therefore narrower and clearer:

> **Define the environmental subject, functions, dependencies, state, evidence, materiality, compatibility and recovery semantics that existing Concord architectures need in order to operate correctly in environmental and habitat contexts.**
