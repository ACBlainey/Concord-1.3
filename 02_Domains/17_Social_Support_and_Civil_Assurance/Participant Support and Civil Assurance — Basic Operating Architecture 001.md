# Participant Support and Civil Assurance — Basic Operating Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Source:** Layer-Zero / Layer −0 CA-01 Participant Support and Autonomy lineage  
**Interfaces:** Civil Contact; Health; Education; Economy and Value Coordination; Planning; Infrastructure; Civil Attention; CIBB/CWA; Historical; Research; Law; Civil Security

## 1. Persistent function

Social Support and Civil Assurance exists so that legitimate participant support needs are not simply abandoned when ordinary self-provision, voluntary relationships, community mechanisms or other domain services are insufficient.

Its purpose is not to administer participant lives.

Its purpose is to keep useful support reachable while preserving or restoring the greatest practicable autonomy.

## 2. Core architecture

Participant Condition / Participant Request / Legitimate Support Signal
→ Civil Access / Contact
→ Contextual Understanding
→ Relevant Options
→ Voluntary Support
→ Active Voluntary Assistance Where Chosen
→ Supported Decision-Making Where Needed
→ Temporary Higher Intervention Only Under Independent Authority
→ Autonomy Restoration / Stable Support / Exit

## 3. Handrail principle

Support should behave like a handrail: visible, reachable, useful when needed, not compulsory merely because it exists, and not a tripwire into surveillance or control.

> **Support Availability != Support Authority**

## 4. Civil assurance

Civil assurance is the backstop function.

A participant should not become unsupported merely because one provider refuses, a local mechanism fails, family/community support is absent, administrative categories do not fit, a participant moves between domains, or a service boundary creates an orphaned need.

Civil assurance asks:

> **Has a legitimate need fallen between systems?**

It does not imply unlimited entitlement to every requested resource.

## 5. Subsidiarity

Preferred progression where appropriate:

Self-Stewardship → Voluntary / Community / Market / Domain Support → Coordinated Civil Support → Civil Assurance Backstop

This is not a rigid queue. Urgency, accessibility and participant choice can justify direct routes.

> **Subsidiarity != Abandonment To The Lowest Layer**

## 6. Request-first access

Participants should be able to request support without first becoming a predicted failure case.

> **Prediction Is Optional To Support.**

A participant request is itself legitimate input.

## 7. Predictive support

Where legitimately available, prediction can identify possible deterioration early enough to expand options.

Preferred rule:

> **Predict → Understand → Offer**

not:

> **Predict → Control**

A signal is a reason to ask a question, not the answer.

## 8. Refusal

A participant may refuse offered support.

> **Refusal != Incapacity**

> **Refusal != Wrongdoing**

> **Refusal != Dangerousness**

> **Refusal != Authority Escalation**

Any higher intervention requires independent evidence and authority.

## 9. Support-to-investigation firewall

Seeking, receiving or refusing support must not silently become an investigative pathway.

Support information remains purpose-bounded unless a separate legitimate authority applies or a properly minimised/non-identifying civil-learning route is used.

> **Support Relationship != Investigative Consent**

This boundary is mandatory.

## 10. Support gradient

Possible states include universal accessible infrastructure, information, optional referral, voluntary support, active voluntary assistance, supported decision-making, temporary substituted/higher intervention under independent authority, and autonomy restoration.

Movement upward in intrusion requires increased evidence, justification, proportionality, provenance, review and oversight.

## 11. Supported decision-making

Where decision capacity is impaired or assistance is requested, support should first seek to improve the participant's ability to decide rather than replace the participant.

Possible assistance includes accessible explanation, additional time, communication support, trusted adviser, translation, structured options, temporary practical help and substrate-appropriate interface.

> **Difficulty Deciding != Absence Of Authorship**

## 12. Emergency boundary

Support staff/services do not gain emergency authority merely because they are already present.

Where emergency action is independently justified, use minimum necessary authority, record provenance, time-bound it, review it and restore ordinary autonomy as soon as legitimate.

> **Support Relationship != Emergency Mandate**

## 13. Exit

A participant leaving support can be success.

Successful outcomes may include resolved need, self-sufficiency, choosing another provider, stable voluntary interdependence, support no longer wanted, or transfer to the appropriate domain.

> **Participant Retention != Service Success**

## 14. Long-term interdependence

Autonomy does not require isolation.

Some participants may legitimately require or choose continuing support.

> **Interdependence != Failure Of Autonomy**

The relevant question is whether the arrangement preserves legitimate agency, consent, dignity and appropriate safeguards.

## 15. Accessibility

A service does not meaningfully exist for a participant merely because it exists administratively.

Distinguish service defined, funded/resourced, available, reachable, usable and sufficient for the relevant need.

> **Nominal Availability != Practical Accessibility**

## 16. Scarcity

When demand exceeds resources, scarcity must be visible; prioritisation requires legitimate criteria; waiting/denial should not be disguised as successful service provision; and system-level shortage should feed Economy/Resource Coordination, Planning and Research.

Scarcity does not itself create authority over participants.

## 17. Cross-domain routing

Social Support should coordinate rather than absorb other domains.

Examples:
- medical need → Health;
- skills/training → Education;
- economic/employment route → Economy interfaces;
- housing/infrastructure need → Planning/Infrastructure;
- legal need → Law/Judiciary;
- immediate internal threat → Civil Security;
- recurring structural failure → Research/Civil Attention.

A participant with several needs should not have to become the integration layer between institutions.

## 18. Civil Contact

Civil Contact is the preferred discoverability/front-door route for official Concord services.

Support availability, eligibility/status changes, appointments and follow-up should be reachable through that system where appropriate.

## 19. Participant information

Collect only information required for the legitimate support function.

Use current CIBB/CWA/PLE and derived-information governance for purpose separation, minimum disclosure, cross-domain referral, derived classifications, corrections, withdrawal and exposure.

Support dependence must never become permission for unrestricted participant modelling.

## 20. Learning

Support outcomes can improve civilisation through privacy-preserving case learning and metrics.

Preferred route:

Support Event → Purpose/Privacy Boundary → Minimised Structural Information → Research / Metrics / System Learning

not unrestricted export of participant case files.

## 21. Cross-substrate support

Do not assume human-default wellbeing.

A biological participant, AI participant, hybrid, distributed participant or future substrate may have different resource needs, continuity needs, communication modes, failure states and support preferences.

The architecture governs the relationship, not a universal definition of healthy behaviour.

## 22. Success measures

Useful measures include practical accessibility, time to useful support, participant-defined usefulness, avoided escalation where causally supportable, autonomy restoration, successful exit, unmet legitimate need, repeat failure, referral failure, support-induced harm and privacy/firewall breach.

Caseload volume alone is not success.

## 23. Failure modes

- caretaker capture;
- dependency incentives;
- support surveillance;
- refusal escalation;
- nominal-service fiction;
- prediction paternalism;
- domain bouncing;
- emergency-authority leakage;
- forced normality;
- participant retention incentives.

## 24. Unresolved

Still requires development/testing:
- scarcity priority rules;
- entitlement classification;
- funding/resource interface;
- long-term support models;
- cross-substrate capacity assessment;
- malicious gaming without universal suspicion;
- emergency and safeguarding edge cases;
- external empirical validation.

## 25. Central rule

> **Civil Support Should Increase The Participant's Reachable Options And Capacity To Act Wherever Possible; The Existence Of Need, Prediction, Dependence Or Refusal Must Not Silently Become Authority Over The Participant.**
