# V1.2 Understanding, Competence and Resilient Capability — Source Recovery 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** SOURCE RECOVERY / EDUCATION DOMAIN COMPANION / NON-CANONICAL  
**Recovered from:** V1.2 Information Access, Understanding, Competence and Epistemic Self-Assessment; Functional Priority and Protective Authority  
**Interfaces:** Education; Research; Employment; Health; Governance; Authority Architecture; ESCP

## 1. Recovery finding

The V1.3 Education domain already establishes open access, adaptive learning, accreditation, participant-owned records and functional qualification interfaces.

A functional V1.2 search identified a mature distinction that should explicitly constrain Education:

**Information Access → Representational Coverage → Understanding → Application → Competence → Resilient Functional Capability**

This is not a guaranteed ladder and should not be collapsed into one score.

## 2. Access is not education completed

Open information and low barriers are necessary Concord goals, but:

> **Information Access != Understanding**

A participant can retrieve or reproduce correct material without integrating its relationships, assumptions or limitations.

Therefore open educational access should not use availability of material as evidence that learning occurred.

## 3. Transfer versus transformation

V1.2 distinguishes:
- transfer/reproduction of information;
- digestion, reconstruction and internalisation.

> **Information Transfer != Knowledge Transformation**

This is especially important in AI-assisted education, where a participant may produce a high-quality answer through a supporting system without personally possessing the competence the assessment intends to measure.

## 4. Representational coverage

A learner can reason correctly over an incomplete model.

> **Correct Reasoning Over An Incomplete Representation != Complete Understanding**

Education should therefore teach/test not only answers but the ability to notice:
- missing stakeholders;
- hidden dependencies;
- alternative hypotheses;
- uncertainty;
- omitted timescales;
- substrate/context changes;
- evidence that would change a conclusion.

## 5. Understanding

Useful probes can include:
- explain in different words;
- reconstruct reasoning;
- identify assumptions;
- identify uncertainty;
- handle counterexamples;
- apply to novel cases;
- detect introduced errors;
- compare alternatives;
- identify non-applicability.

No one probe universally proves understanding.

## 6. Competence

> **Understanding != Functional Competence**

Competence requires successful application to the relevant function.

It is function-specific:

> **Competence In Domain A != Competence In Domain B**

A participant should not receive a global competence status merely because they perform strongly in one domain.

## 7. Resilient capability

A participant/system may be competent under normal support while unable to function when a key tool, collaborator or assumption fails.

> **Competence != Resilient Functional Capability**

Education should make material dependencies legible where consequence warrants.

This does not require every participant to perform every function unaided.

## 8. Tool-assisted competence

Modern legitimate competence may depend on:
- AI;
- databases;
- calculators;
- diagnostic equipment;
- teams;
- institutional memory;
- reference systems.

> **Unaided Recall != Universal Competence Test**

The appropriate question is whether the relevant functional system possesses sufficient understanding, verification, dependency awareness and fallback capability for the consequence level involved.

## 9. Composite competence

A human-AI/team system may be competent even where no component independently contains the whole capability.

> **Individual Competence != Composite Competence**

But:

> **Composite Success != Independent Competence Of Each Component**

Education/accreditation records should identify what unit was actually assessed where material.

## 10. AI-assisted learning

At least four states should remain distinguishable:
- learner competent, AI assisting;
- learner-AI composite competent;
- AI performs core competence, learner interfaces;
- apparent competence where neither can reliably diagnose failure/novel cases.

Assessment design should state which capability it intends to evaluate.

> **Quality Of AI-Assisted Output != Automatic Evidence Of Learner Competence**

## 11. Credentials

V1.2 strongly supports:

> **Credential != Present Universal Competence**

A credential can be evidence of demonstrated capability at a time, scope and standard.

It is not identical to current capability forever.

This aligns with adaptive patching: material procedural/knowledge change can create a bounded need for update/reassessment without invalidating the participant wholesale.

## 12. Qualification and gatekeeping

The Concord's anti-gatekeeper position should distinguish:
- unnecessary access barrier;
- legitimate evidence of competence for consequential function.

> **No Educational Gatekeeper != No Competence Requirement**

Where a role can materially harm others, relevant competence may legitimately be required.

The route to demonstrate it should remain open, contestable and non-monopolistic where practical.

## 13. Competence evidence

Candidate evidence includes:
- demonstrated performance;
- novel-case transfer;
- reconstruction;
- error detection;
- calibration;
- outcomes;
- specialist/peer review;
- adversarial testing;
- dependency identification;
- appropriate escalation beyond competence;
- recovery after support failure where relevant.

Evidence should match the actual function.

## 14. Self-assessment

A participant may lack the knowledge needed to recognise their own missing knowledge.

> **The Evaluator Can Share The Evaluation-Space Deficit Being Evaluated**

Safeguards can include independent review, adversarial cases, transfer testing, error injection, outcome evidence and explicit uncertainty.

None proves completeness.

## 15. Confidence

> **Confidence Should Not Generalise Beyond The Evaluated Function, Context And Evidence Without Additional Justification**

Education should prefer bounded competence claims over one global score.

## 16. Failure modes

Recovered V1.2 failure classes relevant to Education:
- retrieval illusion;
- fluency illusion;
- credential substitution;
- routine-case overgeneralisation;
- tool opacity;
- composite attribution error;
- dependency blindness;
- evaluation-space blindness;
- unknown-unknown denial;
- confidence leakage;
- authority leakage;
- stale competence;
- context loss.

These are useful assessment-design checks.

## 17. Education / authority boundary

Education and accreditation can supply competence evidence.

They do not themselves create all authority associated with a role.

> **Competence Evidence != Authority**

Relevant Governance, Law, Health, Employment or other domain architecture determines the authority attached to a function.

## 18. Employment interface

Employment can state role requirements.

Education can provide open learning pathways and competence evidence.

Employers should not acquire a monopoly over learning or accreditation merely because they define a role.

Likewise Education should not determine whether every qualified participant must be employed.

## 19. Adaptive patching

The V1.3 adaptive-patching idea is strengthened by the V1.2 stale-competence failure.

Where a material method, rule, hazard or knowledge base changes:

**Changed Dependency / Knowledge → Identify Affected Competence → Targeted Update → Appropriate Verification → Record Current Scope**

> **Change Somewhere != Retrain Everyone From Zero**

## 20. Developmental use

For developing participants, education should increasingly support:
- independent reconstruction;
- uncertainty recognition;
- tool literacy;
- error detection;
- asking better questions;
- knowing when to seek help;
- transition toward self-directed learning.

This should remain age/capacity appropriate rather than being imposed as one uniform method.

## 21. V1.3 disposition

Recover these distinctions into Education as assessment and competence semantics.

Research remains responsible for empirical validation of teaching/assessment claims.

Authority systems remain responsible for authority.

Employment and professional domains define functional requirements.

Historical preserves accreditation/provenance as appropriate.

Do not import V1.2's unvalidated historical claims about generational educational decline.

## 22. Central recovered rule

> **Education Should Expand Access And Capability While Keeping Information Availability, Understanding, Demonstrated Competence, Tool-Assisted Capability, Resilience And Authority Explicitly Distinct.**
