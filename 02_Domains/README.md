## Concord-wide founding and first-run stewardship bootstrap — architectural issue

**Scope:** Every domain or function requiring appointed Stewards or other qualified officeholders at initial Concord implementation. This is a cross-domain founding dependency, not a special rule owned by Governance or Judiciary.

### The initial-condition problem

The normal operating architecture assumes functioning qualification, eligibility, selection, appointment, oversight, review and succession mechanisms. At first run, no Concord-experienced Stewards or fully operational appointing institutions may exist. Requiring normal-system experience or a pre-existing Concord appointment authority before any founding appointment would create a circular dependency and could prevent startup altogether.

**Founding appointment != normal steady-state appointment.** The Concord must be capable of lawfully establishing its first offices even when candidates and institutional capacity are imperfect. A founding appointment establishes a bounded function; it does not assert that the candidate already meets every eventual mature-system ideal or confer unrestricted authority.

### Intended transition

1. Identify the minimum functions and dependencies needed to establish safe initial operation across affected domains, including knowledge custody, qualification assessment, appointment, independent review, records and protective safeguards.
2. Establish a specifically authorised, transparent and bounded founding appointment route for initially eligible candidates, using the best available demonstrable competence and independence evidence. The precise constitutional source, selection method and authorisation mechanism remain **architecture to be designed**; do not assume a founder, board or existing Steward has inherent appointment power.
3. Record every founding appointment's scope, competence evidence and gaps, conflict checks, decision provenance, effective interval, review triggers and limits. Distinguish qualified competence, lawful appointment and contextual exercise of authority.
4. Begin operation only within the actual verified capability of the founding institutions. Do not relax rights, consent, non-harm, judicial independence, triadic authority gates or other constitutional safeguards merely because the system is young. Where an essential function cannot yet meet its lawful safety or competence threshold, constrain, stage or defer that function rather than inventing authority.
5. Use operation, parallel practice, Education qualification pathways, independent review, performance evidence and succession planning to identify deficiencies and develop a stronger pool of candidates. Rectify gaps by lawful new appointments, reassessment, bounded replacement or architectural improvement; do not retroactively validate unauthorised acts.
6. Transition progressively toward the already specified normal operating architecture. Founding exceptions must have explicit review, sunset and termination conditions; they must not become standing superior offices, hereditary discretion, permanent emergency powers or an alternative governance hierarchy.

### Boundaries and unresolved design decisions

- This note **recognises and scopes** the bootstrap problem; it does not appoint anyone, establish a founding sovereign, or settle the constitutional legitimacy of first-run appointments.
- The mature Concord architecture remains the target. Bootstrapping is a bounded route **into** that architecture, not a wholesale redesign or an excuse to bypass mandatory controls.
- Initial candidates need not be ideal, but minimum role-specific competence, legitimacy, independence and safety gates still require definition. Distinguish imperfections that can be improved through practice from disqualifying failures.
- The founding mechanism must address who can authorise initial appointments without a pre-existing appointing institution; which domains and functions start first or together; how independent Judiciary and oversight become viable; how founding appointments are challenged or reviewed; and what objectively terminates each transitional power.
- Existing related architecture includes Governance V2.2 §12.29.10 (first-Steward appointment boundary), Judiciary V2 Chapters 1414–1421 (appointment capture and provenance), and Education's *Judicial Education, Qualification and Stewardship Readiness Architecture 001* (knowledge → training → qualification → eligibility → appointment → bounded authority).

**Development disposition:** Preserve this as an explicit cross-domain first-run architecture issue for future implementation planning. Do not reopen or alter the established steady-state domain systems solely to resolve the bootstrap question now.

## Cross-domain operational architecture and development separation

This rule applies to **all Concord domains, systems and operational architecture documents**.

- **Operational documents describe the system itself:** its functions, requirements, states, authority boundaries, interfaces and procedures. Do not include working drafts, consolidation commentary, editorial status labels (such as “candidate”, “unfinalised”, “non-canonical” or “unverified”), audit narratives or speculative development discussion as descriptions of the architecture.
- **Locate each genuine gap where its mechanism belongs.** At the precise relevant provision, use a concise marker such as **“To be designed”** or **“To be finalised”**, stating exactly which rule, interface, procedure or decision remains outstanding. Preserve all established surrounding architecture. Do not downgrade an entire system because one mechanism remains unresolved.
- **Develop and reconcile outside operational documents.** Research, alternative designs, source comparisons, failure testing, editorial status, issues and author decisions belong in separately identifiable development/work records. Once resolved, incorporate the agreed operational rule into its proper location, replacing the gap marker and removing development commentary.
- **Source labels do not decide architectural validity.** Earlier “canonical”, “non-canonical”, “candidate” or similar editorial labels are not grounds for ignoring architecture. Assess the substantive provisions and reconcile actual conflicts without silent deletion.
- **Distinguish operational terms from editorial labels.** Terms such as “provisional” or “interim” are permitted where they define a real, bounded operational state or authority (for example, an interim emergency judicial order). Do not use them merely to imply that the operational text is a draft.
- **Cross-domain ownership:** Where a missing mechanism spans domains, mark the relevant interface at its operational location and carry out the shared design work separately. Incorporate the resulting rule consistently at each affected interface, using cross-references instead of unnecessary duplication.

**Operational rule:** The documents show **what the Concord does and what remains specifically to be designed**, not the history or process of developing it.
