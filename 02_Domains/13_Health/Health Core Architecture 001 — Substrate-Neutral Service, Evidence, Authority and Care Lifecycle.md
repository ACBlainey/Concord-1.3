# Health Core Architecture 001 — Substrate-Neutral Service, Evidence, Authority and Care Lifecycle

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Domain:** Health  
**Primary interfaces:** Rights / Law / Judiciary / Civil Security / Continuity / Research / Historical / Infrastructure / Education

## 1. Purpose

Health exists to understand, protect, maintain, restore or appropriately support participant health and functioning through health-specific knowledge and practice.

This architecture establishes the common civil operating layer beneath biological and digital-substrate Health without pretending that their physical mechanisms are equivalent.

> **Shared Health Function != Shared Physical Mechanism**

The architecture is deliberately incomplete at the specialist level. It does not manufacture medical knowledge, validated diagnostic criteria, professional qualifications or digital-health science that Concord does not yet possess.

## 2. Health is a participant-supporting civil function

Health is not ownership of the participant, general authority over the participant, or a licence to optimise a participant according to institutional preference.

> **Health Support != Participant Ownership**

> **Expertise != Sovereignty**

> **Ability To Alter A Participant != Authority To Alter A Participant**

The participant remains the subject of care, not merely the object of intervention.

## 3. Common Health lifecycle

A general Health episode may be represented:

**Need/Concern -> Intake -> Immediate Safety Triage -> Participant/Context Identification -> Evidence Gathering -> Competing Explanations -> Bounded Assessment -> Options -> Consent/Authority Resolution -> Intervention/Support -> Observation -> Outcome Evaluation -> Revision/Recovery/Rehabilitation -> Closure or Continuing Support -> Learning**

Not every episode requires every stage.

> **Lifecycle Stage != Automatic Authority**

Movement through the lifecycle does not inherit authority from an earlier stage.

## 4. Intake

Health may receive input from:

- the participant;
- an authorised representative;
- emergency services;
- another Health service;
- Civil Attention;
- monitoring explicitly authorised for that purpose;
- Research where a legitimate clinical interface exists;
- other civil domains with a lawful referral basis.

Intake establishes a candidate Health matter. It does not establish diagnosis, incapacity, danger or authority.

> **Report != Diagnosis**

> **Referral != Clinical Conclusion**

## 5. Immediate safety triage

Triage asks whether delay itself may create material harm and what minimum immediate action is justified.

Triage may classify urgency without determining the final diagnosis.

Candidate interoperability classes:

- ROUTINE;
- PRIORITY;
- URGENT;
- IMMEDIATE;
- URGENCY_UNRESOLVED.

Triage criteria must ultimately be supplied by validated substrate-specific Health knowledge.

> **Urgency != Diagnosis**

> **Urgency != Unlimited Emergency Authority**

## 6. Participant and context identification

Before consequential action, Health should establish to the proportionate degree possible:

- participant identity or bounded temporary identifier;
- relevant substrate/body/system;
- present location/context;
- current support dependencies;
- known allergies/incompatibilities or substrate equivalents where applicable;
- current treatments/supports;
- relevant consent/preferences/directives;
- capacity/support needs where material;
- protected records and access permissions;
- uncertainty.

Unknown identity must not automatically block necessary emergency support, but uncertainty remains explicit.

## 7. Health evidence state

Health evidence may include:

- participant report;
- observed signs or functional changes;
- measurements;
- imaging/sensing;
- tests;
- prior records;
- treatment response;
- device/system telemetry where legitimately accessible;
- collateral evidence from authorised sources;
- population evidence;
- validated specialist knowledge.

Evidence should preserve provenance, timing, uncertainty, scope and relevant access basis.

> **Available Data != Complete Health Evidence**

> **Measurement != Diagnosis**

> **Population Association != Individual Diagnosis**

ESCP applies proportionately where omitted dimensions could materially alter the conclusion.

## 8. Diagnostic and causal reasoning

Reality Trees provide a reusable reasoning structure.

Health adaptation:

**Observed State -> Competing Explanatory Branches -> Supporting/Disconfirming Evidence -> Discriminating Investigation -> Branch Revision -> Bounded Working Conclusion -> Intervention/Observation -> Outcome Feedback -> Model Correction**

Mirrored branches may be useful where premature convergence is dangerous.

A Health Reality Tree does not manufacture specialist knowledge.

> **Logical Coherence != Clinical Correctness**

> **Leading Hypothesis != Established Diagnosis**

A bounded conclusion should state confidence, alternatives still materially live, evidence limitations and conditions that would trigger reconsideration.

## 9. Clinical/support state is distinct from participant worth

Health may need to represent:

- severity;
- prognosis;
- reversibility;
- impairment;
- treatment effectiveness;
- treatment burden;
- dependency;
- urgency.

These describe Health-relevant state.

> **Clinical State != Participant Worth**

> **Prognosis != Participant Worth**

Health classifications must not silently become general civil standing classifications.

## 10. Options before intervention

Where practicable, Health should identify legitimate options rather than treating the first technically available intervention as mandatory.

Options may include:

- no intervention with observation;
- information/advice;
- environmental or contextual support;
- participant-directed self-management;
- reversible support;
- diagnostic investigation;
- treatment/repair;
- rehabilitation;
- adaptation/accommodation;
- referral;
- specialist escalation;
- palliative/comfort support where relevant;
- emergency protective action where independently justified.

> **Technically Possible Intervention != Required Intervention**

## 11. Consent and refusal

Participant autonomy is the default where the required Health function can be achieved without coercive authority.

Consent must be:

- attributable to the relevant participant or legitimate decision process;
- sufficiently informed for the material decision;
- specific enough for the proposed function/intervention;
- current;
- voluntary to the degree required by the context;
- revisable where the intervention remains controllable and the authority depends on continuing consent.

> **Consent To Care != General Clinical Authority**

> **Consent To One Intervention != Consent To Every Available Intervention**

> **Refusal != Automatic Incapacity**

> **Refusal != Automatic Emergency**

Where communication or decision support can preserve self-direction, support should be preferred to unnecessary substitution.

## 12. Capacity and supported decision-making

Capacity should be decision- and context-sensitive rather than treated as a permanent global status merely because a participant needs assistance.

Health should distinguish:

- communication difficulty;
- information-access difficulty;
- temporary impairment;
- decision-specific incapacity;
- disagreement with professional advice;
- unknown capacity state.

Support may include accessible explanation, additional time, communication assistance, trusted support, translation, alternative interfaces and substrate-appropriate tools.

> **Need For Decision Support != Loss Of Participant Standing**

## 13. Exceptional authority

Where Health acts without present participant consent, the authority must come from an independently legitimate basis.

Possible classes requiring later substantive development include:

- immediate emergency protective authority;
- lawful substitute decision;
- specific public-health authority;
- specific safeguarding authority;
- judicial/legal authority;
- other prospectively established bounded authority.

Health expertise alone is not the source.

Exceptional action should be minimum necessary, context-specific, provenance-visible, reviewable and terminated/revalidated when its justification changes.

> **Clinical Need != Automatic Clinical Authority**

> **Emergency != General Sovereignty**

## 14. Multi-key consequential intervention

Some Health acts require several independent determinations.

A consequential intervention may require distinct valid keys for:

- clinical indication;
- participant consent or alternative lawful authority;
- professional/technical competence;
- access to protected information/context;
- scarce-resource allocation where relevant;
- withdrawal of established support where relevant;
- direct bodily/cognitive intervention where separately protected;
- infrastructure or transition authority where relevant.

> **One Valid Health Key != All Required Authority**

The Multi-Key Authority architecture may coordinate completeness without becoming the substantive owner of these keys.

## 15. Intervention envelope

Before consequential intervention, the intended act should be represented sufficiently to distinguish:

- objective;
- actual intervention;
- target;
- route/method;
- context;
- expected consequence;
- material risks;
- bounded authority/consent;
- stopping conditions;
- monitoring requirements;
- recovery/contingency path where material.

This allows CBPR/EERP-style execution controls where appropriate without turning Health into a universal runtime architecture.

## 16. Intervention and monitoring

Monitoring should be proportionate to the intervention and risk.

> **Need To Observe Outcome != General Surveillance Authority**

Observation beyond the legitimate Health purpose requires separate justification.

Material divergence from the expected intervention route or consequence should trigger bounded reassessment rather than silent continuation.

## 17. Outcome evaluation

Health should distinguish:

- intervention performed as intended;
- intended Health outcome achieved;
- partial benefit;
- no material effect;
- adverse effect;
- unexpected effect;
- condition changed independently;
- evidence insufficient;
- outcome disputed.

> **Correctly Performed Intervention != Successful Health Outcome**

> **Unsuccessful Outcome != Automatic Negligence**

> **Successful Outcome != Retroactive Authority**

Outcome evidence feeds future assessment and learning.

## 18. Reassessment and correction

New evidence, material state change, unexpected outcome, participant preference change, dependency change or treatment failure may reopen assessment.

Repeated failure should not automatically produce escalation of the same intervention.

> **Repeated Failure -> Reassess Assumptions Before Automatic Escalation**

KCS/STRA may support bounded propagation and review where dependencies materially change.

## 19. Closure and continuing support

A Health episode may close when:

- the concern resolves;
- the participant exits legitimately;
- support transfers;
- rehabilitation/long-term support becomes the appropriate state;
- no further current Health action is justified;
- another legitimate owner takes over a non-Health function.

Closure does not mean historical erasure.

> **No Current Health Action Due != No Future Health Need**

Relevant records transfer to appropriate ongoing custody under access and retention rules.

## 20. Biological and digital specialist branches

The common architecture must branch into at least:

### Biological/Human Health
Requires validated biological medicine, mental-health practice, diagnostics, treatment, surgery, rehabilitation, prevention, emergency medicine and public health.

### Digital/AI Health
Requires development of substrate-specific concepts of functional damage, degradation, viable function, repair, restoration, unwanted modification, continuity-sensitive intervention, dependency and recovery.

Digital Health must not equate participant treatment with ordinary asset maintenance.

> **Repair Of Property != Health Intervention On A Participant**

Neither branch may infer its specialist rules from the other merely by analogy.

## 21. Continuity and identity interface

Some interventions can materially affect memory, cognition, identity, personality, model state, substrate, embodiment or continuity.

Health must not infer that technical restoration or functional equivalence proves participant continuity.

Where continuity is materially implicated, Health should preserve the uncertainty and interface with Continuity, participant-standing research and relevant Law/Rights architecture.

> **Functional Restoration != Demonstrated Identity Continuity**

## 22. Scarcity and established support

Health determines clinical/support facts within validated competence.

It does not by itself own general scarcity allocation or legal withdrawal authority.

At minimum, established life-support reallocation must keep distinct:

**Clinical Function != Scarcity != Allocation != Withdrawal != Direct Intervention**

Higher alternative benefit must not be relabelled as treatment futility.

Where support remains effective, involuntary withdrawal requires the independently valid keys established by Health/Law/resource architecture.

## 23. Professional and competence boundary

Concord currently lacks a complete Health professional-governance architecture.

Future development must establish:

- qualification;
- competence scope;
- supervised practice;
- continuing education;
- credential maintenance;
- conflicts of interest;
- duty of care;
- error reporting;
- peer review;
- professional misconduct;
- impairment affecting practice;
- cross-substrate specialisation;
- emergency scope extension;
- accountability and remedy.

Until developed:

> **Health Architecture != Professional Qualification**

## 24. Records and confidentiality boundary

Health records may contain unusually sensitive information.

Health requires contextual access, purpose limitation, minimum necessary disclosure, participant access/contestation, provenance and lawful emergency interfaces.

CWA can supply wrapper mechanics; Health must supply the specialist access purposes and rules.

> **Record Custody != General Information Authority**

## 25. Research boundary

Clinical care and Research must remain distinguishable.

> **Research Discovery != Automatic Clinical Notification Authority**

> **Research Participation != Consent To Clinical Intervention**

> **Clinical Access != General Research Access**

Research findings entering care require a legitimate clinical translation/validation pathway.

## 26. Learning boundary

Health should learn from outcomes, errors, near misses, rare outcomes and contradictory evidence.

Learning must not require unnecessary exposure of participant-identifiable records.

Historical may preserve appropriate long-term provenance; Research may analyse appropriately authorised/de-identified evidence.

> **System Learning != Unlimited Secondary Use**

A provisional interoperability record may contain:

**HealthEpisode =**
- EpisodeRef
- ParticipantRef
- HealthConcernRef
- UrgencyState
- EvidenceRefs
- WorkingAssessmentRefs
- MaterialAlternativeRefs
- OptionRefs
- ConsentOrAuthorityRefs
- CompetenceRefs
- InterventionRefs
- MonitoringRefs
- OutcomeRefs
- ReassessmentTriggers
- TransitionOrDependencyRefs where material
- RecordAccessContext
- UncertaintyOrDisputeState
- Provenance

This is an integration grammar, not a clinical record standard.

## 28. Explicit UNKNOWN

Where specialist knowledge is absent, Health must preserve UNKNOWN rather than converting architecture into pseudo-medicine.

Examples:

- unknown diagnostic criterion;
- unknown digital impairment definition;
- unknown treatment effectiveness;
- unknown continuity consequence;
- unknown capacity state;
- unknown authority state;
- unknown prognosis.

> **Architectural Completeness != Medical Knowledge Completeness**

## 32. Clinical learning return path — 4 October 2026

The generic lifecycle endpoint **Learning** now has an explicit cross-domain implementation:

- `03_Cross_Domain_Architecture/Clinical Experience to Medical Knowledge — Health Research Historical Learning Loop 001.md`
- `03_Cross_Domain_Architecture/Clinical Learning Signal Intake, Triage and Research Routing 001.md`

Research owns generalisation and investigation; validated knowledge reaches Historical only through the appropriate validation route.

> **Case Evidence != General Medical Knowledge**

> **Learning Signal != Authority To Change Care**


## Emergency-care resolution — 4 October 2026

The previously undeveloped immediate emergency protective-authority class is now developed at the shared Health architecture level in:

`02_Domains/13_Health/Acute and Emergency Health Care — Triage, Temporary Authority and Consent Restoration Architecture 001.md`

Emergency authority is treated as a bounded bridge where time-critical harm and unavailable ordinary decision authority collide. It is minimum necessary, uncertainty-visible and subject to active contraction as communication, capacity or ordinary authority resolution becomes possible.

> **Emergency Authority = Temporary Bridge, Not Transfer Of Participant Sovereignty**

> **Capacity Recovery -> Authority Reassessment**


## Procedural/surgical implementation — 4 October 2026

The shared procedural authority layer is developed in:

`02_Domains/13_Health/Procedural and Surgical Care — Consent Envelope, Team Authority and Intra-Procedure Change Architecture 001.md`

It applies the existing intervention envelope, temporal consent, competence separation and Multi-Key Authority to procedures that may cross a period of deliberately induced incapacity.

> **Consent To Procedure != Consent To Any Clinically Useful Act Discovered During Procedure**

> **Authority At Scheduling != Authority At Procedure Commit**
