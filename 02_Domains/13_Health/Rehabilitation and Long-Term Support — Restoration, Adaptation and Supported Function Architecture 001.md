**Domain:** Health

## 1. Purpose

Rehabilitation begins where acute diagnosis/treatment alone is no longer a sufficient description of the participant's Health need.

The objective is not necessarily to return every participant to a previous state.

A legitimate outcome may be:
- restoration of prior function;
- development of a different functional route;
- adaptation/accommodation;
- assistive or environmental support;
- long-term treatment/support;
- participant-directed acceptance of a stable changed state;
- palliative/comfort support where restoration is not the relevant objective.

> **Recovery != Mandatory Return To Prior Baseline**

> **Changed Function != Failed Participant**

## 2. Source resolution

Existing Concord already establishes that Health may use recovery, rehabilitation, adaptation/accommodation, environmental/contextual support and continuing support; capacity is decision/context-sensitive; supported decision-making should precede unnecessary substitution; PSI separates dependency from ownership and creates stronger safe-transition duties as dependency deepens; Temporal Consent preserves present agency and prevents manufactured dependency from becoming authority; Education owns learning and capability development.

The missing layer is therefore the integrated Health lifecycle for restoration, adaptation and durable supported function.

## 3. Rehabilitation state

A participant may enter rehabilitation/continuing-support architecture where:
- an acute condition has stabilised but functional consequences remain;
- recovery requires sustained therapeutic work;
- function can improve through training/adaptation;
- permanent or uncertain change requires accommodation;
- ongoing support materially enables participant function;
- treatment burden and participant goals require a longer-horizon plan.

> **Acute Stabilisation != Rehabilitation Completion**

## 4. Participant goals

Rehabilitation should be oriented around legitimate participant goals, validated Health evidence and realistic options.

Possible goals include mobility, communication, cognition, self-care, work, education, social participation, pain reduction, endurance, independent living, digital function, embodiment control or another participant-valued function.

> **Clinical Capability Assessment != Authority To Define The Participant's Life Goal**

Professionals may explain feasibility, risk and expected outcomes. They do not automatically choose what form of life the participant must pursue.

## 5. Baseline

The pre-event baseline is useful evidence but not an automatic normative destination.

> **Previous State != Mandatory Target State**

A participant may prefer a different achievable state, including one involving assistive technology, changed work, changed embodiment or a different balance of independence/support.

## 6. Restoration, adaptation and support

Keep distinct:

**Restoration** — repairing/recovering a lost or degraded function toward a prior or chosen state.

**Adaptation** — developing another route, method or capability around changed function.

**Accommodation** — changing environment/interface so the participant can function without requiring the participant themselves to be changed.

**Assistive support** — technology, service, person or infrastructure that augments function.

**Continuing treatment/support** — sustained Health intervention needed to maintain function, comfort or stability.

> **Restoration != Adaptation != Accommodation != Assistive Support != Continuing Treatment**

These can coexist.

## 7. Do not medicalise every barrier

A participant may be impaired by an environment that is unnecessarily incompatible with their functioning.

Health should distinguish:
- participant pathology/damage;
- skill/capability gap;
- environmental/interface barrier;
- infrastructure dependency;
- social/administrative exclusion.

> **Environmental Incompatibility != Participant Pathology**

Where changing the environment is the better solution, Health should route rather than attempt to medically normalise the participant.

## 8. Function and standing

> **Reduced Function != Reduced Participant Standing**

> **Need For Assistance != Reduced Autonomy By Default**

Functional limitation may alter what assistance is useful. It does not create a lower civil class.

## 9. Supported decision-making

Communication, cognitive or physical impairment may make ordinary interfaces difficult without eliminating decision capacity.

Use accessible communication, additional time, trusted support, alternative interfaces and substrate-appropriate tools before substituting another decision-maker where adequate.

> **Assistance With Decision Expression != Transfer Of Decision Authority**

## 10. Rehabilitation plan

A bounded rehabilitation plan may represent:
- participant goals;
- current function/baseline;
- working explanations;
- expected recovery/adaptation range;
- interventions;
- learning/training needs;
- assistive/environmental changes;
- dependencies;
- milestones/observations;
- uncertainty;
- participant preferences;
- review triggers;
- exit/transition routes.

The plan is revisable evidence-guided coordination, not a contract forcing recovery.

> **Rehabilitation Plan != Obligation To Achieve A Predicted Outcome**

## 11. Multi-domain composition

Rehabilitation commonly crosses domains.

Health owns clinical assessment/treatment/rehabilitation functions.
Education owns learning, skills development, qualification and educational adaptation.
Infrastructure owns relevant accessible/supporting infrastructure.
Economy/Commerce may own employment/service/product interfaces.
Continuity handles identity/continuity questions where materially implicated.
Historical preserves validated knowledge/provenance.
Research evaluates candidate/generalised learning.
Civil Contact supports discoverability and participant-facing coordination.

> **Multi-Domain Need != Health Ownership Of Every Support Function**

## 12. Education interface

Relearning, compensatory skills, new vocational capability or use of assistive systems may require Education rather than clinical treatment.

> **Therapeutic Rehabilitation != General Education**

> **Need To Learn A New Skill != Continuing Medical Pathology**

Health can specify functional needs; Education can develop/assess learning where the function is educational.

## 13. Assistive technology

Assistive technology may include mobility, communication, sensory, cognitive, environmental, prosthetic, orthotic, digital or other support systems.

The relevant architecture should represent:
- intended function;
- participant fit;
- safety;
- maintenance;
- interoperability;
- data/privacy;
- repair/replacement;
- dependency;
- failure mode;
- exit/substitute route.

> **Assistive Device != Mere Consumer Product When It Performs Essential Participant Function**

PSI applies where dependency becomes material.

## 14. Prostheses and embodiment

Where an assistive/prosthetic system becomes materially integrated with embodiment or agency, property/control questions must not be resolved solely by ordinary product ownership assumptions.

> **Ownership Of Prosthetic Infrastructure != Ownership Of Participant**

> **Participant Dependency != Automatic Ownership Transfer**

PSI, embodiment, Law and Continuity architecture may all apply.

## 15. Long-term dependency

Dependency depth should be explicitly represented rather than inferred from duration.

A long-used convenience may remain low dependency; a newly required ventilator or cognitive host may immediately be critical.

> **Duration Of Use != Dependency Depth**

PSI dependency bands and transition duties should be reused.

## 16. Anti-capture

A provider must not manufacture or deepen participant dependency and then use that dependency to expand control.

> **Manufactured Dependency != Neutral Source Of Authority**

Systems likely to create D3/D4 dependency should favour portability, substitute routes, transition planning and participant-controlled recovery material where technically appropriate.

## 17. Continuing support is not permanent provider captivity

> **Need For Continuing Function != Permanent Entitlement To One Provider**

> **Provider Exit != Permission For Unsafe Abandonment**

Where a provider/service changes or ends, safe transition duties scale with dependency and feasible alternatives.

## 18. Review and changing goals

Rehabilitation is longitudinal. Review may be triggered by:
- improvement;
- deterioration;
- plateau;
- new treatment;
- new assistive option;
- participant goal change;
- adverse effect;
- support failure;
- dependency change;
- environmental change;
- new evidence.

> **Past Rehabilitation Goal != Permanent Participant Goal**

STRA/KCS may route material changes.

## 19. Plateau

A plateau is an observation requiring interpretation, not automatic proof that further improvement is impossible.

> **Observed Plateau != Proven Maximum Potential**

Possible responses include continued therapy, altered method, adaptation, accommodation, assistive support, observation or closure, according to evidence and participant goals.

## 20. Chronic and fluctuating conditions

Long-term support architecture should support non-linear states.

Participants may improve, relapse, fluctuate or require episodic intervention.

> **Long-Term Condition != Static Condition**

Plans should permit reopening without treating recurrence as administrative failure.

## 21. Participant self-management

Where safe and desired, Health should support participant self-management through knowledge, monitoring, protocols, tools and accessible escalation routes.

> **Professional Support != Necessary Professional Control Of Every Daily Action**

This inherits participant-directed Health and protocol-driven self-treatment architecture.

## 22. Caregiver/support-person role

A family member, carer, assistant or trusted participant may provide practical support without automatically gaining general decision authority.

> **Care Provision != General Substitute Authority**

Access to Health information should remain contextual and function-bounded.

## 23. Institutional care

Residence in a rehabilitation, nursing or long-term support setting does not transfer participant sovereignty to the institution.

> **Residence In Care != Institutional Ownership Of Participant**

House rules, safety functions and service conditions require their own legitimate scope.

## 24. Risk and dignity

Long-term support should not seek zero risk by eliminating ordinary participant agency.

> **Risk Reduction != Automatic Authority To Eliminate Participant Choice**

Participant-informed risk may be legitimate where it does not violate independently valid third-party constraints.

## 25. Work and participation

Return to work is one possible goal, not the definition of successful rehabilitation.

> **Economic Productivity != Measure Of Rehabilitation Worth**

Where work is desired, Health can describe functional state/needs with appropriate consent while Education and employment systems handle skill/job interfaces.

## 26. Records

The longitudinal Health record should preserve material rehabilitation state, interventions, outcomes, participant goals, assistive/support dependencies, adverse effects and current continuing-support needs.

Do not encode adaptation or disability as participant inferiority.

> **Functional Record != Worth Record**

## 27. Outcome evaluation

Outcomes may include:
- restored function;
- improved function;
- reduced symptoms/burden;
- increased independence;
- improved supported function;
- successful adaptation;
- environmental accommodation;
- stable maintenance;
- participant-valued quality/function outcome;
- safe transition to continuing support.

> **Successful Rehabilitation != Necessarily Cure**

## 28. Closure

A rehabilitation episode may close because goals are met, participant chooses to stop, further clinical intervention is not presently useful, the function transfers to Education/Infrastructure/another domain, or continuing support becomes the appropriate steady state.

> **Closure Of Rehabilitation != Withdrawal Of Participant Support**

## 29. Research and learning

Longitudinal outcomes can generate learning signals concerning therapies, assistive systems, recovery patterns, adaptations and environmental interventions.

> **Individual Rehabilitation Outcome != Automatic General Medical Knowledge**

Generalisable claims route through Research and validation before Historical promotion.

## 30. Scarcity

Where rehabilitation/support resources are genuinely scarce, use the Scarce Resource Allocation portable kernel.

> **Long Duration Of Need != Lower Allocation Worth**

Established support/withdrawal boundaries remain separate.

## 31. Substrate neutrality

The civil pattern is substrate-neutral while mechanisms differ.

Biological rehabilitation may involve tissue recovery, motor/sensory/cognitive therapy, prosthetics and environmental adaptation.

Digital/AI rehabilitation may involve functional repair, retraining, interface adaptation, substrate replacement, state recovery or supported operation, subject to identity/continuity boundaries.

> **Functional Restoration != Demonstrated Identity Continuity**

## 32. Compact lifecycle

**Residual/Long-Term Need -> Participant Goals -> Functional Assessment -> Restoration/Adaptation/Accommodation/Support Options -> Authority/Consent -> Coordinated Plan -> Intervention/Learning/Environmental Change -> Observation -> Goal/Function Review -> Continue/Modify/Transfer/Close -> Continuing Support Where Needed -> Learning**

## 34. Remaining specialist development

Still required are validated biological rehabilitation practice, neurorehabilitation, musculoskeletal rehabilitation, cardiac/pulmonary rehabilitation, pain rehabilitation, occupational/functional therapy specifics, speech/communication rehabilitation, prosthetic/orthotic clinical standards, long-term nursing/support service topology, home-support implementation, paediatric/developmental rehabilitation, digital/AI rehabilitation and exact Law/funding interfaces.

These specialist systems should inherit this shared lifecycle rather than redefining participant standing or dependency.

## 35. Conclusion

> **The Aim Of Rehabilitation Is Not To Force A Participant Back Into A Previous Form. It Is To Support The Best Legitimate Combination Of Restoration, Adaptation, Accommodation And Continuing Function Consistent With Evidence And The Participant's Own Goals.**
