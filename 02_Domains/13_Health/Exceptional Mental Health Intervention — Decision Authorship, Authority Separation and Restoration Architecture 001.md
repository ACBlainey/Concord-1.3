# Exceptional Mental Health Intervention — Decision Authorship, Authority Separation and Restoration Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST AND LEGAL VALIDATION REQUIRED
**Domain:** Health
**Interfaces:** Constitutional Core / Law / Judiciary / Civil Security
**Sources:** Mental Health Crisis and Exceptional Authority; Self-Harm Contextual Protective Intervention Envelopes; Self-Harm Protective Action Matrix; Competent Grave Self-Harm Source Resolution; Decision Capacity and Apparent Subjective Reality Source Resolution; Emergency Health; Temporal Consent; Multi-Key Authority

## 1. Purpose

This architecture carries the decision-authorship distinction into involuntary assessment, detention/location restriction, treatment and restraint.

The core rule is:

> **Authority May Respond To Failure Of Decision Authorship; It Must Not Manufacture That Failure From Disagreement With The Decision.**

A mental-health intervention chain must not behave as a pipeline in which one exceptional act automatically authorises the next.

## 2. Separate questions

For every proposed exceptional intervention ask separately:

1. What is the participant's current clinical state?
2. What exact decision is relevant?
3. What is the participant's current decision-authorship state?
4. What protective function is claimed?
5. What act is proposed?
6. What independent authority permits that act?
7. What less restrictive adequate alternative exists?
8. What ends the authority?
9. Who reviews it?

> **Clinical State != Capacity State != Authority State**

## 3. Decision-authorship states

Use the source-resolved capacity states:

- **C0 — capacity sufficiently established**
- **C1 — capacity sufficiently established with support**
- **C2 — materially uncertain capacity**
- **C3 — materially diminished decision-specific capacity**
- **C4 — no present decision capacity**

These states apply only to the defined decision, context and time.

> **C3 For Decision A != C3 For Decision B**

> **Past C3/C4 != Present C3/C4**

## 4. Exceptional intervention chain

A possible sequence may be:

**Concern
-> Voluntary Engagement
-> Capacity/Decision-Authorship Question
-> Temporary Protective Assessment Where Independently Justified
-> Current Authorship Classification
-> Function Identification
-> Act-Specific Authority Verification
-> Minimum Intervention
-> Review
-> Restoration/Contraction
-> Ordinary Participant Authority**

This is not a mandatory escalation ladder.

> **Earlier Stage != Permission For Later Stage**

## 5. Involuntary assessment

Compulsory assessment is itself an intervention.

It may sometimes be justified where there is a genuine time-critical authority/decision-validity gap and the assessment is necessary to resolve it.

But:

> **Assessment Authority != Predetermined Assessment Outcome**

The purpose is to discover the participant's state, not to prove incapacity.

A legitimate assessment must permit C0/C1 as real outcomes.

> **Assessment That Cannot Find Capacity Is Not A Capacity Assessment**

Where C0/C1 is established, incapacity-derived assessment authority contracts.

Any continued compulsory assessment requires another independently valid purpose and authority.

## 6. C2 — uncertainty

C2 is particularly important.

Uncertainty does not mean incapacity.

> **Uncertain Capacity != Incapacity**

Where consequence is grave and time allows, C2 should trigger clarification, support, better evidence and reassessment.

Where grave harm is imminent and uncertainty cannot safely be resolved first, Emergency Health may provide a temporary option-preserving bridge.

> **C2 Can Justify Inquiry; It Does Not Automatically Justify Substitution**

## 7. C3/C4 — diminished or absent authorship

Where decision-specific authorship is materially diminished or absent, incapacity may become a legitimate predicate for bounded support/substitute/protective authority.

But:

> **Incapacity Predicate != Complete Authority**

The proposed intervention must still identify:
- legitimate protective function;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- act-specific authority;
- review;
- sunset.

The objective should include restoration of participant authorship where realistically possible.

## 8. C0/C1 — authorship established

Once C0/C1 is sufficiently established:

> **Incapacity-Derived Authority Ends For That Decision**

This does not mean every desired action becomes lawful.

Other independent authority may remain, for example:
- protection of third parties;
- ordinary criminal/civil law;
- another constitutional restriction;
- a separately established SH-6 civil rule if Concord later creates one.

But Health may not continue coercion by silently preserving the incapacity predicate.

## 9. Detention / location restriction

Detention must identify what function requires the participant to remain.

Possible functions are not interchangeable:
- immediate emergency stabilisation;
- compulsory assessment;
- public protection;
- execution of a lawful Judiciary order;
- another explicitly established function.

> **Location Restriction Requires A Location-Restriction Authority**

A diagnosis, treatment recommendation or C3/C4 finding alone is insufficient.

Where the claimed purpose was capacity clarification and C0/C1 is established:

> **Capacity Clarified -> Assessment-Based Detention Revalidation**

If no independent basis remains, authority contracts.

## 10. Treatment without present consent

Treatment authority must be separated from detention and assessment.

For C3/C4, a legitimate substitute/emergency authority may potentially support treatment where independently established and proportionate.

For C0/C1:

> **Capacity To Refuse -> Incapacity-Derived Treatment Authority Unavailable**

A clinician's belief that treatment is strongly beneficial does not recreate incapacity.

> **Clinical Benefit != Treatment Authority**

## 11. Restraint

Restraint is justified by the immediate function requiring restraint, not by diagnosis or detention status.

A detained participant may not require restraint.

A participant with C0/C1 may in some circumstances be restrained under an independent public-protection/self-defence authority if they are actively threatening others.

That does not make them incapable.

> **Restraint Authority Can Exist Without Incapacity**

and:

> **Incapacity Can Exist Without Restraint Authority**

This is a critical separation.

## 12. Seclusion and environmental restriction

Seclusion and highly restrictive environments should be treated as consequential interventions.

Their functional purpose must be stated.

If used because the institution lacks staff, beds, suitable architecture or de-escalation capability:

> **Institutional Resource Failure != Participant-Derived Restriction Authority**

Scarcity should trigger resource/operations escalation rather than silently expanding coercive authority.

## 13. Chemical restraint

Where medication is primarily used to suppress behaviour or movement to achieve a restraint function, the system must not classify it solely as ordinary treatment.

> **Functional Purpose Determines The Authority Question**

Treatment indication and restraint function may coexist, requiring multiple keys.

## 14. Apparent subjective reality

Assessment may consider apparent subjective reality only to the extent relevant to decision authorship, clinical understanding or immediate safety.

The evaluator does not gain authority to enforce worldview conformity.

> **Unusual Subjective Reality != Authority Trigger**

The relevant question is whether the participant can navigate the material consequence structure of the specific decision.

## 15. Insight

The term "insight" is dangerous if it means agreement with clinician diagnosis.

A participant may understand:
- what clinicians believe;
- the evidence;
- the proposed diagnosis;
- treatment consequences;
- alternatives;

and still disagree.

> **Clinical Disagreement != Lack Of Insight By Definition**

For authority purposes, use explicit decision-navigation functions rather than a vague requirement for agreement.

## 16. Restoration objective

Where exceptional authority is grounded partly in impaired authorship, the system should actively support restoration.

Possible measures include:
- treating reversible causes where legitimately authorised;
- communication support;
- time;
- reduced distress;
- accessible information;
- removal of coercive influences;
- sleep/nutrition/medical stabilisation;
- participant-chosen support;
- environmental adjustment.

> **Protective Authority Should Seek The Conditions Of Its Own Legitimate Termination**

## 17. Restoration does not require conformity

A participant may regain full authorship and continue making the same decision.

> **Same Decision After Recovery != Evidence That Recovery Failed**

This is one of the strongest tests against circular paternalism.

If C0/C1 is established, the evaluator must not preserve C3 merely because the outcome remains unwanted.

## 18. Authority transition table

| Transition | Consequence |
|---|---|
| C2 -> C3/C4 | Incapacity may become an authority predicate; act-specific authority still required |
| C2 -> C0/C1 | Incapacity-derived authority unavailable |
| C3/C4 -> C2 | certainty of substitute authority falls; reassess and narrow where possible |
| C3/C4 -> C0/C1 | incapacity-derived authority terminates for that decision |
| C0/C1 -> C2 | new material evidence may justify renewed inquiry, not automatic coercion |
| C0/C1 -> C3/C4 | requires new evidence of impaired authorship, not persistence of disfavoured choice |

> **Authority Tracks Current Predicate State**

## 19. New evidence requirement

Reclassification from C0/C1 to C2-C4 requires material new evidence or material state change.

> **Repeated Refusal != New Evidence Of Incapacity**

> **Persistence Of Choice != Deterioration By Definition**

Examples of legitimate new evidence might include acute confusion, loss of consciousness, newly observed decision-defeating factual model, intoxication, neurological deterioration or demonstrated inability to navigate a material consequence.

## 20. Independent review

The stronger and longer the restriction, the less appropriate it is for the original clinician/institution alone to determine continuing authority.

Independent review should examine:
- exact decision;
- authorship evidence;
- contrary evidence;
- current clinical state;
- claimed authority;
- intervention necessity;
- less restrictive routes;
- institutional conflicts;
- current sunset predicate.

> **Reviewer Must Be Able To Find That Authority Has Ended**

## 21. Participant challenge

Challenge to diagnosis, capacity finding or authority is evidence of a challenge, not a symptom by definition.

The participant should be able to state:
- what they believe is wrong;
- what evidence they dispute;
- what support they request;
- what alternative explanation they offer;
- what intervention they contest.

Mirrored Reality Trees should preserve these competing explanations.

## 22. Institutional conflict

An institution may benefit from continued restriction through:
- administrative convenience;
- liability avoidance;
- bed management;
- staff convenience;
- reputational protection;
- risk aversion.

These do not automatically invalidate action, but they are conflicts requiring visibility.

> **Institutional Benefit From Continued Control != Protective Justification**

## 23. Wrongful intervention

Because coercive protection can itself harm, systems should preserve evidence needed to review wrongful:
- assessment;
- detention;
- treatment;
- restraint;
- disclosure.

Potential remedies belong to Law/Judiciary, but Health must preserve provenance.

> **Protective Intent != Immunity From Review**

## 25. Result

Decision authorship gives exceptional Mental Health a stable authority boundary.

It permits strong protection when the participant cannot presently author the relevant decision, while preventing the protective system from defining unwanted decisions as proof of incapacity.

The remaining architecture can now be divided cleanly:

1. **Health** — clinical facts, decision-authorship assessment, treatment/support.
2. **Law** — substantive legal authority for liberty restriction or other civil override.
3. **Judiciary** — independent contested-authority review and remedy.
4. **Civil Security** — public-protection action under its own authority.
5. **Participant** — primary self-authority wherever current decision authorship remains established.

Next development should formalise the Health-Law-Judiciary handoff for involuntary assessment/detention and continuing review.
