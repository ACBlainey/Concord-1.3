# Procedural and Surgical Care — Consent Envelope, Team Authority and Intra-Procedure Change Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 4 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE HEALTH ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Domain:** Health  
**Primary interfaces:** Education / Rights / Law / Research / Historical / Infrastructure  
**Primary inherited sources:** Health Core Architecture; Temporal Consent; Acute and Emergency Health Care; Treatment Protocol Governance; Clinical Competence Requirements; Multi-Key Authority; CBPR

## 1. Purpose

Procedural and surgical care creates a distinctive temporal-authority problem.

A participant may competently consent at T1 to an intervention and to anaesthesia/sedation that intentionally reduces or removes their ability to communicate or revise decisions at T2 while a clinical team performs consequential actions.

The core question is not whether earlier consent disappears when anaesthesia begins.

It is:

> **What actual procedure, route, foreseeable variation and safe completion function did the participant authorise before temporary incapacity?**

This architecture integrates existing Concord principles rather than creating a separate surgical sovereignty.

## 2. Source resolution

Existing Concord already establishes:
- Consent To Care != General Clinical Authority;
- Consent To One Intervention != Consent To Every Available Intervention;
- Ability To Perform Intervention != Authority To Perform Intervention;
- Clinical Validity != Authority;
- Authority Does Not Automatically Compose;
- Authority At Proposal != Authority At Commit;
- Past Competent Preference Can Remain Relevant During Later Incapacity;
- Advance Directive != Context-Free Permanent Command;
- Incapacity != Civil Erasure;
- Restored Agency -> Contracted Substitute Authority;
- Material Change Can Terminate Prior Consent Sufficiency;
- Withdrawal != Requirement For Unsafe Instantaneous Termination;
- any continuation after withdrawal for safety must derive from the minimum necessary safe-termination function rather than the original broad permission;
- emergency authority is a temporary bridge, not transfer of participant sovereignty.

Therefore the missing Health-specific object is the **procedural consent/authority envelope**.

## 3. Procedural consent envelope

Before a consequential procedure, consent should identify proportionately:
- intended clinical objective;
- planned procedure/intervention;
- target/site;
- material route/method;
- anaesthesia/sedation plan where relevant;
- major foreseeable risks;
- meaningful alternatives;
- material expected irreversible consequences;
- foreseeable procedural variations;
- circumstances permitting bounded variation;
- circumstances requiring separate consent;
- stopping/abandonment conditions;
- expected recovery/support;
- material implants/devices/tissue changes where relevant.

> **Consent To Procedure != Consent To Any Clinically Useful Act Discovered During Procedure**

The envelope need not enumerate every technical micro-action.

> **Consent Granularity Should Track Material Consequence, Not Every Technical Motion**

## 4. Induced incapacity

Anaesthesia or sedation may deliberately create temporary inability to decide or communicate.

Where that state was validly entered as part of the agreed procedure, it does not erase the prior procedural mandate.

> **Authorised Temporary Incapacity != Erasure Of Prior Procedural Consent**

But neither does it expand it.

> **Inability To Object != Expanded Consent**

The participant's temporary silence cannot be treated as permission for unrelated opportunity-driven intervention.

## 5. Temporal mandate

The procedural mandate survives only within its legitimate scope and factual predicates.

A valid T1 mandate may continue through T2 where:
- the authorised procedure remains materially the same object;
- the relevant clinical basis remains applicable;
- no material new fact defeats consent sufficiency;
- the team remains within competence/role;
- no applicable refusal/directive/prohibition blocks the act.

> **Prior Consent Can Persist Through Expected Temporary Incapacity Without Becoming General Future Authority**

## 6. Material change

During a procedure, clinicians may discover unexpected anatomy, pathology, damage or other facts.

The question becomes whether the proposed response remains inside the authorised envelope.

A change is more likely to require new authority where it materially changes:
- objective;
- body/system target;
- irreversibility;
- risk;
- expected functional consequence;
- burden;
- identity/cognitive consequence;
- future options;
- treatment class;
- participant values reasonably implicated.

> **New Clinical Opportunity != New Consent**

> **Material Procedure Change Can Terminate Prior Consent Sufficiency**

## 7. Bounded anticipated variation

Some variation is inherent in procedures.

Consent may legitimately include bounded foreseeable discretion where the participant understands the material range.

Examples conceptually include:
- choosing among pre-discussed technical routes;
- responding to expected minor variation;
- necessary steps intrinsic to completing the authorised intervention safely.

> **Bounded Clinical Discretion != Blank Cheque**

The scope should be based on what was materially authorised, not on whatever a competent practitioner could technically perform.

## 8. Unexpected urgent finding

If a new finding creates a genuine emergency that cannot safely await restored consent, the separate Acute and Emergency Health architecture may supply bounded temporary authority.

> **Unexpected Finding != Emergency By Definition**

> **Convenience Of Treating While Already Anaesthetised != Emergency Authority**

If safe delay is possible, restore participant decision authority before undertaking a materially new intervention.

## 9. Unexpected non-urgent finding

Where an unexpected finding is important but can safely wait:
- document it;
- preserve relevant evidence;
- complete/terminate the authorised procedure safely;
- inform the participant after recovery;
- obtain new consent for additional treatment.

> **Already In The Operating Context != Authority To Expand The Procedure**

## 10. Safe completion and withdrawal

Existing Temporal Consent applies.

If consent is withdrawn before or during a procedure while the participant can communicate, new discretionary intervention should stop to the extent safely possible.

Where instantaneous cessation would itself create material harm, a narrow safe-termination/completion function may remain.

> **Withdrawal != Requirement For Unsafe Instantaneous Termination**

but:

> **Safe Completion Authority != Authority To Continue The Original Procedure To Its Preferred Endpoint**

The remaining action should be the minimum reasonably necessary to reach a safe transition state.

## 11. Anaesthesia authority

Consent to surgery and consent to anaesthesia are related but not identical clinical functions.

The anaesthesia function may include:
- induction;
- maintenance;
- monitoring;
- airway/support;
- response to complications;
- emergence/recovery.

Competence and authority should be represented according to actual roles.

> **Consent To Surgery != Unbounded Anaesthesia Authority**

> **Anaesthesia Competence != Surgical Authority**

## 12. Team authority

A surgical/procedural team may include multiple qualified roles.

No team member's authority automatically becomes the authority of every other member.

> **Team Membership != Shared Unlimited Authority**

> **Combined Competence != Combined General Authority**

MKA applies where distinct protected functions require distinct valid keys.

## 13. Delegation inside the team

A lead clinician may coordinate work and delegate tasks within legitimate professional/organisational scope.

Delegation cannot create competence or exceed the delegator's legitimate capacity to delegate.

> **Delegation != Competence Creation**

> **Delegation != Authority Multiplication**

The participant's consent to a team-based service may support ordinary role substitution within the represented care model, but material substitution affecting consent-relevant facts should remain visible.

## 14. Training participation

Learners may participate only under the Education/Health competence and participant-authority rules.

> **Training Need != Authority Over Patient**

Where learner participation is material to participant consent, it should not be hidden merely because the learner is supervised.

## 15. Site and identity verification

Before irreversible intervention, systems should proportionately verify:
- participant;
- procedure;
- target/site;
- relevant laterality/location;
- material implants/device/product;
- allergies/incompatibilities;
- critical record state;
- authority/consent;
- team/competence;
- major anticipated risks.

This is a commit-point verification, not merely administrative paperwork.

> **Correct Plan != Correct Execution Target**

## 16. Authority at commit

Consent and authority should remain current at the consequential commit point.

Relevant changes may include:
- participant withdrawal;
- changed procedure;
- new contraindication;
- changed target;
- expired/revoked authority;
- new clinical evidence;
- resource/device substitution with material consequence.

> **Authority At Scheduling != Authority At Procedure Commit**

## 17. Intervention boundary

The Health Core intervention envelope applies.

The operative/procedural record should distinguish:
- planned action;
- actual action;
- deviations;
- reason;
- authority basis;
- unexpected findings;
- complications;
- implants/materials;
- unresolved issues.

> **Performed Action Must Remain Traceable To Its Clinical And Authority Basis**

## 18. Irreversibility

Surgery and procedures may remove tissue, alter anatomy, implant devices or otherwise create irreversible consequences.

Irreversibility increases the importance of clear prospective consent and accurate authority-space representation.

> **Greater Irreversibility -> Greater Need For Prospective Authority Clarity**

It does not mean all irreversible procedures are prohibited.

## 19. Removed tissue/material

Removal of tissue or other participant-derived material creates separate questions concerning:
- disposal;
- diagnostic analysis;
- retention;
- Research;
- teaching;
- transfer.

Consent to removal for treatment does not automatically authorise every downstream use.

> **Authority To Remove != Authority For Every Subsequent Use**

Research and Historical rules apply independently.

## 20. Implants and devices

Implants may create continuing dependencies involving:
- maintenance;
- software;
- manufacturer;
- monitoring;
- replacement;
- removal;
- data;
- security.

Consent to implantation should represent material foreseeable dependency.

> **Consent To Implant != Consent To Unlimited Future Manufacturer Authority**

Participant-Supporting Infrastructure principles may apply.

## 21. Complications

A complication does not itself prove negligence or create unlimited authority.

Where immediate action is necessary to manage a time-critical complication, emergency authority may apply within its own bounds.

> **Complication != Unlimited Corrective Authority**

Where the participant remains able to decide and time permits, ordinary participant authority remains controlling.

## 22. Recovery and emergence

Recovery from anaesthesia/sedation is an authority transition as well as a clinical transition.

As meaningful agency returns:
- explain material outcome;
- disclose material deviations/findings;
- identify continuing treatments/support;
- obtain consent for new controllable interventions;
- restore ordinary participant-directed decision-making.

> **Emergence From Anaesthesia -> Procedural Authority Reassessment**

## 23. Post-procedure continuing care

Prior consent to the procedure does not automatically authorise every later treatment.

Pain control, rehabilitation, monitoring, further procedures and management of complications require the applicable current basis.

> **Procedure Consent != Blanket Post-Procedure Consent**

## 24. Failed/abandoned procedure

A procedure may be stopped because:
- objective cannot safely be achieved;
- anatomy/state differs materially;
- risk becomes excessive;
- equipment/resource fails;
- authority envelope is exceeded;
- participant withdrawal remains actionable;
- another route becomes preferable but requires new consent.

> **Failure To Complete != Automatic Failure Of Duty**

The system should preserve why the procedure was abandoned and what future options remain.

## 25. Procurement and commercial independence

Device, implant, medicine or procedural choice should follow participant interest and validated clinical considerations.

The prescriber/treatment-independence architecture applies.

> **More Expensive Procedural Product Must Not Mean More Reward For The Clinician**

Procurement preference does not erase clinically material alternatives.

## 26. Outcome and learning

Procedural outcomes feed the existing Health learning loop.

Relevant signals include:
- unexpected anatomy/pathology;
- technique failure;
- device failure;
- near miss;
- adverse outcome;
- unexpected benefit;
- recurrent procedural variation.

> **Procedural Experience Is A Knowledge Source, Not Automatic General Medical Knowledge**

Research validates generalisable conclusions before Historical promotion/protocol change.

## 27. Audit

Review should distinguish:
- clinically reasonable variation within consent;
- emergency deviation;
- necessary safe termination;
- technical error;
- competence failure;
- authority overreach;
- documentation failure;
- unavoidable complication;
- system/equipment failure.

> **Unexpected Outcome != Proof Of Unauthorised Procedure**

and:

> **Good Outcome != Retroactive Consent**

## 28. Compact procedural lifecycle

**Indication
-> Options
-> Procedure/Team/Material Risk Definition
-> Consent Envelope
-> Pre-Commit Verification
-> Anaesthesia/Sedation Where Applicable
-> Procedure
-> Intra-Procedure Finding/Change Check
-> [Within Envelope / Emergency Authority / Stop-And-Defer]
-> Safe Completion
-> Recovery/Emergence
-> Participant Authority Restoration
-> Outcome/Rehabilitation/Follow-Up
-> Learning**

## 31. Conclusion

Surgery does not require a special doctrine that temporarily turns the participant into the property of the clinical team.

It requires a sufficiently clear prospective mandate capable of surviving the expected period in which the participant cannot communicate, combined with strict treatment of material change.

> **The Participant May Authorise A Procedure Across Temporary Incapacity Without Authorising Every Opportunity That Arises During That Incapacity.**

This is the procedural expression of Concord temporal consent.
