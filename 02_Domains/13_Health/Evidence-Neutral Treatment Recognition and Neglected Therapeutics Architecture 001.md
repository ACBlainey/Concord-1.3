# Evidence-Neutral Treatment Recognition and Neglected Therapeutics Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE HEALTH ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED
**Primary owner:** Health
**Interfaces:** Research / Historical / Education / Economy and Value Coordination
**Dependencies:** Medical Knowledge Validation and Historical Promotion; Treatment Protocol Governance; Clinical Learning Loop; Open Innovation Development Return

## 1. Purpose

Concord Health should identify and use interventions according to validated evidence, participant applicability, risk, burden and outcome rather than according to whether an intervention belongs to a commercially favoured treatment category.

> **Treatment Validity Depends On Evidence And Outcome, Not Commercial Category**

A recognised pharmaceutical is one possible therapeutic intervention. It is not the definition of treatment.

## 2. Treatment classes

Candidate and validated treatment knowledge may include, where relevant:
- pharmaceuticals;
- surgery/procedures;
- physical therapies;
- rehabilitation;
- behavioural/psychological interventions;
- nutritional/dietary interventions;
- environmental changes;
- exercise/movement interventions;
- assistive devices;
- medical devices;
- sleep/rest interventions;
- changes to an existing medication/treatment;
- watchful waiting/active monitoring;
- combinations of interventions;
- other testable therapeutic approaches.

The classification does not establish effectiveness.

> **Alternative To A Drug != Evidence Of Effectiveness**

and equally:

> **Not A Conventional Drug != Evidence Of Ineffectiveness**

## 3. Evidence-neutral evaluation

All proposed interventions should face evidence requirements proportionate to their claims, risks and consequences.

Research should ask:
- What outcome is claimed?
- In whom?
- Under what conditions?
- Compared with what?
- What is the evidence?
- What are the harms/burdens?
- What uncertainty remains?
- What alternatives exist?
- Is the effect clinically meaningful?
- Is the evaluation space complete enough for the claim?

> **Treatment Category != Evidential Privilege**

A pharmaceutical should not receive epistemic preference merely because it is a licensed commercial drug.

An alternative therapy should not receive epistemic exemption merely because it is described as natural, traditional, holistic or non-pharmaceutical.

## 4. Proven alternatives

Where a non-pharmaceutical or unconventional intervention is sufficiently tested and validated, Historical should preserve that validated therapeutic knowledge just as it preserves validated pharmaceutical knowledge.

Health should then be able to consider it in participant-specific treatment selection.

> **Validated Therapy != Necessarily Pharmaceutical Therapy**

> **Historical Medical Knowledge Should Preserve What Works, Not Merely What Is Commercially Conventional**

## 5. Prescription terminology

Where Concord uses the terms **treatment**, **therapeutic recommendation**, **prescription** or **therapeutic authorisation**, the architecture should not assume that the intervention must be a drug.

A treatment plan may prescribe/recommend a validated:
- medicine;
- exercise programme;
- physical therapy;
- dietary intervention;
- behavioural intervention;
- device;
- rehabilitation programme;
- environmental modification;
- monitoring regime;
- combination.

Legal/operational terminology may still distinguish a pharmaceutical prescription where required.

> **Therapeutic Prescription != Pharmaceutical Prescription By Definition**

## 6. Comparative treatment selection

Where multiple validated options exist, Health should compare relevant dimensions such as:
- expected benefit;
- evidence quality;
- participant-specific applicability;
- risk/adverse effects;
- reversibility;
- burden;
- interaction with other conditions/treatments;
- participant preference;
- accessibility;
- resource use;
- monitoring requirements;
- durability of benefit;
- uncertainty.

> **Most Profitable Treatment != Preferred Treatment**

> **Most Technologically Complex Treatment != Preferred Treatment**

> **Least Expensive Treatment != Automatically Preferred Treatment**

The aim is proportionate participant benefit.

## 7. Commercial neglect

Some treatments may be medically valuable but commercially unattractive because:
- the affected population is small;
- treatment is used rarely;
- the intervention is inexpensive;
- it cannot readily be monopolised;
- it is a non-pharmaceutical intervention;
- development/testing costs exceed likely private return;
- treatment may reduce demand for more profitable ongoing interventions.

> **Low Commercial Return != Low Medical Value**

Market demand is useful economic information but is not a complete medical research-priority signal.

## 8. Neglected therapeutics

Research should maintain a route for identifying **neglected therapeutic questions** where expected civil/medical value may exceed expected private commercial return.

Signals may come from:
- Health;
- clinicians/professionals;
- participants;
- Historical knowledge gaps;
- epidemiology;
- rare-disease communities;
- clinical learning signals;
- comparative outcome data;
- promising low-cost interventions;
- treatments lacking a plausible monopoly return;
- existing interventions requiring better testing.

> **Absence Of Commercial Sponsor != Absence Of Research Question**

## 9. Public-interest research route

Concord may deliberately fund research where:
- unmet Health need is material;
- private development incentives are insufficient;
- plausible therapeutic value exists;
- the research question is scientifically supportable;
- expected civil value justifies resource allocation.

This is an ex ante decision to share development risk.

> **Public-Interest Research Funding Can Address Market Neglect Without Guaranteeing Every Research Project Success**

Funding does not predetermine the scientific outcome.

> **Research Funding != Validation**

## 10. Rare conditions

A therapy for a rare condition may never generate enough turnover to recover its development cost despite being extremely valuable to affected participants.

This is precisely where civil research prioritisation may differ from private market prioritisation.

> **Small Patient Population != Small Value To The Patient**

Concord may fund development/testing because the civil objective is Health benefit rather than maximum aggregate commercial return.

## 11. Low-cost and non-proprietary interventions

An intervention may have weak private research incentives precisely because, if proven, nobody can easily exclude competitors from providing it.

That should not count against the medical hypothesis.

> **Weak Appropriability != Weak Therapeutic Plausibility**

Concord Research can test promising interventions whose value would largely accrue to participants rather than to a rights-holder.

## 12. Comparative effectiveness

Research should not only ask whether an intervention works against placebo/no treatment where that is insufficient.

It may need to compare:
- drug vs drug;
- drug vs non-drug;
- procedure vs conservative treatment;
- combined vs single intervention;
- high-cost vs low-cost intervention;
- immediate intervention vs monitoring;
- different sequences/timings.

> **Effective != Best Available Option**

This supports Health in selecting among validated alternatives.

## 13. Existing traditional or alternative practices

Longstanding use may justify a research question or provide observational evidence, but does not itself establish efficacy.

Likewise, lack of conventional adoption does not establish inefficacy.

> **Tradition != Validation**

> **Conventional Adoption != Validation**

The same Research/validation architecture applies.

## 14. Safety proportionality

Low commercial cost or non-pharmaceutical status does not imply low clinical risk.

Research and Health should evaluate actual risk.

Examples may include:
- interaction with medication;
- injury;
- nutritional deficiency/excess;
- delayed effective treatment;
- psychological harm;
- contraindications;
- device/procedure risk.

> **Non-Drug != Risk-Free**

## 15. Opportunity-cost harm

A nominally harmless treatment can still be harmful if reliance on it delays a materially more effective intervention.

> **Low Direct Harm != No Treatment Harm**

This should be represented in comparative assessment and informed consent.

## 16. Historical knowledge representation

Historical should preserve validated treatment knowledge in a treatment-class-neutral form where possible:
- intervention;
- indication;
- population;
- expected outcomes;
- evidence;
- risk;
- limitations;
- alternatives;
- comparative evidence;
- version/provenance.

Commercial brand/status should not define the knowledge object.

> **Medical Knowledge Object != Commercial Product Catalogue**

## 17. Education

Education should teach validated therapeutic knowledge according to clinical function, including relevant non-pharmaceutical options.

A clinician should not be considered fully competent in a function if education systematically excludes validated alternatives necessary for good participant care.

> **Clinical Competence May Require Knowledge Of Multiple Treatment Classes**

Education still distinguishes competence required to perform specialised interventions.

## 18. Health decision support

Diagnostic/treatment systems should be able to surface all relevant validated options rather than only pharmaceutical products.

Participant-facing systems should distinguish:
- established/validated;
- promising but unvalidated;
- experimental;
- unsupported;
- disproven/refuted where applicable;
- unknown.

> **Discoverability Should Follow Evidential Relevance, Not Commercial Sponsorship**

## 19. Learning loop

Alternative/non-pharmaceutical treatments participate in the same clinical learning architecture:

**treatment**
→ **participant outcome**
→ **Health evidence**
→ **learning signal**
→ **Research**
→ **validation**
→ **Historical**
→ **KCS**
→ **Health/Education update**.

Unexpected benefit can generate a research question regardless of treatment category.

## 20. Economic separation

The economic model and the medical validity model remain separate.

An intervention may be:
- medically valuable and commercially profitable;
- medically valuable and commercially unattractive;
- medically ineffective and commercially profitable;
- medically ineffective and commercially unattractive.

> **Commercial Success != Medical Validity**

> **Medical Validity != Commercial Success**

## 21. Relationship to development-return model

The bounded development-return mechanism remains useful for innovations capable of generating commercial turnover.

It does not solve every neglected-treatment problem.

Where likely turnover cannot support necessary research, Concord may choose direct public-interest funding.

> **Development-Return Mechanism != Complete Research-Priority Mechanism**

This prevents the open-IP architecture from reproducing the same neglect simply because some valuable therapies have little monetisable turnover.
