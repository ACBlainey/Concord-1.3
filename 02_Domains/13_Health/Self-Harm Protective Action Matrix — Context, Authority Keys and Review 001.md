**Project:** The Concord Framework

**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST AND LEGAL VALIDATION REQUIRED
**Domain:** Health
**Interfaces:** Law / Judiciary / Civil Security / Emergency Health
**Parent:** Self-Harm and Suicide — Contextual Protective Intervention Envelopes 001

## 1. Purpose

This matrix operationalises SH-0 through SH-6 by mapping contextual envelopes against specific consequential actions.

It does not itself create authority.

> **Matrix Entry != Authority Source**

Every consequential act must still possess the independently legitimate keys required for that act, in the current context, at the current time.

## 2. Matrix notation

- **O** — ordinary Health function may be offered/performed under normal consent and access rules.
- **V** — voluntary/participant-authorised route.
- **E** — may be available under bounded Emergency Health authority only where the emergency predicates and act-specific necessity are satisfied.
- **L** — requires separate Law/Judiciary/Civil Security authority not supplied merely by Health status.
- **N** — not justified by this envelope alone.
- **R** — unresolved at present; explicit constitutional/Law resolution required.
- **K** — act-specific additional authority key required even if another exceptional key exists.

Combined notation such as **E+K** means emergency context may support the act only if its own act-specific necessity/authority conditions are satisfied.

## 3. Context bands

- **SH-0** — no material current self-harm signal.
- **SH-1** — distress/thoughts/low-specificity concern.
- **SH-2** — credible developing/elevated risk without established immediate grave action.
- **SH-3** — serious/imminent risk but ordinary participant authority remains obtainable within the safe action window.
- **SH-4** — imminent grave harm plus genuine temporary authority gap.
- **SH-5** — immediate harm interrupted/stabilised; continuing authority unresolved and must be reassessed.
- **SH-6** — competent, informed, sustained grave self-harm intention outside the temporary emergency authority gap; constitutional boundary unresolved.

## 5. How to read the matrix

The matrix is intentionally asymmetric.

SH-2 and SH-3 may involve serious concern without supplying coercive authority. A participant can be at elevated or even imminent risk while still retaining ordinary decision authority and engaging voluntarily.

> **Risk Severity != Automatic Authority Severity**

SH-4 is exceptional because both grave time-sensitive harm and a genuine authority gap are present.

Even then, only acts necessary to bridge that gap are potentially available.

> **SH-4 != All Emergency Acts Authorised**

SH-5 is not a weaker SH-4. It is a mandatory reassessment state.

> **SH-4 Action Success -> SH-5 Reassessment, Not Automatic SH-4 Continuation**

SH-6 is intentionally unresolved.

## 6. Act-specific keys

### 6.1 Assessment key

Requires legitimate Health purpose and ordinary consent unless a separate emergency/legal basis permits compulsory assessment.

> **Concern != Compulsory Assessment Authority**

### 6.2 Information key

Requires ordinary contextual access or a separately justified exceptional access basis.

> **Need To Decide != General Record Access**

### 6.3 Supporter-contact key

Participant-authorised contact is ordinary.

Non-consensual contact requires an independently justified disclosure/protective basis.

> **Helpful Supporter != Automatic Disclosure Recipient**

### 6.4 Hazard-interruption key

Immediate interruption may be justified where the hazard is functionally part of the imminent emergency and action is necessary/proportionate.

Continuing deprivation or seizure after the emergency changes requires reassessment and potentially Law authority.

> **Emergency Hazard Removal != Permanent Property Authority**

### 6.5 Transport key

Voluntary transport is preferred where adequate.

Involuntary transport requires its own emergency or legal basis.

> **Transport Authority != Destination Detention Authority**

### 6.6 Location/detention key

Required location restricts liberty and must not be inferred from clinical need alone.

> **Treatment Need != Detention Authority**

### 6.7 Treatment key

Consent is ordinary.

Emergency non-consensual treatment is limited to independently justified necessary stabilising action within the authority gap.

> **Emergency Treatment != Continuing Treatment Authority**

### 6.8 Observation key

Observation must be proportionate to the protective function.

> **Observation Need != General Surveillance Authority**

### 6.9 Restraint key

Physical restraint, chemical restraint and seclusion are separately consequential.

> **Detention Authority != Restraint Authority**

> **Medication Form != Ordinary Medication Authority When Functional Purpose Is Restraint**

### 6.10 Search/seizure key

Search and property removal engage separate protected interests.

Immediate emergency action may be distinct from continuing seizure.

> **Emergency Access To Hazard != General Search Authority**

### 6.11 Disclosure key

Disclosure must identify recipient, purpose, minimum information and authority basis.

> **Protective Concern != General Confidentiality Waiver**

### 6.12 Public-protection key

Where the protected party is another participant or the public, Law/Civil Security authority must be independently established.

> **Health Emergency Authority != Public-Protection Authority**

## 7. Escalation rule

Movement upward through SH envelopes is an evidence/state transition, not an administrative decision to use stronger powers.

> **Escalation Of Concern != Automatic Escalation Of Authority**

A higher envelope requires material facts supporting its predicates.

A stronger intervention within the same envelope requires its own act-specific justification.

Therefore:

**New Evidence / State Change
-> Reclassify Envelope If Supported
-> Identify Proposed Act
-> Verify Act-Specific Authority Keys
-> Compare Less Restrictive Adequate Routes
-> Commit Only Within Current Envelope**

## 8. De-escalation rule

Every exceptional intervention should carry explicit de-escalation predicates.

Examples:
- immediate action has ceased;
- means/opportunity changed;
- capacity clarified/restored;
- participant accepts an adequate voluntary route;
- environmental hazard removed;
- evidence reliability falls;
- less restrictive adequate support becomes available.

> **Predicate Contraction -> Authority Contraction**

Administrative delay is not a legitimate reason to preserve a higher envelope.

> **No Available Discharge Administration != Continuing Clinical Authority**

## 9. Reassessment clock

Exact clinical/legal intervals require specialist development, but event-triggered reassessment is already derivable.

Mandatory reassessment triggers include:
- arrival after involuntary transport;
- immediate harmful action interrupted;
- restraint begins;
- restraint ends;
- participant regains consciousness/communication;
- material capacity change;
- new contradictory evidence;
- participant challenges current authority;
- proposed transition from assessment to treatment;
- proposed transition from emergency bridge to continuing detention;
- material change in threat/immediacy.

> **Material State Change -> Authority Revalidation**

## 10. Challenge route

A participant's challenge to classification or authority should itself trigger review proportionate to the intervention.

> **Challenge To Envelope != Evidence Supporting The Envelope**

For continuing material liberty restriction, review should become increasingly independent of the original decision-maker.

## 11. False-positive protection

Because self-harm prediction is uncertain, the architecture must resist treating false-positive cost as negligible.

Unnecessary coercion can cause:
- physical harm;
- trauma;
- loss of trust;
- avoidance of future Health care;
- employment/social consequences;
- privacy loss;
- dependency;
- institutional capture.

> **Protective Error Can Also Cause Harm**

This does not imply paralysis where immediate grave harm is credibly present. It requires both sides of the risk equation to remain visible.

## 12. False-negative protection

Under-intervention can also produce irreversible harm.

The architecture therefore cannot make certainty a prerequisite for every protective action.

Where time is short, evidence credible, potential harm grave and action reversible/proportionate, bounded precaution may be justified under an established emergency envelope.

> **Uncertainty Must Be Managed, Not Pretended Away**
