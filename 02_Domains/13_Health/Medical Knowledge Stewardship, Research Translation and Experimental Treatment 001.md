# Medical Knowledge Stewardship, Research Translation and Experimental Treatment 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE HEALTH CROSS-DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST AND LEGAL VALIDATION REQUIRED
**Domains:** Health / Historical / Research
**Primary dependencies:** Health Core Architecture / Health Record and Contextual Access / Blainey's Laws / Bounded Contextual Authority / CWA / ESCP / Reality Trees

## 1. Purpose

Concord requires a clear distinction between:

1. what is presently medically known;
2. what is presently being investigated;
3. what is applied to an individual participant's Health;
4. what a participant may voluntarily choose when established options are absent, inadequate or less desirable to them.

The domain ownership model is:

> **Historical Preserves Medical Knowledge**

> **Research Develops Candidate Medical Knowledge**

> **Health Applies Medical Knowledge To Participant Care**

These functions interact but should not collapse into one another.

## 2. Historical as medical knowledge custodian

Historical should preserve the current validated state of medical knowledge and its provenance.

This may include, as appropriate:
- established diagnostic knowledge;
- validated clinical criteria;
- known treatment evidence;
- known contraindications and interactions;
- known adverse effects;
- validated protocols;
- screening evidence;
- medical classifications;
- prior/superseded knowledge where historically important;
- provenance showing how current knowledge was established;
- confidence, uncertainty and dispute state.

Historical custody does not make Historical a clinician.

> **Knowledge Custody != Clinical Authority**

Historical answers, in bounded form:

**What does the current validated medical knowledge state say, and what is its provenance?**

Health answers:

**What does that knowledge mean for this participant in this context?**

## 3. Research as advancement owner

Research owns the process by which candidate medical knowledge is investigated, challenged, tested, replicated, revised or rejected.

Research may include:
- basic research;
- observational studies;
- diagnostic studies;
- treatment studies;
- comparative-effectiveness work;
- device/system studies;
- participant studies;
- experimental treatment evidence;
- substrate-specific Health research.

> **Research Result != Established Medical Knowledge**

A Research result must not silently overwrite Historical's validated knowledge state or become ordinary Health practice merely because it is new.

## 4. Translation lifecycle

A candidate knowledge lifecycle is:

**Existing validated knowledge in Historical**
→ unresolved question / anomaly / proposed improvement
→ Research
→ study/evidence
→ replication/challenge where appropriate
→ evidence synthesis
→ validation/review
→ accepted/rejected/qualified result
→ Historical medical knowledge state updated
→ Health protocols/diagnostic systems reviewed
→ operational clinical use.

KCS change propagation may identify which Health protocols are materially affected when the validated knowledge state changes.

> **Publication != Validation**

> **Novelty != Clinical Superiority**

> **Research Discovery != Automatic Clinical Practice**

## 5. Historical preserves change, not just latest state

Medical knowledge changes.

Historical should preserve:
- current state;
- prior state;
- reason for change;
- evidence/provenance;
- date/version;
- known uncertainty;
- contested areas.

This allows Health to understand why a protocol changed and prevents silent rewriting of medical history.

> **Current Knowledge != Timeless Truth**

## 6. Health application

Health consumes validated knowledge but applies it through participant-specific evidence.

Population evidence does not automatically determine individual diagnosis or treatment.

> **Population Evidence != Individual Diagnosis**

> **Validated Treatment != Mandatory Treatment**

Health combines validated knowledge with:
- participant state;
- diagnostic evidence;
- contraindications;
- preferences;
- consent;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- available competence/resources.

## 7. Research participation and clinical care

Research and ordinary Health care may interact, but the contexts remain distinct.

A participant receiving Health care does not thereby become a research participant.

A participant joining a study does not thereby consent to every clinical intervention.

> **Clinical Care != Research Participation**

> **Research Participation != General Clinical Consent**

Contextual access to Health records for a study requires its own legitimate basis and bounded projection.

## 8. Studies and testing

Research may work with Health to conduct participant studies where:
- the research question is explicit;
- participant eligibility is appropriately determined;
- material known risks and uncertainties are disclosed;
- consent is informed and voluntary;
- required competence/facilities exist;
- data access is bounded;
- outcomes and adverse events are recorded;
- withdrawal routes are clear;
- independent safeguards appropriate to risk exist.

This architecture does not specify detailed clinical-trial regulation; that remains specialist work.

## 9. Experimental treatment as a distinct route

Experimental treatment should not be treated simply as ordinary validated care, nor should it be inaccessible merely because it is experimental.

A participant may rationally prefer an experimental option when:
- no established effective option exists;
- established options have failed;
- established options are unacceptable to the participant;
- the experimental option may offer a benefit the participant values;
- the participant accepts greater uncertainty.

> **Experimental != Forbidden**

> **Experimental != Proven Effective**

The distinction must remain visible.

## 10. Autonomy and risk

A competent participant may voluntarily choose to accept substantial personal risk.

The fact that others would make a different choice does not itself invalidate consent.

> **Risk Acceptance != Incapacity**

> **Unconventional Choice != Invalid Consent**

However:

> **Consent To Risk != Proof Of Benefit**

and:

> **Autonomy Does Not Require False Reassurance**

The system must present uncertainty rather than convert hope into evidence.

## 11. Informed consent requirements

Experimental-treatment consent should be proportionate to consequence and include, where material:

- what is known;
- what is unknown;
- why the treatment is considered experimental;
- evidence supporting the proposed mechanism or benefit;
- evidence against it or important contrary findings;
- known risks;
- plausible but uncertain risks;
- irreversible consequences;
- established alternatives;
- supportive/palliative alternatives where relevant;
- option of no treatment;
- expected burdens;
- monitoring requirements;
- stopping conditions;
- rescue/recovery options;
- conflicts of interest;
- cost/resource implications where relevant;
- effect on future treatment options where known;
- data/research use, separately consented where applicable.

> **Informed Consent Requires Material Uncertainty To Be Visible**

## 12. No artificial last-resort gate

Experimental treatment need not always be restricted to participants who are literally at the final stage of illness or who have exhausted every established treatment.

If an established treatment exists but the participant reasonably prefers an experimental alternative, the decision should be evaluated through informed consent, evidence, risk, competence and externality rather than a blanket rule that experimentation is available only after conventional failure.

> **Existing Standard Treatment != Automatic Prohibition On Experimental Choice**

This does not mean every requested intervention must be supplied.

## 13. Availability is not entitlement to any intervention

Participant autonomy establishes strong authority over what may be done to the participant.

It does not automatically create:
- a competent practitioner;
- a safe facility;
- a scarce resource;
- a manufactured therapy;
- another participant's cooperation;
- permission to expose others to material risk;
- professional obligation to perform an intervention they are not competent or legitimately willing to perform.

> **Right To Choose != Right To Compel Another Participant**

> **Consent != Competence**

> **Consent != Resource Creation**

The civil system should minimise artificial gatekeeping while preserving real constraints.

## 14. Practitioner relationship

A professional may support an experimental treatment by:
- explaining evidence;
- assessing participant-specific risk;
- determining technical feasibility;
- administering treatment;
- monitoring;
- managing adverse effects.

Professional involvement should be based on actual functional need.

Where an intervention can safely be self-administered without specialised capability, professional status should not be introduced solely as a permission ritual.

Where specialised competence is materially required, participant consent cannot substitute for that competence.

## 15. Evidence bands

Experimental options should visibly distinguish their evidence state.

A future specialist architecture may use bands such as:
- theoretical/preclinical;
- early human/participant evidence;
- preliminary safety evidence;
- preliminary efficacy signal;
- replicated evidence;
- comparative evidence;
- validated ordinary care.

Exact categories require specialist validation.

The purpose is legibility, not prohibition.

> **Evidence Classification != Participant Worthiness Classification**

## 16. Reality Tree for experimental choice

A participant considering experimental treatment may use a Reality Tree including:

**What outcome is sought?**
→ what established options exist?
→ why are they inadequate/unwanted?
→ what evidence supports the experimental option?
→ what evidence challenges it?
→ what is unknown?
→ what participant-specific risks exist?
→ what harms are reversible/irreversible?
→ what monitoring can detect failure early?
→ what stopping/rescue routes exist?
→ does the intervention affect others?

The tree informs choice; it does not make the choice for the participant.

## 17. Research-study route versus individual experimental-care route

Two routes should remain distinguishable.

### Research study
Primary structured function includes generating generalisable knowledge.

### Individual experimental care
Primary function is the participant's Health objective, even though evidence generated may also be scientifically valuable.

> **Experimental Care != Automatically A Research Study**

If data from individual experimental care is proposed for Research use, that use requires an appropriate Research basis/consent.

## 18. Learning from experimental treatment

With appropriate consent/governance, experimental-treatment outcomes can feed Research.

Research can aggregate and evaluate:
- efficacy signals;
- adverse effects;
- responder characteristics;
- failure modes;
- dose/procedure effects;
- unexpected outcomes.

Research may then produce stronger evidence that eventually changes Historical's validated knowledge state.

Thus:

**Health experimental use**
→ appropriately governed outcome evidence
→ Research
→ validation/challenge
→ Historical knowledge update
→ Health protocol update.

## 19. Negative results matter

Failure and adverse outcomes are medically valuable evidence and should not disappear merely because a treatment was unsuccessful.

> **Treatment Failure != Research Failure**

Preserving negative evidence reduces repeated exposure to ineffective or harmful approaches and helps prevent publication/survivorship bias.

## 20. Withdrawal

Consent to experimental treatment is not necessarily irrevocable.

Where the intervention remains controllable, the participant should ordinarily be able to stop or withdraw.

Some interventions may create irreversible consequences that cannot physically be withdrawn after administration. That must be explicit before consent.

> **Irreversible Intervention != Irrevocable Continuing Authority**

Withdrawal from a study does not necessarily erase legitimately obtained prior evidence, but future use/collection must follow the applicable consent and governance model.

## 21. Vulnerability and desperation

Severe illness, lack of alternatives or fear of death can make participants vulnerable to exploitation without automatically making them incapable of autonomous choice.

Safeguards should improve the quality of choice rather than automatically remove choice.

Potential safeguards include:
- independent explanation;
- comprehension check;
- cooling-off period where delay is safe;
- second opinion if desired;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- independent advocate;
- clear separation of evidence from promotional claims.

> **Vulnerability May Increase Support Duty Without Automatically Removing Autonomy**

## 22. Commercial and promotional boundary

Experimental status must not become a route for selling unsupported claims as medicine.

Providers should not misrepresent:
- evidence strength;
- success rate;
- certainty;
- regulatory/validation status;
- known risk;
- participant testimonials as proof.

> **Participant Freedom To Take Risk != Provider Freedom To Deceive**

Truth and transparency remain essential to meaningful consent.

## 23. Externalities

A participant may accept risks to themselves more readily than risks imposed on others.

Examples may include:
- infectious-agent exposure;
- environmental release;
- heritable/genetic effects affecting future persons;
- shared infrastructure risk;
- antimicrobial resistance.

Such externalities require separate evaluation.

> **Self-Regarding Consent != Consent On Behalf Of Others**

## 24. Record and provenance

The Health record should clearly distinguish:
- validated ordinary care;
- off-label or non-standard use where applicable;
- experimental care;
- research-study participation;
- evidence state at time of decision;
- consent record;
- monitoring plan;
- outcomes/adverse events.

This prevents later records from falsely presenting an experimental intervention as established care.

## 25. Knowledge update boundary

A successful experimental treatment in one participant does not automatically become validated medicine.

> **Individual Success != General Clinical Validation**

Similarly:

> **Individual Failure != Universal Refutation**

Research owns the aggregation and evaluation needed to determine broader meaning.

Historical changes the preserved medical knowledge state only through the legitimate knowledge-validation pathway.

Health then consumes that updated state.

## 26. Compact domain relationship

**Historical**
→ preserves validated medical knowledge and provenance.

**Health**
→ applies knowledge to participant-specific care and generates clinical outcomes.

**Research**
→ investigates unknowns, studies outcomes and develops candidate improvements.

Then:

**Research evidence**
→ validation
→ **Historical knowledge-state update**
→ **Health protocol review/update**.

No domain gains the other's substantive authority merely because information crosses the interface.
