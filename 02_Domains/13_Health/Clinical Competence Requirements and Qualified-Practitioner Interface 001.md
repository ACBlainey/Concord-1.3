# Clinical Competence Requirements and Qualified-Practitioner Interface 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Domain:** Health
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE HEALTH INTERFACE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Primary interface:** Education / Historical

## 1. Purpose

Health does not own medical education or qualification.

Health owns the clinical function and therefore identifies what competence is required to perform that function safely and legitimately.

Education provides the learning, assessment, qualification and requalification route.

Historical preserves validated medical knowledge and enduring qualification provenance.

> **Health Competence Requirement != Education Ownership**

> **Qualification != Clinical Authority**

## 2. Clinical function specification

A Health function may specify:
- required knowledge/capability;
- practical skill;
- judgement requirement;
- substrate/specialty scope;
- minimum current competence;
- function-critical educational patches;
- supervision requirement where competence is developing;
- escalation/limit recognition;
- required contextual resources.

Requirements should follow the actual function and consequence, not professional title alone.

> **Title != Demonstrated Competence**

> **Broad Qualification != Automatic Competence For Every Clinical Function**

## 3. Recognition interface

Conceptually:

**Clinical function**
→ competence requirement
→ Education/Historical qualification evidence
→ current-patch/revalidation state
→ relevant competence satisfied / unresolved / not satisfied
→ separate participant/context authority
→ action.

Health should normally ask only for the qualification evidence relevant to the function.

> **Need To Verify Competence != Need For Complete Educational History**

Private Local Evaluation may support bounded verification where the underlying educational record should remain protected.

## 4. Competence does not create authority

Even fully demonstrated clinical competence does not create participant-specific authority.

Clinical action may separately require consent, emergency justification, contextual role authority, record access, facilities/resources and protocol applicability.

> **Competence != Consent**

> **Competence != Authority**

> **Ability To Perform Intervention != Authority To Perform Intervention**

## 5. Qualification does not replace current clinical reasoning

A qualification establishes demonstrated capability against a standard. It does not guarantee that every future clinical decision is correct.

> **Qualification != Infallibility**

> **Current Competence != Correctness Of Every Decision**

Health's diagnostic, evidence, protocol, consent, monitoring and outcome architectures remain applicable.

## 6. Granular recognition

Health should be able to recognise bounded competence rather than relying only on all-or-nothing professional categories.

A participant may be competent for function A and not function B.

A specialist may possess competence beyond a general qualification in one area without acquiring general authority elsewhere.

> **Competence In Function A != Competence In Function B**

## 7. Changed knowledge

When Historical's validated medical knowledge changes, Education determines the affected educational dependencies and provides patch/reassessment routes.

Health determines the consequence for continued performance of affected clinical functions.

> **Knowledge Change != Automatic Loss Of All Clinical Eligibility**

> **Outstanding Function-Critical Competence Gap May Suspend That Function Without Erasing Other Competence**

## 8. Supervised practice

Learners may participate in Health functions where Education requires practical experience, but training status does not create authority over participants.

The clinical service remains responsible for ensuring:
- participant consent/legitimate basis;
- appropriate supervision;
- bounded learner role;
- competent escalation;
- patient safety;
- clear responsibility.

> **Training Need != Authority Over Patient**

## 9. Professional refusal and access

A practitioner cannot be compelled to perform a function merely because qualified, absent an independently legitimate obligation.

Likewise, one practitioner's refusal should not automatically create authority to block a participant from seeking another legitimate qualified route.

> **Qualification != Duty To Accept Every Case**

> **Professional Refusal != General Veto Over Alternative Legitimate Care**

## 10. Boundary

This interface does not:
- teach medicine;
- award qualifications;
- preserve the medical knowledge state;
- create participant consent;
- create emergency authority;
- determine disciplinary liability;
- establish employment status.

Those functions remain with their legitimate domains/processes.

## 11. Core interface

**Health defines required competence.**
**Education develops and assesses competence.**
**Historical preserves validated knowledge and enduring qualification provenance.**
**Health verifies relevant current competence.**
**Participant/context authority remains separately required.**

This prevents both professional gatekeeping and unsafe collapse of competence into authority.
