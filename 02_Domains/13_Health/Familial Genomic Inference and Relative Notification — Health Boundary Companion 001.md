# Familial Genomic Inference and Relative Notification — Health Boundary Companion 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / HEALTH BOUNDARY COMPANION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Parent:** Genomic Health Record and Private Local Knowledge Matching 001  
**Interfaces:** Relational Information Rights and Shared-Subject Claims; CIBB; PLE; Historical genomic custody; Research; Civil Contact

## 1. Purpose

Existing genomic architecture establishes that a participant's genome can reveal information about biological relatives and that one participant's consent does not resolve another relative's privacy.

This companion addresses the narrower Health question:

> When may a genomic finding concerning participant P legitimately create a Health relevance event concerning relative R?

## 2. Starting rule

> **Genomic Relation != Relative-Specific Clinical Conclusion**

and:

> **Finding In P != Diagnosis Of R**

A familial implication can create a candidate reason for review without creating a clinical fact about R.

## 3. Separate objects

Keep distinct:
- P's genomic observation;
- validated general genomic knowledge;
- inferred familial relation;
- candidate relevance to R;
- R's own clinical/genomic evidence;
- any eventual R-specific Health conclusion.

Do not collapse them.

## 4. Ordinary route

Preferred topology:

`P Finding`
-> `Validated Familial Relevance Rule`
-> `Does Legitimate Route To R Exist?`
-> if yes: `Minimum Necessary Health Relevance Signal`
-> `R Health Review`
-> `R-Specific Evidence/Testing If Appropriate`
-> `R-Specific Conclusion`

The route should avoid exporting P's genome or unnecessary diagnosis.

## 5. Notification authority

Research discovery alone does not create authority to contact R.

Historical custody does not create authority to contact R.

P's consent does not automatically create authority to contact R.

The participant-facing Health function is the natural candidate route where a legitimate Health notification basis exists.

> **Knowledge Of Possible Benefit != Automatic Contact Authority**

## 6. P-mediated route

Where appropriate, P may be offered the option to communicate relevant familial information to R.

But P should not automatically be forced to disclose P's diagnosis/genomic state merely because R might benefit.

Likewise, P-mediated communication may be inadequate for severe, actionable and time-sensitive risks.

These cases require a separate Health/Law authority basis.

## 7. Direct bounded notification

Where direct notification is independently legitimate, the minimum necessary message may be closer to:

> A validated familial Health finding may be relevant to you; consider contacting Health for assessment.

rather than:

> P has diagnosis/variant X and therefore you probably have it.

Thus:

**Need To Alert R != Need To Reveal P's Full Protected State**

## 8. Actionability and confidence

Notification burden should consider:
- validity of association;
- magnitude of risk;
- actionability;
- severity;
- time sensitivity;
- likelihood R is actually affected;
- harms of notification;
- P's privacy;
- whether R has expressed notification preferences.

Low-confidence or non-actionable findings should not automatically generate participant-specific alerts.

## 9. Right not to know

Existing Health architecture recognises prospective preferences not to receive some genomic findings.

Where applicable, R's notification preference should be considered.

It is not necessarily absolute in every extreme case; any override requires independent legitimate authority.

## 10. Local matching preference

Where R already has a protected genomic record, prefer:

`Validated Familial/Genomic Rule -> Local Query Against R's Protected Record`

rather than disclosing P's genomic details to R's system.

This can convert a relational possibility into an R-specific result without exposing P's source state.

## 11. No reverse disclosure

If R's local record matches, that does not create authority to report R's result back to P or Research.

> **Familial Relevance Can Be Bidirectional Without Disclosure Authority Being Bidirectional.**

## 12. Provenance

R's eventual Health record should distinguish:
- familial trigger;
- R-specific observation/testing;
- inference;
- diagnosis;
- uncertainty.

P's identity should be omitted where unnecessary.

## 13. Central rule

> **Familial Genomic Information May Legitimately Trigger A Bounded Health Review Route Without Converting One Participant's Genome, Consent Or Diagnosis Into Another Participant's Clinical State.**
