# Health Service Topology and Participant-Directed Access 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Domain:** Health  
**Depends on:** Health Core Architecture 001 / Reality Trees / Contextual Wrapper Architecture / Civil Attention / bounded authority  
**Primary interfaces:** Education / Civil Contact / Historical / Research / Law / Emergency Services

## 1. Purpose

Concord Health should minimise unnecessary gatekeeping between a participant and legitimate health support.

The participant should not normally need to belong to a particular clinician, clinic or institutional list in order to begin understanding a health concern or to seek appropriate care.

> **Health Access != Institutional Membership**

> **Primary Care != Ownership Of The Patient**

> **Professional Expertise != Exclusive Right To Initiate Care**

The ordinary route into Health should therefore be participant-directed, universally discoverable and usable without first obtaining permission from a professional gatekeeper.

## 2. Participant ownership of ordinary Health navigation

The participant owns their ordinary relationship with Health in the same broad civil sense that a learner should own their route through Education.

This does not mean that the participant owns medical facts, can compel inappropriate treatment, or can self-authorise controlled interventions.

It means that the participant should ordinarily be able to:

- access health information;
- describe symptoms or concerns;
- use diagnostic-navigation tools;
- review the system's bounded assessment;
- seek a general practitioner;
- seek an appropriate specialist;
- request investigation;
- obtain another professional opinion;
- change provider;
- leave a provider relationship;
- access their own records;
- contest errors in those records;
- continue care elsewhere without avoidable institutional obstruction.

> **Participant-Directed Access != Participant-Determined Diagnosis**

> **Self-Referral != Self-Authorisation Of Treatment**

## 3. No mandatory single-provider gate

A participant may choose a continuing GP/generalist relationship where continuity is useful.

That relationship should not ordinarily prevent the participant from consulting another legitimate provider.

Therefore:

> **Continuity Of Care != Provider Exclusivity**

and:

> **Registration With Provider A != General Prohibition On Consulting Provider B**

A participant may retain a preferred generalist while independently seeking specialist or alternative generalist review.

Clinical services may still require legitimate capability, scheduling, resource or safety conditions. Such constraints must not be disguised institutional ownership.

## 4. Health front door

The primary Concord Health front door should be an easily accessible self-service health navigation system.

It should be available through Civil Contact and other legitimate Health interfaces.

A participant may begin with:

- free-text symptom description;
- structured symptom selection;
- check boxes;
- drop-down categories;
- body/system maps where appropriate;
- measurements the participant possesses;
- uploaded or linked Health evidence where authorised;
- known conditions;
- current treatments/supports;
- duration and progression;
- severity;
- contextual factors;
- participant concerns or objectives.

Accessibility should support different languages, literacy levels, disabilities, communication modes and substrate-appropriate interfaces.

The system should allow a participant to bypass detailed self-assessment and seek human/professional help directly.

> **Self-Service Available != Self-Service Mandatory**

## 5. Smart self-diagnostic navigation

The front door should perform **diagnostic navigation**, not pretend to replace validated clinical diagnosis.

Its primary functions are to:

1. identify immediately dangerous presentations requiring urgent escalation;
2. organise symptoms and observations;
3. generate plausible explanatory branches using validated Health knowledge;
4. identify discriminating questions or observations;
5. identify uncertainty;
6. identify benign/common explanations where sufficiently supported;
7. identify reasons professional assessment may be useful;
8. route the participant toward appropriate next steps.

The system should be designed to reassure where reassurance is evidentially justified rather than treating every symptom as pathology.

It must also avoid false reassurance where material uncertainty remains.

> **Diagnostic Navigation != Final Diagnosis**

> **Possible Pathology != Established Pathology**

> **No Dangerous Branch Identified != Proof Of No Danger**

## 6. Reality Tree diagnostic engine

Reality Trees provide the core reasoning form.

A participant-facing diagnostic tree may operate:

**Symptoms/Concern**
→ **Immediate danger screen**
→ **candidate causal/pathology branches**
→ **supporting and disconfirming questions/evidence**
→ **branch weighting/revision**
→ **benign / monitor / generalist / specialist / urgent / emergency routing options**
→ **new evidence**
→ **tree revision**

The system should preserve multiple live hypotheses rather than forcing premature single-diagnosis convergence.

Where useful, mirrored reasoning can explicitly ask:

- what evidence supports this explanation?
- what evidence argues against it?
- what alternative explanation better fits the evidence?
- what expected observation is missing?
- what question/test would most distinguish the remaining branches?

The diagnostic system should expose uncertainty in understandable form.

## 7. Reassurance is a legitimate Health output

A Health system should not require disease in order to be useful.

A valid bounded output may be:

- current symptoms are consistent with a common benign process;
- no current evidence supports escalation;
- monitor specified changes;
- seek further assessment if defined triggers occur.

Thus:

> **Health Assessment != Requirement To Find Pathology**

Reassurance must remain conditional on the evidence actually available.

## 8. Routing outputs

A diagnostic-navigation episode may produce one or more candidate routes:

- SELF_CARE_INFORMATION;
- MONITOR;
- ROUTINE_GENERALIST;
- RELEVANT_SPECIALIST;
- DIAGNOSTIC_SERVICE;
- PHARMACY_OR_EQUIVALENT;
- REHABILITATION_OR_SUPPORT;
- MENTAL_HEALTH_SERVICE;
- URGENT_ASSESSMENT;
- EMERGENCY_SERVICE;
- ROUTE_UNRESOLVED.

These are routing states, not diagnoses or authority grants.

The system should explain why a route was suggested and what evidence could change it.

## 9. Participant self-referral

Where a specialist service is appropriate and no independent safety or resource reason requires an intermediary, the participant should be able to self-refer.

> **Referral Need != Gatekeeper Need**

A GP/generalist can provide useful synthesis, longitudinal care and navigation, but should not exist merely as an administrative permission layer.

Self-referral may carry the participant's diagnostic-navigation record so that the specialist can see:

- presenting concern;
- symptom history;
- relevant evidence;
- live hypotheses;
- questions already asked;
- reason for route;
- uncertainty.

The specialist remains responsible for their own professional assessment.

> **Machine Routing != Specialist Diagnosis**

## 10. Generalist role

Generalist clinicians remain valuable for:

- broad undifferentiated problems;
- multi-system presentations;
- longitudinal care;
- multimorbidity;
- medication/support interaction;
- uncertainty where specialist route is unclear;
- participant preference;
- synthesis across specialist findings;
- continuing relationship where desired.

Their role is therefore substantive rather than gatekeeping.

> **Generalist Coordination != Mandatory Generalist Permission**

## 11. Specialist access constraints

Participant-directed access does not require every specialist to perform every requested service.

Legitimate constraints may include:

- service competence/scope;
- clinical appropriateness;
- safety;
- scarce capacity;
- required prerequisite evidence;
- intervention-specific legal requirements;
- emergency prioritisation.

Where a requested specialist route is not appropriate, the system should provide an intelligible reason and, where possible, a better route.

> **Declining An Inappropriate Route != Owning The Participant's Health Access**

## 12. Second opinions and provider mobility

A participant should ordinarily be able to obtain another opinion without needing permission from the first clinician.

The new provider receives only the contextual access legitimately needed for the new relationship.

> **Prior Clinical Conclusion != Binding Future Clinical Conclusion**

Prior evidence and reasoning remain available with provenance, but the new clinician may reassess them.

Provider mobility should preserve continuity of evidence without creating institutional captivity.

## 13. Participant-owned longitudinal Health view

The participant should have a coherent longitudinal view of their own Health information across providers.

This should not require one provider to own the master civil record.

The participant-facing view may include:

- encounters;
- diagnoses/working diagnoses;
- investigations;
- results;
- interventions;
- medications/supports;
- allergies/incompatibilities;
- relevant devices/dependencies;
- care plans;
- referrals;
- consent/preferences;
- corrections/disputes;
- provenance.

Special protections may apply to information whose disclosure itself could create serious harm, but such exceptions require explicit architecture and must not become a general professional secrecy rule against the participant.

## 14. Contextual professional access

Health professionals do not receive general access to Health records merely because they are Health professionals.

Access arises from a legitimate care function involving the participant.

Candidate access relation:

**Participant P -> Health Relationship H -> Professional/Service S -> Function F -> Minimum Necessary Record Scope R -> Time/State T**

Examples of F include:

- diagnosis;
- investigation;
- treatment;
- rehabilitation;
- medication/support review;
- emergency care;
- continuing care explicitly accepted by the participant.

Therefore:

> **Professional Status != General Record Access**

> **Care Relationship -> Bounded Contextual Access**

> **Access For Diagnosis != Access For Unrelated Curiosity Or Research**

## 15. Relationship activation

An ordinary Health access context may activate when:

- the participant initiates or accepts an encounter;
- the participant self-refers;
- a legitimate referral is accepted;
- continuing care has been explicitly established;
- an emergency basis independently authorises necessary access;
- another lawful Health authority applies.

The record-access wrapper should know the function for which access was activated.

Simply being employed by the Health system is insufficient.

## 16. Minimum necessary projection

The professional should normally receive a contextual projection rather than unrestricted access to every Health record.

The projection may include:

- information materially relevant to the presenting concern;
- safety-critical information;
- relevant history;
- current treatments;
- allergies/incompatibilities;
- prior relevant investigations;
- participant-selected additional context.

The clinician may request expansion where another record area becomes materially relevant.

Expansion requires a reason connected to the care function and is provenance-visible.

> **Need To Know != Right To Browse**

## 17. Access duration and contraction

Ordinary contextual access persists only while the legitimate care relationship/function remains active.

When the episode closes or the professional leaves the care relationship:

- active access contracts or terminates;
- records created during care remain properly preserved;
- the professional does not retain a continuing right to browse future records;
- later re-entry requires a new or continuing legitimate basis.

> **Past Care != Permanent Future Access**

> **Record Contribution != Record Ownership**

Continuing care may justify continuing bounded access where that continuing relationship is real and accepted.

## 18. Emergency access

Emergency access must be possible where delay would create serious avoidable harm and ordinary participant authorisation cannot practicably be obtained.

Emergency access should be:

- minimum necessary;
- explicitly marked;
- provenance-logged;
- reviewable;
- time/context bounded;
- visible to the participant afterward where lawful and safe.

> **Emergency Record Access != General Emergency Record Ownership**

Emergency access terminates when its justification ends unless another legitimate care relationship replaces it.

## 19. Record provenance and audit

Every consequential professional access should preserve:

- who accessed;
- professional/service role;
- care relationship;
- declared function;
- scope accessed;
- time;
- expansion requests;
- exceptional/emergency basis;
- material edits/additions.

The participant should ordinarily be able to see meaningful access history.

Audit systems may detect unusual access patterns without themselves obtaining unrestricted semantic authority over records.

## 20. Clinical contribution and correction

Professionals contribute observations, assessments, diagnoses, interventions and outcomes with provenance.

A later professional should not silently overwrite an earlier conclusion.

Where conclusions differ:

- both may remain;
- current status is represented;
- reasons/evidence for revision are linked;
- the participant may contest factual inaccuracies.

> **Correction != Historical Erasure**

## 21. Research access is separate

A clinician's contextual access for care does not create research authority.

> **Care Access != Research Access**

Research use requires its own legitimate route, data protection and participant/ethical authority as applicable.

Likewise, employment in Research does not create access to identifiable Health records.

## 22. Education interface

Health self-navigation should have an educational component.

The participant should be able to understand:

- what symptoms mean;
- what warning signs matter;
- why questions are being asked;
- what a test can and cannot establish;
- why a route is suggested;
- what uncertainty remains.

This parallels Education's anti-gatekeeping approach: expertise should be made accessible rather than used primarily as an access barrier.

## 23. Civil Contact and discoverability

Civil Contact should expose the Health front door as a primary official service.

A participant should not need prior institutional knowledge to discover:

- self-diagnostic navigation;
- generalist services;
- specialist services;
- emergency routes;
- records;
- second opinions;
- complaints;
- accessibility support.

## 24. Safety boundaries

Participant-directed Health does not mean uncontrolled automated medicine.

The self-service system must not silently acquire authority to:

- prescribe controlled treatment;
- perform consequential intervention;
- declare incapacity;
- deny emergency care;
- make irreversible decisions;
- allocate scarce life-support resources;
- withdraw established support;
- disclose protected records;
- replace professional competence where professional competence is materially required.

> **Diagnostic Assistance != Consequential Clinical Authority**

## 25. Failure modes to design against

The topology must actively guard against:

### Institutional gatekeeping
A provider becomes the required permission route to unrelated legitimate care.

### Algorithmic gatekeeping
The self-diagnostic system becomes a new mandatory barrier.

### False reassurance
A bounded result is presented as certainty.

### Pathology inflation
Every concern is routed toward disease/treatment.

### Specialist shopping without synthesis
Multiple isolated specialist episodes produce contradictory or interacting interventions without adequate coordination.

### Record overexposure
A care relationship grants unnecessarily broad access.

### Persistent access
Past treatment becomes permanent professional visibility.

### Automation authority creep
A navigation tool starts making decisions beyond its legitimate function.

The answer to these risks should be bounded architecture, not restoration of blanket gatekeeping.

↓
**Participant Self-Service Diagnostic Navigation**
↓
**Reality-Tree Assessment + Urgency Screen**

one or more of:
- reassurance/self-care;
- monitor;
- generalist;
- specialist self-referral;
- investigation;
- rehabilitation/support;
- urgent/emergency care;
- unresolved-route assistance
↓
**Contextual Care Relationship Opens**
↓
**Minimum Necessary Record Projection**
↓
**Professional Assessment / Treatment**
↓
**Outcome + Record Contribution**
↓
**Relationship Continues, Transfers or Closes**
↓
**Access Contracts/Ends**
↓
**Participant Longitudinal Health View Persists**
