# Mental and Cognitive Health — Participant-Directed Care, Capacity and Recovery Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED
**Domain:** Health
**Scope:** ordinary mental and cognitive Health care across participant substrates
**Source resolution:** Mental and Cognitive Health — Legacy and Development Source Resolution 001

## 1. Purpose

This architecture develops the ordinary Health pathway for mental and cognitive health before developing exceptional involuntary authority.

It applies the common Health lifecycle while recognising that mental/cognitive Health can directly affect communication, interpretation, preference, memory, judgement, distress, behaviour and decision capacity.

Those facts increase the need for careful assessment. They do not create general authority over the participant.

> **Mental/Cognitive Health Care != Participant Governance**

> **Clinical Expertise != Cognitive Sovereignty**

## 2. Scope

Mental/Cognitive Health includes Health functions concerning, where supported by validated substrate-specific knowledge:
- subjective distress;
- mood and affect;
- cognition;
- memory;
- attention;
- perception;
- thought processes;
- behaviour where clinically relevant;
- decision-making function;
- trauma-related effects;
- neurocognitive change;
- dependency/addiction where Health is the relevant function;
- developmental cognitive/mental conditions;
- recovery, adaptation and continuing support;
- substrate-specific analogues in non-biological participants.

This architecture does not manufacture diagnostic categories or treatment standards.

> **Architecture != Diagnostic Manual**

## 3. Ordinary pathway

The ordinary pathway is:

**Participant Concern / Self-Referral / Bounded Third-Party Signal
-> Immediate Safety Triage
-> Participant Experience + Goals
-> Functional/Context Assessment
-> Evidence Gathering
-> Physical/Medical/Substance/Environmental/Technical Differential
-> Competing Mental/Cognitive Explanations
-> Decision-Specific Capacity Where Material
-> Support To Preserve Self-Direction
-> Options
-> Consent
-> Voluntary Intervention/Support
-> Outcome Observation
-> Reassessment
-> Recovery/Adaptation/Continuing Support
-> Closure Or Continuing Care
-> Learning**

Exceptional authority is a separate branch.

## 4. Participant-directed access

A participant may directly seek mental/cognitive Health support without requiring a generalist gatekeeper.

Entry may begin from:
- distress;
- changed functioning;
- unusual experiences;
- memory/cognitive concern;
- sleep or behavioural change;
- medication effects;
- substance/dependency concern;
- relationship or environmental stress;
- request for assessment;
- request for therapy/support;
- request for second opinion;
- concern raised by a trusted supporter;
- a bounded safety signal.

Self-service and assisted routes should coexist.

> **Seeking Mental-Health Support != Admission Of Incapacity**

> **Self-Referral != Self-Diagnosis**

## 5. Third-party concerns

A family member, supporter, colleague, service or other participant may sometimes raise a concern.

A third-party report is evidence of a report, not proof of pathology, incapacity or danger.

**Third-Party Concern != Diagnosis**

**Third-Party Concern != Authority Over Participant**

Where feasible, the participant should know what concern is being evaluated and have opportunity to provide their own account.

Malicious, mistaken, coercive or conflict-laden reports must remain possible hypotheses.

## 6. Participant experience is evidence

Mental/cognitive Health frequently concerns states known partly through participant report.

The participant's description of experience is therefore evidence and must not be discarded merely because it is subjective.

But subjective report is not automatically a complete causal explanation.

> **Subjective Experience Is Evidence; It Is Not Automatically Complete Diagnosis**

The system should distinguish:
- what the participant reports experiencing;
- observed behaviour/function;
- measured state;
- contextual events;
- third-party reports;
- clinician interpretation;
- working explanation;
- validated diagnosis where established.

## 7. Distress, difference and disorder

The system should not infer pathology merely from difference, rarity, unconventional belief, unusual communication or social nonconformity.

Assessment asks whether validated Health knowledge supports a clinically meaningful state and whether there is distress, dysfunction, risk, impairment or other relevant Health consequence.

> **Difference != Disorder By Definition**

> **Social Disagreement != Clinical Diagnosis**

> **Unusual Expression != Automatic Incapacity**

This does not mean every unusual belief or behaviour is benign. It means the Health conclusion requires evidence.

## 8. Competing explanations

Mental/cognitive presentations should use Reality-Tree reasoning rather than premature diagnostic closure.

Candidate branches may include:
- ordinary response to circumstance;
- physical illness;
- neurological cause;
- medication effect;
- substance use, intoxication or withdrawal;
- sleep deprivation;
- pain;
- metabolic/endocrine or other biological cause;
- trauma/stress;
- environmental conditions;
- interpersonal coercion/abuse;
- mental-health condition;
- developmental/neurocognitive condition;
- multiple interacting causes;
- measurement/reporting error;
- unknown.

For digital/AI participants, substrate-specific branches may include hardware, software, memory, model/state, input/environment, integrity, resource or continuity failures where validated knowledge supports them.

> **Mental Presentation != Necessarily Mental Cause**

## 9. Mirrored diagnostic reasoning

Where a consequential diagnosis or capacity conclusion is contemplated, mirrored Reality Trees should test both:
- evidence supporting the working conclusion; and
- evidence supporting important alternatives or disconfirming it.

The root question should itself remain reviewable.

For example, instead of only asking:

**What mental disorder explains this behaviour?**

the system may need to ask:

**Is a mental disorder the best-supported explanation for this participant's current state at all?**

> **Diagnostic Search Must Not Assume The Diagnosis It Is Searching For**

## 10. Capacity is not diagnosis

Mental/cognitive diagnosis and decision capacity are separate questions.

**Mental Illness != Incapacity**

**Cognitive Impairment != Universal Incapacity**

**Diagnosis != Authority**

Capacity is represented as:

**CAPACITY(participant, decision/function, context, time)**

A participant may:
- understand one decision but not another;
- need communication support without lacking capacity;
- have fluctuating capacity;
- retain values/preferences while having difficulty with a specific operation;
- recover capacity;
- possess capacity despite making a decision others consider unwise.

> **Unwise Decision != Automatic Incapacity**

> **Refusal != Automatic Incapacity**

> **Disagreement != Incapacity**

## 11. Capacity assessment

Capacity assessment should be performed only where materially relevant, not as a routine penalty for seeking mental-health care.

It should ask the functionally necessary question and preserve uncertainty.

Relevant evidence may include ability to:
- receive/access material information in an appropriate form;
- understand the material decision;
- retain/use relevant information sufficiently for the decision;
- compare consequences/options where required;
- communicate or otherwise express a choice;
- maintain a sufficiently coherent decision process for the specific function.

Exact clinical/legal criteria remain subject to validated specialist knowledge and Law.

> **Capacity Assessment != Personhood Assessment**

## 12. Support before substitution

Where communication or decision support can preserve participant self-direction, it should ordinarily precede substituted authority.

Support may include:
- clearer or slower explanation;
- alternative communication;
- sensory/accessibility support;
- additional time;
- familiar environment;
- trusted participant chosen where possible;
- decision aids;
- reduced distress/stimulation;
- interpreter/translator;
- substrate-specific interface assistance;
- revisiting the decision at a better time where safe.

> **Need For Support != Loss Of Capacity**

> **Supported Decision != Substitute Decision**

## 13. Supporter boundary

A supporter does not become owner of the participant's decisions, record or treatment.

Supporter authority, if any, must come from:
- participant delegation;
- an independently legitimate representative role;
- another bounded lawful basis.

The system should identify conflicts where the supporter benefits materially from a finding of incapacity or continued control.

> **Support != Ownership**

> **Support Relationship != General Decision Authority**

> **Supporter Benefit From Continued Control -> Conflict Review**

## 14. Voluntary care

Ordinary mental/cognitive Health care is participant-directed and consent-based.

Options may include, where validated:
- information and reassurance;
- self-management;
- environmental/context changes;
- psychological/behavioural therapy;
- social/support interfaces;
- medication;
- physical/medical treatment of contributing causes;
- rehabilitation;
- assistive tools;
- monitoring;
- planned follow-up;
- specialist assessment;
- no current intervention.

> **Treatment Availability != Treatment Obligation**

> **Diagnosis != Requirement To Accept Treatment**

## 15. Participant goals

Care should distinguish:
- reduction of distress;
- restoration of function;
- adaptation;
- symptom management;
- safety;
- improved decision support;
- participant-defined life goals;
- externally preferred conformity.

The last category does not become a Health goal merely because an institution prefers it.

> **Health Outcome != Institutional Conformity**

## 16. Therapeutic disagreement

A participant may disagree with a clinician about diagnosis, cause, treatment or desired outcome.

Disagreement should be recorded accurately rather than converted into evidence of incapacity merely because it persists.

Second opinions and provider mobility remain available subject to genuine service constraints.

> **Disagreement With Clinician != Evidence Of Incapacity**

Clinical evidence may still support a conclusion the participant disputes. The rule prevents circular reasoning; it does not make diagnosis dependent on agreement.

## 17. Cognitive privacy

Mental/cognitive Health information may expose unusually intimate information about thoughts, experiences, memory, relationships, trauma, preferences and identity.

Contextual access should therefore remain minimum necessary.

> **Need For Mental-Health Care != General Access To Inner Life**

> **Clinical Curiosity != Record Authority**

Internal mental/cognitive state should not become a general civil surveillance source.

Health information used for another function requires its own legitimate authority.

## 18. Confidentiality

Ordinary therapeutic confidentiality should support trust and care while remaining subject to independently established exceptions.

The exact exceptions require Health-Law source resolution.

A clinician's belief that disclosure would be useful is not itself sufficient to manufacture disclosure authority.

> **Potential Usefulness Of Disclosure != Disclosure Authority**

Where disclosure is legitimately required, scope should be minimum necessary and provenance/audit preserved.

## 19. Longitudinal and fluctuating state

Mental/cognitive Health may vary over time.

The record should preserve:
- baseline where known;
- episodes;
- triggers/context where relevant;
- participant-reported experience;
- working and confirmed conclusions;
- interventions;
- response;
- adverse effects;
- capacity state only for the relevant function/time;
- preferences/directives;
- recovery;
- residual support needs.

> **Past Incapacity != Present Incapacity**

> **Past Diagnosis != Complete Present State**

## 20. Advance preferences and recurrent episodes

A participant with known recurrent impairment may document advance preferences concerning:
- preferred contacts;
- communication/support methods;
- preferred/avoided treatments;
- known effective interventions;
- known adverse responses;
- environmental needs;
- decision supporters;
- early warning signs;
- crisis preferences.

Temporal Consent governs their continuing force.

> **Advance Preference Can Preserve Autonomy Across Impairment**

> **Past Preference != Unlimited Future Authority**

## 21. Medication interface

Psychiatric or cognitive medication remains governed by the general Medication, Prescribing, Dispensing and Pharmacovigilance architecture.

Mental/cognitive Health adds particular need to distinguish:
- therapeutic benefit;
- adverse cognitive/emotional effects;
- withdrawal effects;
- interaction with capacity;
- participant goals;
- adherence/actual use;
- longitudinal outcome.

> **Prescription != Proof Of Continuing Appropriateness**

## 22. Recovery and adaptation

Recovery need not mean return to a prior state in every case.

It may mean:
- restored prior function;
- improved function;
- reduced distress;
- adaptation;
- stable supported function;
- new baseline;
- improved self-management;
- restored decision authority;
- continuing support chosen by the participant.

> **Recovery != Mandatory Return To Prior Normality**

## 23. Learning

Cases may generate bounded clinical learning signals through:
- unexpected response;
- adverse treatment effect;
- diagnostic mismatch;
- recurrent pattern;
- participant feedback;
- professional observation;
- previously unknown interaction.

These route through the existing Health -> Research -> validation -> Historical learning loop.

> **Clinical Experience != Automatic General Medical Knowledge**

## 24. Exceptional branch boundary

This architecture deliberately does not resolve involuntary assessment, involuntary treatment, restraint, seclusion or long-duration substituted authority.

Where immediate danger creates a genuine emergency, the existing Acute and Emergency Health architecture may supply only the authority it independently establishes.

Beyond that point:

> **Clinical Need != Automatic Coercive Authority**

> **Risk != General Control**

> **Diagnosis != Detention Authority**

The exceptional mental-health branch requires explicit Health-Law-Judiciary development.

## 25. Failure modes

The architecture must be tested against:
- diagnosis from social nonconformity;
- circular incapacity findings based on disagreement;
- third-party malicious reporting;
- supporter capture;
- institutional convenience becoming treatment rationale;
- diagnostic overshadowing of physical illness;
- medication effects mistaken for disease progression;
- historical diagnosis permanently biasing new assessment;
- overbroad record access;
- coercive disclosure;
- permanent authority based on past crisis;
- culturally/substrate-specific expression misclassified as pathology;
- automated diagnostic confidence becoming authority;
- self-service system becoming gatekeeper;
- repeated treatment failure producing escalation without root-question review.
