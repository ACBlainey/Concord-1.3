# Acute and Emergency Health Care — Triage, Temporary Authority and Consent Restoration Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 4 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE HEALTH ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Domain:** Health  
**Primary interfaces:** Rights / Law / Civil Security / Continuity / Historical / Research / Infrastructure  
**Primary inherited sources:** Health Core Architecture; Temporal Consent; Constitutional Emergency; Health Record and Contextual Access; Bounded Contextual Authority; Multi-Key Authority; CBPR; Concord Consequential Action and Feedback Lifecycle; Established Life-Support Reallocation Source Resolution

## 1. Purpose

Acute and emergency Health must operate when delay itself may create serious harm and ordinary participant-directed care cannot always be completed before action is required.

The problem is not whether emergency care should exist.

The architectural problem is:

> **What minimum temporary authority exists to protect a participant when ordinary consent cannot presently be obtained in time, and how does that authority terminate as ordinary participant control becomes possible again?**

This architecture develops the shared Health layer. It does not manufacture clinical emergency criteria, resuscitation standards, specialist procedures, drug doses or professional qualifications.

## 2. Source resolution

Existing Concord already establishes:

- Health Support != Participant Ownership;
- Expertise != Sovereignty;
- Ability To Alter A Participant != Authority To Alter A Participant;
- Urgency != Diagnosis;
- Urgency != Unlimited Emergency Authority;
- unknown identity must not automatically block necessary emergency support;
- participant autonomy is the default;
- Refusal != Automatic Incapacity;
- Refusal != Automatic Emergency;
- exceptional authority requires an independently legitimate basis;
- exceptional action should be minimum necessary, context-specific, provenance-visible, reviewable and terminated/revalidated when justification changes;
- Emergency != General Sovereignty;
- emergency record access should be minimum necessary, purpose-bounded, time-bounded, logged, reviewed and terminated when the emergency basis ends;
- Past Emergency Authority != Current Authority;
- authority should contract or terminate when its justification ends;
- urgency can contract procedure without creating new substantive authority values;
- successful outcome does not retroactively create authority.

Therefore the missing architecture is principally an **activation, bounded-action and restoration transition**.

## 3. Emergency state

A Health emergency is not merely a severe diagnosis.

For authority purposes, a candidate emergency state exists where validated Health knowledge supports that:

1. material harm is sufficiently serious;
2. delay is materially relevant to that harm;
3. ordinary consent/authority resolution cannot reasonably be completed within the safe action window;
4. an available action may materially protect the participant or preserve future options;
5. the action requires authority not otherwise presently available.

> **Serious Condition != Automatic Emergency Authority**

> **Urgency + Missing Ordinary Authority != Unlimited Substitute Authority**

Clinical criteria for seriousness and time sensitivity remain specialist Health knowledge.

## 4. Emergency authority is a bridge

Emergency authority should be understood as a temporary bridge across an authority gap, not as replacement sovereignty.

**Ordinary Participant Authority
-> Time-Critical Authority Gap
-> Minimum Temporary Emergency Authority
-> Stabilisation / Communication / Capacity Recovery
-> Participant Authority Restoration
-> Reconciliation / Review / Learning**

> **Emergency Authority = Temporary Bridge, Not Transfer Of Participant Sovereignty**

## 5. Activation test

Before acting without present consent, the responder should resolve as much as time permits:

- Is material harm plausibly imminent or rapidly worsening?
- Would waiting for ordinary consent materially increase that harm?
- Is the participant presently able to make/communicate the relevant decision with reasonable support?
- Is valid consent/refusal/advance direction already available?
- Is there a less intrusive action that preserves the safe decision window?
- Is the proposed action proportionate to the uncertainty and consequence?
- What stopping/review condition applies?

Where seconds matter, this resolution may be compressed. Compression of procedure does not erase the substantive questions.

> **Reduced Decision Time != Reduced Need For Legitimate Basis**

## 6. Supported consent remains preferred

Urgency does not justify bypassing a participant who can still decide.

Where feasible within the safe action window, use:
- concise explanation;
- yes/no or simplified communication;
- translation;
- communication aids;
- accessible interface;
- trusted support;
- prior recorded preferences;
- other proportionate decision support.

> **Emergency Setting != Automatic Incapacity**

> **Communication Difficulty != Consent Absence**

## 7. Present valid consent

Where valid present consent can be obtained in time, it remains the ordinary authority basis.

Emergency conditions may alter the amount and form of information that can reasonably be communicated, but should not turn consent into a fiction.

Material facts should be communicated proportionately to:
- time;
- participant condition;
- decision consequence;
- uncertainty.

## 8. Present refusal

A participant's competent present refusal remains highly significant.

> **Refusal != Emergency Trigger**

> **Clinical Disagreement != Incapacity**

A responder should not manufacture incapacity merely because the participant refuses the recommended intervention.

Where refusal validity/capacity is genuinely uncertain and time is critical, the uncertainty must be represented rather than silently resolved in favour of intervention.

Exact Law/Health rules for exceptional override remain a separate authority question.

## 9. Known advance preferences and directives

Known, applicable and sufficiently reliable advance decisions should constrain emergency action according to the authority they legitimately possess.

Emergency authority should not exist merely to defeat an inconvenient prior refusal.

However:
- applicability may be uncertain;
- identity may be uncertain;
- directive authenticity/currentness may be uncertain;
- circumstances may differ materially from those anticipated.

Therefore:

> **Known Applicable Refusal != Empty Space For Emergency Authority**

and:

> **Unverified Directive != Automatically Binding Directive**

When uncertainty cannot be resolved before action, responders should preserve the uncertainty and prefer reversible option-preserving action where clinically appropriate.

## 10. Unknown preference

Where:
- the participant cannot presently decide/communicate;
- no applicable reliable preference is available in time;
- delay threatens material harm;

bounded emergency authority may support minimum protective action.

The objective is not to choose the responder's preferred life for the participant. It is to preserve health, life, function or future participant choice where reasonably possible.

> **Unknown Preference != Presumed General Consent**

> **Emergency Protection Should Prefer Preservation Of Future Choice Where Clinically Feasible**

## 11. Minimum necessary intervention

Emergency authority extends only as far as required by its legitimate emergency purpose.

Possible actions may include, subject to validated clinical knowledge:
- immediate stabilisation;
- reversible protective support;
- urgent diagnostic action necessary to guide immediate care;
- time-critical treatment;
- transport/transfer;
- temporary monitoring;
- minimum necessary record access.

It does not automatically authorise:
- unrelated treatment;
- elective intervention;
- broad data access;
- research use;
- permanent continuing control;
- unrelated restraint;
- non-urgent optimisation.

> **Authority To Stabilise != Authority To Optimise**

## 12. Reversibility and option preservation

Where clinically reasonable among materially adequate emergency options, preference should be given to actions that:
- are reversible;
- preserve later participant choice;
- avoid unnecessary irreversible change;
- maintain viable alternatives;
- buy safe decision time.

This is not an absolute rule: the only effective emergency intervention may itself be irreversible.

> **Reversibility Is Authority-Relevant, Not An Absolute Clinical Command**

## 13. Uncertainty

Emergency medicine often acts before diagnosis is complete.

The responder may use a bounded working conclusion supported by available evidence and validated emergency knowledge.

> **Emergency Action Need != Final Diagnosis**

Uncertainty should affect:
- intervention scope;
- reversibility preference;
- monitoring;
- reassessment frequency;
- escalation threshold;
- documentation.

Greater uncertainty should not automatically mean inaction where delay itself is dangerous.

> **Uncertainty != Automatic Permission**

> **Uncertainty != Automatic Prohibition**

## 14. Unknown identity/history

Unknown identity must not automatically block necessary emergency support.

Use a bounded temporary identifier where necessary.

Unknown history should trigger proportionate caution concerning:
- allergies;
- medication;
- implanted devices;
- prior conditions;
- advance directives;
- interactions;
- substrate-specific dependencies.

Emergency systems should search only as broadly as justified by the immediate function.

> **Unknown Identity != No Participant Standing**

> **Need To Identify != General Search Authority**

## 15. Emergency record access

Existing Health record architecture applies.

Exceptional access should be:
- minimum necessary;
- purpose-bounded;
- time-bounded;
- authenticated where feasible;
- automatically logged;
- visibly marked as exceptional;
- reviewed afterward;
- terminated when emergency basis ends.

> **Emergency Access != General Record Access**

Break-glass is an exceptional state, not an ordinary professional role.

## 16. Professional competence and scope extension

Emergency conditions do not erase competence requirements.

However, a future validated system may define bounded emergency scope extension where:
- no normally preferred practitioner is available;
- delay creates greater expected harm;
- the responder has relevant minimum competence;
- the action is within an emergency-defined envelope;
- escalation/transfer occurs when possible.

> **Emergency Need != Unlimited Professional Scope**

> **Best Available Competent Help != Pretence Of Specialist Competence**

Education owns the qualification/competence evidence; Health defines emergency functional requirements.

## 17. Multi-key emergency action

Even under urgency, distinct determinations may remain separate:
- emergency clinical indication;
- consent or emergency authority;
- competence;
- record access;
- resource availability/allocation;
- direct intervention authority;
- transfer/transport authority.

Urgency may compress verification but does not make one key become all keys.

> **Emergency Clinical Indication != Every Required Authority Key**

## 18. Stabilisation threshold

Emergency authority should contract when the emergency justification contracts.

Stabilisation does not necessarily mean full recovery. It means the immediate time-critical authority gap has changed sufficiently that ordinary decision processes can resume or more complete authority resolution is feasible.

> **Stabilised != Healthy**

> **Stabilised != Continuing Emergency Authority**

## 19. Consent restoration

As soon as the participant can meaningfully participate, Health should restore participant-directed decision-making proportionately.

This may include:
- explaining what occurred;
- current state;
- interventions already performed;
- continuing interventions;
- material consequences;
- available choices;
- opportunity to refuse/modify future controllable care.

> **Capacity Recovery -> Authority Reassessment**

> **Past Emergency Authority != Continuing Treatment Authority**

If an intervention cannot be physically reversed, that fact does not create authority for additional continuing interventions.

## 20. Withdrawal after restoration

A participant may refuse continuing treatment once ordinary authority is restored, subject to independently valid surviving obligations/authority.

Health should distinguish:
- irreversible consequences already produced;
- treatment that can stop;
- support requiring safe transition;
- monitoring needed because of prior intervention;
- independent legal authority.

> **Irreversible Consequence != Irrevocable Continuing Authority**

## 21. Emergency handoff

Transfer from ambulance/responder to emergency department, specialist team or other service does not automatically transfer every prior authority.

The receiving service receives necessary clinical state and provenance and establishes its own current basis.

> **Emergency Handoff != Automatic Authority Inheritance**

> **Care Transfer != Consent Transfer**

## 22. Emergency transport and location

Moving a participant can itself be consequential.

Where the participant cannot presently decide and remaining in place creates material danger or prevents necessary emergency care, bounded emergency authority may include necessary transport.

Transport authority should not become general detention authority.

> **Authority To Transport For Emergency Care != General Custodial Authority**

## 23. Restraint

Physical, chemical, digital or other restraint is a distinct consequential intervention.

Need for treatment does not automatically create restraint authority.

Where immediate restraint is necessary to prevent imminent material harm within a legitimate emergency function, it should be:
- minimum necessary;
- time-limited;
- monitored;
- reassessed;
- independently documented;
- ended when its basis ends.

> **Treatment Need != Automatic Restraint Authority**

Specialist mental-health and Law development must refine this interface.

## 24. Scarcity

Individual emergency need and scarcity allocation are different questions.

> **Emergency Need != Scarcity Priority**

Where several participants require an insufficient resource, scarcity/allocation architecture must determine the allocation basis.

Emergency clinicians establish relevant clinical facts; they do not acquire general participant-worth or allocation sovereignty from urgency.

Existing life-support resolution remains applicable:

**Clinical Function != Scarcity != Allocation != Withdrawal != Direct Intervention**

## 25. Pre-initiation allocation versus withdrawal

Selecting among claimants before support begins differs from withdrawing effective established support.

> **Pre-Initiation Allocation != Established-Support Reallocation**

Emergency architecture does not silently resolve the currently underdetermined established life-support withdrawal problem.

## 26. Mass-casualty conditions

Multiple simultaneous emergencies can contract procedure and increase scarcity pressure.

They do not change participant intrinsic standing.

Future mass-casualty architecture may require rapid allocation protocols grounded in validated Health facts and Concord aggregation/scarcity principles.

> **Mass Casualty != Suspension Of Participant Standing**

> **Triage Category != Participant Worth**

## 27. Direct threat to others

A participant's medical condition may sometimes create imminent material danger to others.

The authority to treat that participant and the authority to protect third parties are not automatically the same authority.

> **Clinical Treatment Authority != Public Protection Authority**

Public Health, Law or Civil Security may supply separate bounded authority where legitimately established.

## 28. Emergency research boundary

An emergency participant is not automatically a Research subject.

> **Emergency Treatment Need != Research Consent**

Experimental treatment may sometimes be relevant, but must follow the separately developed experimental-treatment and consent/authority architecture.

Urgency must not be used to disguise research as ordinary care.

## 29. Documentation under urgency

Immediate documentation may be abbreviated where recording would materially interfere with care.

The minimum contemporaneous record should preserve, as feasible:
- participant/temp identifier;
- emergency trigger;
- material observations;
- actions;
- time;
- actor;
- authority basis/uncertainty;
- major treatment/medication;
- response.

Documentation should be completed/reconciled when the immediate action window permits.

> **Documentation Delay != Documentation Erasure**

## 30. Post-emergency reconciliation

After emergency authority ends, Health should reconcile:
- what was believed;
- what was done;
- authority used;
- records accessed;
- outcomes;
- adverse/unexpected effects;
- participant preferences once known;
- deviations from expected protocol;
- unresolved consequences;
- follow-up needs.

The participant should ordinarily be informed of material exceptional access/action and why it occurred.

## 31. Audit and accountability

Emergency conditions can explain procedural compression. They do not create immunity from review.

Review should distinguish:
- reasonable action under uncertainty;
- unavoidable adverse outcome;
- competence error;
- negligent delay;
- unjustified intervention;
- authority overreach;
- record-access abuse;
- system/resource failure.

> **Bad Outcome != Automatic Emergency Misconduct**

> **Good Outcome != Retroactive Emergency Authority**

## 32. Learning

Emergency cases feed the established clinical learning loop.

Unexpected patterns, failures, near misses and successful innovations may generate Research signals.

> **Emergency Exception != Epistemic Dead Zone**

Learning use still requires appropriate Research transformation/authority.

## 33. Infrastructure dependency

Emergency care depends on:
- communications;
- transport;
- energy;
- medicine;
- equipment;
- blood/material supply;
- records;
- staffing;
- facilities.

Infrastructure failure may create Health urgency but does not automatically transfer general infrastructure authority to Health.

> **Health Dependency != Infrastructure Sovereignty**

Continuity and infrastructure architectures remain relevant.

## 34. Death and irreversible loss

Emergency architecture must recognise that not every participant can be saved and not every intervention is clinically capable of preventing irreversible loss.

Failure to achieve an impossible outcome does not itself prove failure of duty.

Specialist architecture is still required for:
- death determination;
- resuscitation limits;
- palliative transition;
- end-of-life decisions;
- post-death record/provenance;
- relatives/support persons.

## 35. Biological and digital emergency branches

The authority architecture is substrate-neutral.

The clinical mechanisms are not.

Biological emergency medicine requires validated biological emergency science.

Digital/AI emergency Health may involve:
- imminent corruption;
- destructive process;
- loss of critical function;
- substrate failure;
- continuity-sensitive shutdown/restart;
- dependency loss;
- unauthorised modification.

Neither branch may import specialist rules from the other merely by analogy.

> **Shared Emergency Authority Pattern != Shared Emergency Treatment**

## 36. Compact emergency lifecycle

**Emergency Signal
-> Immediate Safety Triage
-> Time-Criticality Check
-> Present Consent/Preference/Capacity Check
-> Minimum Evidence
-> Emergency Authority Activation If Independently Justified
-> Minimum Necessary Option-Preserving Action
-> Monitoring/Reassessment
-> Stabilisation
-> Consent/Authority Restoration
-> Continuing Care Decision
-> Reconciliation/Audit
-> Learning**

At every stage:

**Authority At Previous Stage != Automatic Authority At Next Stage**

This architecture deliberately leaves specialist content unresolved:

These should inherit this shared authority architecture rather than creating independent emergency sovereignty.

## 39. Conclusion

Concord emergency Health is not an exception to participant standing.

It is a bounded response to a temporary collision between time-critical harm and unavailable ordinary decision authority.

The correct design objective is:

> **Act Only As Far As Necessary To Protect The Participant Through The Emergency Authority Gap, Then Return Authority To The Participant As Soon As The Gap Closes.**

Emergency medicine therefore preserves rather than suspends the central Concord distinction between capability, clinical need and legitimate authority.
