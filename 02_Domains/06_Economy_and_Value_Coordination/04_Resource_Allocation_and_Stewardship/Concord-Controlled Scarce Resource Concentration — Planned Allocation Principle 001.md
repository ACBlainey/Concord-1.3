# Concord-Controlled Scarce Resource Concentration — Planned Allocation Principle 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Economy and Value Coordination / Resource Allocation and Stewardship  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** ARCHITECTURALLY RESOLVED / QUANTITATIVE POLICY DEFERRED / NON-CANONICAL DEVELOPMENT  
**Trigger:** Bootstrap Resource Capacity and Admission Model 002 — unresolved wealthy-participant concentration dependency.

## 1. Problem

A participant may possess enough economic resources to rent or purchase a very large share of scarce Concord-controlled capacity.

Neither of the following is acceptable as an automatic rule:

- wealth creates a right to consume all spare Concord capacity; or
- wealth itself disqualifies a participant from legitimately using substantial resources.

The issue is concentration of a scarce shared capability, not wealth as participant character.

## 2. Planned solution

Where a Concord-controlled resource is scarce or capacity-limited, Concord may apply a **maximum-use or maximum-allocation limit derived from the capacity actually available**.

The purpose is to preserve meaningful access, resilience and future option value for the participant population and to prevent economic wealth from becoming de facto control over shared Concord resources.

**Available Capacity != Unlimited Individual Entitlement**

**Ability To Pay != Unlimited Claim On Concord-Controlled Capacity**

**Wealth != Resource Governance Authority**

**Payment For Resource != Control Of Resource System**

The existence of a maximum does not require equal consumption. Different participants may legitimately use materially different quantities where capacity and policy permit.

**Participant Equality != Equal Resource Consumption**

## 3. Capacity dependence

A fixed universal numerical cap is not established.

Appropriate limits depend on facts that do not yet exist at zero-infrastructure bootstrap, including:

- actual resource quantity;
- deliverable rather than headline capacity;
- existing commitments;
- safety and resilience reserve;
- current and expected participant demand;
- service-sustainment requirements;
- resource class and substitutability;
- failure/degradation conditions;
- operational evidence.

Accordingly:

**Allocation Principle = Established Now**

**Numerical Allocation Limit = Determined From Real Capacity Later**

A limit may change as capacity, demand, resilience requirements and evidence change. A limit appropriate to a small bootstrap pool need not remain appropriate after substantial expansion.

## 4. Concord allocation is not a total-consumption limit

The concentration safeguard applies to **Concord-controlled capacity**. It does not establish a general limit on the resources a participant may possess or consume.

A participant who reaches a Concord allocation limit remains free, subject to ordinary applicable law and legitimate external terms, to obtain additional resources from external providers.

**Concord Allocation Limit != Participant Consumption Limit**

**Concord Resource Scarcity != Prohibition On External Supply**

**Concord Provision != Required Exclusivity**

Concord should not create compulsory single-supplier dependence merely because it provides a trusted service.

## 5. External supplier boundary

A participant's independent relationship with an external provider does not automatically transfer responsibility for that provider to Concord.

Unless Concord has separately and explicitly undertaken an obligation:

**External Supply != Concord Authority**

**External Contract != Concord Obligation**

**External Provider Failure != Automatic Concord Liability**

Concord may provide interoperability, portability, information, standards or other bounded assistance where separately established, but must not imply that it controls or guarantees contracts to which it is not a party.

## 6. Non-coercive capacity-development pressure

If participants prefer Concord-controlled resources because they judge them more trustworthy, safer, more private, more reliable or better governed, demand may exceed available Concord capacity.

The response need not be exclusion of external alternatives. Continued external choice can coexist with pressure to expand Concord capacity.

A possible development loop is:

**Trust -> Demand -> Capacity Pressure -> Voluntary Investment / Contribution / Infrastructure Development -> Increased Concord Capacity -> Greater Availability**

This is a non-coercive growth mechanism because participants may continue using external alternatives rather than being forced to wait for Concord provision.

Expansion may be supported through legitimate payment, investment, donated capacity, participant infrastructure, sponsorship, civil provision or other declared mechanisms.

However:

**Contribution To Capacity != Authority**

**Funding Infrastructure != Ownership Of Governance**

**Building Capacity != Preferential Civil Standing**

Any contractual benefit arising from a contribution must be explicitly constituted. It must not be inferred as civil authority from wealth, funding or infrastructure ownership.

## 7. Anti-monopoly and resilience effect

External alternatives are not inherently failures of Concord provision. Provider diversity can reduce dependency and single-point failure.

Concord should therefore avoid treating external providers as competitors that must be displaced merely because Concord can provide the same function.

Where compatible with safety and participant choice:

**Provider Diversity Can Increase Resilience**

**Trusted Concord Provision != Concord Monopoly**

**Federation / External Supply != Authority Union**

This is consistent with the existing preference to federate before centralising where appropriate.

## 8. Scope boundary

This note resolves the architectural concentration question only.

It does **not** establish:

- a percentage cap;
- a per-participant quantity;
- a pricing formula;
- a universal allocation algorithm;
- a universal priority hierarchy;
- a competition-law regime;
- a definition of monopoly;
- an entitlement to any external provider;
- a Concord guarantee of external services.

Those require real resource conditions and/or later Economy, Law, Markets and operational development.

## 9. Bootstrap dependency disposition

The Bootstrap Resource Capacity and Admission Model may now consume the following external policy result:

> Scarce Concord-controlled capacity may be subject to capacity-dependent maximum-use/allocation limits sufficient to preserve population access, resilience and anti-capture objectives. Numerical limits are deferred until real capacity and utilisation evidence exist. The limit applies to Concord-controlled capacity and does not prohibit external supply.

Therefore:

**Economic Capacity Concentration Policy = ARCHITECTURALLY RESOLVED / QUANTITATIVE POLICY DEFERRED**

The absence of present numerical limits is not an unresolved architecture gap because Concord currently has no material resource base from which responsible limits could be derived.

## 10. Current disposition

No further concentration formula should be invented during zero-infrastructure bootstrap.

Reopen quantitative development when actual or credibly planned resource pools exist and there is sufficient evidence to determine meaningful capacity, reserve, demand and resilience conditions.

The principle itself remains corrigible if later empirical operation exposes a better allocation mechanism.
