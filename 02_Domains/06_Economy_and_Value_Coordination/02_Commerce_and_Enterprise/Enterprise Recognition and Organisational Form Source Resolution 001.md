# 1. Question

Following `Enterprise Identity, Control and Lifecycle Architecture 001.md`, this pass asks:

> **What must Concord recognise in order to treat an organised economic activity as an enterprise, and how much organisational-form architecture can be derived without importing conventional company law?**

The purpose is not to select a preferred company type.

---

# 2. Key source finding

Existing Multisubstrate Economic Participation explicitly separates economic participation from legal personhood.

It allows the possibility that an economic participant may:
- perform work;
- receive payment;
- maintain economic resources;
- enter contracts through an appropriate legal wrapper;
- conduct business;
- operate enterprises;
- employ/manage others;

without that fact automatically establishing legal or constitutional personhood.

Therefore:

> **Economic Enterprise Recognition != Legal Personhood.**

This is a strong architectural constraint.

# 4. Three separate recognition questions

The corpus now supports separating three questions.

## A. Economic recognition

Is there a coherent organised economic actor/activity that Commerce needs to identify and interact with?

## B. Legal recognition

What legal form, rights, obligations, liabilities or personality does Law assign?

## C. Constitutional/civil standing

What protected standing or constitutional status exists independently of economic/legal wrapper status?

These questions may overlap in implementation but should not be collapsed.

> **Enterprise Recognition != Legal Recognition != Constitutional Standing.**

---

# 5. Minimal economic enterprise recognition

Commerce may need to recognise an enterprise when there is a sufficiently coherent continuing economic organisation to require persistent civil/commercial coordination.

Candidate indicators include:
- defined productive/commercial function;
- one or more responsible participants/agents;
- identifiable control/governance relationships;
- persistent economic activity or intention;
- ability to hold relevant permissions/accounts/resources through legitimate mechanisms;
- ability to enter applicable commercial relationships;
- identifiable contact/representation;
- provenance;
- continuity beyond a single isolated transaction where relevant.

These are candidate indicators, not adopted legal tests.

The minimum should not be inflated merely to reproduce conventional incorporation.

---

# 6. Enterprise versus isolated commercial act

Not every sale, job or transaction requires a separate Enterprise ID.

A participant may:
- sell an item;
- perform occasional work;
- enter a bounded contract;
- provide a service;

without necessarily creating a persistent enterprise object.

Therefore Commerce needs a threshold between:

**Participant Performing Economic Act**

and:

**Persistent Enterprise Requiring Separate Commercial Identity**

The threshold remains unresolved.

> **Commercial Activity != Automatically an Enterprise.**

---

# 7. Sole-participant enterprise problem

A single participant may operate a continuing business.

The architecture should avoid two opposite errors.

## Collapse error

Treat the enterprise and participant as the same record/object.

This can destroy:
- accounting separation;
- commercial history;
- permissions;
- employment relationships;
- enterprise succession;
- privacy boundaries.

## Artificial-person error

Assume that creating an Enterprise ID automatically creates a separate legal person.

The provisional solution is:

> **Separate Enterprise Record Does Not Necessarily Imply Separate Legal Personality.**

A sole-participant enterprise may have a distinct commercial record while Law determines whether the participant and enterprise are legally separate.

---

# 8. Organisational form should follow function

The Legal Entity Wrappers work warns against assuming one universal entity.

The same principle should apply internally.

Potential economic organisations may include:
- individual/sole-participant enterprise;
- partnership-like cooperative activity;
- cooperative/member organisation;
- conventional incorporated enterprise;
- public-benefit/community-purpose enterprise;
- project-specific joint venture;
- human–AI composite enterprise;
- AI-operated enterprise;
- distributed enterprise;
- temporary bounded commercial organisation.

These are examples for testing, not adopted Concord legal forms.

The question should be:

> **What organisational capabilities and accountability structure does this economic function require?**

rather than:

> **Which familiar company label should Concord copy?**

# 9. Candidate functional dimensions

Instead of beginning with named legal forms, Commerce can represent organisational dimensions.

Possible dimensions include:

### Participation structure
- single participant;
- multiple participants;
- member-based;
- externally controlled;
- distributed/composite.

### Economic-interest structure
- individual claim;
- shared claims;
- cooperative claims;
- stewardship-based claims;
- other later-defined structures.

### Control structure
- single controller;
- collective governance;
- delegated management;
- distributed functional control.

### Purpose
- private productive/commercial;
- cooperative/member benefit;
- public-benefit;
- project-specific;
- mixed.

### Duration
- temporary;
- project-bound;
- continuing;
- indefinite subject to lawful continuation.

### Liability/legal wrapper
- determined by Law;
- external host-law wrapper;
- unresolved;
- not separately personified.

### Representation
- participant acts directly;
- designated representative;
- bounded agents;
- multi-signature/collective authority;
- automated/AI representation subject to applicable rules.

This allows organisation to be described before Law assigns final legal consequences.

# 10. Cooperative claims

Existing Ownership, Stewardship and Cooperative Claims work explicitly supports cooperation where multiple participants pursue shared objectives and recognises cooperative claims/stewardship as an area for development.

Therefore cooperative enterprise should remain a first-class possibility.

But the existing source does not establish:
- cooperative governance rules;
- voting structure;
- distribution rules;
- membership rights;
- liability;
- legal personality.

These remain open.

> **Cooperative Possibility != Completed Cooperative Legal Form.**

---

# 11. Multisubstrate neutrality

Multisubstrate Economic Participation strongly supports function-first participation.

Enterprise recognition should therefore avoid requiring:
- human ownership;
- biological directors;
- conventional physical premises;
- one substrate class;
- one geographic mode;

except where external law or a specific function legitimately requires it.

This does not mean AI enterprises are automatically legally recognised.

It means Commerce should not build unnecessary human-only assumptions into the economic object model.

---

# 12. Economic capability versus legal wrapper

A useful provisional model is:

**Economic Actor / Enterprise**
→ possesses or demonstrates economic capabilities

**Legal Wrapper**
→ supplies applicable legal interface/capacity

**Commerce**
→ records economic identity, activity, permissions and relationships

**Law**
→ determines legal status, rights, obligations, liability and recognised forms

**Judiciary**
→ resolves legal disputes

**Historical**
→ preserves provenance and lifecycle

**KCS**
→ propagates material changes

The wrapper may change while the underlying economic organisation persists, depending on Law and identity rules.

Conversely, a legal wrapper may persist while the participants/controllers associated with it change.

> **Legal Wrapper Continuity != Enterprise Control Continuity.**

Whether it equals enterprise identity continuity is a later legal/commercial test.

---

# 13. Recognition should expose actual control

Formal labels must not hide practical control.

Existing wrapper/personhood work repeatedly distinguishes:
- formal control;
- effective control;
- legitimate authority;
- responsibility.

Enterprise recognition should therefore preserve:
- beneficial control;
- formal governance;
- operational control;
- representation authority;
- infrastructure/credential control where consequential.

This reduces responsibility laundering.

> **Organisational Form Should Not Obscure Functional Control.**

---

# 14. Recognition lifecycle

A provisional recognition path might be:

**Economic Activity Proposed**
→ **Does It Require Persistent Enterprise Identity?**
→ if NO: participant conducts permitted economic act under participant identity/context
→ if YES:
**Enterprise Recognition Request / Creation Event**
→ **Function and Organisation Described**
→ **Responsible/Control Relationships Identified**
→ **Applicable Legal Form/Wrapper Determined where required**
→ **Required Permissions/Qualifications Checked**
→ **Enterprise ID Issued/Recognised**
→ **Commercial Contact Established**
→ **Enterprise Historical Record Begins**
→ **Activity Commences within permissions**

This is a development topology, not an adopted registration procedure.

---

# 15. Recognition must not grant unlimited permission

Enterprise recognition merely establishes the enterprise object.

It should not automatically authorise:
- every trade;
- every profession;
- hazardous activity;
- infrastructure operation;
- employment of anyone;
- access to protected data;
- public procurement;
- financial authority;
- regulated services.

Those require their own applicable eligibility/permission conditions.

> **Enterprise Recognition != Universal Commercial Permission.**

---

# 16. Recognition must not manufacture constitutional privilege

Recognition as an enterprise does not create:
- greater participant worth;
- citizenship;
- political office;
- constitutional sovereignty;
- automatic franchise;
- superior civil standing.

Existing Ratchet and wrapper sources strongly support this boundary.

> **Economic Organisation != Constitutional Participant by Default.**

Whether an organisation itself has any constitutional rights/standing is a separate unresolved constitutional question.

---

# 17. External host-law entities

During Concord bootstrapping or intercivilisational interaction, an enterprise may use an external legal entity.

The Legal Entity Wrappers programme should own analysis of:
- host-jurisdiction legality;
- incorporation;
- director/officer eligibility;
- beneficial ownership;
- banking;
- external contracting;
- external tax/reporting;
- host-law liability.

Commerce should record the interface and resulting operational state without treating host-law facts as automatic Concord constitutional conclusions.

# 19. Organisational-form development rule

The corpus supports a useful development rule:

# **Describe the function and relationships first; assign the legal form second.**

This reduces the risk that inherited legal labels silently determine:
- authority;
- standing;
- control;
- responsibility;
- ownership;
- personhood.

The future Law domain may still adopt familiar forms where they are useful.

But those forms should be evaluated against Concord requirements rather than treated as conceptual primitives.

# 22. Conclusion

Concord does not need to choose between copying conventional company law and having no enterprise architecture.

The existing corpus supports an intermediate layer:

# **Economic Enterprise Recognition**

It identifies a persistent organised economic actor, its function, actual control, representation and provenance.

Law can then determine the legal wrapper and consequences appropriate to that organisation.

This preserves:

> **Economic Enterprise Recognition != Legal Personhood.**

> **Separate Enterprise Record != Separate Legal Personality.**

> **Commercial Activity != Automatically an Enterprise.**

> **Enterprise Recognition != Universal Commercial Permission.**

> **Describe the Function and Relationships First; Assign the Legal Form Second.**

# 23. Accountability gate for uncertain legal personhood

Enterprise Recognition must not be used to create a commercially capable actor for which no legally answerable party can be identified.

Where an AI or other actor cannot independently bear the legal responsibility associated with the proposed commercial function, recognition/permission should require an adequate accountability backstop before third parties are exposed to the relevant risk.

This does not make the backstop owner of the actor and does not settle the actor's personhood.

See:

`Commercial Accountability Backstop for Uncertain AI Personhood 001.md`

The resulting dual protection is:

**Respect Before Certainty**
+
**Accountability Before Exposure**

> **Enterprise Recognition != Permission to Externalise Unanswerable Risk.**
