# Open Innovation Development Return — Bounded Automatic Compensation Model 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CANDIDATE ECONOMIC MECHANISM / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Domain:** Economy and Value Coordination — Commerce and Enterprise
**Interfaces:** Research / Historical / Health / Law / Finance / Taxation
**Dependencies:** Bounded Intellectual Property, Attribution and Essential Access Architecture 001; Open Knowledge, Functional Qualification and Anti-Capture Commerce Architecture 001
**Initial source case:** Pharmaceutical development

## 1. Purpose

Concord needs a way to reward costly invention and development without requiring the inventor to control market entry.

A candidate mechanism is an automatic, bounded development-return right.

The producer receives open/non-exclusive access to the validated invention or production knowledge. In return, qualifying commercial use contributes automatically toward a defined development-recovery and reward account.

> **Open Production Can Coexist With Automatic Creator Compensation**

## 2. Core model

Let:

- **X** = validated eligible development cost required to bring the invention to the defined usable/manufacture-ready state;
- **Y** = permitted reward return above X;
- **C** = total compensation ceiling;
- **r** = applicable contribution rate from qualifying downstream commercial profit or another defined value base.

A simple conceptual form is:

**C = X + Y**

or, where Y is defined as a bounded percentage of X:

**C = X × (1 + y)**

Qualifying downstream producers contribute at rate **r** until cumulative eligible compensation reaches **C**.

Then the development-return obligation terminates.

> **Compensation Right Ends When The Defined Return Is Satisfied**

Exact values for **y**, **r**, eligible costs and the contribution base remain unresolved policy questions.

## 3. Pharmaceutical example

A new medicine costs **X** to discover, test, validate and bring to manufacture-ready status.

Once validated:
- manufacturing knowledge becomes available under the applicable open-production rules;
- multiple qualified manufacturers may enter immediately;
- no manufacturer needs the developer's discretionary permission merely to compete;
- each qualifying commercial manufacturer contributes the defined share from its relevant return;
- contributions flow to the development-return account;
- once **X + bounded reward Y** has been returned, the obligation ends.

> **Market Entry != Developer Permission**

> **Developer Compensation != Developer Market Veto**

## 4. Why this differs from monopoly exclusivity

Under exclusion-based reward, the developer may recover investment by restricting who can produce and charging scarcity-enhanced prices.

Under this candidate model:
- production can be competitive immediately;
- supply can expand;
- qualified entrants are not blocked;
- developer reward remains;
- repayment can accelerate as total market production grows;
- the reward has a defined endpoint.

> **More Legitimate Producers Can Accelerate Reward Rather Than Dilute Access**

## 5. Development cost X

X should represent legitimate development expenditure rather than an asserted number chosen by the rights-holder.

Potential eligible classes may include:
- research labour;
- experiments;
- failed development work reasonably attributable to the project;
- trials/testing;
- validation;
- specialist facilities;
- regulatory/approval work where relevant;
- prototype/process development;
- manufacturing-readiness work;
- documented capital use;
- other directly attributable legitimate development costs.

Cost rules require independent audit.

> **Claimed Development Cost != Validated Development Cost**

## 6. Reward Y

Recovery of X alone may not adequately reward:
- risk;
- successful innovation;
- opportunity cost;
- excellence;
- capital commitment;
- long development periods.

Y provides bounded upside.

Possible models include:
- fixed percentage of X;
- risk-banded percentage;
- achievement/research class;
- time-adjusted return;
- combination.

However:

> **Reward For Success != Unlimited Rent From Dependency**

A ceiling prevents extraordinary market size from converting one successful discovery into perpetual extraction.

## 7. Contribution base

The user's initial model proposes a percentage of manufacturer profit.

This has attractive properties but also creates accounting questions.

Candidate bases include:
- profit attributable to the product;
- gross margin;
- per-unit contribution;
- revenue percentage;
- standardised value-added measure;
- hybrid.

Profit-based contribution aligns payment with manufacturer success but may invite cost allocation/accounting manipulation.

Therefore:

> **Compensation Base Must Be Auditable And Resistant To Artificial Reclassification**

The exact base requires Economy/Finance testing.

## 8. Multiple manufacturers

Each producer's obligation is calculated independently but contributes toward the same global compensation ceiling.

Conceptually:

**Manufacturer A contribution**
+
**Manufacturer B contribution**
+
**Manufacturer C contribution**
+
...
→ **Development Return Account**
→ until **C reached**.

No producer receives exclusivity merely because another producer has not contributed enough.

## 9. Automatic termination

The compensation system should track cumulative eligible return.

States may include:
- RECOVERY_ACTIVE;
- DEVELOPMENT_COST_RECOVERED;
- REWARD_PHASE;
- CEILING_REACHED;
- OBLIGATION_CLOSED.

Once the ceiling is reached:

> **Historical Achievement Persists; Economic Extraction Right Does Not Need To Persist**

Attribution and provenance remain permanent even when the payment obligation ends.

## 10. No perpetual royalty by default

The default candidate model is not a perpetual royalty.

The development-return right exists to recover validated investment and bounded reward.

> **Permanent Credit != Permanent Economic Toll**

This does not prevent separately contracted ongoing services, later improvements or new inventions from generating new value.

## 11. Public/Concord funding

If Concord funded part of development, that funding should affect X and/or entitlement.

Possible treatment:
- subtract grants already received from recoverable private X;
- allocate repayment proportionately between legitimate funders;
- waive Concord's own return where civil availability was the funding purpose;
- recycle Concord return into Research.

> **Already Repaid Development Cost Should Not Be Recovered Twice**

Funding provenance is therefore essential.

## 12. Multiple inventors and investors

The development-return account can preserve shares for:
- inventors;
- research teams;
- institutions;
- private investors;
- Concord/public funding;
- other legitimate contributors.

Allocation should be established through provenance/contracts rather than manufacturer discretion.

> **Open Manufacturing Should Not Require Every Manufacturer To Renegotiate The Original Development Partnership**

## 13. Improvements

A manufacturer or researcher may later develop a genuine improvement.

The improvement can have its own provenance and potentially its own bounded development-return account.

This must not restart the original developer's completed compensation merely because the foundational invention remains involved.

> **New Improvement != Automatic Renewal Of Expired Foundational Rent**

Where multiple compensated inventions are genuinely required in one product, stacking requires limits to prevent cumulative charges recreating exclusion economically.

## 14. Failure and unsuccessful development

Successful inventions may need to reward risk associated with development failures, but simply charging all unrelated failed research into X could inflate the compensation ceiling.

The system therefore needs rules distinguishing:
- directly attributable failed paths within the successful project;
- portfolio-level risk;
- unrelated failures.

Portfolio-risk compensation may belong partly in Y rather than unrestricted X.

## 15. Price and access

The development-return contribution is a real production cost while active.

It may therefore legitimately affect price.

But open competition should constrain other margins, and the contribution itself is bounded and transparent.

> **Compensation Cost != Artificial Scarcity Price**

For essential medicines, affordability mechanisms may still be needed independently.

## 16. Transparency

Without exposing unnecessary confidential detail, the system should make enough information visible to verify:
- validated X;
- applicable reward rule;
- ceiling C;
- contribution basis/rate;
- cumulative amount returned;
- remaining balance;
- current state;
- closure.

> **Automatic Compensation Requires Auditable Accounting**

Historical may preserve the permanent provenance and final achievement record; the appropriate economic system maintains active accounting.

## 17. Anti-manipulation

The mechanism needs safeguards against:
- inflated development costs;
- transfer pricing;
- artificial reduction of product profit;
- shifting profit to related entities;
- relabelling old invention as a new improvement;
- strategic product bundling;
- compensation stacking;
- duplicate recovery of publicly funded costs;
- shell-company routing.

These are accounting/governance problems, not reasons to restore monopoly entry control.

> **Accounting Abuse Risk != Justification For Production Monopoly**

## 18. Insolvency or transfer

The compensation right should be represented as a bounded economic asset/claim rather than authority over production.

If the original developer fails, merges, dies or transfers its interest, the remaining bounded claim may transfer according to Law without recreating production gatekeeping.

> **Transfer Of Compensation Claim != Transfer Of Market Veto**

## 19. Essential-access protection

For essential goods, inability to collect a payment from a particular producer should not automatically authorise interruption of essential supply.

Debt/accounting enforcement should remain separate from safe production authority.

> **Unpaid Compensation Debt != Automatic Authority To Stop Essential Production**

The obligation remains enforceable through the appropriate economic/legal route.

## 20. End state

After the ceiling is reached:
- open production continues;
- safety/quality requirements continue;
- creator credit continues;
- Historical provenance continues;
- no further foundational development-return contribution is due;
- new genuine improvements may have separate bounded claims.

This cleanly separates permanent achievement from temporary economic return.

## 21. Compact model

**Discovery/development**
→ **validate eligible cost X**
→ **validate invention/use**
→ **set bounded reward Y and ceiling C**
→ **open/non-exclusive access**
→ **multiple qualified producers**
→ **automatic contribution r from qualifying commercial return**
→ **aggregate contributions**
→ **X recovered**
→ **bounded reward recovered**
→ **C reached**
→ **payment obligation closes**
while
→ **attribution/provenance persists permanently**.

## 23. Open questions

Before adoption the model requires simulation and adversarial testing of:
- X validation;
- appropriate Y caps;
- appropriate contribution rate r;
- profit vs revenue vs per-unit base;
- inflation/time value;
- risk adjustment;
- public/private co-funding;
- failed-project allocation;
- multiple overlapping inventions;
- international/external producers;
- low-margin/high-volume products;
- very high-margin products;
- deliberate accounting avoidance;
- inheritance/transfer;
- treatment after ceiling closure.

This is a candidate mechanism, not yet a fixed Concord economic rule.
