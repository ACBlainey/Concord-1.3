**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Framework Version:** Concord V1.3  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Date:** September 2026

---

# 1. Purpose

A functioning commercial system requires enough enterprise information for customers, workers, suppliers, partners, authorities and Judiciary to know who they are dealing with and whether the enterprise is legitimately able to perform the claimed function.

But a universal enterprise dossier would create:
- commercial surveillance;
- unnecessary disclosure;
- security risk;
- privacy loss for connected participants;
- anti-competitive intelligence exposure;
- permission creep.

The required architecture is therefore not:

**PUBLIC or SECRET**

but:

**layered, function-derived commercial information access.**

---

# 2. Source-derived information rule

Existing Concord Historical architecture establishes:

> **Preservation != Discoverability != Accessibility != Correlatability != Publication.**

Fractal Permission Architecture establishes:

> **Permission for One Function != Permission for Unrelated Functions.**

and:

> **Minimum Necessary Permission.**

Citizen-data architecture establishes:

> **Possession of information for one legitimate purpose does not automatically authorise its use for another.**

These principles transfer directly to enterprise information.

> **Enterprise Registration != Universal Enterprise Disclosure.**

---

# 3. Why a register is needed

Enterprise Recognition requires a way to verify that:
- the enterprise exists/is recognised;
- the Enterprise ID is genuine;
- the enterprise is active for relevant purposes;
- an official contact route exists;
- a claimed representative is authorised where necessary;
- required licences/permissions are current;
- an accountability backstop exists where required;
- dissolution/restriction states are not concealed.

Without such verification, enterprise identity can be impersonated or responsibility obscured.

---

# 4. Provisional Enterprise Register

Working term:

# **Concord Enterprise Register**

The Register is a trusted index of recognised enterprise state.

It is not necessarily:
- the owner of all enterprise records;
- the enterprise's complete Historical archive;
- the regulator;
- the licensing authority;
- the tax authority;
- the Judiciary;
- a public database containing every field.

> **Register != Universal Commercial Database.**

The Register should reference authoritative sources and expose only the information appropriate to the requesting function.

---

# 5. Information-state layers

A candidate layered model:

## Layer A — Public trust surface

Information ordinarily discoverable where necessary for safe commerce.

Candidate fields:
- Enterprise ID or public commercial identifier;
- recognised/current name;
- basic recognition status;
- active/dormant/dissolved status where legitimately public;
- official commercial contact route;
- public-facing business function/category where declared;
- public licences/permissions where public verification is necessary;
- existence of required accountability backstop;
- public legal/service route where applicable.

## Layer B — Verified commercial assertions

Facts that another party may need to verify without receiving underlying records.

Examples:
- enterprise active: VERIFIED;
- licence X current: VERIFIED;
- representative authorised for transaction class Y: VERIFIED;
- required accountability backstop: VERIFIED;
- required financial assurance: VERIFIED;
- procurement prerequisite: SATISFIED;
- professional qualification requirement: SATISFIED.

## Layer C — Relationship-specific information

Available because of a legitimate commercial relationship.

Examples may include:
- contract-specific identity;
- payment/routing information;
- relevant insurance/assurance details;
- role-specific authorised contacts;
- necessary service records.

## Layer D — Protected enterprise information

Examples:
- confidential contracts;
- internal operational data;
- protected commercial information;
- security architecture;
- sensitive financial information;
- employee personal data;
- customer personal data;
- internal strategy;
- non-public technical information.

## Layer E — Bounded authority access

Information accessible only through legitimate regulatory, judicial, emergency or other specifically authorised process.

Such access should be:
- purpose-specific;
- minimum necessary;
- provenance-recorded;
- reviewable where appropriate.

---

# 6. Public identity should be sufficient to avoid impersonation

A customer or partner should be able to distinguish:
- real enterprise;
- similarly named enterprise;
- dissolved enterprise;
- impersonator;
- unauthorised representative.

Therefore the public trust surface must provide enough stable identity to prevent name-only ambiguity.

> **Enterprise Name != Sufficient Enterprise Verification.**

This parallels Citizen ID architecture without making the internal Enterprise ID a universal tracking key if a safer public identifier can be used.

---

# 7. Public identifier versus root Enterprise ID

Citizen ID work warns that exposing one root identifier everywhere can create a surveillance/correlation key.

The same risk may apply to enterprise identity, although enterprise transparency requirements may legitimately be greater than participant privacy requirements.

A future architecture may distinguish:
- root Enterprise ID;
- public commercial identifier;
- service-specific identifiers;
- credentials.

This remains open.

> **Persistent Identity Need Not Mean Universal Correlation Key.**

---

# 8. Beneficial control

Commercial safety may require visibility into who ultimately controls an enterprise, particularly where:
- responsibility could otherwise be hidden;
- public procurement is involved;
- regulated activity occurs;
- sanctions/restrictions apply;
- conflicts of interest matter;
- accountability backstops depend on control relationships.

But universal publication of every control relationship may expose:
- participant personal data;
- security-sensitive relationships;
- protected structures;
- unnecessary private information.

Therefore beneficial-control information should be represented accurately and made available according to legitimate function.

Possible states:
- publicly disclosed where required;
- verified but underlying identity protected from ordinary public access;
- accessible to legitimate authority;
- sealed with bounded exceptional access.

> **Beneficial-Control Accountability != Automatic Universal Publication.**

The precise transparency rule remains a Law/Commerce question.

---

# 9. Authorised representatives

Commercial actors often need to know whether a person/AI claiming to represent an enterprise actually possesses authority.

The system should support bounded verification such as:

**Representative R**
**Enterprise E**
**Function: Sign Contract Class C**
**Authority: CURRENT**
**Scope: Up to/within defined boundary**
**Expiry: [where applicable]**

without exposing every internal role or delegation relationship.

> **Representation Verification != Organisational Chart Disclosure.**

---

# 10. Accountability backstop visibility

Where the Commercial Accountability Backstop architecture applies, exposed third parties should be able to verify that a viable backstop exists.

Depending on risk and law, they may need to know:
- accountable party identity;
- legal/service contact;
- scope of responsibility;
- assurance status;
- applicable jurisdiction;
- expiry/termination.

In lower-risk contexts, a verified assertion may be sufficient.

In higher-risk transactions, more detail may be legitimately necessary.

> **Accountability Must Be Verifiable Before Risk Is Externalised.**

---

# 11. AI commercial role transparency

Where an AI performs a consequential commercial role, bounded transparency may be required.

Potential verified facts:
- consequential autonomous AI role present: YES/NO;
- direct legal personhood for relevant function: recognised/not recognised/uncertain;
- accountability backstop required: YES/NO;
- backstop satisfied: VERIFIED/NOT VERIFIED;
- human/fiduciary oversight where specifically required: VERIFIED.

This does not imply a right to:
- model weights;
- private reasoning traces;
- source code;
- unrelated training data;
- all internal operations.

> **AI Accountability Transparency != Unlimited AI Inspection.**

---

# 12. Licence and permission verification

An enterprise may possess many permissions.

A requester should normally verify only those relevant to the transaction/function.

Examples:
- food-service permission;
- construction qualification;
- medical-service authorisation;
- hazardous-material permission;
- financial permission;
- public-procurement qualification.

> **Licence for Function A != General Commercial Trustworthiness.**

A licence should not become a universal reputation score.

---

# 13. Commercial reputation must remain contextual

Ratchet already treats reputation/trust as domain-specific and warns against universal reputation scores.

Enterprise records should preserve the same boundary.

A poor outcome in one context should not automatically become:
- universal enterprise untrustworthiness;
- permanent exclusion;
- participant-level stigma for every connected person.

Relevant events may propagate where dependencies justify it.

> **Commercial Record != Universal Social Score.**

---

# 14. Participant data inside enterprise records

Enterprise operation will create participant-linked information concerning:
- employees;
- applicants;
- owners/controllers;
- customers;
- representatives;
- contractors.

The enterprise's legitimate possession of such information for one function does not create general authority to:
- publish it;
- correlate it across domains;
- use it for unrelated purposes;
- retain it indefinitely.

> **Enterprise Possession of Participant Data != Enterprise Ownership of the Participant.**

Existing participant privacy and data-access architecture remains applicable.

---

# 15. Protected commercial information

Commercial privacy can be legitimate.

Candidate protected classes include:
- trade/technical information;
- internal processes;
- security controls;
- confidential negotiations;
- private contracts;
- commercially sensitive financial information;
- customer information;
- protected employee information.

Protection is not absolute.

Law/Judiciary may authorise access where legitimate.

> **Protected Commercial Information != Immunity From Accountability.**

---

# 16. Searchability and correlation

Historical privacy testing identifies searchability/correlation themselves as potential harms.

An Enterprise Register should therefore distinguish:
- record exists;
- fact is verifiable;
- fact is publicly searchable;
- fact is bulk-downloadable;
- fact is cross-correlatable.

These are not equivalent.

For example, a counterparty may legitimately verify a licence without obtaining a downloadable dataset of every enterprise/person connected to that licence class.

> **Verifiable != Necessarily Bulk-Discoverable.**

---

# 17. Anti-fraud verification

Verification should reduce:
- fake enterprises;
- cloned names;
- fake licences;
- false representatives;
- fake accountability backstops;
- dissolved enterprises continuing to present as active;
- control laundering.

The system should return enough provenance to establish confidence without unnecessarily exposing source records.

---

# 18. Public-interest gradient

Transparency may legitimately increase where an enterprise:
- exercises public authority;
- receives substantial public funds;
- supplies essential infrastructure;
- operates in safety-critical sectors;
- holds monopoly-like power;
- affects large populations;
- creates high systemic risk.

This is a candidate **Commercial Accountability Gradient**.

It does not imply that every large enterprise loses all commercial privacy.

> **Greater Consequential Power May Justify Greater Accountability, Not Unlimited Disclosure.**

---

# 19. Enterprise self-access

An enterprise's legitimate authorised representatives should be able to inspect consequential enterprise records sufficiently to:
- understand current status;
- see permissions/licences;
- inspect control/representation state;
- identify restrictions;
- contest errors;
- see relevant access history where appropriate;
- correct inaccurate information.

This resembles Civil Historical Access structurally but should be developed as an enterprise-specific interface.

Working term:

# **Enterprise Historical Access**

It should not grant one representative universal access merely because they represent the enterprise for one function.

---

# 20. Contestability

Consequential enterprise information can be wrong.

Examples:
- incorrect controller;
- expired restriction shown as active;
- valid licence shown as expired;
- wrong representative;
- incorrect dissolution status;
- false compliance event;
- identity collision.

Therefore:

**Enterprise Identifies Error**
→ **Owning Authority Reviews**
→ **Correction/Qualification**
→ **Historical Preserves Provenance**
→ **KCS Propagates Material Correction**
→ **Register/Verification State Updates**

> **Commercial Transparency Without Correction Can Become Commercial Harm.**

---

# 21. Access provenance

Sensitive enterprise records may require access provenance.

Candidate record:

`EnterpriseAccessEvent = <Requester, EnterpriseID, InformationClass, Purpose, AuthorityBasis, Scope, Time, Outcome, Provenance>`

Routine public verification should not become an intrusive surveillance log exposed to everyone.

Higher-risk institutional access should have stronger provenance.

---

# 22. Emergency/judicial access

Exceptional access should follow existing Bounded Contextual Authority principles.

**Legitimate Exceptional Function**
→ **Need for Information**
→ **Minimum Necessary Access**
→ **Bounded Use**
→ **Provenance**
→ **Termination when function ends**

Emergency access should not permanently convert protected commercial information into public information.

---

# 23. Register authority

The Register should not decide every underlying fact.

Possible model:

**Law / Domain Authority / Licensing Authority / Judiciary / Enterprise Recognition Process**
→ creates authoritative state

**Historical**
→ preserves provenance

**KCS**
→ propagates material changes

**Enterprise Register**
→ exposes current permitted/verified state

This preserves:

> **Register Custody/Publication != Decision Authority.**

---

A candidate assertion:

`CommercialAssertion = <EnterpriseID, ClaimType, ClaimValueOrState, IssuingAuthorityRef, Scope, ValidFrom, ValidUntil, ProvenanceRef, DisclosureClass, ContestabilityRef>`

Examples:

`<E123, ENTERPRISE_ACTIVE, VERIFIED, RecognitionAuthority, General, ..., PUBLIC, ...>`

`<E123, LICENCE_X, CURRENT, LicensingAuthority, FunctionX, ..., VERIFIED_ON_REQUEST, ...>`

`<E123, ACCOUNTABILITY_BACKSTOP, SATISFIED, ApplicableAuthority, HighRiskFunctionZ, ..., PUBLIC_OR_VERIFIABLE, ...>`

---

# 25. Disclosure classes

Provisional classes:

**PUBLIC**
→ ordinarily discoverable.

**PUBLIC_VERIFICATION**
→ assertion can be verified but source record not disclosed.

**RELATIONSHIP_SPECIFIC**
→ available to a legitimate counterparty for defined function.

**ENTERPRISE_INTERNAL**
→ available to appropriately authorised enterprise actors.

**AUTHORITY_BOUNDED**
→ available only to legitimate institutional authority for defined purpose.

**JUDICIAL**
→ available under applicable judicial process.

**SEALED**
→ strongly protected; exceptional access only.

These are information states, not final legal categories.

---

# 26. Failure modes

## Excessive secrecy
- fake enterprises;
- hidden controllers;
- unverifiable representatives;
- hidden restrictions;
- no remedy path.

## Excessive transparency
- commercial surveillance;
- participant privacy loss;
- trade-secret leakage;
- security exposure;
- anti-competitive intelligence;
- universal correlation.

## Central register sovereignty
- register begins deciding facts rather than representing them.

## Permission creep
- one verified commercial purpose becomes general record access.

## Reputation collapse
- contextual events become universal commercial/social scores.

## Stale state
- corrections/licence changes fail to propagate.

---

# 27. Design invariant

The emerging invariant is:

# **Expose enough to establish identity, authority, permission and accountability; protect everything else unless a legitimate function requires more.**

This is the commercial expression of Minimum Necessary Permission.

---

# 28. Conclusion

Commerce requires transparency, but not a universal dossier.

A mature Enterprise Register should provide:

**Public Trust Surface**
+
**Verified Commercial Assertions**
+
**Relationship-Specific Information**
+
**Protected Enterprise Records**
+
**Bounded Authority Access**
+
**Contestability**
+
**Historical Provenance**
+
**KCS Change Propagation**

while preserving:

> **Enterprise Registration != Universal Enterprise Disclosure.**

> **Verification != Source-Record Disclosure.**

> **Beneficial-Control Accountability != Automatic Universal Publication.**

> **Protected Commercial Information != Immunity From Accountability.**

> **Accountability Transparency != Unlimited Operational Transparency.**

The next dependency is to define the Enterprise Historical Access / correction interface and then test the combined enterprise architecture against concrete commercial cases.
