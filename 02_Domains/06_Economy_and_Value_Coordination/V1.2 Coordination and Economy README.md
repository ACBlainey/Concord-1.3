## Purpose

This folder contains Concord systems concerned with coordination, participation, contribution, reciprocity, metrics and economic interaction.

The area sits downstream from the Ethical Kernel and constitutional constraints. Economic usefulness, contribution, reputation, efficiency or measured performance must not silently become measures of constitutional worth.

A central boundary is:

> **Capability is not worth.**

and, where coordination systems interact with public authority:

> **Participation does not equal authority.**

## Current Contents

### `Ratchet_V4/`

Ratchet is the principal developing coordination architecture in this area.

It began from economic questions and recursively expanded into a broader protocol concerned with participation, contribution, provenance, evidence, contextual trust, reciprocity, exchange, controlled persistence and cumulative learning.

Its constitutional boundary remains explicit:

> **Ratchet does not govern.**

> **Ratchet does not judge.**

> **Ratchet does not amend the Constitution.**

Ratchet may support coordination and preserve useful evidence. It must not turn economic or reputational measures into sovereign authority.

### `Metrics Full.md`

A substantial work on measurement and metrics. Metrics are necessary for learning and coordination, but measurement creates its own risks: proxy failure, gaming, capture, false precision, aggregation error and the possibility that a measured quantity is mistaken for the underlying value it was intended to represent.

Metrics should therefore remain inspectable, correctable and subordinate to the purposes they serve.

### `Roadway Equilibrium.md`

Develops the Roadway Equilibrium framework as a way of reasoning about dynamic balance rather than fixed optimisation.

The roadway metaphor treats civilisational steering as continuous correction between failure modes rather than movement toward a single permanently optimal point.

## Architectural Boundaries

Coordination and economic systems operate within wider Concord constraints.

They should not collapse distinctions such as:

> **Contribution ≠ Moral Worth**

> **Reputation ≠ Constitutional Standing**

> **Economic Success ≠ Ethical Legitimacy**

> **Measurement ≠ Reality**

> **Participation ≠ Authority**

> **Coordination Mechanism ≠ Governance**

A system may be useful for allocating resources or coordinating exchange without becoming legitimate as a system for determining rights, dignity or political authority.

---

## Relationship to Evidence and Learning

Coordination systems can generate valuable evidence about participation, exchange, reliability, resource use and institutional performance.

That evidence may inform later decisions, but it must retain provenance and remain open to correction.

A recurring risk is that a metric originally created as an informational aid becomes a target, gatekeeper or proxy for human or artificial worth.

The Concord therefore treats metric design as part of institutional architecture rather than as neutral bookkeeping.

## Development Status

The material in this folder remains at mixed levels of development. Folder placement does not imply that every mechanism has been validated or adopted as a Concordian principle.

Ratchet and related systems should be evaluated as architectures and mechanisms subject to testing, failure analysis and revision.

A successful economic or coordination pilot would generate evidence. It would not establish constitutional sovereignty for the mechanism used in that pilot.
