# 3. Marketplace exchange

Ratchet explicitly supports ordinary marketplace exchange.

It distinguishes:
- ordinary exchange;
- community/public-benefit contribution;
- contribution recognition;
- payment.

It does not require abolition of ordinary markets.

This is important because Commerce need not be invented as a rejection of existing exchange structures.

However, Ratchet's marketplace material is an application layer inside a wider general coordination architecture.

Therefore:

> **Marketplace Recognition != Developed Commerce Domain.**

---

# 4. Contracts

Ratchet explicitly recognises:
- contractual exchange;
- contracts surviving economic exit;
- contractual obligations;
- contract as part of marketplace exchange.

But it does not supply a complete body of contract law.

The Law domain is explicitly responsible for legitimate general rules, rights and obligations.

Therefore the likely relationship is:

**Commerce**
→ creates/uses commercial agreements and transaction processes.

**Law**
→ defines enforceability, legal obligations, invalidity, remedies and legal standards.

**Judiciary**
→ adjudicates disputes.

> **Commercial Contracting Interface != Contract Law.**

---

# 5. Consumer protection

Ratchet explicitly states that marketplace systems may require consumer protection.

This establishes the function as internally recognised.

It does not yet establish:
- consumer status;
- information duties;
- product/service standards;
- unfair terms;
- fraud/misrepresentation rules;
- cooling-off/withdrawal;
- refunds;
- liability;
- enforcement;
- collective redress.

Classification:

**RECOGNISED / OPERATIONALLY UNDEVELOPED**

---

# 6. Collusion, manipulation and competition

Ratchet contains explicit concern with:
- marketplace collusion;
- fake transactions;
- market manipulation;
- network patterns;
- gaming.

This is a useful anti-capture seed.

It does not yet constitute a full competition/market-integrity system.

Missing or unresolved areas include:
- monopoly/dominance;
- anti-competitive exclusion;
- cartel rules;
- platform gatekeeping;
- merger/concentration;
- conflicts between efficiency and resilience;
- cross-substrate market power;
- remedies/enforcement.

Classification:

**PARTIAL SEED / SYSTEM GAP**

---

# 7. Corporate participation

Ratchet explicitly recognises corporate participation and identifies:
- employees;
- contractual capacity;
- large-scale economic influence;
- beneficial-control and responsibility records;
- separation of economic power from constitutional authority.

This is a significant Commerce foundation.

It establishes at minimum:

> **Economic Organisation May Participate Without Acquiring Constitutional Sovereignty.**

However, the corpus does not yet provide a developed lifecycle for:
- enterprise creation;
- legal form;
- registration;
- governance;
- ownership/control;
- merger;
- dissolution;
- insolvency;
- successor liability.

Classification:

**PARTIAL FOUNDATION / ENTERPRISE-LIFECYCLE GAP**

---

# 8. Employment

Employment is now supported by multiple sources.

Existing Multisubstrate Economic Participation recognises:
- performance of work;
- receipt of payment;
- employment;
- employment of others;
- demonstrated competence;
- domain-specific reliability.

Ratchet distinguishes contribution from conventional paid employment.

Current Participation Architecture adds:
- role requirements;
- qualification matching;
- aspirational pathways;
- education integration;
- contextual trust;
- recovery;
- civic/private sector-neutral matching.

Therefore employment is no longer simply absent.

Classification:

**PARTIAL / ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT**

The largest missing areas are now:
- employer/worker legal relationship;
- terms/conditions;
- compensation interfaces;
- working protections;
- dismissal/exit;
- dispute processes;
- employer identity/accountability;
- multisubstrate-specific labour questions.

---

# 9. Ownership and stewardship

Existing research explicitly treats:
- legal ownership;
- resource claims;
- information ownership;
- stewardship;
- cooperative claims;
- provenance.

But it states that the precise constitutional balance between individual ownership and shared stewardship remains open.

Therefore Commerce may use property/stewardship architecture, but should not prematurely settle the constitutional property question.

Classification:

**PARTIAL / UPSTREAM CONSTITUTIONAL AND LAW DEPENDENCY**

# 14. Taxation and public finance

Ratchet explicitly warns that voluntary Give Back should not disguise compulsory taxation or confiscation.

This proves taxation is conceptually distinguished from voluntary reciprocal mechanisms.

No developed V1.3 taxation/public-revenue architecture was found in this pass.

Classification:

**SYSTEM GAP**

Taxation should not be silently invented inside Ratchet.

# 15. Insolvency and commercial failure

No developed architecture was found for:
- business insolvency;
- creditor priority;
- restructuring;
- orderly closure;
- failed obligations;

Classification:

**SYSTEM GAP**

# 16. Commercial identity and records

Concord already has strong reusable civil identity, provenance and Historical architecture.

But:

> **Participant Civil Identity != Enterprise Identity.**

A future enterprise/commercial identity architecture may need:
- persistent enterprise identifier;
- legal/operating status;
- beneficial control;
- authorised representatives;
- licences;
- obligations;
- provenance;
- commercial history;
- dissolution/successor state.

The participant Civil Historical record should not be casually merged with an enterprise record merely because a participant owns or operates the enterprise.

Classification:

**SYSTEM GAP WITH REUSABLE IDENTITY/HISTORICAL FOUNDATIONS**

---

# 17. Commerce and Historical

Commerce will generate records such as:
- employment;
- enterprise participation;
- qualifications relevant to roles;
- licences;
- contracts;
- commercial restrictions;
- ownership/stewardship interests;
- business control;
- commercial disputes;
- insolvency;
- economic exit.

Participant-specific consequential records may interface with Civil Historical Access.

Enterprise records likely require their own coherent historical object.

> **Participant Record != Enterprise Record.**

Linkage may exist without collapse.

---

# 18. Commerce and Civil Contact

Civil Contact can support participant-facing communication concerning:
- employment opportunity;
- qualification changes;
- commercial licence status;
- employment decisions;
- relevant commercial obligations;
- role eligibility;
- enterprise status where participant is authorised to receive notice.

Civil Contact should not become the commercial regulator, employer or marketplace.

---

# 19. Commerce and KCS

KCS is particularly relevant where:
- qualification changes affect employment;
- licence changes affect business permission;
- legal changes affect contracts;
- enterprise dissolution affects obligations;
- security restrictions affect trusted roles;
- correction of identity/control records affects commercial authority.

KCS propagates material dependency change.

It does not make the underlying commercial decision.

---

# 20. Commerce and Law

Commerce has a major Law dependency.

Likely Law interfaces include:
- contracts;
- property;
- employment;
- company/enterprise forms;
- consumer protection;
- competition;
- insolvency;
- commercial liability;
- licensing;
- taxation;
- procurement/public contracting;
- commercial evidence/procedure.

This reinforces the existing V1.3 distinction:

> **Judiciary != Law**

and adds:

> **Commerce != Commercial Law.**

---

# 21. Commerce and Economy

The audit supports keeping Economy and Commerce together at top-level for now because their interfaces are dense.

Economy provides system-level treatment of:
- value;
- exchange;
- scarcity;
- allocation;
- aggregate economic coordination;
- monetary/economic mechanisms.

Commerce provides operational participant/enterprise-level treatment of:
- work;
- enterprise;
- transactions;
- markets;
- opportunity;
- employment;
- commercial participation.

The boundary can be summarised:

> **Economy asks how value and scarce resources coordinate across civilisation.**

> **Commerce asks how participants and enterprises conduct productive and exchange activity within that economy.**

# 23. Recommended next architectural step

Do not immediately design all missing commercial systems.

First establish the Economy and Commerce domain structure and source register.

Then migrate/link existing commercial seeds into that structure without duplicating canonical cross-domain sources.

A useful first subdivision set remains:

1. Economic Architecture and Value Coordination
2. Commerce and Enterprise
3. Employment and Productive Participation
4. Resource Allocation and Stewardship
5. Public Finance and Civil Economic Obligations
6. Markets, Exchange and Commercial Infrastructure
7. Economic Rights, Protections and Contestability

This audit now supplies evidence for those candidate subdivisions.

---

# 24. Conclusion

The addition of Commerce clarifies rather than complicates the economy architecture.

The corpus already contains enough commercial material to show that Commerce is a real persistent Concord function.

It also contains enough gaps to show that Ratchet alone is not a Commerce system.

> **Ratchet Coordinates. Economy Coordinates Value and Scarcity. Commerce Enables Productive and Exchange Relationships. Law Supplies the Legal Framework. Judiciary Resolves Disputes.**

The V1.3 Economy/Commerce structure should now be normalised around those distinctions.
