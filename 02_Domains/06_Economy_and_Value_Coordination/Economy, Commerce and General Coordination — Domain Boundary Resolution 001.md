# 2. Existing source finding: Ratchet is no longer simply an economic system

The surviving README under the V1.3 `04_Economy_and_Resource_Coordination` root describes Ratchet as:

> a general coordination architecture concerned with participation, contribution, evidence, provenance, contextual trust, reciprocity, exchange, and optional economic mechanisms.

The V1.3 Development Source Register under `06_Economy_and_Value_Coordination` is even more explicit:

> Ratchet V4.2 is current canonical architecture for general coordination/contribution/trust/reciprocal participation and is a major reusable foundation, but Economy remains correctly marked partial because general coordination does not by itself supply every economic function.

Therefore:

> **Ratchet Has Economic Origins != Ratchet Is the Economy Domain.**

Current canonical Ratchet already resides appropriately in:

`03_Cross_Domain_Architecture/Ratchet_General_Coordination/`

This is strong evidence that **general coordination** and **economic/commercial operation** should remain distinguishable.

# 3. Three functions now visible

The source material supports at least three related but non-identical functions.

## A. General Coordination

Concerned with:
- participation;
- contribution;
- evidence;
- provenance;
- contextual trust;
- reciprocity;
- coordination;
- cumulative learning;
- optional exchange mechanisms.

Primary current architecture:
**Ratchet General Coordination**

This is cross-domain because the same coordination architecture can support Research, Governance, Commerce, Economy, projects and other cooperative systems.

## B. Economy and Resource / Value Coordination

Concerned with civilisation-scale:
- creation of value;
- exchange;
- allocation;
- scarcity;
- resource coordination;
- monetary/value systems;
- aggregate economic state;
- reciprocal economic participation;
- resource stewardship;
- economic resilience;
- possibly taxation/public finance;
- system-level economic steering.

This is a persistent civilisational domain.

## C. Commerce and Employment

Concerned with participant/enterprise-level economic activity:
- employment;
- private enterprise;
- civic and private job markets;
- commercial exchange;
- businesses/organisations;
- buyers and sellers;
- professional activity;
- contracts as commercial interfaces;
- commercial permissions/licensing;
- opportunity discovery;
- role matching;
- apprenticeships;
- enterprise participation;
- commercial reputation where legitimate;
- movement between education, qualification and productive roles.

Commerce is strongly coupled to Economy but is not identical to macro/resource coordination.

# 5. Does Commerce require its own top-level domain?

The current evidence does not yet require a separate top-level Commerce domain.

Commerce appears to have:
- a persistent objective;
- distinct participant-facing state;
- specialist rules and interfaces;
- substantial operational functions.

However, it is tightly coupled to:
- exchange;
- value;
- resource allocation;
- economic participation;
- contracts;
- property;
- taxation/public finance;
- scarcity;
- enterprise.

Creating separate Economy and Commerce top-level domains too early could produce an artificial boundary between macroeconomic coordination and everyday economic activity.

The stronger current hypothesis is therefore:

# **One top-level Economy and Commerce domain with explicit major subdivisions.**

This remains provisional pending source-resolution and system-gap testing.

# 7. Law interface

Commerce will depend heavily on the Law domain.

Commercial systems may require:
- contract law;
- property/stewardship law;
- company/enterprise forms;
- liability;
- consumer protections;
- employment law;
- insolvency;
- commercial dispute rules;
- licensing law.

This does not make Commerce the owner of Law.

> **Commercial Activity != Commercial Law Ownership.**

Law supplies legitimate legal frameworks.

Commerce operates within them.

Judiciary adjudicates disputes under applicable law.

---

# 8. Education interface

Education develops and verifies capabilities relevant to employment and enterprise.

The emerging employment pathway is:

**Role Aspiration**
→ **Requirement Gap**
→ **Education / Training / Experience**
→ **Qualification / Competence**
→ **Eligibility**
→ **Opportunity Matching**
→ **Selection / Employment**

Education does not become employer.

Commerce does not become education authority.

---

# 9. Participation Architecture interface

Participation Architecture supplies:
- general participation state;
- Civil Historical Access;
- Civil Contact;
- qualification/eligibility interfaces;
- contextual trust/recovery architecture.

Commerce can consume legitimate participant state without owning the participant's civil identity.

Private employers should receive only the minimum information legitimately necessary for the employment function.

Example:

**Required Qualification: VERIFIED**

rather than:

**Full Education Record: DISCLOSED**

> **Employment Verification != Civil Dossier Access.**

---

# 10. Civic and private employment share infrastructure

The employment matching architecture should be sector-neutral.

Common infrastructure:
- Role Requirement Profile;
- participant qualification state;
- education pathways;
- competence evidence;
- matching;
- opportunity notification;
- aspirational roadmap;
- contestability;
- accessibility/support.

Civic roles may additionally require legitimate:
- participation thresholds;
- citizenship;
- public-trust conditions;
- constitutional responsibilities;
- security/suitability.

Private/commercial roles should not inherit those requirements merely because civic roles use the same infrastructure.

> **Shared Employment Infrastructure != Shared Employment Requirements.**

---

# 11. Resource coordination boundary

Economy and Commerce will overlap strongly with resources.

But operating physical resources may belong elsewhere.

Examples:

- Energy pricing/allocation may have an Economy interface.
- Energy generation/grid operation belongs to Infrastructure.
- Land value/property exchange may have a Commerce interface.
- Spatial development permission belongs to Planning/Law.
- Food markets may have a Commerce/Economy interface.
- Food production itself is not automatically owned by Economy.
- Health employment may use Commerce/Employment infrastructure.
- Clinical authority remains Health.

> **Economic Coordination of a Resource != Operational Ownership of the Resource System.**

---

# 12. Ratchet relationship

Ratchet should remain in Cross-Domain Architecture.

Economy and Commerce may use Ratchet functions where appropriate:
- provenance;
- contribution;
- contextual trust;
- reciprocal coordination;
- evidence;
- exchange support.

But Ratchet should not become:
- the employer;
- the market;
- the currency;
- the economic sovereign;
- the commercial regulator;
- the allocation authority merely because it coordinates evidence.

> **General Coordination Supports Commerce; It Does Not Own Commerce.**

# 17. Provisional conclusion

The duplicate economy roots plus the newly exposed Commerce/Employment architecture suggest that the problem is not simply:

> **Which Economy folder is correct?**

The more useful question is:

> **What are the distinct persistent functions currently hidden beneath the word economy?**

Current source resolution produces:

**General Coordination**
→ cross-domain Ratchet architecture.

**Economy**
→ system-level value, exchange, scarcity, allocation and resource/economic coordination.

**Commerce**
→ participant/enterprise-level productive and exchange activity.

**Employment**
→ a major Commerce subsystem spanning civic and private work.

The strongest present structural hypothesis is:

# **Economy and Commerce — one top-level domain with explicit major subdivisions, supported by cross-domain Ratchet rather than containing it.**

This should now be tested against the wider corpus before any destructive folder migration or rename.
