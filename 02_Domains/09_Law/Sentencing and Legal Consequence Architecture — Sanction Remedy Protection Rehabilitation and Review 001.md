## 1. Purpose

This document defines a candidate general architecture for legal consequences after responsibility has been established.

It does **not** yet establish fixed sentence lengths, tariff tables or numerical sentencing scores.

Its immediate purpose is to prevent several distinct civil functions from being collapsed into one undifferentiated concept of punishment.

Candidate decomposition:

**Established Legal State**  
→ **Remedy / Restoration**  
+ **Accountability / Sanction**  
+ **Protection**  
+ **Rehabilitation / Treatment**  
+ **Review / Sunset**

Each output requires its own justification.

---

## 2. Ethical provenance

Primary Ethical Kernel sources:
- **BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm**
- **BL2 — Respect Autonomy**
- **BL5 — Act Fairly**
- **BL6 — Accept Responsibility**
- **BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity**
- **BL9 — Seek Peaceful Coexistence**
- **BL10 — Remain Open to Correction**

Legal consequence must therefore balance:
- accountability;
- restoration;
- prevention of further unjustified harm;
- proportionality;
- participant dignity;
- correction of error;
- termination of authority when justification ends.

> **Legal Consequence Must Remain Traceable To Legitimate Function.**

---

## 3. Inputs

A sentencing/consequence decision may receive structured findings concerning:

- base offence;
- actual result;
- intended result;
- completion state;
- mental state;
- participation state;
- consent/context;
- justification/defence;
- capacity/authorship;
- aggravating factors;
- mitigating factors;
- victim harm/restoration needs;
- present risk;
- treatment/rehabilitation needs.

These dimensions should remain visible rather than compressed into a single opaque seriousness score.

---

## 4. Five consequence functions

### 4.1 Remedy / restoration

Purpose:

To repair, restore or compensate for established harm where legitimately possible.

### 4.2 Accountability / sanction

Purpose:

To impose a proportionate consequence for established culpable unlawful conduct.

### 4.3 Protection

Purpose:

To prevent sufficiently evidenced serious future harm where a present protective authority exists.

### 4.4 Rehabilitation / treatment

Purpose:

To address relevant causal conditions, skills, behaviours, impairments or capability deficits.

### 4.5 Review / sunset

Purpose:

To test whether each continuing intervention remains justified and to correct error or changed state.

> **One Offence != One Undifferentiated Punishment Function.**

---

## 5. Remedy is not punishment

Victim remedy follows established harm rather than the offender's need for punishment.

Potential forms may include:
- repair;
- replacement;
- medical treatment;
- rehabilitation;
- compensation;
- restoration of access/property/function;
- correction of records;
- other proportionate restoration.

> **Remedy != Punishment.**

> **Victim Remedy != Offender Punishment.**

A highly mitigated offender does not make the victim less harmed.

Conversely, severe punishment does not itself repair the victim.

---

## 6. Sanction

Sanction expresses legal accountability for established culpable violation.

Possible forms may later include:
- formal finding/censure;
- restrictions;
- community obligations;
- financial consequence where fair;
- loss/suspension of permissions specifically related to the offence;
- custodial sentence;
- other proportionate measures.

The available sanction must itself be ethically and legally derived.

> **Offence Established != Unlimited Punitive Authority.**

Punishment should not exist merely to satisfy anger or symbolic severity.

> **Moral Outrage != Independent Sentencing Authority.**

---

## 7. Proportionality

A punitive consequence should be proportionate to the legally established state.

Relevant dimensions can include:
- actual harm;
- intended harm;
- completion;
- mental state;
- participation;
- capacity/authorship;
- aggravation;
- mitigation.

No one dimension should silently substitute for all others.

> **Severe Outcome != Automatic Maximum Culpability.**

> **Severe Intent != Fictional Completed Harm.**

Both can legitimately matter while remaining distinct.

---

## 8. Custody

Custody is a major interference with autonomy and therefore requires strong justification.

A custodial sentence should not be assumed merely because an offence is criminal.

Candidate principle:

> **Custody Requires A Custody-Level Justification, Not Merely An Offence Label.**

Where custody is imposed, the judgment should identify its legitimate punitive/accountability basis and duration.

Custody may also provide a location in which separate protective or treatment functions operate, but those functions must not silently extend punitive authority.

---

## 9. Sentence duration and custodial destination

The duration of a custodial sentence and the environment in which it is served are separate questions.

> **Sentence Duration != Custodial Destination.**

Where the general Mental Health legal factor applies, lawful custody may be served in an appropriate mental-health custodial environment rather than ordinary prison custody.

This changes placement, not automatically sentence duration.

The dedicated Mental Health general-factor architecture governs that interface.

---

## 10. Protection

Protection is based on present sufficiently evidenced risk, not moral blame.

Protective measures may overlap temporally with punishment but require their own authority.

> **Punitive Authority != Protective Authority.**

> **Present Danger != Retrospective Culpability.**

A participant may be:
- highly culpable but no longer especially dangerous;
- substantially mitigated in culpability but presently dangerous.

These are different legal questions.

---

## 11. Protection cannot secretly extend sentence

If a punitive sentence expires, punishment ends.

> **Sentence Expiry = End Of Sentence-Derived Punitive Authority.**

If an independent protective authority exists at that point, it must be separately established.

For mental-health-related danger, the existing Mental Health Exceptional Authority architecture applies with its ordinary safeguards and thresholds.

> **Past Conviction != Continuing Detention Authority.**

> **Criminal History May Inform Evidence; It Does Not Lower The Independent Authority Threshold.**

This architecture must not create preventive detention merely by relabelling expired punishment.

---

## 12. Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation concerns improved future capability and reduced likelihood of repeated harmful conduct.

Possible forms may include:
- education;
- skills;
- behavioural programmes;
- restorative processes;
- supported reintegration;
- treatment where separately legitimate;
- capability rebuilding.

> **Rehabilitation != Punishment.**

A participant's failure to become morally approved by authorities is not itself a legitimate basis for continued control.

The relevant focus is lawful function, capability and risk rather than ideological conformity.

> **Rehabilitation != Required Conformity Of Belief.**

---

## 13. Treatment

Treatment addresses health-related needs.

Treatment authority is not created merely by criminal conviction.

> **Conviction != General Treatment Consent.**

> **Custody Authority != Treatment Authority.**

Ordinary consent and the existing Health/Mental Health exceptional-authority architecture remain applicable.

Where a custodial participant requires mental-health custody, placement and treatment should remain distinguishable even if delivered in one institution.

---

## 14. Review

Every continuing consequential intervention should have a review architecture appropriate to its function.

Review may consider:
- factual/legal error;
- changed state;
- completed remedy;
- sentence progression;
- present risk;
- treatment need;
- compliance;
- changed capability;
- new evidence;
- proportionality.

> **Initial Justification != Permanent Justification.**

But review does not imply every fixed consequence can be continually reopened without legal basis.

The review mechanism should correspond to the nature of the consequence.

---

## 15. Sunset

Authority derived from a temporary function should end when that function ends.

Examples:

**Sentence completed**  
→ punitive authority ends.

**Restoration completed**  
→ restoration order ends.

**Protective threshold no longer met**  
→ protective authority ends.

**Exceptional treatment authority no longer justified**  
→ compulsory treatment authority ends.

> **Function Ends → Function-Derived Authority Ends.**

This is consistent with Concord's general authority architecture.

---

## 16. Multiple functions may coexist

One case may legitimately produce several concurrent consequences.

Example:

A participant commits serious deliberate physical harm.

The outcome may include:
- compensation/medical restoration for victim;
- punitive custodial sentence;
- temporary protective restrictions;
- voluntary rehabilitation programme;
- treatment if independently justified;
- scheduled review.

The court should state which authority supports each component.

> **Coexistence Of Consequences != Merger Of Their Authority Bases.**

---

## 17. Consequence substitution must be explicit

If one consequence replaces another, the legal reason should be recorded.

Example:

A custodial sentence is routed from ordinary custody into mental-health custody.

The sentence remains identifiable; treatment authority remains separately identifiable.

Likewise a community sanction should not quietly become indefinite supervision.

> **Change Of Delivery Mechanism != Automatic Change Of Legal Function.**

---

## 18. No wealth-based escape

Financial sanctions and restitution create particular fairness risks.

> **Ability To Pay != Ability To Purchase Freedom From Equivalent Accountability.**

A fixed financial amount may be trivial for one participant and devastating for another.

Future financial-sanction design should therefore examine proportional burden rather than assuming nominal equality is substantive fairness.

However wealth-sensitive sanctions must also avoid becoming arbitrary confiscation.

This remains for later development.

---

## 19. Restitution and sanction

Voluntary restitution may mitigate sentence where legitimately relevant.

But:

> **Restitution != Purchase Of Immunity.**

Mandatory restoration may also be ordered independently of punishment where lawful.

The architecture should avoid making wealthy offenders systematically better able to reduce punitive consequence through payment.

---

## 20. Restrictions linked to function

Some offences may justify restrictions related to the capability or permission used in offending.

Examples might include:
- temporary suspension of a professional permission;
- restricted access to a particular protected system;
- driving restrictions following relevant dangerous conduct.

Such restrictions require their own proportional relationship to the demonstrated risk/function.

> **Misuse Of Capability A != Automatic Loss Of Unrelated Capability B.**

---

## 21. Collective punishment prohibited

Responsibility remains individual unless another participant's own legal responsibility is established.

> **Offence By Participant A != Punitive Authority Over Participant B.**

Family, associates, communities, organisations or identity groups should not be punished merely through relationship to an offender.

Independent complicity or organisational responsibility must be established through its own legal architecture.

---

## 22. Indefinite punishment

Punishment without a knowable limiting architecture creates severe anti-capture and autonomy problems.

Candidate principle:

> **Punitive Custody Requires A Defined Sentence Or Defined Lawful Determination Mechanism.**

Indefinite protective intervention, where ever legitimate, must not be represented as indefinite punishment.

Any open-ended protective state requires ongoing independent review and authority renewal according to the relevant protective architecture.

---

## 23. Release and transition

Release from custody is a state transition, not merely a door opening.

Potential transition needs include:
- identity/document access;
- housing;
- health continuity;
- education/employment continuity;
- lawful restrictions;
- victim-protection conditions where justified;
- Civil Contact;
- support interfaces.

The purpose should be safe civil reintegration rather than manufacturing failure through discontinuity.

> **End Of Custody != End Of Civil Continuity.**

This may later interface with Continuity, Social Support, Health, Education and Employment architectures.

---

## 24. Evidence of rehabilitation

Rehabilitation should not be judged by performative agreement with authorities.

Relevant evidence may include:
- demonstrated capability;
- conduct over time;
- successful treatment where relevant;
- compliance with legitimate boundaries;
- understanding of consequences;
- practical risk reduction;
- repair efforts;
- stable reintegration.

> **Professed Conformity != Proven Rehabilitation.**

---

## 25. Appeals and correction

A consequence architecture must remain corrigible.

Where conviction, classification or sentence is overturned or materially corrected, downstream consequences should be updated accordingly.

Potential needs include:
- release;
- record correction;
- restoration;
- compensation;
- correction of restrictions;
- Historical provenance preserving both the original decision and its correction.

> **Correction Of Judgment Must Propagate To Dependent Legal Consequences.**

This interfaces with KCS/change propagation and Historical custody.

## 29. Development result

The legal consequence architecture should not ask only:

**"What punishment does this offence receive?"**

It should ask:

**What harm requires remedy?**  
**What culpability justifies sanction?**  
**What present danger justifies protection?**  
**What capability or health need supports rehabilitation/treatment?**  
**What review and sunset prevents authority from outliving its legitimate function?**

This decomposition allows the same legal system to remain accountable, protective and corrective without granting punishment authority over functions that require separate justification.
