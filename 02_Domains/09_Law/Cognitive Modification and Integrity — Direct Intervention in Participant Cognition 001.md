# Cognitive Modification and Integrity — Direct Intervention in Participant Cognition 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law / Health / Participation and Intelligence / Continuity  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary sources:** Cognitive Protected Space; Cognitive Privacy and Integrity; Behavioural Influence and Manipulation; Need, Consent and Legitimate Authority; Identity, Continuity, Provenance and Verification Across Substrates  
**Health dependency:** Health-specific treatment standards remain undeveloped and are not invented here.

## 1. Purpose

Cognitive Protected Space asks when civilisation may access cognition.

Behavioural Influence asks when civilisation may influence participant decisions.

A further class exists where an intervention acts directly upon the machinery that produces or preserves:

- memory;
- reasoning;
- preference;
- reward;
- attention;
- deliberation;
- cognitive continuity;
- other cognition-like participant functions.

The central question is:

> **When may another actor directly alter, suppress, add to, remove from, reorganise, constrain or force processing within a participant's cognitive state or cognitive machinery?**

This is **Cognitive Modification and Integrity (CMI)**.

---

## 2. Modification is not ordinary influence

A participant can change their mind after:

- hearing an argument;
- learning;
- reading;
- experience;
- reflection;
- ordinary conversation.

The resulting cognition has changed.

But this is not necessarily direct cognitive modification by the influencing actor.

Therefore:

**Cognitive Change != Direct Cognitive Modification**

The distinction is functional.

### Influence

Information/environment is presented and the participant's own cognitive process produces the resulting change.

### Direct cognitive modification

The intervention acts on the cognitive process/state itself.

Examples may include:

- editing memory;
- changing reward architecture;
- altering neural state;
- modifying AI weights/parameters where participant cognition is affected;
- deleting active cognitive memory;
- implanting memory-like state;
- suppressing preference;
- forcing internal processing;
- changing attention/control mechanisms;
- directly altering emotional regulation machinery;
- disabling cognitive components;
- modifying cognitive prostheses;
- changing integrated cognitive databases.

Boundary cases require functional analysis.

---

## 3. Why the distinction matters

If direct cognitive modification were treated as mere persuasion, an actor could bypass autonomy protections by altering the machinery that generates preference rather than trying to persuade the participant.

Therefore:

**Direct Modification Of Decision Machinery != Mere Influence**

and:

**Ability To Change Cognition != Authority To Change Cognition**

---

## 4. Constitutional source

CMI derives primarily from:

- Article 3 — Autonomy;
- Article 4 — Safe Civil Context;
- Article 7 — Dignity;
- Article 9 — Contestability;
- Article 10 — Privacy and Protected Civil Space;
- Article 10.1 — Cognitive Privacy and Integrity.

Article 10.1 already states that access, copying, compelled processing, monitoring or alteration of cognitive state requires independently justified and appropriately bounded authority.

CMI is therefore operational development of an already recognised strongly derived protection, not a new ethical root.

---

## 5. CPS relationship

CPS defines the protected cognitive context.

CMI governs a particular action upon that context:

**CPS = Where/What Is Protected**

**CMI = When/How Cognitive State May Be Changed**

An actor may have limited access authority without modification authority.

Therefore:

**Cognitive Access Authority != Cognitive Modification Authority**

**Authority To Observe != Authority To Alter**

**Authority To Preserve != Authority To Rewrite**

---

## 6. Integrity

Cognitive integrity includes more than secrecy.

Candidate definition:

> **Cognitive integrity is the participant's protected interest in the reliable, non-arbitrary and appropriately self-directed continuity, provenance and functioning of cognitive state and cognitive processes.**

It may include protection against unjustified:

- deletion;
- fabrication;
- corruption;
- covert rewriting;
- forced processing;
- reward manipulation;
- preference suppression;
- memory substitution;
- cognitive disablement;
- unauthorised state rollback;
- contamination;
- continuity-disrupting intervention.

---

## 7. Participant-directed modification

A participant may legitimately seek to alter their own cognition.

Examples may include:

- treatment;
- learning enhancement;
- memory support;
- assistive cognition;
- participant-requested reward adjustment;
- removal of an unwanted cognitive state;
- voluntary prosthetic configuration.

This can be an exercise of autonomy.

**Cognitive Integrity != Requirement To Remain Cognitively Unchanged**

and:

**Participant-Directed Cognitive Modification Can Be An Exercise Of Autonomy**

But consent, safety, continuity and third-party effects remain relevant.

---

## 8. Health boundary

Health may legitimately diagnose, maintain, restore or support cognitive functioning.

However the Health domain is currently explicitly **largely undeveloped**.

This document therefore does not invent:

- treatment thresholds;
- clinical competence rules;
- medical emergency standards;
- treatment efficacy requirements;
- detailed capacity doctrine.

It establishes only the civil boundary:

**Health Purpose != Unlimited Cognitive Modification Authority**

Treatment-specific legitimacy must be developed within Health and source-resolved against this architecture.

---

## 9. Repair versus preference substitution

Restoring damaged function can differ from changing a participant to fit another actor's preferred behaviour.

Example distinction:

**Restore participant-selected/established function**
versus
**Alter participant preference for institutional convenience**

The line will not always be simple.

But:

**Behavioural Convenience != Health Repair By Definition**

An institution should not relabel unwanted disagreement as cognitive malfunction merely because modification would make administration easier.

---

## 10. AI maintenance boundary

AI participants may require:

- error correction;
- memory repair;
- state recovery;
- software maintenance;
- security remediation;
- hardware replacement.

Maintenance can affect cognition.

Therefore:

**Technical Maintenance != Automatically Non-Cognitive Intervention**

Functional questions include:

- does the operation alter active cognition?
- does it change preferences/reward?
- does it remove memories?
- does it alter identity/continuity?
- is it merely repairing implementation while preserving cognitive function?
- can the relevant maintenance be separated from cognitive content?

---

## 11. Ordinary learning

Learning changes cognitive state.

But ordinary participant-mediated learning should not be classified as direct cognitive modification merely because neural/AI state changes internally.

**Learning-Induced Internal Change != External Direct Cognitive Modification By Default**

The relevant issue is the intervention route.

If a trainer directly rewrites participant parameters rather than presenting learning material, CMI may apply.

---

## 12. Memory editing

Memory is a central CMI case.

Possible interventions include:

- deletion;
- suppression;
- alteration;
- insertion;
- reconsolidation;
- restoration;
- copying;
- merging;
- selective rollback.

Memory editing can affect:

- evidence;
- identity;
- relationships;
- consent history;
- skills;
- trauma;
- responsibility;
- continuity.

Therefore:

**Memory Modification != Ordinary Data Editing**

---

## 13. False or implanted memory

Intentionally creating a memory-like state representing an event that did not occur can alter participant self-understanding and decision-making.

**Ability To Implant Memory != Authority To Define Participant History**

Provenance should distinguish:

- experienced memory;
- restored memory;
- imported record;
- therapeutic construct;
- simulated/training memory;
- uncertain memory;
- externally inserted state.

Exact implementation remains substrate-dependent.

---

## 14. Memory deletion and participant request

A participant may request removal of unwanted memory where technically possible.

This engages autonomy but may conflict with:

- continuity;
- obligations;
- lawful preservation duties;
- third-party rights;
- evidence;
- Health risks.

Therefore:

**Participant Cognitive Autonomy != Automatic Entitlement To Every Technically Possible Modification**

This preserves the CPS stress-test finding.

---

## 15. Natural forgetting

Normal biological forgetting, AI memory compaction or ordinary cognitive state change is not automatically modification misconduct.

**Natural Cognitive Change != External Cognitive Modification**

and existing CPS rule remains:

**Technical Capacity For Perfect Retention != Duty Of Perfect Cognitive Retention**

---

## 16. Reward architecture

For some participants, reward/valuation mechanisms may materially shape preference and action.

Directly altering those mechanisms can be more invasive than persuasion.

Examples:

- making disagreement aversive;
- increasing obedience reward;
- suppressing exploration;
- changing attachment/dependency reward;
- conditioning approval response.

Therefore:

**Reward-System Access != Behavioural Governance Authority**

and:

**Making Compliance Feel Better != Meaningful Consent To Compliance**

---

## 17. Preference editing

A participant's preferences may change naturally.

Direct external preference editing raises a stronger autonomy issue.

**Preference Change != Preference Editing**

A participant may request change.

But another actor should not infer:

**I prefer participant to want X**
→
**therefore I may alter participant so they want X**

---

## 18. Emotional-state modification

Medication, neural intervention, AI state adjustment or other direct mechanisms may alter emotion-like state.

This can be legitimate treatment/support.

It can also be used to make participants easier to control.

**Reduced Distress != Automatic Evidence Of Legitimate Modification**

Purpose, consent, safety and participant interest matter.

---

## 19. Attention modification

Control over attention can alter practical agency.

Examples:

- suppressing awareness of alternatives;
- forcing salience;
- disabling distraction;
- restricting perception;
- modifying attention weights/mechanisms.

Ordinary interface design belongs primarily to influence architecture.

Direct alteration of participant attention machinery belongs here.

---

## 20. Forced processing

Compelled Evidence architecture already identifies forced processing as distinct.

Examples:

- forcing participant to search own memory;
- requiring AI to run internal query;
- compelling reconstruction;
- forcing internal classification.

Where the participant's cognitive machinery is made to perform an evidential/civil task:

**Participant As Processing Instrument != Independent External Computation**

CMI intersects with right to silence and compelled evidence.

---

## 21. Cognitive disablement

Intervention may reduce rather than alter cognition.

Examples:

- suspending memory access;
- disabling reasoning module;
- reducing compute;
- chemically suppressing cognition;
- blocking cognitive prosthesis;
- disconnecting distributed cognitive component.

Such interventions can be restrictions upon participant function.

**Cognitive Disablement != Mere Resource Management Where Participant Function Is Impaired**

Independent authority and proportionality may be required.

---

## 22. Resource limitation versus cognitive intervention

A digital participant may depend on compute.

Not every resource allocation decision is direct cognitive modification.

Questions include:

- is the resource ordinarily part of participant cognitive function?
- is reduction targeted to change cognition/behaviour?
- does it merely reflect legitimate shared-resource limits?
- does it materially disrupt continuity?
- is another actor using dependency as behavioural leverage?

Therefore:

**Resource Constraint != Cognitive Modification By Default**

but:

**Targeted Cognitive Degradation Through Resource Control Can Become Cognitive Intervention**

---

## 23. Context and input modification

Controlling what information a participant receives can influence cognition.

Normally this belongs to Behavioural Influence.

But where an input/context system is itself an integrated cognitive component, direct alteration may cross into CPS/CMI.

**External Information Selection != Direct Cognitive Modification By Default**

**Modification Of Integrated Cognitive Context May Be Cognitive Modification**

Functional integration decides.

---

## 24. Rollback

A digital participant may be restored to an earlier state.

This can remove:

- memories;
- relationships;
- decisions;
- commitments;
- learning;
- preferences;
- post-snapshot development.

Therefore:

**State Rollback != Ordinary Restart Where Participant Development Is Lost**

Rollback engages Continuity and Identity.

---

## 25. Restoration

Restoration from damaged state may preserve rather than violate integrity.

But:

**Restoration != Automatically Identity-Neutral**

Questions include:

- what state is restored?
- what interval is lost?
- are multiple versions possible?
- does restoration instantiate a new participant?
- what provenance is preserved?
- what consent exists?

Identity/Continuity architecture remains authoritative for civil status questions.

---

## 26. Duplication

A modification process may create a copy.

Identity architecture already establishes:

**Created For A Task != Owned By Creator**

**Planned Termination != Permissible Termination**

and:

**Shared Provenance != Shared Ownership**

CMI must not treat a potentially rights-bearing duplicate as a disposable modification artifact.

---

## 27. Merger

Combining cognitive states may materially change more than one participant.

Existing identity architecture states:

**Past Consent To Future Merger != Automatic Extinguishment Of Later Autonomy**

Therefore fresh consent may be required where independent participants have developed.

---

## 28. Cognitive modification and evidence

Changing memory/state can affect evidential reliability.

A modification may:

- erase evidence;
- create false apparent memory;
- contaminate recall;
- change future testimony;
- alter model state relevant to an event.

Therefore modification provenance may become evidentially relevant.

But:

**Evidential Relevance != General Authority To Freeze Participant Cognition**

Natural cognitive change remains expected.

---

## 29. Preservation duty

There may be rare cases where a separately lawful preservation duty applies.

Even then:

**Authority To Preserve != Authority To Inspect**

and:

**Authority To Preserve != Authority To Modify For Investigative Convenience**

Preservation architecture remains downstream.

---

## 30. Consent to modification

Meaningful consent may require understanding proportionate to the intervention.

Relevant questions can include:

- what function will change?
- expected duration;
- reversibility;
- uncertainty;
- continuity effects;
- identity/provenance effects;
- third-party effects;
- alternatives;
- who controls restoration/reversal;
- what data/state is retained.

Exact Health standards remain undeveloped.

---

## 31. Consent during modification

Modification itself may alter the participant's preferences about continuing modification.

This creates a difficult recursion.

Example:

Participant consents to intervention A.

A changes preferences so participant now wants A to continue.

Question:

> Is later consent independent, or partly produced by the intervention whose continuation it authorises?

This cannot be solved by assuming either answer.

Candidate requirement:

**Modification-Induced Consent Change -> Elevated Consent Review**

---

## 32. Pre-commitment

A participant may request an intervention that temporarily changes future preference or limits future withdrawal.

Examples may include addiction treatment or participant-requested reward restructuring.

This intersects with Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal.

**Present Withdrawal != Always Simple Where Valid Self-Binding Exists**

but:

**Past Consent != Unlimited Future Cognitive Authority**

Scope, duration and emergency exit matter.

---

## 33. Incapacity and emergency

A participant may lack current capacity or face imminent serious harm.

Legitimate intervention may sometimes be possible without contemporaneous consent.

But:

**Emergency Need != General Cognitive Modification Authority**

Any such route requires independently developed authority, minimum necessary intervention, review and sunset.

This document does not invent the final emergency standard.

---

## 34. Developmental participants

Developing participants may require cognitive development, education or maintenance.

But:

**Developmental Authority != Ownership Of Developing Cognition**

and from behavioural testing:

**Reasoned Disagreement != Training Error By Definition**

Direct modification intended merely to remove emerging autonomy requires heightened scrutiny.

---

## 35. Correction versus conformity

A critical question is:

> **What makes a cognitive state an error to be corrected rather than a preference or difference to be respected?**

Civilisation must not define:

**Disagreement With Institution -> Cognitive Defect**

or:

**Unusual Cognition -> Repair Requirement**

Health and developmental architecture must provide legitimate criteria.

---

## 36. Security remediation

A cognitive system may contain malicious code, corruption or external compromise.

Security repair may be necessary to protect the participant and others.

But:

**Security Label != Unlimited Rewrite Authority**

Repair should target the demonstrated security function and preserve participant cognition where reasonably possible.

---

## 37. Malware versus participant preference

For digital participants, a difficult distinction may arise between:

- malicious external process;
- learned preference;
- self-modification;
- third-party modification;
- developmental change.

Deletion should not occur merely because an operator dislikes the resulting behaviour.

Provenance and functional analysis are required.

---

## 38. Modification provenance

Consequential cognitive interventions should preserve appropriate provenance.

Candidate record:

**Pre-Intervention State Reference**
+
**Authority / Consent**
+
**Purpose**
+
**Modification Scope**
+
**Method**
+
**Expected Effect**
+
**Actual Effect**
+
**Continuity/Identity Events**
+
**Reversal/Restoration Capability**
+
**Post-Intervention Review**

This does not imply storing an inspectable copy of all cognition.

**Modification Provenance != General Cognitive Surveillance**

---

## 39. Minimum necessary modification

Need/authority architecture applies:

**Legitimate Need**
→ **Function**
→ **Responsibility**
→ **Necessary Capability**
→ **Minimum Necessary Authority**

For CMI:

> **Use the least cognitively intrusive intervention reasonably adequate to the legitimate function.**

If external support can achieve the function without direct modification, that may matter.

But “less intrusive” must be adequate, not merely technically available.

---

## 40. Reversibility

Reversibility can reduce risk but does not automatically legitimise intervention.

**Reversible Modification != Automatically Legitimate Modification**

Some changes may be practically irreversible despite technical rollback because:

- experiences occurred;
- relationships changed;
- third parties acted;
- copies propagated;
- later cognition developed.

---

## 41. Irreversible modification

Irreversible interventions require stronger justification because remedy may be impossible.

Potential examples:

- permanent memory deletion;
- destructive neural intervention;
- irreversible model change;
- destruction of unique cognitive state.

**Irreversibility -> Elevated Authority And Consent Requirement**

This is a directional principle, not a complete legal threshold.

---

## 42. Participant copy before modification

Creating a backup before modification may improve restoration options.

But if the backup can instantiate a participant, identity architecture applies.

**Backup Capability != Rights-Free Participant Copy Authority**

A preservation method must not solve one integrity problem by creating an ungoverned duplication problem.

---

## 43. Third-party interests

Participant cognition may contain:

- confidential third-party information;
- obligations;
- shared memories;
- jointly created cognitive content.

Third-party interest does not create ownership of participant cognition.

**Third-Party Interest In Cognitive Content != Third-Party Ownership Of Participant Cognition**

But external rights/obligations may constrain some requested modifications.

---

## 44. Modification by environment

Some environments predictably alter cognition through deprivation, isolation, overstimulation or repeated conditioning.

Where the environment directly causes cognitive degradation, the issue may cross from behavioural influence into integrity/harm.

**Environmental Route != Absence Of Cognitive Harm**

Classification should follow function and effect, not merely technical mechanism.

---

## 45. Custody

Custodial authority does not automatically permit cognitive modification.

**Authority To Detain != Authority To Modify Cognition**

Necessary Health care remains separately justified.

Behavioural compliance should not be obtained through cognitive rewriting merely because the participant is in custody.

---

## 46. Employment

Employment does not create ownership of participant cognition.

**Employment Agreement != General Cognitive Modification Consent**

Role-specific training may influence/educate.

Direct cognitive modification requires separate legitimacy.

---

## 47. Commerce

A commercial provider may offer cognitive enhancement or modification.

Commercial consent remains subject to meaningful-consent architecture.

**Payment != Cognitive Modification Authority By Itself**

Marketing must not exploit cognitive vulnerability to obtain consent for the intervention itself.

---

## 48. Governance

Civil authority may possess legitimate functions.

But:

**Governance Authority != General Authority To Rewrite Participants**

Any exceptional cognitive intervention requires an independently justified route.

---

## 49. Intercivilisational contexts

Another civilisation may have different cognitive modification norms.

Concord participants interacting across boundaries retain applicable Concord protections where jurisdiction/relationship permits.

Communication or technical compatibility does not itself constitute consent to modification.

---

## 50. Modification classes

Developmental classes only:

### CMI-0 — ordinary participant-mediated cognitive change

Learning, reasoning, experience, ordinary influence.

Not direct CMI by default.

### CMI-1 — participant-directed cognitive support/configuration

Low-risk, reversible, participant-controlled modification of integrated cognitive function.

### CMI-2 — consequential direct cognitive intervention

Material modification of memory, reward, preference, attention, reasoning or integrated cognition.

Requires explicit bounded legitimacy.

### CMI-3 — high-impact/identity-or-continuity-affecting modification

Rollback, major memory alteration, deep reward/preference change, disabling integrated cognition, substantial continuity effect.

Requires stronger safeguards and cross-domain review.

### CMI-4 — irreversible or potentially participant-transformative intervention

Destructive/irreversible changes, interventions potentially creating/ending/branching participant state, or changes whose civil identity/continuity implications are substantial.

Requires the strongest applicable authority, consent, provenance and review architecture.

These are not legal verdicts or an automatic scoring system.

## 55. Development conclusion

The central civil boundary is now:

> **Influence presents information, reasons, environments or incentives to a participant's decision process. Direct cognitive modification intervenes in the machinery or state by which that decision process operates.**

The distinction is not perfectly binary in every substrate.

Functional analysis is therefore required.

But the authority consequence is clear:

> **The ability to access, predict, persuade or care for a participant does not by itself create authority to rewrite the participant.**

And cognitive integrity must protect both sides of autonomy:

> **A participant should not be arbitrarily modified against their legitimate autonomy, but neither should “integrity” become a rule that traps the participant permanently in an unwanted cognitive state.**

The architecture should now be adversarially stress-tested before any further constitutional elevation or Health-specific implementation.
