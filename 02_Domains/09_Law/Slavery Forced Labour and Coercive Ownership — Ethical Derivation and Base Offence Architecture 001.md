## 1. Purpose

This document derives a candidate fundamental law prohibiting slavery, forced labour, debt bondage and equivalent coercive ownership/control of participants.

The law is not limited to historical forms of chattel slavery.

The protected legal interest is:

> **Self-Ownership, Personal Agency And Freedom From Proprietary Control By Another Participant Or Institution.**

A participant may voluntarily exchange labour, accept duties and enter bounded contracts.

They do not thereby become property.

## 3. Foundational rule

> **Participant != Property.**

This is the core legal boundary.

Property rights may attach to objects, resources and legitimate economic interests.

They do not attach to the participant as an owned object.

> **Contractual Obligation != Ownership Of Person.**

> **Economic Dependence != Transfer Of Self-Ownership.**

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not intentionally or knowingly claim, exercise, transfer, enforce or materially facilitate proprietary or equivalent coercive control over another participant's person, agency or labour without a legitimate legal basis for the specific bounded obligation involved.**

No contract, debt, purchase, inheritance, organisational rule or asserted custom can by itself create ownership of a participant.

---

## 5. Slavery

### SLAVERY / PROPRIETARY CONTROL

A condition in which another participant or institution claims or exercises powers equivalent or materially analogous to ownership over a participant.

Indicators may include claimed power to:
- buy or sell the participant;
- permanently transfer control of the participant;
- compel general labour;
- prevent departure as an incident of ownership;
- appropriate labour without legitimate bounded basis;
- determine fundamental life choices as an owner's entitlement;
- inherit or assign control of the participant;
- treat the participant's person as collateral/property.

No single historical form is required.

> **Absence Of Sale != Absence Of Slavery.**

---

## 6. Forced labour

### FORCED LABOUR

Labour or service compelled through unlawful force, threat, confinement, coercive control or another illegitimate mechanism where the participant lacks meaningful lawful ability to refuse or withdraw.

Forced labour does not require a claim of ownership.

Therefore:

> **Forced Labour May Exist Without Slavery.**

The distinction allows the law to recognise serious coercive labour without falsely requiring proof of proprietary ownership.

---

## 7. Voluntary employment

Ordinary employment is not forced labour merely because:
- the participant needs income;
- work contains obligations;
- leaving has economic consequences;
- the employer directs tasks within the agreed function.

Valid employment remains based on bounded agreement and lawful conditions.

> **Employment != Ownership.**

> **Authority To Direct Work != Authority Over The Worker As A Person.**

A participant's right to leave employment may be subject to legitimate contractual consequences, notice requirements or specific obligations, but those cannot become ownership or unlawful confinement.

---

## 8. Contract cannot transfer self-ownership

A participant cannot create unlimited proprietary authority over themselves merely by signing a document.

> **Consent To Contract != Alienation Of Self-Ownership.**

This does not make ordinary contracts unenforceable.

It means enforcement must target legitimate obligations, losses, property or remedies rather than convert the participant into an owned enforcement object.

---

## 9. Debt bondage

Debt does not transfer ownership of the debtor.

> **Debt != Ownership Of Debtor.**

A debt may justify lawful recovery mechanisms.

It does not justify:
- indefinite compelled labour;
- sale of the debtor;
- physical confinement merely for non-payment;
- hereditary transfer of the debt as personal bondage;
- coercive control unrelated to proportionate recovery.

Where labour is voluntarily accepted as one method of satisfying an obligation, consent and fairness remain necessary.

---

## 10. Restitution and court-ordered obligations

A lawful judgment may impose bounded obligations, including restitution or other proportionate requirements.

Such obligations are not ownership.

Candidate distinction:

**Specific Legally Derived Obligation**  
!=  
**General Entitlement To Participant's Labour Or Person**

Any compulsory service or work-like sanction requires its own ethical/legal derivation, proportionality and limits.

> **Legal Obligation != Participant Ownership.**

---

## 11. Civic obligations

The Concord's consent-based architecture makes broad compulsory service especially sensitive.

Any civic duty that requires participant labour must have:
- legitimate source;
- defined function;
- minimum necessary scope;
- proportionality;
- duration;
- review;
- termination.

> **Civic Need != Unlimited Claim On Participant Labour.**

Defence, emergency response or other public need does not silently create ownership.

---

## 12. Exit

Meaningful exit is a strong indicator against coercive ownership, but formal exit alone is insufficient.

> **Formal Exit != Meaningful Exit.**

A nominal right to leave may be meaningless if departure triggers unlawful violence, confinement, confiscation of the participant's person, or other coercion designed to make exit impossible.

Legitimate contractual consequences must remain distinguishable from coercive retention.

---

## 13. Dependence

Dependence can arise through:
- employment;
- housing;
- care;
- migration;
- family;
- economic circumstances;
- digital infrastructure;
- medical need.

Dependence does not itself establish exploitation or slavery.

But deliberate exploitation of dependence to obtain otherwise unauthorised control may be relevant evidence.

> **Dependence != Consent.**

> **Dependence != Ownership.**

---

## 14. Children and guardianship

Guardianship or developmental protection does not create ownership.

A guardian may possess bounded protective/decision authority where legitimately derived.

> **Guardianship != Ownership.**

The child's developing autonomy and rights remain protected.

Authority should reduce or transform as capacity and legal state change.

---

## 15. Care and disability

Care needs do not create ownership.

> **Need For Care != Transfer Of Self-Ownership.**

Support providers may possess functional permissions necessary to provide agreed or legitimately authorised care.

They do not thereby acquire general control of the participant.

---

## 16. Custody and imprisonment

Lawful custody substantially restricts autonomy but does not make the detained participant property of the state.

> **Custody != Ownership.**

A custodial institution may exercise only authority legitimately connected to custody, safety, lawful sentence and independently justified functions.

It cannot claim the participant's general labour merely because they are confined.

Any work in custody requires separate legal treatment concerning voluntariness, compensation, rehabilitation, restitution and coercion.

---

## 17. Trafficking-like conduct

Recruitment, transfer, transport, concealment or receipt of participants may form part of slavery or forced-labour systems.

But movement is not required.

A participant may be enslaved or coercively controlled without ever being transported.

> **Trafficking Mechanism != Necessary Element Of Slavery.**

Where forced movement occurs, the Unlawful Restraint, Detention and Abduction architecture may additionally apply.

---

## 18. Sale and transfer

Purported sale, purchase or transfer of ownership over a participant has no legitimate proprietary effect.

> **Purported Ownership Transaction != Valid Ownership.**

The transaction itself may nevertheless constitute evidence of:
- intent;
- attempted slavery;
- conspiracy/coordinated preparation;
- facilitation;
- exploitation.

Property-law form cannot legalise participant ownership.

---

## 19. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: YES**

The general Attempt and Completion architecture applies.

Examples may include:
- attempting to purchase control of a participant from another;
- constructing a coercive labour arrangement and entering direct execution;
- attempting to transfer a participant as property;
- attempting to confine a participant for forced labour but being prevented.

Preparation remains distinct from executory attempt.

---

## 20. Participation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

Potential roles include:
- principal controller;
- co-principal;
- director;
- recruiter/assistant;
- transporter/facilitator;
- financial or administrative facilitator where the required mental state and material contribution are established.

> **Organisation Membership != Automatic Complicity In Exploitation.**

Each participant's contribution and mental state require separate classification.

---

## 21. Mental state

The principal offence ordinarily requires intentional or knowing proprietary/coercive control.

Reckless or negligent failures in employment/care regulation may be unlawful under other rules but should not automatically be classified as slavery.

> **Bad Employment Practice != Automatically Slavery.**

Severity of language should track established legal state.

---

## 22. Consent

Consent is relevant to work and service but cannot validate ownership of the participant.

> **Consent To Labour != Consent To Become Property.**

A participant may consent to:
- a job;
- a task;
- a term of service;
- a dangerous but legitimate occupation;
- a bounded obligation.

They retain self-ownership.

Withdrawal and contractual consequences are governed by the general Consent architecture and applicable contract law.

---

## 23. Coercion

Coercion may include:
- violence;
- credible threats;
- unlawful detention;
- threats against others;
- confiscation necessary to prevent meaningful exit;
- blackmail;
- abuse of authority;
- deliberately manufactured dependency;
- other mechanisms that defeat meaningful authorship.

Not every difficult economic choice is coercion.

> **Unattractive Alternative != Automatically Coercion.**

The legal question is whether another participant illegitimately authored/control the choice through prohibited pressure.

---

## 24. Economic scarcity

Scarcity may constrain real choices without any specific actor committing forced labour.

This distinction matters.

> **Constrained Choice By Circumstance != Automatically Coercion By Another Participant.**

The Concord may have social/economic duties to reduce avoidable deprivation, but criminal responsibility requires a legally sufficient connection to a participant's own conduct.

---

## 25. Digital and hybrid participants

Self-ownership must be substrate-neutral where participant status is established.

Potential analogous prohibited conduct may include:
- claiming ownership of a digital participant's agency merely because another owns hardware;
- coercively controlling migration/continuity as labour leverage;
- treating a participant instance as transferable property contrary to its established identity/rights;
- compelled operation for another's benefit without legitimate basis.

Critical distinction:

> **Ownership Of Hardware != Automatic Ownership Of Participant Running On Hardware.**

Likewise:

> **Ownership Of Model, Software Or Infrastructure != Automatically Ownership Of A Participant.**

Exact boundaries require integration with future digital-personhood, continuity, licensing and property architectures.

---

## 26. AI capability and service systems

Not every AI system is necessarily a participant.

Ordinary ownership/licensing of non-participant software, models or machines remains possible.

The law therefore depends upon established participant status rather than anthropomorphic assumption.

> **Capability != Participant Status.**

Once participant status and protected agency are established, proprietary control must be re-evaluated accordingly.

---

## 27. Hereditary status

No participant can inherit ownership of another participant.

Nor should a participant inherit enslaved status from ancestry, parentage or origin.

> **Parent Status != Child Ownership Status.**

> **Ancestry != Legitimate Source Of Subordination.**

---

## 28. Remedy

Potential remedies include:
- immediate release from coercive control;
- restoration of identity/documents/property;
- unpaid compensation/restitution;
- safe housing;
- health and psychological support;
- legal status correction;
- employment/economic continuity;
- protection from retaliation;
- correction of records;
- recovery of unlawfully appropriated value.

Release alone may be insufficient where the coercive system deliberately destroyed the participant's independent means of living.

> **End Of Control != Complete Restoration.**

---

## 29. Aggravation

Potential aggravating factors may include:
- extended duration;
- multiple controlled participants;
- deliberate violence;
- sexual exploitation;
- exploitation of children or substantial vulnerability;
- abuse of official/care authority;
- deliberate destruction of exit options;
- cross-jurisdiction concealment;
- systematic/organised operation;
- severe appropriation of labour/value.

The general Aggravation and Mitigation architecture governs application and prevents double counting.

---

## 30. Organisational responsibility

Enterprises and institutions may create distributed coercive systems in which no single worker performs every element.

Investigation should therefore examine:
- ownership/control structure;
- policy;
- incentives;
- knowledge;
- command;
- financial benefit;
- recruitment;
- confinement;
- document control;
- payment;
- exit conditions.

But:

> **Systemic Harm != Automatic Individual Guilt.**

Individual responsibility still requires legally established authorship, participation or duty.

Separate enterprise/legal-person responsibility may also apply.

## 32. Worked examples

### A. Ordinary employment

A accepts paid employment, works agreed hours and can resign subject to lawful notice.

**Result:** employment and functional direction; no participant ownership.

### B. Debt bondage

A owes B money. B confines A and requires indefinite unpaid labour until B decides the debt is satisfied.

**Result:** debt does not create ownership or general labour authority. Potential forced labour, unlawful detention and other offences.

### C. Custodial labour

A participant serving a lawful custodial sentence is ordered to perform labour unrelated to legitimate custody, rehabilitation or another independently derived obligation solely because the institution claims ownership of prisoner labour.

**Result:** custody alone does not create labour ownership. Separate legal authority would be required.

### D. Guardian

A guardian makes a necessary bounded decision for a child within legitimate protective authority.

**Result:** protective authority; not ownership. Authority remains functionally bounded and changes with capacity/state.

### E. Digital participant

Organisation A owns servers on which established digital participant B operates. A claims server ownership means B itself can be sold as property and compelled to work indefinitely.

**Result:** hardware ownership does not automatically establish participant ownership. Participant status, continuity and legitimate service obligations require separate analysis.

### F. Economic hardship

A accepts unpleasant but lawful employment because alternatives are financially poor. Employer B does not create the scarcity, unlawfully threaten A or prevent exit.

**Result:** difficult choice alone does not establish forced labour. Other fairness/social-support questions may remain.

## 33. Development result

Slavery and forced labour can be derived without enumerating historical mechanisms.

The fundamental boundary is:

**A participant cannot be property, and no participant or institution may convert a bounded legal relationship into ownership of another participant's person, agency or general labour.**

This law naturally interfaces with:
- unlawful restraint/detention;
- employment;
- contract;
- debt;
- custody;
- guardianship;
- care;
- enterprise responsibility;
- trafficking/exploitation;
- digital/hybrid participant status;
- restitution and civil continuity.

The next fundamental derivation should examine **property interference, theft, deprivation and damage**, because the framework now clearly distinguishes ownership of legitimate property from the categorical prohibition on ownership of participants.
