## 1. Purpose

This document defines a reusable architecture for aggravating and mitigating factors after the underlying legal state has been established.

Aggravation and mitigation should not be independently reinvented inside every offence.

Candidate structure:

**Established Offence / Responsibility State**  
→ **Base Consequence Range / Response**  
→ **Aggravation Tree ↔ Mitigation Tree**  
→ **Reasoned Proportionality Assessment**  
→ **Sanction / Other Consequence**

This architecture does not yet define numerical sentencing ranges.

---

## 2. Ethical provenance

Primary Ethical Kernel sources include:
- **BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm**
- **BL5 — Act Fairly**
- **BL6 — Accept Responsibility**
- **BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity**
- **BL10 — Remain Open to Correction**

A proportionate legal response should reflect established responsibility without ignoring facts that materially increase or reduce culpability.

> **Equal Offence Label != Necessarily Equal Culpability.**

But:

> **Different Circumstances != Unlimited Sentencing Discretion.**

Modifiers require explicit reasons, evidence and reviewability.

---

## 3. Position in legal grammar

Aggravation and mitigation occur after:
- offence elements;
- actual/intended result;
- completion;
- mental state;
- participation;
- consent/context;
- justification/defence;
- capacity/authorship;
- responsibility

have been determined to the legally required standard.

They do not repair a missing offence element.

> **Aggravation Cannot Create An Offence.**

> **Mitigation Cannot Convert Unlawful Conduct Into Lawful Conduct.**

A complete defence belongs earlier in the legal grammar.

---

## 4. Aggravation

An aggravating factor is an independently evidenced circumstance that legitimately increases culpability, seriousness, justified sanction, or another relevant consequence beyond that represented by the base classification alone.

Candidate factors include:
- premeditation;
- repeated or sustained conduct;
- deliberate exploitation of known vulnerability;
- abuse of entrusted authority;
- abuse of protective/care relationship;
- leadership or direction of coordinated offending;
- multiple affected participants;
- continuation after lawful justification or authority clearly ended;
- deliberate obstruction or concealment where legally relevant;
- deliberate increase of foreseeable prohibited danger;
- targeting because doing so increases the victim's vulnerability or the harmful meaning of the act, where independently legally derived;
- relevant repeated offending where its use is justified and not double counted.

No factor applies merely because its label sounds serious.

> **Aggravating Label != Established Aggravation.**

---

## 5. Mitigation

A mitigating factor is an independently evidenced circumstance that legitimately reduces culpability or justified sanction without making the underlying conduct lawful.

Candidate factors include:
- reduced but legally sufficient capacity through the separate general capacity/mental-health architecture;
- duress insufficient to establish complete defence;
- genuine mistake insufficient to establish complete defence;
- materially reduced foreseeability;
- materially limited participation;
- voluntary cessation;
- genuine action preventing further harm;
- immediate assistance to affected participants;
- voluntary restoration/restitution;
- early truthful admission/cooperation;
- relevant developmental or functional circumstances;
- other evidenced facts materially reducing culpability.

> **Mitigation != Exoneration.**

---

## 6. Mental health remains separate

Mental health is not duplicated as an offence-specific or modifier-specific doctrine here.

Where relevant:

**Mental-Health Evidence**  
→ **General Mental Health / Capacity Architecture**  
→ **Legally Established Functional Effect**  
→ **Mitigation Input Where Applicable**

The separate `Mental Health as General Legal Factor — Responsibility Mitigation and Custodial Routing 001.md` governs that interface.

> **Diagnosis Alone != Mitigating Factor.**

The degree, if any, by which mental-health impairment should change sentence duration remains unresolved pending dedicated sentencing development.

Custodial routing remains separately determined.

---

## 7. Mirrored assessment

Consequence reasoning should deliberately inspect both directions.

### Aggravation tree
What evidenced facts legitimately increase culpability or justified consequence?

### Mitigation tree
What evidenced facts legitimately reduce culpability or justified consequence?

Both trees should be constructed from the same established case record.

> **Search For Aggravation Must Be Matched By Search For Mitigation.**

> **Search For Mitigation Must Not Suppress Established Aggravation.**

This mirrors Concord's broader use of opposing Reality Trees to reduce one-direction reasoning.

---

## 8. Coexistence

Aggravating and mitigating factors may simultaneously be true.

Example:
- deliberate intent;
- premeditation;
- serious actual harm;
- substantial but not responsibility-defeating capacity impairment;
- voluntary cessation of further attack;
- immediate assistance to victim.

The legal record should preserve all established dimensions.

> **Aggravation Does Not Erase Mitigation.**

> **Mitigation Does Not Erase Aggravation.**

---

## 9. No crude cancellation

Factors should not initially be compressed into a points system such as:

**Premeditation +2**  
**Impairment -2**  
**Net 0**

Two cases with the same arithmetic result can have radically different legal meaning.

> **Equivalent Numerical Total != Equivalent Legal State.**

Any later quantitative support must preserve inspectable source factors and remain subordinate to reasoned adjudication.

---

## 10. No double counting

A fact already represented in the base offence or another classifier should not automatically be counted again as aggravation.

Example:
If actual H4 harm already determines the applicable harm band, the mere fact of H4 should not automatically be added again as an aggravator unless a separate legal reason exists.

Likewise an intended result already represented by the intent classifier should not automatically be counted twice.

> **One Fact != Multiple Punitive Increases Without Independent Justification.**

This applies across:
- offence elements;
- harm band;
- mental state;
- attempt/completion;
- participation;
- aggravation.

---

## 11. Premeditation

Premeditation may aggravate because it can evidence sustained authorship, opportunity for reconsideration, preparation or deliberate persistence.

But:

> **Premeditation != Intent.**

> **Premeditation != Full Capacity.**

A planned offence may still involve substantial capacity impairment.

Premeditation must therefore be separately established and separately interpreted.

---

## 12. Vulnerability

Deliberate exploitation of known vulnerability may aggravate where the participant selected or used that vulnerability to facilitate offending or increase harm.

Vulnerability should not become a status label that automatically changes sentence.

> **Victim Status != Automatic Aggravation.**

The relevant issue is the offender's evidenced relationship to the vulnerability and the protected legal interest.

---

## 13. Entrusted authority

Abuse of authority may aggravate because the offender used a role created for a legitimate function as a means of offending.

Examples can include:
- care;
- custody;
- fiduciary responsibility;
- professional authority;
- public authority;
- guardianship.

> **Authority Granted For Function A != Permission To Exploit That Authority For Offence B.**

This aggravator should not substitute for separately prosecuting an independent abuse-of-authority offence where one exists.

---

## 14. Multiple affected participants

Multiple victims or affected participants may increase seriousness, but the architecture must distinguish:
- one act causing multiple harms;
- repeated acts;
- separate offences;
- collective externality.

The same harm should not be multiplied merely through classification.

Case structure should preserve each affected participant and each legally relevant harm.

---

## 15. Repeated offending

Prior or repeated offending requires caution.

Relevant prior conduct may indicate:
- persistence;
- failure of previous interventions;
- knowledge;
- increased foreseeability;
- current risk;
- repeated disregard of legal boundaries.

But:

> **Past Offence != Present Offence.**

> **Prior Conviction != Automatic Proof Of Current Guilt.**

Previous legal history must never substitute for proof of the current offence.

Its legitimate role in sentencing requires later dedicated calibration.

---

## 16. Post-event conduct

Conduct after an offence can be relevant without rewriting what occurred.

Potential mitigation:
- voluntary cessation;
- calling for help;
- preventing further harm;
- truthful admission;
- restitution;
- cooperation.

Potential aggravation or separate offending:
- threats;
- evidence destruction;
- intimidation;
- deliberate concealment;
- obstruction.

> **Post-Event Conduct != Retroactive Change To Historical Event.**

It may change consequence reasoning or create additional legal states.

---

## 17. Restitution and repair

Voluntary repair can reduce outstanding harm and provide evidence relevant to responsibility and rehabilitation.

But:

> **Restitution != Purchase Of Immunity.**

> **Ability To Pay != Ability To Buy Reduced Responsibility.**

A wealthy participant must not obtain systematically lower punitive consequences merely because they can compensate more easily.

Restoration for the victim remains independently valuable.

---

## 18. Cooperation

Truthful cooperation may mitigate where it:
- accepts responsibility;
- reduces investigative burden;
- prevents further harm;
- assists restoration;
- protects other participants.

But cooperation should not be coerced through disproportionate threatened punishment.

Nor should exercising legitimate procedural rights automatically aggravate sentence.

> **Exercise Of Defence Rights != Aggravation.**

> **Silence Where Lawfully Permitted != Automatic Aggravation.**

---

## 19. Causation and contribution

A modifier should have a legitimate relationship to:
- culpability;
- harm;
- abuse of protected relationship;
- responsibility;
- rehabilitation;
- proportionality;
- another stated legal interest.

Purely emotive facts should not alter consequence merely because they produce disapproval.

> **Moral Disapproval != Legal Aggravation.**

---

## 20. Evidence standard

Every aggravating or mitigating factor must be evidentially supported.

Aggravation should not be inferred from the offence label alone.

Mitigation should not be granted from unsupported assertion alone.

The precise standard of proof is a procedural/Judiciary question, but uncertainty should remain visible.

Candidate states:
- alleged;
- evidenced;
- contested;
- established;
- rejected;
- unresolved.

---

## 21. Relation to protection

Aggravation is not a substitute for present-risk assessment.

A highly aggravated offence may not imply high continuing danger.

A strongly mitigated offence may coexist with serious present danger.

> **Aggravation != Present Dangerousness.**

> **Mitigation != Present Safety.**

Protective authority remains separately derived.

---

## 22. Relation to remedy

Aggravation and mitigation principally modify responsibility/consequence reasoning.

Victim remedy should remain tied to established harm and legitimate restoration needs.

> **Offender Mitigation != Reduction Of Victim's Established Harm.**

The victim does not become less injured because the offender had reduced culpability.

## 25. Development result

Aggravation and mitigation are viable as reusable consequence modifiers.

They should be:
- independently evidenced;
- mirrored;
- multidimensional;
- protected against double counting;
- separated from offence elements;
- separated from complete defences;
- separated from present-risk/protective authority;
- separated from victim remedy.

The architecture deliberately does not yet determine numerical sentencing effects.

The next major reusable layer should be **sentencing and consequence architecture**, because the current legal grammar can now supply structured inputs without forcing sanction, remedy, protection and treatment into one undifferentiated punishment.
