## 1. Purpose

This document derives a candidate fundamental law protecting participants against unjustified intrusion into protected physical, informational, communicative and computational contexts.

Privacy is not treated as an absolute prohibition on observation.

Instead:

**Context**  
→ **Legitimate Boundary / Expectation / Protected Interest**  
→ **Access / Observation / Acquisition / Disclosure**  
→ **Consent Or Independent Authority?**  
→ **Scope**  
→ **Legal Classification**

This architecture directly interfaces with Contextual Wrapper Architecture (CWA), Safe Spaces, consent, information protection and bounded authority.

## 3. Protected interest

Privacy protects legitimate participant control over access to protected contexts.

Potential protected contexts include:
- body;
- home/private physical space;
- communications;
- personal records;
- health information;
- financial information;
- credentials;
- private digital storage;
- private computation;
- location/history where protected;
- relationships/associations;
- identity-linked information;
- other contextually protected information or activity.

> **Privacy != Secrecy Of Everything.**

> **Information Exists != Everyone Is Entitled To Access It.**

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not, without valid consent, lawful justification or other independently legitimate authority, intentionally or knowingly access, enter, observe, acquire, intercept, record, retain, use, correlate or disclose another participant's protected private context beyond the scope legitimately available to them.**

Different verbs represent different interference states, not necessarily different ethical roots.

---

## 5. Contextual privacy

The same information or conduct may have different privacy states in different contexts.

Example:
A participant's name may be intentionally public while their medical record remains private.

> **Public In Context A != Public In Context B.**

CWA is therefore central.

Privacy classification should identify:
- protected object/information;
- wrapper/context;
- authorised participants;
- permitted purpose;
- permitted operations;
- temporal scope;
- onward-disclosure rules.

---

## 6. Safe spaces

A safe space can contain physical and informational boundaries.

> **Permission To Enter Space != Permission To Inspect Everything Within It.**

Likewise:

> **Permission To Observe One Context != Permission To Observe Nested Contexts.**

Nested-context rules apply:
- Parent Access != Child Access;
- Parent Rules != Child Rules.

---

## 7. Physical intrusion

Unauthorised entry into a protected physical space may constitute privacy/safe-space intrusion even where nothing is taken or damaged.

> **No Property Taken != No Intrusion.**

Property/access law may overlap but the protected interest is distinct: contextual boundary and private occupancy.

---

## 8. Observation

Observation can itself intrude upon protected context.

Examples may include:
- covert viewing into a protected private space;
- interception of private communications;
- unauthorised sensor access;
- surveillance of protected activity.

But:

> **Observation != Automatically Unlawful Surveillance.**

Context, accessibility, reasonable boundary, consent and authority matter.

---

## 9. Public observation

Conduct deliberately exposed in a genuinely public context ordinarily carries less privacy protection concerning ordinary observation.

However:

> **Public Visibility != Unlimited Secondary Use.**

Persistent tracking, aggregation, identification, correlation or republication may create a materially different context from ordinary momentary observation.

This interfaces with existing exposure/reidentification and derived-information architecture.

---

## 10. Recording

Observation and recording are distinct operations.

> **Permission To Observe != Automatic Permission To Record.**

Recording may increase:
- persistence;
- replicability;
- audience;
- correlation;
- future use;
- exposure risk.

Context determines whether separate permission or authority is required.

---

## 11. Acquisition and retention

Lawful acquisition does not imply indefinite retention.

> **Lawful Acquisition != Unlimited Retention.**

Retention should be tied to:
- purpose;
- legitimate need;
- legal duty;
- review;
- deletion/archive rules;
- Historical custody where applicable.

This mirrors general authority sunset.

---

## 12. Use

Information may be lawfully possessed but unlawfully used for a new purpose.

> **Access For Purpose A != Permission For Purpose B.**

Example:
A medical professional legitimately accesses a health record for treatment but uses it for unrelated commercial targeting.

Initial access may be lawful while later use is not.

---

## 13. Disclosure

Authorised access does not automatically authorise onward disclosure.

> **Right To Know != Right To Broadcast.**

Disclosure should be evaluated separately from acquisition.

A participant may therefore:
- lawfully receive information;
- unlawfully disclose it.

---

## 14. Consent

The general Consent and Withdrawal architecture applies.

Privacy consent may be bounded by:
- data/context;
- observer/recipient;
- purpose;
- duration;
- operation;
- onward disclosure;
- correlation;
- retention.

> **Consent To Collection != Consent To Every Future Use.**

> **Consent To One Recipient != Consent To All Recipients.**

---

## 15. Notice

Notice improves legibility but is not equivalent to consent.

> **Notice != Consent.**

A sign stating "all activity may be monitored forever for any purpose" does not automatically create ethically valid unlimited authority merely because it is visible.

CWA rule:

> **Legibility != Legitimacy != Consent != Precedence.**

---

## 16. Terms and conditions

Acceptance of a service may authorise defined data processing.

But blanket clauses should not automatically erase protected privacy interests.

> **Use Of Service != Unlimited Privacy Waiver.**

Consent remains subject to meaningful authorship, specificity, proportionality and applicable rights.

---

## 17. Credentials and authentication

Credentials protect access but do not define the entirety of legal permission.

> **Technical Access != Legal Permission.**

A participant may possess a password/token yet lack authority to use it for a particular purpose.

Conversely, legitimate emergency authority may sometimes permit access through another lawful route.

---

## 18. Unauthorised digital access

Accessing protected digital systems or records without consent/authority may constitute intrusion even where:
- nothing is deleted;
- nothing is altered;
- no property is physically removed.

> **Read-Only Intrusion != No Intrusion.**

Copying may create additional information/privacy offences without physical deprivation.

---

## 19. Security weakness

A system being technically accessible does not automatically make access authorised.

> **Unlocked != Public.**

> **Vulnerability != Permission.**

However accidental discovery should be distinguished from intentional exploitation.

---

## 20. Accidental access

A participant may inadvertently receive or encounter private information.

> **Accidental Exposure != Automatic Intrusion By Recipient.**

Responsibility may arise if the participant knowingly exceeds legitimate handling after recognising the protected state.

Examples:
- continued browsing;
- copying;
- disclosure;
- exploitation.

---

## 21. Correlation and inference

Individually accessible facts can produce sensitive derived information when correlated.

> **Permission To Access Inputs != Automatic Permission To Derive Every Possible Output.**

This interfaces with the parked Context/Information branch and should not reopen that branch unless specifically directed.

Legal treatment should recognise that correlation itself may create a new protected information state.

---

## 22. Reidentification

Anonymised or compartmentalised information may be intentionally reidentified.

> **Ability To Reidentify != Permission To Reidentify.**

Where anonymity is part of the protection state, deliberate reidentification may constitute intrusion even if each technical input was individually accessible.

---

## 23. Location

Location privacy is contextual.

Being visible at one public moment does not automatically authorise persistent historical tracking.

> **Observed Location At Time T != Unlimited Location History Authority.**

Tracking can alter the information state through aggregation.

---

## 24. Communications

Private communications may be protected against:
- interception;
- unauthorised recording;
- unauthorised access;
- disclosure.

But a recipient ordinarily knows the communication they legitimately received.

The sender cannot always unilaterally impose absolute secrecy after lawful receipt.

> **Communication To Recipient != Communication To Everyone.**

Exact onward-use rules depend on context, confidentiality and legitimate interests.

---

## 25. Confidentiality

Confidentiality is a relationship-specific information duty.

It may arise from:
- health;
- legal/fiduciary roles;
- employment;
- contract;
- protected civil function;
- explicit agreement;
- contextual wrapper.

> **Possession Of Information != Absence Of Confidentiality Duty.**

Privacy and confidentiality overlap but are not identical.

---

## 26. Body privacy

The body is a particularly strong protected context.

Search, examination, imaging, sample collection or monitoring require:
- consent; or
- independently legitimate bounded authority.

> **Access To Participant != Access To Participant's Body.**

This interfaces with Health, Civil Security and Judiciary.

---

## 27. Search

Official search is a form of authorised intrusion where legitimate.

Candidate chain:

**Legally Sufficient Basis**  
→ **Defined Search Objective**  
→ **Minimum Necessary Scope**  
→ **Authority / Warrant Where Required**  
→ **Search**  
→ **Evidence/Property Handling**  
→ **Review**

> **Investigative Convenience != Unlimited Search Authority.**

Future Civil Security/Judiciary procedure must define thresholds.

---

## 28. Emergency access

Emergency conditions may justify otherwise prohibited access where BCA requirements are satisfied.

Example:
Entering a private home to rescue an unconscious participant from immediate danger.

> **Emergency Access != General Future Access Permission.**

Authority ends or changes when the emergency state changes.

---

## 29. Surveillance

Surveillance may range from ordinary observation to persistent systematic monitoring.

Relevant dimensions include:
- duration;
- scale;
- identifiability;
- persistence;
- automation;
- correlation;
- protected context;
- purpose;
- authority;
- retention.

> **Observation Capability != Surveillance Authority.**

The existence of sensors does not itself justify their use.

---

## 30. Public safety systems

CCTV or similar systems may serve legitimate safety functions.

Their operation still requires:
- purpose;
- scope;
- notice where appropriate;
- access controls;
- retention rules;
- audit;
- protection against unrelated use.

> **Safety Purpose != Unlimited Secondary Use.**

This is consistent with existing assumptions about bodycam/CCTV/dashcam archival evidence while preserving contextual rights.

---

## 31. Body-worn cameras

Body-worn recording may support:
- participant protection;
- officer accountability;
- evidential integrity.

But it can also enter homes, medical contexts and other highly private spaces.

Therefore:

> **Duty To Record != Duty To Publish.**

Recording, access, evidential use, public disclosure and Historical retention are separate operations.

---

## 32. Official access

Official role does not create general information entitlement.

> **Official Status != Universal Access Permission.**

Each access should derive from function, need and minimum necessary capability.

This follows MNC:

**Purpose → Function → Need → Minimum Sufficient Capability → Bounded Exercise → Review → Termination/Re-Justification**

---

## 33. Historical custody

Historical may legitimately preserve records unavailable to ordinary participants.

> **Historical Custody != General Public Accessibility.**

Archival preservation and access are separate functions.

Contextual wrappers and ESCP should preserve rights during transfer and storage.

---

## 34. Health information

Health records are strongly protected but may need controlled sharing for treatment, emergency care or research.

> **Health Need != Unlimited Health-Record Access.**

Access should follow minimum necessary purpose and relevant consent/authority.

---

## 35. Research

Research value does not automatically override participant privacy.

> **Research Utility != Automatic Research Authority.**

Anonymisation, aggregation, consent and legitimate research authority may permit use while reducing unnecessary exposure.

---

## 36. Employment

Employment does not create general surveillance authority over workers.

> **Employment != General Privacy Waiver.**

Monitoring required for legitimate work/safety/security functions must remain bounded by purpose and proportionality.

---

## 37. Children and dependants

Protective relationships may justify greater access where legitimately necessary.

But:

> **Guardianship != Unlimited Surveillance Authority.**

Privacy should develop with capacity and context.

Protective access remains functionally bounded.

---

## 38. Intimate contexts

Sexual, medical, sanitary and similarly intimate contexts carry especially strong privacy interests.

Unauthorised observation/recording may produce serious dignity and autonomy harm even without physical contact.

The law should represent that seriousness through harm/aggravation rather than inventing mechanism-specific loopholes.

---

## 39. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: YES** where the underlying intrusion requires intentional execution.

Examples:
- attempting unauthorised system access but authentication blocks entry;
- installing a covert camera that fails to record;
- attempting to intercept a communication but selecting the wrong channel.

The general Attempt architecture applies.

---

## 40. Participation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

Potential roles:
- principal intruder;
- director;
- knowing technical facilitator;
- knowing recipient/requester;
- participant who intentionally republishes protected material where disclosure is independently prohibited.

> **Providing General-Purpose Technology != Automatic Complicity In Privacy Abuse.**

Purpose, knowledge, specificity and contribution remain necessary.

---

## 41. Mental state

Principal intrusion offences ordinarily require intentional or knowing conduct.

Negligent privacy/security failures may require separate treatment for:
- custodians;
- professionals;
- organisations;
- infrastructure operators.

> **Security Failure != Automatically Intentional Intrusion.**

But negligent custodianship can still be serious where a legitimate duty exists.

---

## 42. Harm

Potential harms include:
- loss of safety;
- dignity harm;
- exposure;
- identity misuse;
- financial loss;
- coercion;
- discrimination;
- chilling of lawful activity;
- physical danger;
- reputational consequences;
- persistent loss of contextual control.

Actual harm and intended intrusion should be separately recorded.

---

## 43. Remedy

Potential remedies include:
- cessation of access/surveillance;
- deletion where lawful/possible;
- access revocation;
- return/destruction of unlawfully obtained copies;
- correction of records;
- notification of exposure;
- containment of onward disclosure;
- compensation;
- protective measures;
- audit/correction of systemic access failures.

> **Stopping Intrusion != Automatic Restoration Of Privacy.**

Once information has propagated, complete restoration may be impossible.

---

## 44. Unlawfully obtained evidence

Existing Concord judiciary work holds that unlawfully obtained evidence does not automatically become factually nonexistent.

This architecture preserves the distinction:

> **Unlawful Acquisition != Automatic Factual Falsity.**

But unlawful acquisition can:
- create separate liability;
- affect credibility/provenance;
- require audit;
- affect admissibility/weight according to future procedure;
- trigger remedy.

Evidence law should not incentivise unlawful intrusion merely because information may remain evidentially relevant.

## 46. Worked examples

### A. Open door

A notices B's private home door is unlocked and enters merely because entry is technically possible.

**Result:** unlocked != public; physical/privacy intrusion may be established.

### B. Medical access

Clinician A accesses B's record for B's treatment, then later uses the same information for unrelated commercial targeting.

**Result:** initial access may be authorised; secondary use requires separate classification.

### C. Public observation

A sees B walking through a public square.

**Result:** ordinary observation does not automatically constitute privacy intrusion.

A then creates a persistent cross-system history of B's movements using multiple databases without permission/authority.

**Result:** aggregation/correlation creates a materially different privacy question.

### D. Accidental email

A accidentally receives B's private document due to a mistyped address, recognises the error and stops reading/deletes or returns it appropriately.

**Result:** accidental receipt does not automatically make A an intruder.

### E. Covert recording

A deliberately installs a camera in B's private changing space without consent.

**Result:** intentional observation/recording intrusion; intimate context may strongly aggravate harm.

### F. Emergency entry

A enters B's locked home without permission after seeing B unconscious beside an active fire.

**Result:** intrusion occurred physically, but BCA may justify emergency entry.

### G. Bodycam

An officer lawfully records an incident while entering a private home under legitimate authority.

**Result:** recording may be justified for evidential/accountability purpose; this does not automatically authorise public release or unrelated reuse.

## 47. Development result

Privacy law can be derived as protection of **contextual boundaries**, rather than as ownership of all information about oneself or an absolute prohibition on observation.

Core rule:

**Do not intentionally or knowingly cross, observe, acquire, use, retain, correlate or disclose another participant's protected private context beyond valid consent or independently legitimate bounded authority.**

This formulation is compatible with:
- Safe Spaces;
- CWA;
- consent;
- Health;
- Historical;
- Research;
- Civil Security;
- Judiciary;
- digital systems;
- evidence;
- participant records.

It also preserves necessary distinctions between:
**access, observation, recording, retention, use, correlation and disclosure**.

A natural next fundamental derivation is **damage to reputation, false accusation and harmful false factual assertion**, because BL4 protects truth while autonomy and dignity require care not to criminalise opinion, criticism, mistake or good-faith reporting.
