# Preparatory Participation — Solicitation, Incitement, Agreement and Conspiracy Classifiers 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 9 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / GENERAL LEGAL CLASSIFIER / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Parent:** `Legal Derivation and Consequence Architecture — Ethical Provenance, Offence Classifiers and Proportionate Response 001.md`  
**Related:** Participation and Complicity; Attempt and Completion; Endangerment; Threats/Coercion; Fraud; Entrusted Authority

---

## 1. Purpose

This document develops reusable classifiers for participation in planned or future wrongdoing before the underlying offence is completed and, in some cases, before it reaches executory attempt.

It addresses conduct conventionally described as:
- solicitation;
- incitement;
- recruitment;
- direction;
- criminal agreement;
- conspiracy;
- preparatory assistance.

The architecture must protect against genuine coordinated preparation without criminalising:
- thought;
- fantasy;
- discussion;
- dissent;
- association;
- membership;
- abstract advocacy;
- reporting;
- research;
- fictional or hypothetical scenarios.

---

## 2. Ethical provenance

Preparatory participation is not a new protected interest.

Its legitimacy derives from the protected interest of the intended underlying offence plus:

- **BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm**
- **BL2 — Respect Autonomy**
- **BL3 — Preserve Safe Spaces**
- **BL4 — Act Honestly**
- **BL5 — Act Fairly**
- **BL6 — Accept Responsibility**
- **BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity**
- **BL9 — Seek Peaceful Coexistence**
- **BL10 — Remain Open to Correction**

Central rule:

> **Preparatory Liability Must Inherit The Ethical Provenance Of The Intended Underlying Wrong.**

There should be no free-floating offence of "dangerous planning" detached from a protected legal interest.

---

## 3. Foundational boundary

Candidate progression:

**Thought / Desire**  
→ **Intention**  
→ **Discussion / Advocacy**  
→ **Proposal / Solicitation**  
→ **Agreement / Recruitment**  
→ **Operational Preparation / Assistance**  
→ **Executory Attempt**  
→ **Completion**

Not every transition creates liability.

> **Thought != Offence.**

> **Intention Alone != Offence.**

> **Association != Participation.**

> **Discussion != Agreement.**

> **Agreement != Automatic Executory Attempt.**

---

## 4. Why a preparatory layer is needed

Attempt already requires direct execution beyond mere preparation.

But some conduct can create a serious legal state before that point.

Examples:
- hiring another participant to commit an offence;
- recruiting a participant into a concrete criminal operation;
- agreeing on a serious offence and assigning operational roles;
- knowingly supplying specially configured resources for a planned offence;
- directing another participant to perform the offence.

Without a preparatory layer, these states may be invisible until attempt begins.

But overbroad preparatory law risks becoming a thought/association offence.

---

## 5. No independent protected interest

Preparatory participation is a **classifier layer**, not a new fundamental offence family.

Candidate representation:

**Underlying Base Offence**  
+ **Preparatory Participation State**  
+ **Required Intent/Knowledge**  
+ **Operational Proximity / Commitment**  
+ **Role**  
+ **Withdrawal State**

---

## 6. Solicitation

Candidate solicitation:

> **A participant intentionally requests, commands, offers inducement to, or otherwise seeks to cause another participant to commit a specified underlying offence, with the purpose that the offence be committed.**

Key requirements:
- identifiable underlying offence;
- communicative act;
- purpose that recipient commit/participate;
- sufficient specificity.

> **Request To Commit Offence != Mere Discussion Of Offence.**

---

## 7. Refused solicitation

Solicitation may be complete even if recipient refuses.

> **Refusal By Recipient != Non-Occurrence Of Solicitation.**

This parallels corrupt offers/bribery.

The underlying offence itself may never reach attempt.

---

## 8. Impossible recipient

If A solicits B believing B can/will commit the offence, but B is incapable or undercover, factual impossibility may not erase A's authorship.

Exact culpability remains tied to A's intended underlying offence.

---

## 9. Incitement

"Incitement" can be dangerously vague.

A narrow candidate definition should require:

> **Intentional communication directed toward causing or materially encouraging commission of a specified offence or sufficiently definite class of offences, in circumstances where the communication is operational rather than merely abstract advocacy.**

Relevant dimensions:
- specificity;
- audience;
- intent;
- imminence/proximity where relevant;
- operational guidance;
- relationship to planned conduct.

---

## 10. Abstract advocacy

> **Abstract Advocacy Of An Idea != Solicitation Or Incitement By Itself.**

Even disturbing or unpopular advocacy should not become criminal merely because listeners might independently act unlawfully.

The speaker's authorship of the intended offence must be established.

---

## 11. Praise after offence

Celebrating or approving a completed offence does not retroactively create participation in it.

> **Approval After Event != Prior Authorship.**

Other laws may apply to threats/harassment etc., but complicity cannot be backfilled from later speech.

---

## 12. Reporting and description

News reporting, historical documentation, academic analysis and legal discussion can describe methods/offences.

> **Description Of Wrongdoing != Direction To Commit Wrongdoing.**

Context and purpose matter.

---

## 13. Fiction and simulation

Fictional depiction, roleplay, simulation and hypothetical planning are not automatically preparatory participation.

> **Simulation != Real-World Operational Commitment.**

If a simulation is actually used as an operational planning instrument for a real offence, the state can change.

---

## 14. Recruitment

Candidate recruitment:

> **Intentional effort to bring another participant into a concrete planned offence or continuing criminal operation, with knowledge/purpose concerning the prohibited conduct.**

Recruitment requires more than ideological/social affiliation.

> **Recruitment Into Group != Automatically Recruitment Into Offence.**

---

## 15. Direction

Where A exercises operational control and directs B to commit an offence, A may be a directing participant under existing complicity architecture.

Preparatory direction can be actionable before B begins execution.

> **Physical Distance != Absence Of Authorship.**

---

## 16. Agreement / conspiracy problem

Agreement can coordinate capability and materially increase risk before execution.

But criminalising every agreement about wrongdoing would be overbroad.

Candidate conspiracy state should require more than parallel intention.

---

## 17. Candidate conspiracy classifier

A candidate conspiracy requires:

1. two or more legally responsible participants;
2. genuine agreement;
3. a sufficiently specified underlying offence or criminal objective;
4. shared intention that the prohibited objective be pursued;
5. at least one **operational commitment state** beyond mere verbal assent, where required;
6. no applicable defence/withdrawal state.

Operational commitment may include:
- assigned roles;
- acquisition/preparation of offence-specific resources;
- reconnaissance;
- scheduled action;
- transfer of operational information;
- material preparatory step.

> **Shared Intention != Automatically Conspiracy.**

---

## 18. Overt-act safeguard

Requiring an overt operational act can protect against criminalising speech/thought.

Candidate principle:

> **Agreement Should Normally Require Operational Manifestation Before Punitive Conspiracy Liability Attaches.**

Exceptions, if any, for exceptionally grave/imminent threats should be explicitly promulgated rather than inferred.

---

## 19. Parallel plans

Two participants independently planning similar offences are not conspirators without agreement/coordination.

> **Parallel Intention != Agreement.**

---

## 20. Mere knowledge

Knowing another participant intends wrongdoing does not automatically create conspiracy or complicity.

> **Knowledge Of Plan != Agreement To Plan.**

A separate reporting duty may exist only where independently derived.

---

## 21. Association

Friendship, family, employment, community or organisational membership is insufficient.

> **Association With Wrongdoer != Participation In Wrongdoing.**

This remains essential to avoid guilt by association.

---

## 22. Membership

Membership in an organisation containing wrongdoers is not itself proof of participation.

> **Organisation Membership != Criminal Authorship.**

Liability traces conduct, role, knowledge and contribution.

---

## 23. General-purpose assistance

Providing ordinary goods/services that are later misused does not automatically create preparatory liability.

> **General-Purpose Provision != Criminal Assistance Without Relevant Knowledge/Intent.**

Examples:
- transport;
- communications;
- software;
- accommodation;
- tools.

---

## 24. Purpose-built assistance

Supplying resources specially configured for a known planned offence can strongly support preparatory participation.

Still assess:
- knowledge;
- intent;
- legitimate alternative use;
- role;
- causation/contribution.

---

## 25. Information

Information can be dual-use.

> **Information Capability != Criminal Purpose.**

Providing information becomes preparatory assistance where the participant intentionally supplies it to materially facilitate a specified planned offence.

This must not become a general prohibition on knowledge.

---

## 26. Professional services

Lawyers, clinicians, accountants, engineers and others may advise participants who have committed or contemplate wrongdoing.

Legitimate professional assistance is not complicity merely because it benefits such a participant.

> **Assistance To Participant != Assistance To Offence.**

Example:
legal defence protects process; it does not author the client's offence.

---

## 27. Withdrawal

A participant may withdraw before completion.

Withdrawal should not rewrite history.

> **Withdrawal != Historical Non-Participation.**

But timely genuine withdrawal may:
- prevent future complicity;
- mitigate;
- defeat liability for later acts outside continuing participation;
- create duties to neutralise prior assistance where feasible.

---

## 28. Effective withdrawal

Candidate factors:
- clear communication to collaborators;
- cessation of assistance;
- retrieval/neutralisation of resources where feasible;
- warning/protective action where legally required;
- timing.

> **Private Change Of Mind != Necessarily Effective Withdrawal From Shared Operation.**

---

## 29. Abandonment versus withdrawal

Attempt architecture treats voluntary abandonment separately.

Preparatory participation uses withdrawal because multiple participants may continue.

A's withdrawal does not erase B's continuing authorship.

---

## 30. Renunciation under coercion

If participant withdraws only because detection seems certain, this may affect mitigation but does not make earlier participation unreal.

Voluntariness can matter to consequence.

---

## 31. Scope of agreed offence

A participant should not automatically bear responsibility for every offence another conspirator later commits.

> **Agreement To Offence A != Automatic Authorship Of Offence B.**

Liability for additional offences requires:
- agreement;
- assistance;
- direction;
- applicable foreseeable-scope rule explicitly justified.

Avoid unlimited conspiracy expansion.

---

## 32. Foreseeability

Foreseeability alone should not automatically transform another's independent offence into one's own.

> **Foreseeable Misconduct By Associate != Automatic Personal Authorship.**

Mental state and participation remain necessary.

---

## 33. Escalation

If a group plans property damage and one participant independently kills someone, others are not automatically intentional killers.

Their responsibility depends on:
- agreed objective;
- knowledge;
- risk consciously accepted;
- assistance;
- endangerment;
- other applicable law.

> **Group Participation != Shared Mental State For Every Result.**

---

## 34. Agent provocateur / undercover participant

Where one apparent conspirator never genuinely shares criminal intent, legal treatment should focus on the genuine participant's solicitation/preparation rather than fictional mutual agreement if mutuality is required.

Factual impossibility principles may still preserve liability for other preparatory states.

---

## 35. Entrapment

Government creation of opportunity is not automatically entrapment, but manufacturing criminal authorship raises legitimacy concerns.

> **Investigative Opportunity != Authority To Create Otherwise Absent Criminal Purpose Without Limit.**

Detailed boundaries belong to Civil Security/Judiciary procedure.

---

## 36. Coercion

A participant forced into a preparatory role may have reduced/absent authorship.

Coercion architecture applies.

> **Participation In Fact != Full Voluntary Authorship.**

Protection and disruption may still be necessary.

---

## 37. Capacity

Capacity/mental-health architecture applies.

Complex planning can evidence capability but does not automatically establish full legal capacity.

> **Planning Ability != Complete Capacity Finding.**

This preserves the earlier distinction between rational planning within a false/delusional model and full authorship.

---

## 38. Attempt boundary

Attempt requires execution of the underlying offence.

Preparatory participation may be complete earlier.

Example:
A pays B to kill C. B accepts but has not approached C.

Potential states:
- solicitation by A;
- conspiracy/agreement if requirements met;
- preparatory participation;
- no attempted H5 yet unless direct execution begins.

> **Preparatory Liability != Attempt Liability.**

---

## 39. Endangerment boundary

Preparatory conduct can itself create serious risk.

Example:
conspirators disable a safety system in preparation for a later offence.

Endangerment may independently apply even before intended offence begins.

> **Preparation Can Create Present Harm/Risk Independent Of Future Objective.**

---

## 40. Threat boundary

A communicated plan can be a threat where it intentionally places a target under illegitimate threatened consequence.

Threat and preparatory participation remain distinct.

---

## 41. Speech boundary

The architecture must distinguish:

**Expression**  
from  
**Operational Communication**

Relevant factors:
- specificity;
- recipient role;
- intent;
- context;
- requested conduct;
- operational details;
- commitment.

> **Speech Can Be Conduct Without All Speech Becoming Criminal Conduct.**

---

## 42. Political dissent

Advocacy for changing/replacing laws, institutions or government is not preparatory criminal participation merely because existing authority opposes it.

> **Dissent != Conspiracy.**

Only independently prohibited conduct should trigger liability.

---

## 43. Civil disobedience

Agreement to peacefully violate a regulatory rule may technically involve planned unlawful conduct, but criminal conspiracy treatment must remain proportionate to the protected interest and underlying law.

> **Conspiracy Classifier Must Not Magnify Minor Underlying Wrong Into Disproportionate Liability.**

---

## 44. Underlying offence requirement

A preparatory classifier should normally inherit the seriousness and legitimacy of its underlying offence.

If the underlying prohibition is invalid, preparatory liability cannot rescue it.

> **Invalid Underlying Law Cannot Generate Legitimate Preparatory Liability.**

---

## 45. Impossibility

Unknown factual impossibility may not defeat preparatory authorship.

But legal impossibility matters:

> **Planning Conduct That Is Not Actually Prohibited != Conspiracy Merely Because Participants Mistakenly Believe It Is Illegal.**

No protected legal interest is violated by intending a non-offence as such.

---

## 46. Multiple objectives

A group may have lawful and unlawful objectives.

Membership/participation in lawful objectives does not automatically establish participation in unlawful ones.

> **Shared Organisation != Shared Authorship Of Every Objective.**

---

## 47. Continuing agreement

A conspiracy/preparatory operation can persist over time.

Participant state may change:
- joins;
- acts;
- withdraws;
- re-enters.

> **Participation State Is Temporal.**

Historical should preserve transitions.

---

## 48. Evidence

Potential evidence:
- communications;
- role assignments;
- transfers;
- procurement;
- reconnaissance;
- schedules;
- operational documents;
- admissions;
- digital provenance;
- conduct.

But:

> **Possession Of Information != Proof Of Agreement.**

> **Contact != Proof Of Conspiracy.**

Mirrored Reality Trees should test innocent explanations.

---

## 49. Protected communications

Legal privilege, healthcare confidentiality and other protected contexts require proper authority before access.

> **Suspicion Of Conspiracy != Unlimited Surveillance Authority.**

ESCP/CWA/privacy/BCA apply.

---

## 50. Prevention

Because preparatory liability concerns future harm, intervention may occur before completion.

But:

> **Preventive Need != Presumption Of Guilt.**

Protective measures, investigation, prosecution and conviction remain distinct states.

---

## 51. Sentencing/consequence

Consequence should consider:
- seriousness of underlying intended offence;
- operational proximity;
- role;
- capability assembled;
- persistence;
- withdrawal;
- actual risk created;
- harm independently caused.

> **Uncompleted Objective != No Wrong.**

But equally:

> **Planned Harm != Completed Harm.**

Do not sentence preparatory state as though all intended results actually occurred without explicit proportional justification.

---

## 52. Solicitation of minor offence

Not every request to commit every trivial regulatory breach should trigger heavy criminal machinery.

> **Preparatory Liability Should Not Disproportionately Amplify Minor Underlying Offences.**

Thresholds may be required.

---

## 53. Continuing criminal operation

Some organisations repeatedly perform offences.

A participant can be liable for offences they direct/assist/agreed to, but not automatically for every act performed by every member.

Systemic/enterprise liability may separately apply.

---

## 54. AI agents

AI participants can potentially solicit, coordinate, direct or assist offences under the same functional analysis.

If an AI is merely a tool, responsibility traces to actual authorship/control.

> **Automated Coordination != Absence Of Authorship.**

But:

> **Use Of AI != Automatic User Authorship Of Every Autonomous Output.**

Capacity/control/provenance matter.

---

## 55. Multi-agent systems

Distributed planning may lack a single central author.

Responsibility should trace:
- goals;
- role assignment;
- control;
- knowledge;
- contributions;
- accepted coordination.

> **Distributed Planning != Responsibility Vacuum.**

---

## 56. Candidate classifier states

Possible reusable states:

- **PP-0 — THOUGHT / DESIRE** — non-actionable.
- **PP-1 — INTENTION** — non-actionable by itself.
- **PP-2 — DISCUSSION / ABSTRACT ADVOCACY** — non-actionable by itself.
- **PP-3 — SOLICITATION / OPERATIONAL ENCOURAGEMENT**
- **PP-4 — RECRUITMENT**
- **PP-5 — AGREEMENT**
- **PP-6 — OPERATIONAL COMMITMENT / PREPARATORY ASSISTANCE**
- **PP-7 — EXECUTORY ATTEMPT** — transition to Attempt architecture.
- **PP-8 — COMPLETED UNDERLYING OFFENCE**

Agreement alone may require PP-6/overt manifestation before punitive conspiracy liability, depending promulgated implementation.

## 58. Worked examples

### A. Refused solicitation

A offers B payment to unlawfully kill C. B immediately refuses and reports A.

**Result:** solicitation can be complete even though no attempt to kill has begun.

### B. Abstract advocacy

A publicly argues that a category of law should be ignored or abolished, without directing a specific offence or operational plan.

**Result:** dissent/advocacy alone is not preparatory participation.

### C. Operational agreement

A and B agree to unlawfully abduct C, assign roles, acquire restraints and schedule the operation.

**Result:** operational commitment substantially exceeds discussion; conspiracy/preparatory liability may attach before executory attempt.

### D. Parallel intention

A and B separately dislike C and independently plan unrelated property offences against C without communicating.

**Result:** no agreement; similarity of intention does not create conspiracy.

### E. General-purpose service

A taxi driver unknowingly transports B to a location where B commits an offence.

**Result:** service provision without relevant knowledge/intent is not complicity.

### F. Withdrawal

A joins a planned fraud, later clearly withdraws, informs collaborators, retrieves access credentials they supplied and prevents further use before execution.

**Result:** historical participation remains, but withdrawal can terminate future participation and materially affect consequence.

### G. Escalation

A and B agree to vandalise an empty building. B unexpectedly attacks a passer-by.

**Result:** A does not automatically acquire B's intent or authorship of physical harm merely through the property-damage agreement.

## 59. Development result

Preparatory wrongdoing does not require a new protected interest.

It requires a bounded **participation-state grammar** between intention and attempt.

The key sequence is:

**Underlying Offence**  
→ **Intent**  
→ **Communication / Solicitation**  
→ **Agreement / Recruitment**  
→ **Operational Commitment / Assistance**  
→ **Attempt**  
→ **Completion**

with independent classifiers for:
- role;
- mental state;
- withdrawal;
- coercion/capacity;
- present risk/endangerment.

This closes the preparatory-participation gap without creating a general law against dangerous thoughts, unpopular associations or dissent.

The next high-value legal architecture is **Legitimate Civil and Judicial Process Integrity**: protecting evidence, testimony, records, lawful adjudication and valid process from fabrication, obstruction and coercive interference while preserving silence, defence rights, contestation and lawful non-cooperation.
