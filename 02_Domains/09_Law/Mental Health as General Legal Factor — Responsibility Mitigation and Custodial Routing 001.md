# Mental Health as General Legal Factor — Responsibility, Mitigation and Custodial Routing 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 7 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CANDIDATE GENERAL LEGAL ARCHITECTURE / NON-CANONICAL

---

## 1. Purpose

Mental-health considerations should not be independently rewritten into every Concord offence.

They are a general legal factor capable of applying across offence families where relevant.

The architecture therefore separates:

**Offence Definition**  
from  
**Mental-Health / Capacity State**  
from  
**Responsibility / Mitigation**  
from  
**Sentence Duration**  
from  
**Custodial Destination / Conditions**  
from  
**Treatment**

This prevents offence law from becoming cluttered with repeated mental-health caveats and prevents diagnosis from automatically determining liability or sentence.

---

## 2. General factor, not offence element

Unless a particular offence inherently requires a specific mental/capacity condition, mental health should not normally appear inside the offence definition.

Instead:

**Base Offence + Ordinary Classifiers + General Mental-Health Factor Where Relevant**

Therefore:

> **Mental-Health Relevance Should Be Reusable Across Law Rather Than Rewritten Into Every Offence.**

The offence remains the same offence.

---

## 3. Diagnosis is not automatic mitigation

A diagnosis, condition or treatment history does not itself establish reduced culpability.

The relevant question is its functional effect at the legally relevant time.

Candidate chain:

**Mental-Health Evidence**  
→ **Functional Effect**  
→ **Relevant Capacity / Authorship Effect**  
→ **Responsibility / Mitigation Assessment**

Therefore:

> **Mental Illness != Automatic Mitigation.**

> **Diagnosis != Reduced Responsibility.**

> **Diagnostic Label != Functional Legal State.**

---

## 4. Not an automatic exoneration

Mental illness should not operate as a general mechanism by which established offending disappears.

Where the offence elements are established, the legal system should preserve what occurred.

A complete defence or absence of legally sufficient responsibility, if later recognised for a particular capacity state, requires its own defined threshold and evidence.

Therefore:

> **Mental Illness != Automatic Exoneration.**

> **Mental Illness != Automatic Absence Of Responsibility.**

This document does not settle the exceptional threshold at which capacity impairment may defeat ordinary punitive responsibility. That requires separate careful development.

---

## 5. Mitigation requires later calibration

Mental-health impairment may reduce culpability where it materially affected legally relevant capacity, authorship, understanding, reality assessment or behavioural control.

However the amount by which this should alter sentence severity or duration should not be assumed in advance.

> **Mental-Health Mitigation != Automatic Sentence Reduction Formula.**

The interaction among:
- degree of impairment;
- intent;
- knowledge;
- premeditation;
- actual harm;
- intended harm;
- foreseeability;
- aggravation;
- mitigation;
- treatment response;
- present danger

requires later sentencing-specific examination.

The current architecture therefore recognises **possible mitigation** while deliberately leaving sentence calibration unresolved.

---

## 6. Custodial sentence and custodial destination are separate

Where a custodial sentence is independently justified, a material mental-health condition may make ordinary prison/general custody an inappropriate environment.

The architecture should distinguish:

**Custodial Authority / Sentence**  
from  
**Custodial Placement / Environment**

Therefore:

> **Sentence Duration != Custodial Destination.**

A participant can remain subject to a lawful custodial sentence while being held in a mental-health custodial environment rather than ordinary prison custody.

This is not release from custody.

> **Mental-Health Custody != Freedom From Sentence.**

---

## 7. Candidate custodial routing rule

Where:

1. a custodial sentence is lawfully imposed;
2. a clinically and legally relevant mental-health condition is established;
3. appropriate treatment, safety or functioning cannot adequately be provided in ordinary custody; and
4. mental-health custody can provide the required secure and therapeutic environment;

the custodial sentence may be served, wholly or partly, within an appropriate mental-health custodial setting.

The exact clinical/legal threshold requires Health-Law-Judiciary development.

---

## 8. Treatment and punishment remain distinct

Mental-health custody may simultaneously provide:
- lawful custody;
- protection;
- clinical assessment;
- treatment;
- rehabilitation.

But the authority for each function should remain identifiable.

> **Custody Authority != Treatment Authority.**

> **Punishment != Treatment.**

> **Treatment Need != Additional Punitive Authority.**

Where treatment requires consent or exceptional compulsory authority, the relevant Health / Mental Health Exceptional Authority rules remain applicable.

---

## 9. Transfer between custodial environments

Mental-health state may change during a sentence.

A participant may:
- enter ordinary custody and later require mental-health custody;
- begin in mental-health custody and later become suitable for ordinary custody;
- require changing levels of clinical security.

Transfer should therefore be based on current evidence and appropriate review rather than treating initial placement as permanent.

> **Initial Custodial Placement != Permanent Custodial State.**

A transfer changes the environment in which custody is served; it does not by itself erase or extend the underlying sentence.

---

## 10. Sentence expiry and continuing mental-health risk

A difficult boundary occurs if the custodial sentence ends while a serious mental-health-related protective need remains.

The sentence cannot simply be extended by relabelling punishment as treatment.

> **End Of Punitive Authority != Automatic End Of Every Protective Authority.**

but equally:

> **Continuing Treatment Need != Automatic Extension Of Sentence.**

Any post-sentence compulsory detention must satisfy an independent mental-health/protective authority threshold applicable to participants generally, not a lower threshold created merely because the participant was previously convicted.

This preserves the distinction between punishment and protective health authority.

---

## 11. Recovery before sentence expiry

Conversely, successful treatment before the custodial sentence expires does not automatically extinguish independently justified punitive custody.

The participant may become suitable for transfer from mental-health custody to ordinary custody, or another lawful custodial form.

> **Clinical Recovery != Automatic Sentence Expiry.**

Whether recovery should affect parole, review or mitigation is a later sentencing-policy question.

---

## 11A. Post-sentence handoff to existing Mental Health authority

Where a custodial sentence expires, the criminal/punitive authority created by that sentence expires with it.

If the participant is still considered to present a serious mental-health-related danger, continuing detention must not be justified by extending, relabelling or informally continuing the expired sentence.

Instead, the case passes to the **existing Concord Mental Health Exceptional Authority architecture**.

Candidate handoff:

**Custodial Sentence Active**  
→ **Sentence Authority Governs Custody**  
→ **Sentence Expires**  
→ **Punitive Authority Terminates**  
→ **Independent Mental-Health Authority Test**  
→ **If Existing Threshold Is Satisfied: Mental-Health BCA Governs**  
→ **If Threshold Is Not Satisfied: No Continuing Detention Authority From The Expired Sentence**

The existing Mental Health architecture retains its own safeguards, including its established requirements concerning legitimate purpose, necessity, proportionality, bounded authority, review and termination/re-justification. Law should not create a weaker post-conviction version of those safeguards.

A previous offence, sentence, custodial history or evidence arising from them may remain relevant evidence when assessing current state and risk. They do not themselves supply present authority.

> **Past Conviction != Continuing Detention Authority.**

> **Expired Sentence != Residual Punitive Authority.**

> **Criminal History May Inform Evidence; It Does Not Lower The Mental-Health Authority Threshold.**

> **Sentence Expiry != Automatic Release From Independently Justified Mental-Health Authority.**

> **Mental-Health Authority After Sentence Requires Independent Present Justification.**

The participant should not become a permanently exceptional legal class merely because criminal authority once applied.

Once punitive authority ends, any continuing mental-health intervention must be justified through the same applicable Mental Health architecture and safeguards used to determine legitimate intervention for participants generally.

### Incorporation requirement

This handoff should be incorporated into the authoritative Mental Health architecture when that domain is next developed or consolidated.

The Mental Health-side incorporation should explicitly recognise:

1. receipt of a participant at expiry of criminal custodial authority;
2. no automatic continuation of detention;
3. independent application of the ordinary Mental Health Exceptional Authority threshold;
4. criminal/custodial history as potentially relevant evidence but not an authority source;
5. no lowered threshold because of prior conviction;
6. ordinary Mental Health safeguards, review and sunset;
7. clear provenance showing that the continuing authority is mental-health/protective authority rather than residual punishment.

This Law document records the interface requirement; it does not duplicate or replace the Mental Health authority architecture.

---

## 12. Aggravation and mitigation can coexist

A case may establish:
- deliberate intent;
- premeditation;
- serious harm;
- substantial mental-health impairment;
- impaired but legally sufficient capacity.

The mental-health factor does not erase aggravating facts, and aggravating facts do not erase genuine impairment.

> **Aggravation And Mental-Health Mitigation May Coexist.**

Their interaction should be reasoned explicitly rather than mechanically cancelled.

---

## 13. Evidence and decision roles

Health expertise should establish or inform:
- diagnosis where relevant;
- functional impairment;
- treatment needs;
- clinical risk factors;
- suitability of custodial environments.

Law should define:
- legal relevance;
- capacity thresholds;
- mitigation rules;
- custodial authority;
- transfer authority.

Judiciary should determine contested legal application.

Therefore:

> **Clinical Finding != Legal Sentence.**

> **Judicial Finding != Clinical Diagnosis.**

The two systems interface without substituting for one another.

## 15. Open questions

Later development should determine:

1. which functional impairments qualify as mitigation;
2. whether and when impairment changes sentence duration;
3. whether any impairment threshold defeats ordinary punitive responsibility entirely;
4. clinical/legal thresholds for routing into mental-health custody;
5. standards for transfer between ordinary and mental-health custody;
6. treatment consent within lawful custody;
7. relationship between sentence review, recovery and rehabilitation;
8. post-sentence protective detention thresholds;
9. safeguards against mental-health custody becoming indefinite punishment;
10. safeguards against diagnosis being used strategically either to increase or evade punishment;
11. substrate-neutral equivalents for non-biological participants.

---

## 16. Development result

Mental health is best represented as a **general cross-offence legal factor**, not as a repeated caveat embedded in each law.

Its immediate architectural effects are:

**possible responsibility/mitigation relevance**  
plus  
**custodial-routing relevance where custody is independently justified.**

The degree of sentence mitigation remains deliberately unresolved pending dedicated sentencing development.
