# Compelled Participant Evidence — Scenario Stress Test 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Domain:** Law / Judiciary / Civil Security
**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / COMPELLED-EVIDENCE STRESS TEST / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Test purpose

This test challenges **Compelled Participant Evidence — Substrate-Neutral Classification and Authority Boundaries 001** against concrete biological, digital and hybrid cases.

It tests:

- whether C1-C9 remain usable when categories overlap;
- whether functionally equivalent participant states receive equivalent protection across substrates;
- whether right to silence is accidentally converted into immunity from independent investigation;
- whether search authority becomes a backdoor into cognition;
- whether compelled access and compelled expression can be distinguished;
- whether evidential necessity remains bounded;
- whether technical ease of access creates unjustified authority.

The test does **not** yet assign final legal thresholds to every class.

---

## 2. Scenario A — human asked “where were you?”

A detained human participant is asked where they were at the time of an offence. Their answer may incriminate them.

### Classification

C1 — Compelled Expression.

### Analysis

The state is seeking knowledge from participant memory.

The information does not become state-owned merely because it is evidentially useful.

Independent CCTV, witnesses or records remain separately investigable.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Right To Silence Protects Refusal To Supply Cognition; It Does Not Delete External Evidence.**

## 3. Scenario B — human face observed in public CCTV

A participant walks through a legitimately monitored public transport area. Existing footage records their face.

### Classification

Primarily C2 — External Observation.

### Analysis

No participant expression is compelled.

This does not mean all facial surveillance, retention or reuse is automatically legitimate. Observation authority, purpose, retention and later analysis remain independently bounded.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Non-Testimonial Observation Still Requires Legitimate Observation Authority.**

---

## 4. Scenario C — compelled fingerprint

A participant is required to provide a fingerprint for comparison with a print recovered from a relevant scene.

### Classification

C3/C4 collision — compelled presentation plus physical/biometric extraction.

### Analysis

The act requires participant cooperation but ordinarily does not require disclosure of remembered knowledge.

It nevertheless constrains bodily autonomy and creates an evidential biometric object.

The authority must therefore be justified independently of the right-to-silence question.

### Result

**PASS WITH MULTI-CLASS REFINEMENT.**

**One Evidential Act May Occupy Multiple Compulsion Classes Simultaneously.**

Classification should record all materially engaged classes rather than force one exclusive label.

## 5. Scenario D — DNA identity comparison becomes health analysis

A DNA sample is legitimately obtained for comparison with biological material from a scene. The evidence system later analyses disease predisposition without new authority.

### Classification

C4 acquisition followed by unauthorised secondary information use.

### Analysis

The later analysis exceeds the purpose that justified collection.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Lawful Acquisition != Universal Analytical Authority.**

The BCA purpose boundary survives.

## 6. Scenario E — participant asked to state memorised password

Investigators possess lawful authority to search a device but cannot unlock it. The participant is ordered to speak the password remembered only in cognition.

### Classification

C1 + C6.

### Analysis

The desired endpoint is device access, but the imposed action requires disclosure of remembered knowledge.

Search authority over the device does not automatically establish authority to compel cognitive expression.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Authority Over Destination != Authority Over Every Route To Destination.**

## 7. Scenario F — participant ordered to place finger on biometric unlock sensor

The same device can be unlocked by participant fingerprint. Investigators order the participant to touch the sensor without asking for a remembered secret.

### Classification

C3 + C6, potentially C4 depending on implementation.

### Analysis

This is functionally different from speaking a memorised password even though the practical result is the same.

But the difference does not automatically mean compulsion is lawful.

It means the correct protected-function analysis differs.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Equivalent Access Result != Equivalent Compulsion Act.**

## 8. Scenario G — participant ordered to decrypt without revealing password

The participant is ordered to use a memorised secret internally to decrypt a storage volume and hand over the decrypted result.

### Classification

C6 + C9, with C1 implications because success may attest knowledge/control.

### Analysis

No password is verbally revealed, but the participant is being required to use cognition and processing capability to transform inaccessible evidence into accessible evidence.

The original architecture correctly identifies this as more than ordinary object surrender.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Secret Not Spoken != No Cognitive Compulsion.**

## 9. Scenario H — physical key to locked evidence store

Investigators have independent authority to search a locked store. A physical key is found in the participant's coat during a separately lawful search.

### Classification

The key is an independently existing external object. Its seizure/use is closer to C5/C7 than C1.

### Analysis

The participant is not being required to reveal remembered knowledge.

Authority to use the key still depends on authority to enter/search the protected store.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Possession Of Access Instrument != Authority To Access Destination.**

## 17. Scenario P — AI keeps a private diary file

### Analysis

Private thought-like content can deserve strong privacy without being technically constitutive cognition.

- cognitive state;
- cognitive product;

**PASS WITH TAXONOMIC REFINEMENT.**

## 18. Scenario Q — AI reasoning trace generated automatically

An AI architecture automatically stores internal reasoning traces as part of ordinary operation. Investigators seek them.

### Classification

Potential C8.

### Analysis

Whether the trace is cognitive state, cognitive product, diagnostic telemetry or externally governed audit material cannot be answered solely by file format.

Relevant questions include:

- who/what generates it;
- who controls it;
- whether it is constitutive of ongoing cognition;
- whether it is an external audit obligation known in advance;
- whether it exposes protected internal reasoning beyond the legitimate audit purpose.

### Result

**PASS WITH PROVENANCE/FUNCTION REQUIREMENT.**

**File Type != Evidential/Cognitive Classification.**

## 26. Scenario Y — emergency location disclosure

A participant is believed to know the exact location of an abducted child facing immediate serious harm. Investigators demand the location.

### Classification

C1, with a strong immediate Non-Harm conflict.

### Analysis

The ordinary right-to-silence presumption and the immediate protection of another participant collide.

The current architecture correctly says an exceptional authority would have to be separately established; it does not yet define that threshold.

The exception cannot be silently derived from evidential usefulness alone.

### Result

**BOUNDARY IDENTIFIED — REQUIRES SEPARATE EMERGENCY COMPELLED-COMMUNICATION RULE.**

Necessary safeguards likely include:

- serious/imminent harm;
- material likelihood participant possesses the information;
- necessity;
- absence/inadequacy of less intrusive timely routes;
- minimum necessary question/scope;
- provenance;
- immediate review where practicable and retrospective review where not;
- strict prohibition on converting emergency authority into general interrogation authority.

**Emergency Protective Need != General Evidential Compulsion Authority.**

## 39. Cross-scenario findings

### 39.1 C1-C9 should be multi-label, not exclusive

Several acts materially engage multiple classes.

Example:

**memorised password disclosure = C1 + C6**

**biometric unlock = C3 + C6 (+ possibly C4)**

**AI memory snapshot = C8 + potentially C7**

**forced memory search = C9 + C1**

Therefore:

> **Compelled-evidence classification should describe all materially engaged functions rather than force a single exclusive category.**

### 39.2 The access destination and access route are separate authority objects

A valid authority to search a destination does not automatically authorise every means of gaining entry.

**Authority Over Destination != Authority Over Every Route To Destination**

This is a direct BCA application.

### 39.3 Cognitive protection needs its own wrapper

The tests repeatedly encounter a domain that is neither ordinary private data nor ordinary testimony:

**participant cognitive/internal state.**

It can exist:

- biologically;
- in neural prostheses;
- in local AI state;
- remotely;
- across hybrid systems;
- under third-party technical custody.

The likely architecture is a **Cognitive Protected Space** wrapper.

### 39.4 Cognitive state, cognitive product and private record differ

A useful emerging distinction is:

**Cognitive State** — functionally part of current/retained cognition, memory, reasoning or continuity.

**Cognitive Product** — output produced by cognition, potentially private, but no longer constitutive of cognition.

**Private Record** — participant-controlled information not necessarily generated as cognition.

These may all deserve protection, but not necessarily identical authority thresholds.

### 39.5 Custody location does not define function

Remote/cloud cognitive state can remain cognition.

Third-party technical custody does not automatically convert it into an ordinary third-party business record.

### 39.6 Emergency compelled expression remains genuinely unresolved

A narrow serious/imminent harm case may justify an exceptional rule, but evidential usefulness alone cannot create it.

### 39.7 Copying digital cognition may create identity consequences

For some digital participants, forensic duplication may be civilly more significant than copying a document.

The evidence architecture must interface with identity/continuity/duplication architecture before treating full-state copying as routine.

## 41. Test conclusion

**OVERALL RESULT: PASS WITH TWO MAJOR DOWNSTREAM ARCHITECTURES AND FOUR REFINEMENTS.**

The nine-class model survives concrete human, AI and hybrid testing.

Required refinements:

1. **multi-label classification** rather than mutually exclusive C1-C9;
2. **destination authority and route authority** must be separately represented;
3. distinguish **cognitive state, cognitive product and private record**;
4. explicitly preserve functional classification across remote/third-party custody.

Two major downstream architectures are now exposed:

### A. Cognitive Protected Space

A substrate-neutral contextual wrapper for participant cognition/internal state, including biological, digital, prosthetic, remote and hybrid forms.

### B. Exceptional Emergency Compelled Communication

A narrowly bounded rule, if ethically justified, for serious/imminent harm cases where protected participant knowledge may be necessary to prevent severe harm.

A further interface is required with identity/continuity architecture where forensic copying of digital state could create another participant/instance.

The strongest stress-test result is:

> **A civilisation must not gain greater access to a participant's mind merely because that participant's cognition happens to be technically inspectable.**
