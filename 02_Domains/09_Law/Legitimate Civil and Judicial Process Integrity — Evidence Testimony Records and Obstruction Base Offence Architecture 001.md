## 1. Purpose

This document derives candidate law protecting legitimate civil and judicial processes from deliberate corruption.

It addresses conduct conventionally described as:
- perjury;
- evidence fabrication;
- evidence tampering;
- destruction/concealment of evidence where a preservation duty exists;
- witness intimidation/corruption;
- obstruction of justice;
- corrupt interference with adjudication;
- falsification of official/process records;
- deliberate evasion of valid process duties.

The protected interest is not institutional convenience, obedience, conviction rate or protection of official prestige.

It is:

> **The Integrity Of Legitimate Truth-Seeking, Rights-Protecting And Decision-Making Civil Process.**

## 3. Foundational dependency

Process-integrity law is legitimate only where the protected process is itself legitimate.

> **No Legitimate Process → No Legitimate Process-Protection Offence.**

A participant should not be punished for refusing to assist an unlawful process merely because an institution labels its demand official.

---

## 4. Protected process

A protected process may include:
- judicial adjudication;
- lawful investigation;
- prosecution/defence;
- civil dispute resolution;
- administrative adjudication;
- authorised audit;
- election/governance process;
- regulatory decision;
- legally required record process.

Protection level depends on function and legal status.

---

## 5. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not intentionally or knowingly fabricate, falsify, corrupt, conceal, destroy, improperly alter, coercively influence, or otherwise materially interfere with evidence, testimony, records, participants or required functions of a legitimate civil/judicial process where the conduct is capable of materially corrupting that process or its resulting legal state.**

Negligent record/process failures may require regulatory/professional treatment where a specific duty exists.

---

## 6. Process integrity versus outcome

> **Process Integrity != Desired Outcome.**

A process remains legitimate only if it can reach an outcome contrary to the preference of:
- police;
- prosecution;
- government;
- complainant;
- institution;
- majority.

Protecting process therefore includes protecting acquittal, dismissal, correction and successful challenge.

---

## 7. Investigation, prosecution and guilt

Existing Legal Classification Grammar applies:

> **Investigation != Prosecution.**

> **Prosecution != Guilt.**

Process-protection law must not collapse these states.

---

## 8. Evidence integrity

Existing Judiciary recovery architecture distinguishes:

**Reality/Event → Observation → Recorded Evidence → Transformation/Derivative → Interpretation → Investigative Hypothesis → Prosecution Assessment → Judicial Finding → Historical Record**

Therefore:

> **Evidence != Event.**

> **Interpretation != Evidence.**

> **Hypothesis != Finding.**

Process offences can occur by deliberately corrupting these distinctions.

---

## 9. Evidence fabrication

Candidate fabrication:

> **Intentional creation, alteration or presentation of material purporting to be evidence of a fact/event/state while knowingly making its evidential representation materially false.**

Examples:
- staged physical evidence;
- fabricated logs;
- altered timestamps;
- synthetic media represented as authentic observation;
- invented laboratory result.

---

## 10. Synthetic evidence

AI-generated/synthetic material is not inherently unlawful.

> **Synthetic Material != Fabricated Evidence Unless Falsely Represented Or Used To Corrupt Evidential State.**

Simulation/reconstruction can be legitimate if clearly identified.

---

## 11. Derived evidence

Derived evidence must retain provenance.

> **Derived Evidence Must Not Be Represented As Raw Observation.**

Loss/concealment of transformation history can materially corrupt process.

---

## 12. Evidence alteration

Not every modification is tampering.

Legitimate:
- forensic processing;
- redaction;
- format conversion;
- enhancement;
- annotation.

But provenance must preserve what occurred.

> **Transformation != Falsification Where Transformation Is Legible.**

---

## 13. Evidence destruction

Destroying material becomes process interference where:
- preservation duty exists;
- participant knows/should know as required by law;
- destruction materially frustrates process.

> **Destruction Of Information != Automatically Obstruction.**

Ordinary deletion before any preservation duty or foreseeable protected process should not automatically become criminal.

---

## 14. Preservation duty

Positive Duties/Omissions architecture applies.

Potential sources:
- litigation hold;
- lawful evidence seizure/custody;
- professional record duty;
- active investigation notice;
- statutory retention requirement.

> **Potential Future Relevance != Unlimited Preservation Duty.**

---

## 15. Evidence concealment

Concealment can be unlawful where a participant has a duty to disclose/preserve/produce.

But:

> **Possession Of Evidence != Automatic Duty To Volunteer It.**

Privilege, silence, privacy and lawful defence rights remain relevant.

---

## 16. Unlawfully obtained evidence

Existing Concord position:

> **Unlawful Acquisition != Automatic Factual Falsity.**

Evidence can retain factual relevance while unlawful acquisition creates separate liability/remedy and may affect credibility/admissibility/weight according to procedure.

Process-integrity law should not rewrite truth merely to punish misconduct.

---

## 17. Chain of custody

Evidence custody should preserve:
- identity;
- origin;
- handling;
- transformations;
- access;
- transfer;
- integrity.

> **Custody Gap != Automatic Evidence Falsity.**

It affects reliability/weight and may trigger investigation.

Intentional corruption is distinct from imperfect administration.

---

## 18. Perjury

Candidate perjury requires:
1. legitimate formal truth duty;
2. material factual assertion;
3. assertion known by speaker to be false, or other specifically promulgated culpability;
4. assertion made within protected process.

> **False Statement != Automatically Perjury.**

---

## 19. Oath

The ethical wrong does not depend on religious oath.

> **Oath Form != Source Of Truth Duty.**

The duty arises from knowingly entering a legally protected truth-bearing role.

Affirmation or equivalent suffices.

---

## 20. Materiality

Trivial inaccuracies should not become perjury.

> **Immaterial Error != Perjury.**

The falsehood must be capable of materially affecting protected process.

---

## 21. Memory

Human memory is fallible.

> **Incorrect Recollection != Knowing Falsehood.**

Uncertainty should be expressible.

A witness should not be punished for saying "I do not remember" where true.

---

## 22. Opinion

Opinion/inference should be distinguished from claimed fact.

Expert witnesses may offer reasoned opinion within expertise.

> **Disputed Expert Opinion != Perjury Merely Because Another Expert Disagrees.**

Knowing fabrication of underlying data is different.

---

## 23. Correction

A witness who recognises an honest error should be able to correct it.

> **Correction Of Error != Admission Of Prior Perjury.**

Knowing falsehood followed by correction may remain historically relevant, but correction can mitigate/process-repair.

---

## 24. Right to silence

> **Silence != False Testimony.**

A participant exercising a lawful right not to answer does not fabricate evidence.

---

## 25. Self-incrimination

Where law protects against compelled self-incrimination, refusal to provide protected testimony is not obstruction.

> **Exercise Of Defence Right != Obstruction.**

---

## 26. Defence

A defendant may:
- challenge evidence;
- test witnesses;
- require proof;
- offer alternative hypotheses;
- remain silent where permitted;
- appeal.

> **Making Prosecution Prove Its Case != Obstruction.**

---

## 27. Advocate role

An advocate may advance a client's lawful case without personally asserting every client claim as fact.

But an advocate must not knowingly fabricate evidence or knowingly present fabricated evidence as genuine.

> **Advocacy != Personal Adoption Of Every Client Assertion.**

---

## 28. Prosecution role

Prosecution is a fiduciary function for legitimate process, not conviction maximisation.

> **Prosecution Function != Duty To Win.**

Known materially exculpatory evidence should not be suppressed where disclosure duty applies.

---

## 29. Exculpatory evidence

Mirrored Reality Trees require active consideration of inculpatory and exculpatory evidence.

> **Evidence Against Hypothesis Is Still Evidence.**

Deliberate suppression of exculpatory material can corrupt process as seriously as fabrication of inculpatory material.

---

## 30. Police/investigators

Investigators should seek the correct suspect/event model, not merely enough evidence against a selected person.

> **Investigation Function != Construction Of Conviction Narrative.**

Knowingly discarding contradictory evidence to preserve a preferred suspect may constitute process corruption where duties apply.

---

## 31. Witness interference

Candidate prohibited interference includes:
- coercing false testimony;
- bribing testimony;
- threatening witness for truthful participation;
- retaliating for lawful testimony;
- directing destruction/concealment of evidence.

Existing coercion/bribery laws may compose with process integrity.

---

## 32. Witness preparation

Preparing a witness to understand process or recall events is not automatically interference.

> **Witness Preparation != Witness Coaching To Lie.**

The boundary is truth corruption.

---

## 33. Contact with witnesses

Contact is not inherently unlawful.

> **Witness Contact != Witness Tampering.**

Restrictions may be justified in specific proceedings but require lawful basis.

---

## 34. Victim/complainant autonomy

A complainant may have rights concerning participation, privacy and support.

But once a public prosecution legitimately exists, they may not own the entire process.

> **Victim Standing != Ownership Of Prosecution.**

Conversely, prosecution should not erase victim autonomy unnecessarily.

---

## 35. Retaliation

Retaliation against:
- witnesses;
- complainants;
- jurors/adjudicators;
- whistleblowers;
- lawful reporters

can corrupt future process and safe participation.

It may combine process integrity with coercion/harassment/entrusted-authority abuse.

---

## 36. Bribery of process participant

Offering benefit to corrupt:
- witness;
- juror;
- judge;
- investigator;
- expert;
- official

combines bribery/entrusted integrity with process integrity where applicable.

---

## 37. Juror/adjudicator integrity

Neutral adjudicators must decide according to legitimate process and evidence.

External private influence is prohibited where it materially corrupts decision function.

But:

> **Independent Reasoning != Disobedience To Preferred Institutional Outcome.**

---

## 38. Judicial independence

Judicial independence protects decision function from illegitimate external control.

It does not create immunity.

> **Judicial Independence != Judicial Unaccountability.**

Review, appeal, misconduct process and correction remain legitimate.

---

## 39. Obstruction

"Obstruction" should not be a vague catch-all.

Candidate definition requires:
- legitimate protected process;
- material interference;
- required mental state;
- absence of lawful right/justification.

> **Non-Cooperation != Automatically Obstruction.**

---

## 40. Lawful non-cooperation

Examples may include:
- silence;
- refusal absent duty;
- assertion of privilege;
- lawful protest;
- challenge to jurisdiction;
- appeal;
- refusal of unlawful search;
- refusal to consent.

> **Refusal To Consent != Obstruction Where Consent Is Legally Required For The State Action.**

---

## 41. Physical obstruction

Physical interference with lawful execution of process may be actionable where authority is valid and scope clear.

But:

> **Official Demand != Automatically Lawful Order.**

The authority state must be independently testable.

---

## 42. Valid order requirement

No general offence of disobedience should exist.

> **Disobedience Is Not Independently Wrong Merely Because An Authority Issued A Command.**

A process-order offence requires:
- lawful authority;
- jurisdiction;
- applicability;
- notice;
- clarity;
- feasible compliance;
- proportionality.

---

## 43. Contempt

Contempt-like powers should protect actual adjudicative function, not dignity of office.

> **Contempt Power != Authority To Punish Disrespect.**

Conduct must materially interfere with legitimate process or violate a valid specific order.

---

## 44. Criticism of judiciary

Public criticism, satire, disagreement or advocacy for reform is not process obstruction.

> **Criticism Of Court != Contempt By Itself.**

This follows peaceful non-conformity and correction.

---

## 45. Escape from custody

Leaving custody cannot be classified solely by physical fact.

First question:

**Was custody itself lawful and active?**

> **Unlawful Custody Cannot Manufacture A General Duty To Remain Imprisoned.**

Lawful custody may create a specific duty/order not to escape, but exact treatment belongs to procedural/custodial law.

---

## 46. Resisting unlawful authority

> **Resistance To Unlawful Authority != Automatically Obstruction Of Justice.**

Force used may still be independently assessed under physical-harm/self-defence law.

---

## 47. Record falsification

Official/process records carry consequential evidential function.

Intentional material falsification can violate process integrity even before a particular proceeding begins where a legal record duty exists.

Examples:
- custody logs;
- evidence logs;
- inspection records;
- vote records;
- court records.

---

## 48. Record error

> **Record Error != Record Falsification.**

Correction mechanisms should encourage prompt repair rather than incentivise concealment.

---

## 49. Deletion and correction history

Correction should preserve provenance where the historical state matters.

> **Correction != Silent Rewriting Of History.**

Historical and KCS architecture apply.

---

## 50. Election/governance process

Where legitimate voting/governance process exists, deliberate:
- ballot falsification;
- count manipulation;
- eligibility-record corruption;
- coercive vote interference

can be process-integrity offences.

Political persuasion itself is not corruption.

> **Persuasion != Vote Manipulation.**

---

## 51. Administrative process

Applications, licensing, accreditation and benefits systems can have protected decision processes.

Applicant fraud may combine with fraud/process integrity.

Official manipulation may combine with entrusted-authority abuse/discrimination.

---

## 52. Audit

Audit processes can be protected where legitimately authorised.

But:

> **Audit Authority != Unlimited Discovery Authority.**

ESCP/privacy/CWA remain applicable.

---

## 53. Process legitimacy challenge

A participant must be able to challenge:
- jurisdiction;
- authority;
- evidence;
- procedure;
- conflict;
- bias;
- legality.

> **Challenge To Process != Attack On Process Integrity.**

Contestability is part of integrity.

---

## 54. Delay

Delay can obstruct process where intentionally engineered to defeat legitimate function.

But ordinary use of procedural rights can consume time.

> **Delay Result != Automatic Dilatory Misconduct.**

Intent, duty and legitimacy matter.

---

## 55. Resource imbalance

A wealthy/powerful participant may use lawful procedural rights extensively.

The solution to structural unfairness should be fair process design, not arbitrary punishment for exercising rights.

> **Effective Defence Capability != Obstruction.**

---

## 56. Publicity

Public discussion of cases can affect process.

Restrictions require strong justification because expression and transparency matter.

No blanket rule is derived here.

Specific risks:
- witness contamination;
- juror prejudice;
- intimidation;
- privacy harm.

Use least-restrictive mechanisms.

---

## 57. Leaks

Disclosure of process information can be:
- whistleblowing;
- privacy breach;
- evidence interference;
- legitimate transparency;
- entrusted-authority misuse.

> **Leak != Single Legal Category.**

Classify protected interest/context rather than label.

---

## 58. Whistleblowing

Good-faith disclosure of wrongdoing through legitimate routes should not be treated as obstruction merely because it embarrasses an institution.

> **Institutional Embarrassment != Process Harm.**

Protected disclosure architecture may need dedicated implementation.

---

## 59. Destruction to protect privacy

A participant ordinarily deleting private material before any preservation duty arises should not become an obstructor because it later would have interested investigators.

> **Later Investigative Interest != Retroactive Preservation Duty.**

---

## 60. Encryption

Use of encryption is not obstruction by itself.

> **Privacy Technology != Evidence Of Obstruction.**

A valid production/decryption order, if Concord ever recognises such authority, requires separate constitutional/procedural derivation.

---

## 61. Identity and anonymity

Lawful anonymity/pseudonymity is not obstruction merely because it makes investigation harder.

> **Investigative Convenience != General Duty Of Identifiability.**

Fraud/impersonation can separately apply.

---

## 62. AI evidence

AI systems may generate:
- observations;
- classifications;
- reconstructions;
- predictions;
- summaries.

These states must be distinguished.

> **AI Output != Infallible Evidence.**

Model-generated inference should not be represented as direct observation.

---

## 63. AI hallucination/error

An AI error is not automatically fabrication.

Fabrication requires relevant authorship/mental state where criminal culpability is alleged.

Systemic reliability failures may require correction/regulatory action without criminal intent.

---

## 64. Automated record integrity

Systems producing consequential legal records should preserve:
- provenance;
- transformations;
- model/version state where material;
- correction history;
- access/authority state.

This interfaces with Historical/KCS.

---

## 65. Evidence planting by autonomous system

If an autonomous participant knowingly fabricates evidence, ordinary functional rules can apply.

If a non-participant tool produces false output, responsibility traces deployment, knowledge, controls and subsequent representation.

> **Tool Error != Automatic Operator Fabrication.**

---

## 66. Process interference without success

Process corruption may be complete even if detected before affecting final judgment.

> **Failed Corruption Attempt Can Still Be Process Harm.**

Examples:
- fabricated evidence detected;
- bribe refused;
- witness threat reported.

---

## 67. Attempt

Attempt architecture applies where the process-integrity offence requires an unrealised result.

Many forms, such as knowingly submitting fabricated evidence, may already be complete upon submission even if rejected.

---

## 68. Participation

Participation/complicity applies.

But:

> **Employment In Institution With Process Failure != Automatic Personal Liability.**

Trace role, knowledge, duty, authority and contribution.

---

## 69. Organisational responsibility

Institutions may systematically:
- suppress evidence;
- manipulate records;
- pressure witnesses;
- reward biased outcomes.

> **Systemic Process Corruption != Responsibility Vacuum.**

Enterprise/institutional responsibility should coexist with individual attribution.

---

## 70. Aggravation

Potential aggravators:
- entrusted official role;
- repeated/systemic conduct;
- fabrication causing wrongful detention/conviction;
- targeting vulnerable witness;
- retaliation;
- destruction of irreplaceable evidence;
- corruption of multiple dependent cases.

---

## 71. Mitigation

Potential mitigation:
- coercion;
- limited role;
- genuine misunderstanding;
- diminished capacity;
- prompt voluntary correction;
- reliance on defective procedure where reasonable.

Correction does not erase deliberate historical fabrication.

---

## 72. Remedy

Potential remedies:
- exclude/reweight corrupted evidence;
- disclose provenance;
- reopen decision;
- retrial/rehearing;
- release where authority no longer valid;
- correct record;
- restore evidence where possible;
- protect witness;
- compensate;
- sanction;
- investigate systemic dependencies.

> **Punishing Process Corruption != Repairing Corrupted Outcome.**

---

## 73. Dependent decisions

A corrupted evidence source may affect multiple downstream decisions.

Candidate chain:

**Corruption Identified**  
→ **Identify Dependent Findings/Decisions**  
→ **Review Material Dependence**  
→ **Correct/Rehear Where Required**

KCS change-propagation logic is directly relevant.

> **Invalidated Evidence Source != Automatic Invalidity Of Every Dependent Outcome.**

Materiality must be assessed.

---

## 74. Wrongful conviction

Where process corruption materially contributed to wrongful conviction, remedies should address:
- liberty;
- record;
- compensation;
- downstream civil effects;
- responsible misconduct;
- institutional correction.

> **Release != Complete Remedy For Wrongful Conviction.**

---

## 75. Wrongful acquittal / failed process

Process integrity also matters where corruption prevents lawful accountability.

But correction must respect finality/double-jeopardy-like safeguards if Concord adopts them.

Those procedural rules require later derivation.

---

## 76. Historical custody

Historical preserves process record but does not decide legal truth independently.

> **Custody Of Record != Authority To Determine Judgment.**

Historical should preserve:
- original evidence state;
- corrections;
- findings;
- later reversals;
- provenance.

## 78. Worked examples

### A. Fabricated CCTV

Investigator A creates synthetic footage and knowingly represents it as original CCTV observation.

**Result:** evidence fabrication/process corruption; synthetic generation itself is not the wrong—the false evidential representation is.

### B. Honest witness error

Witness B confidently but honestly misremembers a vehicle colour and later corrects the statement.

**Result:** error and correction; no perjury absent required knowing falsity.

### C. Defendant silence

Defendant C lawfully refuses to answer prosecution questions.

**Result:** exercise of defence right, not obstruction.

### D. Exculpatory evidence

Investigator D discovers reliable evidence inconsistent with the preferred suspect and deliberately removes it from the case record to preserve prosecution.

**Result:** potential process corruption and entrusted-duty breach.

### E. Refusal of unlawful search

Officer E demands entry without valid authority where consent would be required. Occupant F refuses.

**Result:** refusal is not obstruction merely because it frustrates investigation.

### F. Witness bribe refused

A offers B money to give false testimony. B refuses and reports A.

**Result:** process-corruption/bribery state may be complete despite failure to alter testimony.

### G. Routine deletion

Participant G deletes old private messages under normal retention practice. Months later an unrelated investigation would have found them useful.

**Result:** no retroactive preservation duty merely from later investigative interest.

### H. Corrupted source affects cases

A laboratory process is discovered to have deliberately falsified results across many cases.

**Result:** identify dependent cases and review material reliance; do not automatically declare every outcome invalid without dependency analysis.

## 79. Development result

Process integrity is a distinct institutional protected interest, but only because legitimate civil processes are necessary to protect underlying rights and correct error.

It must never become a generic offence of frustrating authority.

The protected object is:

# **The Integrity Of Legitimate Truth-Seeking, Rights-Protecting And Decision-Making Civil Process.**

Core chain:

**Legitimate Process**  
→ **Defined Participant Role / Duty / Right**  
→ **Evidence / Testimony / Record / Decision Function**  
→ **Conduct Affecting Process**  
→ **Truth/Integrity Effect**  
→ **Mental State**  
→ **Lawful Right / Privilege / Justification Check**  
→ **Materiality**  
→ **Responsibility**  
→ **Repair Of Process + Consequence + Dependency Review**

This closes the principal institutional-process gap identified in the Compact Basic Law Coverage Audit.

The next architectural need is no longer another obvious fundamental offence. It is **Promulgation, Legal Versioning, Notice and Non-Retroactivity**: the machinery that determines which derived rule was actually enforceable at a particular time, prevents ethical derivability from becoming retroactive criminalisation, and connects Law to Historical custody.
