# Unlawful Restraint, Detention and Abduction — Ethical Derivation and Base Offence Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework

**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / WORKED LEGAL DERIVATION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Purpose

This document derives a candidate fundamental law protecting participants against unjustified restraint, detention, confinement and forced movement.

It is mechanism-neutral.

The protected interest is not merely freedom from a particular historical mechanism such as imprisonment, handcuffs, locked rooms or physical seizure.

The protected legal interest is:

> **Freedom Of Movement And Physical/Civil Liberty Against Unauthorised Control.**

Primary Ethical Kernel sources:

**Autonomy + Safe Space + Dignity + Least-Harm Authority**  
→ **Protected Liberty**  
→ **No Participant May Control Another Participant's Movement Or Confinement Without Consent Or Independently Legitimate Bounded Authority**

---

## 3. Protected interest

The protected interest includes the participant's ability, within otherwise lawful boundaries, to:
- move;
- remain;
- leave;
- refuse transport;
- avoid confinement;
- avoid being held under another participant's control.

This is not an unlimited right to occupy any place.

Property rights, safe spaces, lawful exclusions and contextual access rules may legitimately require a participant to leave a location.

Therefore:

> **Freedom From Unlawful Restraint != Unlimited Right To Remain Anywhere.**

A participant may lawfully be required to leave one space without another participant gaining authority to imprison, abduct or otherwise control them beyond that legitimate boundary.

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not, without valid consent, lawful justification or other independently legitimate authority, intentionally or knowingly restrain, confine, detain, seize, transport or otherwise materially prevent another participant from exercising protected freedom of movement.**

Where a legally specified duty and sufficiently serious risk justify it, reckless or negligent creation of confinement conditions may require separate treatment, but should not be silently imported into the intentional base offence.

---

## 5. Mechanism neutrality

The offence is defined by the protected liberty and prohibited control, not by the tool used.

Potential mechanisms include:
- physical force;
- locks;
- barriers;
- threats of immediate force;
- restraint devices;
- vehicles;
- confinement systems;
- remote control of access;
- disabling a participant's means of safe exit;
- technological restrictions;
- control of a digital or hybrid participant's mobility substrate;
- other effective mechanisms.

> **Mechanism Of Restraint != Fundamental Offence.**

Mechanism may remain relevant evidence concerning:
- intent;
- knowledge;
- preparation;
- duration;
- severity;
- capability;
- escape risk;
- proportionality;
- aggravation.

---

## 6. Restraint

### RESTRAINT

A participant's protected movement is materially prevented or controlled for a legally significant period or purpose.

Restraint need not involve a prison or locked room.

A participant surrounded by credible immediate force may be restrained even if no physical barrier exists.

Conversely, ordinary social pressure or inconvenience should not automatically become legal restraint.

The threshold must remain material.

---

## 7. Detention

### DETENTION

A participant is intentionally held within a bounded place, process or authority state and is not free to leave.

Detention may be lawful or unlawful depending upon authority.

> **Detention Describes A State; It Does Not By Itself Establish Legitimacy.**

Lawful arrest, emergency protective detention or other legitimate custody remains detention, but its legality depends on the independent authority envelope.

---

## 8. Abduction

### ABDUCTION / FORCED REMOVAL

A participant is intentionally moved, transported or compelled from one place/context to another without valid consent or legitimate authority.

The core protected interest remains autonomy/liberty.

Abduction may coexist with:
- restraint;
- physical harm;
- threats;
- exploitation;
- other offences.

It need not be treated as an entirely disconnected moral category merely because movement rather than stationary confinement occurred.

---

## 9. Consent

A participant may validly consent to bounded restraint or transport.

Examples may include:
- medical procedures;
- sport;
- consensual safety systems;
- transport arrangements;
- voluntary secure environments.

The general Consent and Withdrawal architecture applies.

> **Consent To Entry != Consent To Indefinite Confinement.**

> **Consent To Transport != Consent To Arbitrary Destination Change.**

> **Past Consent != Continuing Consent.**

Where consent is withdrawable, further restraint requires cessation as reasonably practicable or an independent authority source.

---

## 10. Property and safe-space boundaries

A property holder or safe-space controller may possess legitimate authority to deny entry or require departure within the applicable wrapper.

That authority does not automatically include general detention authority.

Candidate distinction:

**Authority To Exclude**  
!=  
**Authority To Confine**

> **Right To Make Someone Leave != Right To Prevent Them Leaving.**

Temporary restraint may become independently justified in limited circumstances such as immediate self-defence, protection of others or a lawful apprehension, but the authority must be separately established.

---

## 11. Arrest and civil-security detention

Lawful arrest is a major interference with autonomy.

It requires an independently legitimate civil-security/judicial authority.

Candidate chain:

**Legally Sufficient Grounds**  
→ **Legitimate Apprehension Function**  
→ **Minimum Necessary Capability**  
→ **Bounded Arrest Authority**  
→ **Custody**  
→ **Prompt Procedural Review / Required Judicial Process**  
→ **Release Or Further Independently Authorised Detention**

> **Suspicion != Unlimited Detention Authority.**

> **Arrest Authority != Punishment Authority.**

> **Custody Before Judgment != Sentence.**

The full thresholds belong in Civil Security and Judiciary architecture.

---

## 12. Authority must remain live

A detention may begin lawfully and later become unlawful.

Example:

A valid arrest occurs, but the lawful holding period expires or the legal basis is removed and the participant is nevertheless kept confined.

Therefore:

> **Lawful Initial Detention != Lawful Indefinite Continuation.**

> **Past Authority != Continuing Authority.**

This directly applies the existing Legal Classification Grammar and BCA.

---

## 13. Emergency protective restraint

Immediate danger may justify temporary restraint where the applicable protective authority threshold is satisfied.

Examples may include:
- stopping an imminent serious attack;
- preventing immediate catastrophic accidental harm;
- emergency mental-health protective intervention under the existing Mental Health architecture.

The authority is protective, not punitive.

Candidate:

**Immediate Serious Threat**  
→ **Protective Function**  
→ **Necessary Minimum Restraint**  
→ **Threat/Authority Review**  
→ **Release / Transition To Another Independently Justified Authority**

> **Emergency Need != General Custodial Authority.**

---

## 14. Self-defence and restraint

A participant may sometimes restrain an attacker as part of proportionate self-defence or defence of others.

The restraint remains bounded by the threat.

> **Threat Ends → Threat-Derived Restraint Authority Ends Or Requires New Basis.**

Holding a participant after the immediate defensive need ends may require civil-security handoff or another legitimate authority.

---

## 15. Care relationships

Care, guardianship, medicine or support relationships do not create general confinement authority.

> **Duty Of Care != Ownership Of Participant.**

> **Need For Assistance != Authority To Detain.**

Where diminished capacity and serious danger justify protective intervention, the relevant exceptional-authority architecture applies.

The existence of a caring motive does not itself legalise restraint.

---

## 16. Employment, contract and debt

Employment and contractual relationships do not create ownership of the participant.

> **Employment Contract != Detention Authority.**

> **Debt != Ownership Of Debtor.**

A participant must not be confined merely to compel labour, repayment or continued service absent some independently derived legal authority that itself satisfies the Ethical Kernel.

This architecture therefore supports the prohibition of slavery, forced labour and debt bondage, although those interests may warrant their own worked derivations.

---

## 17. Threat-based restraint

Physical contact is not necessary where a credible threat effectively prevents departure.

Candidate analysis:

**Threat**  
→ **Would departure reasonably trigger immediate prohibited consequence?**  
→ **Did actor intend/know threat would control movement?**  
→ **Was movement materially constrained?**

A mere request or warning of a lawful consequence should not automatically become restraint.

Example:

"Leave this private safe space or lawful access will be withdrawn" is not equivalent to "If you leave this room I will shoot you."

---

## 18. Deception and confinement

Deception may sometimes produce effective confinement without force.

Example:
A participant is deliberately falsely told that every safe exit is lethally dangerous when the deceiver intends that falsehood to prevent departure.

Whether deception alone satisfies the offence should depend on material control of liberty, causation and required mental state.

> **Deception About Exit != Automatically Restraint, But May Become Restraint Where It Materially Controls Liberty.**

---

## 19. Duration

Duration affects seriousness but should not define whether a short unlawful restraint occurred.

> **Brief Restraint != Automatically Lawful Restraint.**

A seconds-long restraint may be legally significant in one context and trivial/incidental in another.

Duration should be recorded alongside:
- purpose;
- intensity;
- harm;
- fear;
- conditions;
- movement;
- vulnerability;
- authority state.

---

## 20. Conditions of detention

A lawful basis for detention does not authorise arbitrary treatment within detention.

> **Authority To Detain != Authority To Harm.**

> **Authority To Detain != Authority To Remove Unrelated Rights.**

A detained participant remains a participant with protected dignity, health, communication, process and other rights subject only to independently justified restrictions.

This should interface with future Custody architecture.

---

## 21. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: YES**

Candidate application:

A participant with the required intent enters direct execution of unlawful restraint/abduction but fails to secure control.

Examples:
- tries to force a participant into a vehicle but is prevented;
- locks an intended confinement room believing the participant is inside when they are not;
- activates a restraint mechanism that fails.

The general Attempt and Completion architecture applies.

---

## 22. Participation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

Potential classifications:
- PRINCIPAL;
- CO-PRINCIPAL;
- DIRECTING;
- ASSISTING;
- FACILITATING;
- CULPABLE OMISSION where an independent duty exists.

Example:
A orders an unlawful abduction, B physically seizes the participant, C knowingly drives the vehicle as part of the plan.

Responsibility is separately classified for A, B and C.

---

## 23. Mental state

The principal offence ordinarily requires intentional or knowing control of another participant's liberty.

Relevant states:

**INTENTIONAL** — restraint/control is intended.

**KNOWING** — actor knows their conduct materially restrains/control liberty.

Reckless/negligent confinement may arise in specialised contexts but should be separately derived where necessary rather than expanding the intentional offence by implication.

---

## 24. Harm and aggravation

Unlawful restraint may cause harms beyond loss of liberty:
- physical injury;
- psychological injury;
- loss of livelihood;
- deprivation of medical care;
- isolation;
- exploitation;
- sexual/physical violence;
- other consequences.

These should be recorded as actual harms and/or separate offences rather than hidden inside the restraint label.

Potential aggravating factors may include:
- extended duration;
- deliberate isolation;
- abuse of official/care authority;
- dangerous conditions;
- deliberate exploitation;
- movement across boundaries to frustrate recovery;
- restraint used to facilitate another serious offence.

Double counting must be avoided.

---

## 25. Mitigation and capacity

The general Aggravation and Mitigation architecture applies.

The general Mental Health legal factor applies where functionally relevant.

A participant may intentionally restrain another while possessing impaired capacity; intent and capacity remain separate findings.

> **Intentional Restraint != Automatic Full Capacity.**

---

## 26. Remedy

Potential remedies may include:
- immediate release;
- restoration of access/property/status;
- medical/psychological support;
- compensation for established harm/loss;
- correction of records;
- restoration of civil rights wrongly suspended;
- other appropriate restoration.

> **Release Ends Ongoing Restraint; It Does Not Erase Historical Wrong.**

---

## 27. Wrongful official detention

Unlawful detention by Concord or an authorised official requires particular transparency because the system itself supplied the capability.

Potential consequences may include:
- immediate release;
- independent review;
- participant remedy;
- official accountability;
- authority/process correction;
- audit of similarly affected cases;
- KCS propagation where systemic error is found.

> **Official Status != Immunity From Unlawful Detention Law.**

The legal system must be able to classify its own restraint as unlawful.

## 29. Substrate neutrality

The protected interest may require substrate-specific expression.

For a biological participant, restraint commonly concerns bodily movement.

For a digital/hybrid participant, analogous liberty interference might include:
- unauthorised confinement to a compute environment;
- disabling legitimate migration capability;
- seizure/control of embodiment;
- coercive isolation;
- blocking authorised exit or transfer.

However:

> **Technical Containment != Automatically Participant Detention.**

The participant's actual agency, identity, mobility capability, rights and lawful operating context must be established.

This requires future interface with digital-personhood and continuity architecture.

## 31. Worked examples

### A. Locked room

A deliberately locks B in a room for twenty minutes without consent or authority.

**Result:** completed unlawful detention/restraint. Duration informs seriousness but does not create the offence.

### B. Lawful arrest, expired authority

An officer lawfully arrests B. The lawful custody basis later terminates, but B remains held without new authority.

**Result:** initial detention may remain lawful; continued detention after authority termination requires separate classification and may become unlawful.

### C. Boxing venue

A participant voluntarily enters a boxing match under the applicable wrapper.

The wrapper permits bounded physical contact; it does not permit organisers to lock the participant in the venue after withdrawal.

**Result:** consent to dangerous activity != consent to confinement.

### D. Immediate self-defence

A restrains B while B is actively attacking C. Once B is no longer an immediate threat, A continues holding B for personal punishment.

**Result:** initial restraint may be justified; later restraint requires independent authority and can transition to unlawful state.

### E. Care setting

A carer prevents a fully capable participant from leaving because the carer believes departure is unwise.

**Result:** benevolent motive alone does not supply detention authority.

### F. Abduction by direction

A orders B to seize C and C is transported against their will.

**Result:** B may be principal physical actor; A may be directing participant. Separate participation classification applies.

## 32. Development result

Unlawful restraint, detention and abduction can be derived from the Ethical Kernel as a mechanism-neutral protection of liberty and autonomy.

The base law remains simple:

**Do not materially control another participant's protected freedom of movement without valid consent or independently legitimate bounded authority.**

The complexity belongs in reusable classifiers and authority analysis rather than in a catalogue of every restraint mechanism.

This derivation also establishes interfaces needed for:
- Civil Security arrest/custody;
- Judiciary review;
- Mental Health exceptional authority;
- Health/care;
- safe spaces/property;
- slavery/forced labour;
- custody conditions;
- digital/hybrid participant liberty.
