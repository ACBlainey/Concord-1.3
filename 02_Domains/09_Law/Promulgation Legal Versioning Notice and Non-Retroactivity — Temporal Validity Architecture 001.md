# Promulgation, Legal Versioning, Notice and Non-Retroactivity — Temporal Validity Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law

**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / GENERAL LEGAL GRAMMAR / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary Interfaces:** Historical; Governance; Judiciary; Civil Contact; Education  
**Related:** Legal Derivation and Consequence Architecture; Legal Classification Grammar; Bounded Transition Architecture; Knowledge Control System

## 1. Purpose

Concord law is intended to remain ethically derivable and corrigible.

That creates a temporal problem.

A rule may:
- be ethically derivable before anyone has formally articulated it;
- be proposed but not adopted;
- be adopted but not yet effective;
- be amended;
- be repealed;
- later be found defective;
- later be interpreted differently.

A participant must be judged under the law that legitimately governed the conduct at the relevant time.

Therefore:

> **Ethical Derivability != Immediate Enforceable Legal Notice.**

and:

> **Corrigibility Of Law != Retroactive Expansion Of Liability.**

## 3. Foundational legal-state distinction

Candidate legal lifecycle:

**Ethically Derivable Principle**  
→ **Draft / Proposal**  
→ **Legitimate Adoption**  
→ **Promulgation**  
→ **Notice / Transition Period Where Required**  
→ **Effective Law**  
→ **Interpretation / Application**  
→ **Amendment / Repeal / Replacement**  
→ **Historical Custody**

These are different states.

> **Proposed Law != Adopted Law.**

> **Adopted Law != Necessarily Effective Law.**

> **Current Law != Necessarily Law At Time Of Conduct.**

---

## 4. Ethical derivability

Ethical derivation answers:

**Would this legal rule be legitimate under the Concord Ethical Kernel?**

It does not by itself answer:

**Was this participant legally on notice that this rule was enforceable at time T?**

> **Ethical Legitimacy Is Necessary But Not Sufficient For Retrospective Punitive Liability.**

---

## 5. Promulgation

Promulgation is the act of making an adopted legal rule officially available through the legitimate civil publication mechanism.

Civil Contact should be a primary discoverability route.

Promulgation should establish:
- authoritative text;
- version identifier;
- adoption authority/provenance;
- publication time;
- effective time;
- territorial/contextual scope;
- superseded/repealed relationships.

> **Secret Law != Legitimate Basis For Punitive Liability.**

---

## 6. Discoverability

Law should be reasonably discoverable by affected participants.

This does not require proof every participant actually read every rule.

> **Notice != Proof Of Personal Reading.**

But the system must provide reasonable access.

Potential mechanisms:
- Civil Contact;
- public legal repository;
- contextual notices;
- Education;
- role-specific updates;
- machine-readable interfaces.

---

## 7. Accessibility

Discoverability also requires practical accessibility.

Relevant dimensions:
- language;
- disability access;
- machine/human-readable forms;
- indexing;
- search;
- version clarity;
- explanation.

> **Technically Published != Necessarily Meaningfully Discoverable.**

---

## 8. Legal certainty

Rules should be sufficiently clear for participants to understand the prohibited/required conduct.

> **Law Need Not Enumerate Every Mechanism To Provide Notice.**

Mechanism-neutral laws can remain clear by defining:
- protected interest;
- prohibited result/conduct;
- mental state;
- scope;
- exceptions/justifications.

---

## 9. Ambiguity

No legal text eliminates all ambiguity.

Where ambiguity materially affects liability:

> **Unclear Law Should Not Be Expanded Retrospectively Against The Participant Through Creative Interpretation.**

Prospective clarification is preferable.

---

## 10. Effective date

Every promulgated law should have an effective state/time.

Possible states:
- immediate;
- future scheduled;
- conditional activation;
- phased implementation.

> **Publication Time != Automatically Effective Time.**

---

## 11. Transition period

New duties may require time for compliance.

Examples:
- infrastructure standards;
- recordkeeping;
- licensing;
- environmental limits;
- enterprise reporting.

> **Duty To Comply != Duty To Have Completed Impossible Transition Instantly.**

BTA applies.

---

## 12. Immediate protective rules

Some rules may legitimately take immediate effect where delay would create serious harm and compliance is immediately possible.

This should be justified, not assumed.

---

## 13. Non-retroactivity

Candidate core rule:

> **A participant must not incur greater punitive liability because a prohibition, offence, aggravator or harsher consequence was created or expanded after the relevant conduct occurred.**

This protects authorship and fair notice.

---

## 14. Later ethical recognition

If Concord later derives that conduct should have been prohibited but no applicable promulgated law existed at the time:

> **Later Ethical Recognition != Retroactive Criminal Offence.**

The discovery can:
- change future law;
- create non-punitive institutional correction;
- expose prior governance failure;
- inform historical analysis.

It cannot manufacture past criminal notice.

---

## 15. More lenient later law

A later reduction in penalty or narrowing of offence raises a different question.

Candidate principle:

> **Later Recognition That Prior Punitive Law Was Excessive Or Illegitimate Should Trigger Review Of Continuing Consequences.**

This does not necessarily rewrite the historical fact that the prior law existed.

---

## 16. Repeal

Repeal ends prospective authority of the rule unless transition provisions specify otherwise.

> **Repealed Law != Continuing General Authority.**

But conduct committed while the rule was valid remains historically classifiable under the law-at-time, subject to later correction/leniency architecture.

---

## 17. Invalid law

If a purported law lacked legitimate authority or violated higher constitutional constraints, its historical existence does not necessarily make its enforcement legitimate.

> **Historical Enactment != Constitutional Validity.**

Judiciary must be able to distinguish:
- text existed;
- text was purportedly effective;
- text was constitutionally valid;
- text was lawfully applied.

---

## 18. Presumption against retroactive cure

An institution should not be able to repair an invalid past prosecution simply by later enacting the missing rule.

> **Later Enactment Cannot Cure Missing Past Legal Authority.**

---

## 19. Version identity

Every authoritative legal text should have a stable version identity.

Candidate minimum metadata:
- legal instrument ID;
- version;
- status;
- adoption timestamp;
- promulgation timestamp;
- effective timestamp;
- supersession timestamp;
- repeal timestamp if applicable;
- authority/provenance;
- scope;
- dependency links;
- amendment history.

---

## 20. Historical custody

Historical should preserve immutable evidence of each legal state.

> **Current Consolidated Law != Complete Historical Legal Record.**

Historical custody should permit reconstruction of:

**What law governed this conduct at T?**

without relying on present text.

---

## 21. Consolidated text

For usability, Law may maintain current consolidated versions.

But consolidation must not erase amendment history.

> **Readable Current Law != Permission To Destroy Prior Versions.**

---

## 22. Law-at-time query

The system should support a canonical query:

**Given conduct time T, location/context C and participant/legal state P, which legal version(s) were effective and applicable?**

This should be answerable reproducibly.

---

## 23. Event time

Some offences occur at one moment.

Others:
- continue;
- repeat;
- span transition;
- involve preparation before amendment and completion after.

Temporal classification must therefore identify legally relevant event intervals.

> **One Case != Necessarily One Legal Time.**

---

## 24. Continuing offences

If prohibited conduct continues across a legal change, post-effective conduct may be governed by new law even if earlier conduct was not.

> **Past Lawful State != Licence For Future Conduct After Valid Legal Transition.**

Adequate notice/transition remains relevant.

---

## 25. Attempt across legal transition

If preparation begins before a rule exists and executory attempt begins after it becomes valid/effective, liability should attach only to conduct occurring within applicable legal state.

> **Pre-Law Intention != Retroactive Offence.**

---

## 26. Aggravators

New aggravating factors should not increase punishment for earlier conduct.

> **Later Aggravator != Earlier Culpability.**

---

## 27. Procedural law

Procedural changes can sometimes apply to ongoing cases without retroactively changing the offence.

But they must not effectively remove substantive rights or increase punishment unfairly.

> **Procedural Label != Permission For Substantive Retroactivity.**

---

## 28. Evidential standards

Changing evidential rules after conduct requires care.

The relevant question is whether the change:
- improves process;
- alters substantive liability;
- unfairly weakens protections.

Judiciary should source-resolve this later.

---

## 29. Judicial interpretation

Courts inevitably interpret law.

Interpretation should clarify existing meaning, not secretly create a new offence after conduct.

> **Interpretation != Unlimited Retrospective Legislation.**

Where a new interpretation materially expands liability beyond reasonable prior notice, prospective application may be required.

---

## 30. Precedent

If Concord adopts precedent, it should remain subordinate to:
- constitutional law;
- promulgated law;
- correction;
- distinguishable context.

> **Past Decision != Permanent Law Merely Because It Is Past.**

Historical stores precedent; Judiciary determines current legal relevance.

---

## 31. Emergency law

Emergency legal measures require:
- clear authority source;
- activation condition;
- scope;
- promulgation;
- effective time;
- sunset/review.

> **Emergency != Temporal Ambiguity.**

Urgency may shorten notice but does not erase provenance.

---

## 32. Local/contextual law

Bounded jurisdictions/safe spaces may have local rules.

Participants need contextual notice.

> **Local Variation Without Discoverability Undermines Authored Entry.**

Entry into context does not validate hidden rules.

---

## 33. Consent to local rules

> **Entry != Unlimited Consent.**

Participants should be able to discover materially unusual local rules before or at meaningful entry where feasible.

Non-waivable rights remain protected.

---

## 34. Machine-enforced rules

Automated systems may enforce legal states.

> **Machine Enforcement != Proof Rule Is Valid.**

The system must reference authoritative legal version/provenance.

A software configuration error cannot silently become law.

---

## 35. Code versus law

> **Code Is Not Law Merely Because It Controls Capability.**

Technical constraints may implement law, but legal authority remains independently sourced.

This is especially important for AI/digital participants.

---

## 36. Version mismatch

If enforcement system uses wrong/outdated version:

> **Enforcement Configuration != Applicable Law.**

The mismatch is an operational/process defect.

It may require remedy and audit.

---

## 37. Automated updates

Legal updates distributed to machines/agents should preserve:
- version;
- checksum/identity;
- effective time;
- dependency;
- rollback state;
- provenance.

KCS is directly applicable.

---

## 38. Failed propagation

A promulgated law may fail to reach some systems.

This creates two distinct questions:
1. Was law legally promulgated with reasonable notice?
2. Did a specific enforcement/compliance system receive it?

> **Propagation Failure != Automatic Non-Existence Of Law.**

But severe discoverability failure can undermine notice/fairness.

---

## 39. Participant-specific notification

Some duties require direct notice because they apply only after an order or status change.

Examples:
- restraining order;
- licence condition;
- specific compliance order.

> **General Publication != Always Sufficient Individual Notice.**

---

## 40. Valid order notice

For liability based on violating a specific order, participant should ordinarily have:
- actual notice;
- or legally sufficient delivery/constructive notice where justified;
- clear terms;
- feasible compliance.

---

## 41. Ignorance of law

A general rule that "ignorance is never relevant" is too crude.

> **Ignorance != Automatic Excuse.**

But equally:

> **Reasonable Inability To Discover Applicable Law Can Be Legally Relevant.**

Especially for:
- hidden local rules;
- defective publication;
- ambiguous transition;
- inaccessible formats.

---

## 42. Role-specific law

Professionals/operators may reasonably be expected to track rules governing their accepted function.

> **Accepted Specialised Role Can Increase Reasonable Notice Duty Within That Role.**

It does not create knowledge of unrelated law.

---

## 43. Education

Education should provide legal literacy sufficient for ordinary participation.

But:

> **Failure Of Education System != Automatic Participant Guilt For Unknowable Rule.**

Institutional failure and individual culpability must remain distinct.

---

## 44. Civil Contact

Civil Contact should act as a primary official route for:
- current law;
- changes;
- effective dates;
- participant-specific notices;
- explanatory material;
- routes to challenge/clarify.

> **Discoverability Is A Civil Function, Not Merely A Repository Feature.**

---

## 45. Explanatory material

Guides can explain law but should not silently replace authoritative text.

> **Guidance != Law.**

If guidance materially misstates law and participants reasonably rely on it, fairness/remedy issues arise.

---

## 46. Conflicting official guidance

Where official sources conflict, punitive interpretation should not exploit institutional inconsistency against the participant.

> **State-Created Ambiguity Should Not Become A Punitive Advantage For The State.**

Correction should be rapid and provenance preserved.

---

## 47. Corrections

Typographical/non-substantive corrections can be versioned without pretending substantive law changed.

Substantive changes require explicit amendment state.

> **Editorial Correction != Permission To Hide Substantive Amendment.**

---

## 48. Errata

Historical should preserve errata and corrected versions.

If an error changed reasonable meaning, affected cases may require review.

---

## 49. Legal dependency graph

Laws depend on:
- definitions;
- rights;
- authority rules;
- procedures;
- classifiers.

KCS-style dependency mapping can identify what requires review when a parent rule changes.

> **Change To Parent Rule May Require Review Of Dependent Law.**

But:

> **Dependency != Automatic Invalidity.**

Material effect must be assessed.

---

## 50. Constitutional change

If constitutional rule changes, dependent ordinary law may need:
- immediate invalidation;
- transition;
- reinterpretation;
- replacement.

Exact mechanism depends on constitutional authority.

Historical must preserve before/after states.

---

## 51. Repealed offence and ongoing sentence

If an offence is repealed because conduct is no longer considered legitimately punishable, continuing punitive custody should be reviewed.

> **Ended Punitive Justification Requires Review Of Continuing Punishment.**

This follows:
**Function Ends → Function-Derived Authority Ends.**

---

## 52. Reduced sentence range

If Concord concludes prior sentencing range was excessive, continuing sentences should be reviewable under a promulgated leniency/correction rule.

This is not retroactive punishment; it reduces continuing authority.

---

## 53. Increased sentence range

A later increase cannot apply to earlier conduct.

> **Later Harsher Consequence != Earlier Legal Consequence.**

---

## 54. Civil remedies

Retroactivity concerns differ for restorative/corrective civil measures.

A later-discovered continuing harmful state may justify present correction even where past punitive liability is unavailable.

Example:
new evidence reveals contamination caused lawfully under old standards but still dangerous now.

> **No Retroactive Punishment != No Present Protective Authority.**

Current protective authority requires current justification.

---

## 55. Historical wrongdoing under invalid law

Historical may record that a past regime punished conduct under a rule now recognised as unethical/invalid.

> **Historical Accuracy != Continuing Endorsement.**

Records should distinguish:
- what happened;
- what law purported to apply;
- later validity judgment;
- remedy/correction.

---

## 56. Amnesty/pardon

If Concord later adopts amnesty/pardon mechanisms, they should not silently rewrite factual/legal history.

> **Removal Of Consequence != Historical Non-Occurrence.**

Exact architecture remains future work.

---

## 57. Legal identity of amendments

Amendment should state whether it:
- clarifies;
- narrows;
- expands;
- changes consequence;
- changes procedure;
- changes definitions;
- corrects error.

This assists temporal application.

---

Suggested legal states:

## 61. Worked examples

### A. Newly derived offence

Concord derives and promulgates a new offence on 1 June, effective 1 July. A performed the conduct in May.

**Result:** ethical derivability does not create retrospective criminal liability. May conduct is assessed under May law.

### B. Continuing conduct

A begins a previously lawful discharge in June. New valid limits take effect 1 July after adequate notice. A knowingly continues after 1 July.

**Result:** June conduct is not retroactively criminalised; post-effective conduct may enter the new legal state.

### C. Harsher sentence

An offence carried maximum consequence X when committed. Later amendment raises it to Y.

**Result:** Y cannot be applied retrospectively to earlier conduct.

### D. Repealed offence

A remains under a continuing punitive sentence for conduct Concord later determines should no longer be punishable.

**Result:** repeal/recognition should trigger review of continuing punitive authority; history is preserved but present justification cannot be assumed.

### E. Wrong software version

An automated enforcement system applies superseded rule V3 although V4 is legally effective.

**Result:** V3 enforcement configuration does not become law; affected action requires correction/review.

### F. Hidden local rule

A enters a local bounded context where an unusual restriction was not reasonably discoverable and is then punished for violating it.

**Result:** authored entry/notice is defective; local rule cannot rely merely on physical presence as meaningful notice.

### G. Official guidance error

Civil Contact guidance incorrectly states an activity is permitted. A reasonably relies on it, while authoritative text is ambiguous.

**Result:** institutional ambiguity/reliance must be considered; the state should not exploit its own misleading guidance to expand punitive liability.

## 62. Development result

The Concord now has a candidate temporal validity architecture separating:

**Ethical Legitimacy**  
from  
**Legal Adoption**  
from  
**Promulgation**  
from  
**Effective Enforceability**  
from  
**Historical Custody.**

The core law-at-time test is:

**Conduct / State At Time T**  
→ **Identify Context/Jurisdiction**  
→ **Retrieve Effective Legal Version At T**  
→ **Verify Valid Authority + Promulgation + Notice Requirements**  
→ **Apply Elements/Classifiers/Defences Existing At T**  
→ **Apply Consequence Range Existing At T**  
→ **Record Later Amendments Separately**  
→ **Review Continuing Consequences If Later Law Removes Their Present Justification**

This provides the Law ↔ Historical interface needed for a corrigible legal system that does not manufacture retroactive guilt.

A logical next step is to create a **Compact Derived Law Register / Code Map** linking the fundamental protected interests, general classifiers, special implementations, compositions and institutional grammars developed so far. That would show whether the Law domain now forms a coherent minimum viable legal code rather than a collection of separate derivation papers.
