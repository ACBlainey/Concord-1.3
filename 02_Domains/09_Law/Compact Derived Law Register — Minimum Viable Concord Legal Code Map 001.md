# Compact Derived Law Register — Minimum Viable Concord Legal Code Map 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 9 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CONSOLIDATION REGISTER / STRUCTURAL AUDIT / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Purpose:** Consolidate the currently derived legal architecture into a compact code map, distinguish fundamental law from classifiers and implementation, and expose remaining structural gaps.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Law domain has now developed enough individual derivations that the next risk is fragmentation.

A mature legal system should not become a catalogue of historically named offences with duplicated logic.

The current design instead follows:

> **Ethical Kernel → Protected Interests → Base Law → Reusable Classifiers → Special Implementations / Compositions → Procedure → Consequence → Temporal Validity**

This register asks:

1. What fundamental protected interests now exist?
2. Which reusable classifiers modify them?
3. Which apparent offences are merely compositions?
4. Which special implementations deserve their own operational treatment?
5. Which institutional/process grammars exist?
6. Which structural gaps remain?

---

## 2. Controlling design principles

> **Minimum Sufficient Law, Not Minimum Possible Law.**

> **Named Historical Offence != Necessarily Fundamental Legal Category.**

> **Classifier != Protected Interest.**

> **Special Severity != New Ethical Root.**

> **Compact Architecture != Inaccessible Law.**

The eventual public-facing code may use familiar names for discoverability while preserving a smaller underlying legal grammar.

**Unlawful Physical Harm — Ethical Derivation and Base Offence Architecture 001**

# PART VIII — CIVIL / REGULATORY BOUNDARIES

## 53. Contract breach

> **Broken Promise != Automatic Crime.**

Ordinary breach belongs primarily to civil obligation/remedy unless fraud, coercion, property interference or another offence exists.

---

## 54. Debt

> **Debt != Ownership.**

Debt creates bounded obligation, not authority over the debtor's person.

---

## 55. Licensing

Licensing should implement legitimate function/safety/competence requirements.

> **Capability != Permission != Licence != Certification != Authority.**

A general licensing/permission grammar may still be useful.

---

## 56. Standards

Many domains need:
- standards;
- certification;
- inspection;
- audit;
- compliance;
- corrective action.

This is not automatically criminal law.

A cross-domain Standards and Assurance architecture remains a major adjacent need.

---

## 57. Regulatory breach

Not every technical violation should be criminal.

Potential ladder:
- notice;
- correction;
- administrative consequence;
- civil remedy;
- licence restriction;
- criminal consequence where culpability/seriousness warrants.

> **Regulatory Noncompliance != Automatically Criminal Wrongdoing.**

# PART X — MINIMUM VIABLE CODE ASSESSMENT

## 61. Is this now a coherent basic law architecture?

**YES — at the level of substantive protected interests and general liability grammar.**

The Law domain now has enough architecture to answer, in principle:

1. **What protected interest is involved?**
2. **What conduct/result occurred?**
3. **What mental state existed?**
4. **Was there participation/attempt/preparation?**
5. **Was consent valid?**
6. **Was authority/justification present?**
7. **Was there a positive duty?**
8. **What capacity/authorship existed?**
9. **What aggravation/mitigation applies?**
10. **What remedy/sanction/protection/treatment is justified?**
11. **What version of law applied at the time?**

This is the skeleton of a minimum viable substantive legal code.

---

## 62. What it cannot yet do alone

It cannot yet reliably answer:

- Which court hears this?
- How is a case filed?
- What authority may police use?
- When can a warrant issue?
- What is the prosecution standard?
- What burden/standard governs trial?
- How are judges/juries selected?
- What appeal exists?
- How are judgments enforced?
- How are civil disputes/remedies processed?

Those are now the primary frontier.

---

## 63. Architectural result

The project has moved from:

**"What should be illegal?"**

toward:

**"How does a legitimate legal system determine, adjudicate, remedy and enforce these derived rules?"**

That is a meaningful maturity transition.

The next work should therefore focus on **Judicial Procedure**, beginning with a top-level process state machine before defining courts in detail.

Candidate starting chain:

**Civil/Criminal Matter Arises  
→ Standing / Referral  
→ Jurisdiction  
→ Filing / Case Creation  
→ Notice  
→ Preliminary Review  
→ Investigation / Disclosure Where Applicable  
→ Pretrial Resolution / Evidential Issues  
→ Adjudication  
→ Finding  
→ Consequence / Remedy  
→ Appeal / Review  
→ Enforcement  
→ Closure / Historical Custody**

This should be developed before attempting a detailed court hierarchy, because function should determine institutional form rather than the hierarchy being assumed first.
