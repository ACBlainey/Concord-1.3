# Harmful False Factual Assertion and False Accusation — Truth, Reputation and Consequential Harm Base Offence Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 8 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / WORKED LEGAL DERIVATION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Purpose

This document derives candidate law governing harmful false factual assertions, false accusations and consequential reputational harm.

The purpose is not to create a general right to be free from criticism, embarrassment or disagreement.

The protected interest is narrower:

> **Protection Against Culpable False Factual Representation That Materially Harms Another Participant's Legitimate Civil Standing, Safety, Rights Or Opportunities.**

The architecture must preserve:
- truthful reporting;
- good-faith allegation;
- opinion;
- criticism;
- uncertainty;
- investigation;
- testimony;
- correction;
- whistleblowing;
- legitimate public-interest communication.

**Truth + Fairness + Dignity + Responsibility**  
→ **Protection Against Culpable False Attribution**  
→ **No Participant May Knowingly Or Where Legally Specified Recklessly Create Or Propagate Materially False Factual Claims About Another In A Manner That Causes Or Creates Serious Foreseeable Protected Harm**

## 3. Reputation

Reputation is not ownership of what others think.

> **Participant != Owner Of Other Participants' Opinions.**

The law therefore should not protect reputation as an entitlement to favourable judgment.

It protects against another participant corrupting the evidential basis of consequential judgment through culpably false factual representation.

> **Unfavourable Truth != Unlawful Reputational Harm.**

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not intentionally or knowingly communicate, fabricate, materially alter or propagate a false factual assertion concerning another participant where the actor knows the assertion is false and intends or knows that it is materially capable of causing protected consequential harm.**

A separately specified reckless form may be appropriate in high-consequence contexts.

Negligent misinformation should not automatically be criminalised as the same offence.

---

## 5. Fact versus opinion

A factual assertion claims a state of reality capable, at least in principle, of evidential evaluation.

Opinion expresses judgment, preference, interpretation or value assessment.

> **Negative Opinion != False Factual Assertion.**

Examples:

"B is unpleasant to work with" may ordinarily be opinion.

"B stole £10,000 from the organisation last Tuesday" is a factual allegation.

Context can blur the distinction, so labels alone are not decisive.

---

## 6. Opinion cannot be used as disguise

Calling a factual accusation an opinion does not automatically transform it.

> **'In My Opinion' != Automatic Conversion Of Fact Into Opinion.**

If the communicated meaning is that a specific factual event occurred, the assertion remains capable of factual analysis.

---

## 7. Truth

Truthful factual reporting is not made unlawful merely because it damages reputation.

> **Reputational Harm != Proof Of Wrongdoing By Speaker.**

Other laws may still govern:
- privacy;
- confidentiality;
- harassment;
- protected records;
- unlawful disclosure.

Thus:

> **Truth != Unlimited Publication Authority.**

A truthful medical record may still be unlawfully disclosed.

---

## 8. Honest mistake

A participant may make a false statement while reasonably believing it true.

> **Falsity != Automatic Dishonesty.**

Mental state must be established independently.

An honest error may require correction or civil remedy in some contexts without establishing intentional false accusation.

---

## 9. Recklessness

Some contexts may justify liability where a participant consciously disregards a substantial risk that a serious factual accusation is false.

Candidate:

**Serious Consequential Allegation**  
+ **Known Substantial Uncertainty**  
+ **Conscious Disregard**  
+ **Material Harm/Risk**

But:

> **Failure To Prove Allegation != Automatic Recklessness.**

The law must not punish good-faith reports simply because evidence later proves insufficient.

---

## 10. Negligence

Negligent false statements may require special treatment in:
- professional reporting;
- journalism;
- official records;
- regulated services;
- fiduciary contexts;
- safety-critical communication.

But ordinary conversational error should not become criminal merely because it was careless.

> **Carelessness != Automatically Criminal Falsehood.**

---

## 11. False accusation

A false accusation alleges wrongdoing, dangerousness, misconduct, incapacity or another materially adverse factual state.

False accusation is especially consequential when directed toward:
- Civil Security;
- Judiciary;
- employer;
- regulator;
- licensing body;
- health/protective authority;
- public audience.

> **Accusation != Guilt.**

> **Report Of Suspicion != Assertion Of Established Guilt.**

The distinction should be preserved in both language and records.

---

## 12. Good-faith reporting

Participants must be able to report suspected wrongdoing without having certainty.

Candidate protection:

> **Good-Faith Report Of Reasonable Suspicion != False Accusation Merely Because Suspicion Is Later Disproved.**

A reporting system should distinguish:
- observation;
- evidence;
- inference;
- suspicion;
- allegation;
- finding.

This aligns with existing Judicial Evidence architecture.

---

## 13. Deliberately fabricated report

A participant who knowingly fabricates an allegation and submits it as fact to trigger investigation or sanction creates a substantially different state.

Potential harms include:
- wrongful investigation;
- detention;
- loss of employment;
- reputational damage;
- resource diversion;
- danger to accused participant;
- corruption of legal process.

This may overlap with:
- fraud/deception;
- obstruction/perversion of process;
- abuse of authority;
- evidence fabrication.

---

## 14. Evidence fabrication

Creating false evidence is more than merely expressing a false opinion.

Examples:
- forged messages;
- altered video;
- fabricated records;
- planted physical evidence;
- synthetic media represented as genuine evidence.

> **Fabricated Evidence != Mere Speech Error.**

A dedicated evidence/process offence may later be warranted.

---

## 15. Uncertainty

Participants should be able to communicate uncertainty honestly.

Examples:
- "I think..."
- "I may have seen..."
- "I cannot verify..."
- "The evidence currently suggests..."

> **Qualified Uncertainty != Assertion Of Certainty.**

The legal system should not incentivise false certainty.

This follows BL10 and Concord epistemic principles.

---

## 16. Repetition

Repeating another person's allegation can propagate harm.

> **"Someone Else Said It" != Automatic Immunity For Knowing Propagation Of Falsehood.**

But a participant may legitimately report that an allegation exists where that fact itself is relevant, provided the allegation is not falsely represented as established truth.

---

## 17. Quotation and reporting

Quoting a false claim for purposes such as:
- investigation;
- criticism;
- correction;
- historical record;
- journalism;
- legal proceedings

is not automatically adoption of the claim.

> **Reporting A Claim != Necessarily Endorsing The Claim.**

Context and represented epistemic status matter.

---

## 18. Retraction and correction

Where a participant discovers that a materially harmful factual assertion was false, prompt correction may reduce ongoing harm.

> **Correction != Historical Non-Occurrence Of False Publication.**

But correction can matter to:
- mitigation;
- remedy;
- continuing harm;
- responsibility after discovery.

A correction should propagate where reasonably possible to audiences materially affected by the original falsehood.

---

## 19. Duty to correct

A general duty to correct every mistaken statement would be excessive.

A stronger duty may arise where:
- the participant authored the harmful claim;
- they learn it is materially false;
- consequences continue;
- correction is reasonably possible.

> **Discovery Of Material Error May Create A Correction Duty Without Proving Original Dishonesty.**

This is an important BL10 application.

---

## 20. Public figures and officials

Public responsibility can justify strong scrutiny and criticism.

> **Public Role != Loss Of Protection Against Deliberate Factual Fabrication.**

Equally:

> **Criticism Of Official Conduct != Defamation Merely Because It Is Harsh.**

Law must not become a shield against accountability.

---

## 21. Whistleblowing

Whistleblowing frequently involves unresolved allegations.

Protection should focus on good-faith reporting through legitimate channels and public-interest contexts.

> **Unproven Whistleblower Allegation != Automatic False Accusation.**

Knowingly fabricated evidence remains different.

---

## 22. Testimony

Witnesses may be mistaken.

> **Incorrect Testimony != Automatically Perjury.**

Intentional false testimony under a formal truth duty may warrant a dedicated legal-process offence.

This document should not collapse mistake, memory error and deliberate perjury.

---

## 23. Investigative hypotheses

Police/research/investigative systems must be able to represent hypotheses without converting them into participant identity.

> **Investigative Hypothesis != Participant Fact.**

> **Suspicion != Persistent Participant Identity.**

Cleared or disproved suspicion should remain historically available as evidence about the investigation where appropriate, not as an enduring assertion that the participant committed the suspected act.

---

## 24. Automated records

Automated systems can propagate false factual states at scale.

Examples:
- false risk flags;
- incorrect criminal status;
- erroneous debt;
- mistaken identity linkage;
- inaccurate eligibility status.

> **Automated Record != Infallible Fact.**

Where a system operator has a duty to maintain consequential records, correction architecture is essential.

Intentional falsehood and negligent record failure remain distinct mental states.

---

## 25. AI-generated claims

AI can generate false assertions.

Relevant questions include:
- whether AI is a participant;
- who deployed/published output;
- knowledge of uncertainty;
- verification duty;
- represented confidence;
- correction after discovery.

> **AI Generation != Automatic Truth.**

> **AI Generation != Automatic Human Dishonesty.**

Responsibility depends on authorship, knowledge, purpose and duty.

---

## 26. Identity confusion

A true statement about participant A can become a false statement when attributed to participant B.

> **True Event + Wrong Identity = Potential False Factual Assertion.**

Identity resolution is therefore part of factual accuracy.

---

## 27. Context collapse

A fact can become misleading when stripped of material context.

Example:
A lawfully recorded historical disciplinary finding is presented as if it were a current unresolved offence despite later exoneration.

> **Literal Fragment Truth != Necessarily Truthful Overall Representation.**

Material omission/manipulation may become deceptive where the communicator intentionally creates a false factual meaning.

---

## 28. Protected consequential harm

Potential protected harms include:
- wrongful investigation;
- wrongful detention;
- employment loss;
- loss of accreditation/licence;
- economic loss;
- physical danger;
- social exclusion where materially consequential;
- corruption of public/legal records;
- severe reputational impairment affecting civil participation.

Mere offence, embarrassment or disagreement should not automatically satisfy the threshold.

> **Hurt Feelings != Automatically Legal Reputational Harm.**

---

## 29. Causation

The false assertion must materially contribute to protected harm or a legally recognised serious risk of harm.

> **Falsehood Present != Falsehood Caused Every Later Consequence.**

Independent decisions and intervening causes should remain visible.

---

## 30. Attempt

Attempt liability may be **CONDITIONAL**.

Where the offence requires communication/publication to another participant, merely composing a false accusation privately may remain preparation.

Entering direct execution of transmission but failing due to technical interruption may constitute attempt.

> **Private Thought Or Draft != Published False Accusation.**

---

## 31. Participation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

Roles may include:
- original fabricator;
- director;
- knowing publisher;
- knowing amplifier;
- evidence fabricator;
- system operator intentionally propagating false state.

Mere technical carriage by a general-purpose communications system is not automatic complicity.

---

## 32. Platforms and intermediaries

A platform hosting participant speech should not automatically become author of every statement.

> **Hosting != Automatic Authorship.**

Separate duties may arise after lawful notice, adjudication or specific safety thresholds, but those require their own derivation.

This prevents liability from collapsing communications infrastructure into universal censorship authority.

---

## 33. Remedies

Potential remedies include:
- correction;
- retraction;
- record amendment;
- notification to materially affected recipients;
- restoration of status/access;
- compensation for established loss;
- removal of fabricated evidence from active decision systems while preserving proper Historical provenance;
- other proportionate restoration.

> **Deletion Of False Claim != Automatic Restoration Of Consequences.**

Dependent decisions may require propagation of correction.

---

## 34. Historical record

Historical should preserve that:
- allegation occurred;
- evidence state;
- decision;
- correction/exoneration;
- later provenance.

But:

> **Historical Preservation Of False Allegation != Endorsement Of Allegation As True.**

The record must preserve epistemic state.

---

## 35. Sanction and chilling effects

Sanctions should avoid creating incentives against legitimate reporting.

> **Protection Against False Accusation != Right To Suppress Good-Faith Allegation.**

High culpability should attach to intentional fabrication and, where legally specified, serious reckless propagation—not mere failure to prove.

## 37. Worked examples

### A. Deliberate false criminal accusation

A knowingly fabricates an allegation that B committed a serious offence and submits fabricated evidence to Civil Security intending B to be arrested.

**Result:** candidate harmful false accusation; evidence/process offences may additionally apply.

### B. Good-faith mistaken report

A sees conduct reasonably believed to be an assault and reports that suspicion accurately. Investigation establishes an innocent explanation.

**Result:** later disproval does not convert good-faith reporting into false accusation.

### C. Harsh opinion

A publicly says, "I think B is an incompetent manager."

**Result:** ordinarily opinion/criticism, not false factual assertion.

### D. Factual fabrication disguised as opinion

A says, "In my opinion, B stole £10,000 yesterday," while knowing no such event occurred.

**Result:** phrase "in my opinion" does not erase factual meaning.

### E. True but private fact

A publishes B's genuine confidential medical diagnosis without permission.

**Result:** false-assertion law is not primary because the information is true; privacy/confidentiality law may be.

### F. Correction

A reasonably publishes a factual claim, later receives decisive evidence that it was wrong, and promptly corrects the claim to the same affected decision system.

**Result:** original dishonesty may be absent; correction reduces ongoing harm and satisfies emerging correction duty.

### G. Automated false status

A system incorrectly labels B as legally disqualified. Operator C learns the record is wrong but intentionally leaves it active to block B's applications.

**Result:** the original system error and C's later knowing maintenance of a false consequential state require separate mental-state classification.

## 38. Development result

Harmful false factual assertion can be derived without creating a general legal entitlement to favourable reputation.

The protected interest is the **truthful evidential basis of consequential civil judgment**.

Core distinction:

**Opinion / criticism / good-faith uncertainty / honest mistake**  
!=  
**knowing or legally specified reckless false factual attribution causing serious protected harm**

This architecture also provides a direct bridge to future law governing:
- perjury;
- evidence fabrication;
- obstruction of justice;
- false official records;
- correction propagation;
- media/public-information duties.

A natural next derivation is **harassment, stalking and persistent unwanted interference**, where individually lawful acts may become unlawful through repetition, targeting, context and cumulative interference with safe space/autonomy.
