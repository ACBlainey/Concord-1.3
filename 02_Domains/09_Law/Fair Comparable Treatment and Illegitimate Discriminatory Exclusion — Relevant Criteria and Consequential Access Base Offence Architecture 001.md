## 1. Purpose

This document derives candidate law protecting participants against illegitimate discriminatory exclusion and materially unfair comparable treatment.

The protected interest is not sameness.

It is:

> **Fair Access To Consequential Civil Decisions According To Criteria Legitimately Relevant To The Function Being Performed.**

The central question is therefore not:

**Were two participants treated differently?**

It is:

**Was the difference in treatment materially consequential, and was it based on a criterion legitimately relevant to the function, context, protected interest and decision being made?**

## 3. Foundational distinction

> **Fairness != Sameness.**

Different participants may legitimately receive different outcomes because relevant facts differ.

Examples:
- competence;
- qualification;
- risk;
- capacity;
- contractual terms;
- resource need;
- location;
- role;
- eligibility;
- performance;
- availability.

The law should prohibit **illegitimate differentiation**, not differentiation itself.

---

## 4. Candidate protected interest

A participant has a protected interest in not being materially disadvantaged in a consequential civil decision by a criterion that lacks legitimate relevance to the function or protected purpose of that decision.

This applies most strongly where the decision controls:
- public services;
- civil rights;
- employment;
- accreditation;
- housing;
- essential services;
- healthcare access;
- education;
- legal process;
- participation rights;
- commercial access where law establishes a fair-access duty.

---

## 5. Candidate base prohibition

> **Where a participant or institution exercises a legally consequential decision function subject to a duty of fair comparable treatment, they must not intentionally or knowingly materially disadvantage, exclude or burden a participant on the basis of a criterion that is not legitimately relevant to that function, unless the distinction is independently justified by a lawful and proportionate protected purpose.**

Negligent/systemic discriminatory outcomes may require separate regulatory/civil treatment where intent/knowledge is absent.

---

## 6. Duty-source requirement

Not every private choice creates a general duty to treat every person comparably.

> **Fair-Treatment Duty Requires A Legitimate Duty Source.**

Potential sources:
- public authority;
- essential civil service;
- employment law;
- education/accreditation;
- healthcare;
- regulated commerce;
- fiduciary role;
- contractual commitment;
- monopoly/critical access function;
- other promulgated law.

This prevents BL5 from becoming universal legal control over every personal preference.

---

## 7. Comparable state

Fairness requires comparison of materially relevant states.

> **Different Relevant State != Unequal Treatment Merely Because Outcome Differs.**

Example:
Two applicants for a safety-critical role may receive different outcomes because one lacks required competence.

The comparison should ask whether similarly relevant cases are treated according to the same legitimate decision architecture.

---

## 8. Relevant criterion

A criterion is potentially legitimate where it bears a defensible relationship to:
- function;
- safety;
- competence;
- capacity;
- resource allocation;
- eligibility;
- contractual performance;
- protected contextual purpose.

> **Difference That Matters To Function May Legitimately Matter To Decision.**

---

## 9. Irrelevant criterion

A criterion becomes suspect where it materially affects outcome without legitimate connection to the decision function.

The architecture should not rely only on a frozen list of protected characteristics.

> **Illegitimate Discrimination Is A Relevance Failure, Not Merely A List-Matching Failure.**

Lists can improve notice and identify historically common abuses, but the ethical root is broader.

---

## 10. Protected characteristics

A mature legal implementation may enumerate characteristics that ordinarily require especially strong justification because they are frequently irrelevant to legitimate civil function.

Examples may include characteristics concerning:
- ancestry;
- ethnicity;
- sex;
- disability;
- religion/belief;
- sexuality;
- age;
- substrate;
- other identity states.

However:

> **Protected Characteristic != Characteristic That Can Never Be Relevant.**

Examples:
- age may matter for developmental capacity;
- disability may matter to accommodation/safety requirements without justifying exclusion;
- substrate may matter to technical compatibility;
- biological sex may be medically relevant in some clinical contexts.

The test remains functional relevance + necessity + proportionality.

---

## 11. Identity versus capability

> **Identity != Capability.**

A participant should not be presumed incapable merely because of group membership.

Where capability matters, assess capability as directly as reasonably possible.

> **Proxy For Capability Should Not Replace Available Capability Evidence Without Justification.**

---

## 12. Statistical proxies

Statistical group differences may sometimes predict outcomes but can create unfair individual treatment.

> **Population Correlation != Individual Fact.**

Use of proxy variables requires scrutiny of:
- necessity;
- accuracy;
- alternatives;
- error distribution;
- impact;
- contestability.

---

## 13. Risk assessment

Risk can be a legitimate criterion in some functions.

But:

> **Group Membership != Automatic Individual Dangerousness.**

Risk models must remain evidence-based, corrigible and contestable.

Prediction != record of conduct.

---

## 14. Competence requirements

Competence can be legitimately required where function demands it.

> **No Gatekeeper != No Competence Requirement.**

This preserves the Education architecture.

But requirements should be:
- relevant;
- proportionate;
- accessible where reasonably possible;
- based on actual function rather than prestige barriers.

---

## 15. Credential requirements

Credentials may evidence competence but should not automatically monopolise proof.

> **Credential != Competence.**

Where alternative reliable evidence can establish required capability, the system should consider it unless another legitimate reason requires the credential.

---

## 16. Accommodation

Fair treatment may require reasonable adjustment where identical treatment would unnecessarily exclude a participant despite their ability to perform the legitimate function.

> **Same Procedure For Everyone != Necessarily Fair Comparable Treatment.**

Accommodation is not automatic lowering of substantive safety/competence requirements.

It can alter the route by which competence/access is achieved or demonstrated.

---

## 17. Disability

Disability should not be treated as automatic incapacity.

> **Disability != Incapacity.**

Relevant functional effects should be assessed.

Where reasonable accommodation permits the function to be performed, exclusion solely from diagnosis/status becomes difficult to justify.

---

## 18. Capacity

Decision-specific capacity can legitimately affect some legal states.

But:

> **Capacity For Decision A != Capacity For Decision B.**

A capacity limitation should not become general civil disqualification.

---

## 19. Age/development

Age may correlate with developmental capacity but is not itself the ethical protected interest.

Bright-line ages may nevertheless be administratively useful where uncertainty and safeguarding justify them.

Such rules should remain:
- evidence-informed;
- reviewable;
- proportionate.

> **Administrative Simplicity != Complete Ethical Justification.**

---

## 20. Substrate

Human, digital and hybrid participants may differ materially in capability and requirements.

> **Substrate Difference != Lesser Participant Worth.**

But:

> **Substrate Neutrality != Pretending Substrates Are Functionally Identical.**

Legitimate technical distinctions can remain.

---

## 21. Religion, belief and non-conformity

Peaceful belief/non-belief should not ordinarily determine access to unrelated civil functions.

> **Disagreement With Belief != Legitimate Exclusion From Unrelated Civil Function.**

But a belief does not exempt conduct from generally legitimate rules protecting others.

> **Belief Protection != Conduct Immunity.**

---

## 22. Political belief

Political disagreement should not become automatic civil exclusion.

Particularly in public functions:

> **Political Disagreement != Civil Unworthiness.**

However specific entrusted roles may legitimately require commitment to the lawful function of that role, not ideological conformity beyond it.

---

## 23. Past conduct

Relevant proven past conduct may sometimes inform current decisions.

But:

> **Past Wrongdoing != Permanent Universal Disqualification.**

Relevance should consider:
- time;
- severity;
- recurrence;
- rehabilitation;
- function;
- present risk.

This aligns with Historical record principles.

---

## 24. Criminal record

A conviction may be relevant to some entrusted/safety-sensitive functions.

It should not automatically exclude a participant from unrelated life opportunities.

> **Conviction != General Civil Disability.**

Any restriction needs independent functional justification.

---

## 25. Wealth

Ability to pay can legitimately matter to ordinary market exchange, but wealth should not become a proxy for civic worth or authority.

> **Wealth != Greater Civil Worth.**

Essential civil access may require separate public/service rules.

---

## 26. Employment

Employers may select participants according to legitimate job requirements.

> **Employment Choice != Unlimited Discretion Where Fair-Treatment Duty Applies.**

Relevant criteria can include:
- competence;
- reliability;
- availability;
- lawful safety requirements;
- role-specific experience.

Irrelevant identity criteria should not substitute for these.

---

## 27. Private association

Participants retain autonomy to form many private relationships and associations.

> **Freedom Of Association != General Public-Service Exclusion Authority.**

But equally:

> **Fair-Treatment Law != Universal Duty To Enter Every Private Relationship.**

Intimate/private association requires stronger autonomy protection than public/essential/entrusted decision functions.

---

## 28. Safe spaces

Some bounded safe spaces may legitimately limit entry based on context-specific protective purpose.

Example categories may include:
- recovery/support spaces;
- developmental spaces;
- confidential professional contexts;
- high-security technical areas.

> **Bounded Safe Space != Automatic Unlawful Discrimination.**

But the exclusion must remain connected to the safe-space function rather than becoming a pretext for unrelated status exclusion.

---

## 29. Property

Property ownership gives substantial control over access but not necessarily unlimited discriminatory authority where property is used for a regulated public-facing/essential function.

> **Property Right != Automatic Exemption From Every Fair-Access Duty.**

Exact boundaries require Commerce/Housing law.

---

## 30. Housing

Housing combines property rights with consequential civil access.

Potential fair-treatment duties may apply strongly to market/public allocation while preserving genuine shared-home/intimate association distinctions.

This requires specialised implementation.

---

## 31. Healthcare

Healthcare decisions may legitimately distinguish by:
- clinical need;
- expected benefit;
- urgency;
- compatibility;
- resource availability.

They should not rank participant worth by unrelated social status.

> **Clinical Relevance != Social Worth.**

---

## 32. Scarcity

Scarce resources necessarily require allocation criteria.

> **Scarcity != Permission For Arbitrary Preference.**

Criteria should be:
- relevant;
- transparent where possible;
- consistent;
- reviewable;
- ethically derived.

---

## 33. Education

Educational pathways may distinguish by demonstrated prerequisite competence or developmental need.

> **Open Access != Automatic Placement Into Any Level Regardless Of Readiness.**

But gatekeeping based on prestige, ancestry, wealth or unrelated status requires justification.

---

## 34. Accreditation/licensing

Certification may legitimately depend on competence.

> **Licence Requirement != Legitimate Monopoly By Default.**

Requirements must serve the protected function and avoid unnecessary exclusion.

---

## 35. Public services

Public civil services carry strong fair-treatment duties because their authority/resources are entrusted.

> **Public Service != Personal Favour.**

Eligibility criteria must be tied to legitimate programme/function purposes.

---

## 36. Voting and participation

The Concord already contemplates participation levels and contextual rights.

Differences must derive from legitimate participation/capacity architecture, not arbitrary identity hierarchy.

> **Different Participation State != Lesser Human Or Participant Worth.**

---

## 37. Automated decision systems

Algorithmic systems can reproduce or amplify illegitimate criteria.

Relevant questions:
- what variables are used?
- what proxies exist?
- what objective is optimised?
- what error patterns occur?
- can the participant contest?
- can decisions be explained sufficiently for review?

> **Automation != Neutrality.**

---

## 38. Proxy discrimination

A formally neutral variable can function as a proxy for an irrelevant characteristic.

> **Neutral Label != Neutral Effect Or Purpose.**

But correlation alone does not prove intentional discrimination.

Systemic/regulatory analysis may be needed separately from individual culpability.

---

## 39. Intentional discrimination

Candidate criminal/serious legal form requires:
- fair-treatment duty;
- materially consequential decision;
- intentional/knowing use of illegitimate criterion;
- resulting disadvantage/exclusion or direct execution thereof.

This is narrower than all unfair outcomes.

---

## 40. Systemic disparate outcomes

A system may produce persistent unequal outcomes without intentional discriminatory authorship.

That can justify:
- audit;
- causal investigation;
- correction;
- accommodation;
- redesign.

But:

> **Outcome Disparity != Automatic Proof Of Discrimination.**

Equally:

> **Absence Of Proven Intent != Proof System Is Fair.**

Civil/regulatory correction and criminal culpability must remain distinct.

---

## 41. Evidence

Potential evidence:
- decision criteria;
- communications;
- comparator cases;
- statistical patterns;
- model variables;
- departures from procedure;
- concealed motives;
- inconsistent explanations.

No single category automatically proves discrimination.

Mirrored Reality Trees are appropriate.

---

## 42. Comparator problem

Sometimes no identical comparator exists.

Fairness analysis should not fail merely because every participant is unique.

The question is whether materially relevant differences explain the treatment.

> **No Identical Comparator != No Fairness Analysis.**

---

## 43. Intersectional states

Several characteristics can interact.

The architecture should evaluate the actual decision rather than require the participant to choose one identity category.

> **Combined Disadvantage != Necessarily Reducible To One Characteristic.**

---

## 44. Positive action / corrective measures

Measures designed to remove established barriers can themselves differentiate.

They are not automatically unfair.

Relevant questions:
- what barrier is established?
- what legitimate objective exists?
- is the measure proportionate?
- is it time/review bounded?
- does it unnecessarily burden others?

> **Corrective Differentiation != Automatically Illegitimate Discrimination.**

---

## 45. Quotas

Fixed quotas can improve representation but may also create new individual fairness issues.

No universal rule is derived here.

Any use requires explicit purpose, evidence, proportionality and review.

---

## 46. Harassment

Discriminatory harassment can be represented through:
- BI-09 harassment/persistent interference;
- discriminatory motive as context/aggravation where legitimately relevant.

A separate duplicate harassment offence is unnecessary.

---

## 47. Violence motivated by identity

Physical harm remains physical harm.

Identity-targeted motive may aggravate where it evidences:
- broader intimidation;
- selection of victim due protected difference;
- community-level safe-space harm.

But:

> **Motive Does Not Create The Underlying Harm Offence.**

Aggravation must not punish mere belief independently of conduct.

---

## 48. Speech

Prejudiced or offensive belief/expression is not automatically discriminatory exclusion.

> **Prejudice != Completed Discriminatory Decision.**

Other laws may apply where conduct becomes threat, harassment, coercion or harmful falsehood.

---

## 49. Refusal

A refusal can be lawful where based on legitimate criteria.

> **Refusal != Automatically Discrimination.**

The reason, duty source and function matter.

---

## 50. Burden of explanation

Where a consequential entrusted/public decision produces apparent differential treatment, decision provenance may require the decision-maker/system to identify legitimate criteria.

This is not automatic presumption of guilt.

> **Requirement To Explain Decision != Presumption Of Discrimination.**

---

## 51. Contestability

Participants should be able to challenge:
- incorrect facts;
- irrelevant criteria;
- misapplied standards;
- inaccessible procedures;
- discriminatory proxies.

BL10 strongly supports correction.

---

## 52. Correction

Where discrimination is established, possible remedies include:
- reconsideration;
- restored access;
- accommodation;
- corrected record;
- compensation;
- process redesign;
- removal of illegitimate criterion;
- review of dependent decisions.

> **Stopping Future Discrimination != Automatically Repairing Past Exclusion.**

---

## 53. Dependency propagation

If a discriminatory decision generated downstream states, those states may require review.

Example:

**Illegitimate Exclusion From Accreditation**  
→ **Employment Rejection Based On Missing Accreditation**

Correction of the source may require review of dependent consequences.

This resembles KCS/BTA propagation.

---

## 54. Aggravation

Potential aggravators:
- abuse of public/entrusted authority;
- repeated/systemic exclusion;
- deliberate concealment;
- retaliation;
- severe livelihood/health/liberty consequences;
- targeting vulnerable participants.

---

## 55. Mitigation

Potential mitigation:
- genuine factual error;
- unclear rule;
- reasonable reliance on defective system;
- limited role;
- diminished capacity/authorship.

But system correction may still be required even where individual culpability is low.

---

## 56. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: CONDITIONAL.**

Where a participant intentionally applies an illegitimate criterion and directly executes an exclusion but external intervention prevents the final consequence, attempt may be appropriate.

Mere prejudiced thought is not attempt.

> **Bias Held != Executory Discrimination.**

---

## 57. Participation

Participation/complicity applies.

But:

> **Employment In Discriminatory Institution != Automatic Personal Liability.**

Actual knowledge, role, authority and contribution matter.

---

## 58. Organisational responsibility

An institution can maintain discriminatory rules even where no single current actor authored them.

> **Inherited Rule != Legitimate Rule.**

BL10 creates a duty to review/correct discovered defects.

Historical provenance should preserve origin without granting continuing authority.

## 60. Worked examples

### A. Safety-critical employment

A rejects B from a role because B cannot demonstrate a competence genuinely required to operate dangerous equipment.

**Result:** different outcome based on relevant functional criterion; discrimination is not established merely because B is excluded.

### B. Irrelevant ancestry criterion

A public service denies B access solely because of B's ancestry where ancestry has no legitimate relationship to eligibility.

**Result:** fair-treatment duty + irrelevant criterion + consequential exclusion supports discriminatory classification.

### C. Disability with accommodation

B has a disability affecting the standard test format but can perform the actual role with a reasonable interface adjustment. A refuses even to consider the adjustment and excludes B solely from diagnosis.

**Result:** identical procedure may not be fair comparable treatment; functional capability and reasonable accommodation require assessment.

### D. Clinical distinction

A clinician chooses treatment based on a biological characteristic directly relevant to drug safety.

**Result:** characteristic-based distinction may be legitimate because it is clinically relevant; identity difference is not automatically discrimination.

### E. Private friendship

A chooses not to form a friendship with B for a personal reason.

**Result:** no general fair-treatment duty to enter private intimate association is established merely by BL5.

### F. Public accommodation

A regulated essential service excludes B because of an unrelated political belief.

**Result:** where fair-access duty exists and belief is irrelevant to service function, exclusion may be unlawful.

### G. Algorithmic proxy

An automated hiring system uses a variable strongly proxying an irrelevant identity characteristic despite direct competence evidence being available.

**Result:** requires audit of purpose, necessity, effect, alternatives and authorship; automation does not establish neutrality.

## 61. Development result

BL5 can be translated into law without requiring either:
- universal identical treatment; or
- a permanently frozen list of forbidden distinctions.

The core architecture is:

**Fair-Treatment Duty Source**  
→ **Consequential Decision Function**  
→ **Participant State**  
→ **Decision Criteria**  
→ **Functional Relevance**  
→ **Necessity / Proportionality**  
→ **Comparator / Equivalent Relevant State**  
→ **Outcome**  
→ **Intent / Knowledge Or Systemic Effect**  
→ **Contest / Correction / Remedy**

The protected interest is:

# **Fair Comparable Treatment According To Criteria Legitimately Relevant To The Function.**

This preserves both equality and legitimate difference.

The remaining strongest protected-interest gap from the Compact Basic Law Coverage Audit is **Commons and Ecological Integrity**, where BL8 may protect shared/future interests even before an individually identifiable participant suffers direct harm.
