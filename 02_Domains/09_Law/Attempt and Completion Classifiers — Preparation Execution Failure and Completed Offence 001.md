# Attempt and Completion Classifiers — Preparation, Execution, Failure and Completed Offence 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 7 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CANDIDATE GENERAL LEGAL ARCHITECTURE / NON-CANONICAL  
**Parent:** `Legal Derivation and Consequence Architecture — Ethical Provenance, Offence Classifiers and Proportionate Response 001.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

This document develops a reusable completion-state classifier for offences where failure to achieve the prohibited intended result can itself create legal responsibility.

Attempt is not treated as a separate duplicate catalogue of offences.

Instead:

**Base Offence + ATTEMPTED**

may represent an incomplete but sufficiently executed offence where the base law permits attempt liability.

---

## 2. Why attempt requires a separate classifier

Actual outcome alone cannot represent culpability.

A participant may deliberately try to cause a prohibited result and fail through:
- victim avoidance;
- intervention by another;
- equipment failure;
- factual mistake;
- chance;
- impossibility unknown to the actor.

Therefore:

> **Failure Of Intended Result != Absence Of Attempt.**

But criminalising intention alone would violate autonomy and create unacceptable authority over thought.

Therefore:

> **Intention Alone != Attempt.**

---

## 3. Candidate progression

A candidate progression is:

**Thought / Desire**  
→ **Intention**  
→ **Preparation**  
→ **Executory Attempt**  
→ **Completion**

These are not automatically five offences or five punishable states.

They identify legally relevant transitions.

---

## 4. Thought and desire

Thought, imagination, fantasy, anger, dislike or desire alone does not constitute attempt.

> **Thought != Crime.**

The legal system should not manufacture attempt liability from private cognition absent legally relevant external conduct.

This does not prevent separately lawful protective action where independent evidence establishes a sufficiently serious present danger under another authority architecture.

Protective authority and criminal liability remain separate.

---

## 5. Intention

A participant may form a definite intention to commit an offence.

Intention is relevant evidence but does not by itself constitute an attempt.

> **Intent Without Executory Conduct != Attempt.**

This protects the distinction between internal authorship and external rights interference.

---

## 6. Preparation

Preparation can include obtaining capability, gathering information, travelling, arranging circumstances or otherwise making later execution easier.

Preparation is not necessarily attempt.

> **Preparation != Necessarily Attempt.**

Some preparatory conduct may constitute an independent offence where it independently violates a protected legal interest, but it should not be relabelled as attempt merely because later offending was contemplated.

Example:

Lawful purchase of a hammer does not become attempted physical harm because the purchaser privately contemplates an attack.

---

## 7. Executory attempt

A candidate attempt requires:

1. a base offence for which attempt liability is legally recognised;
2. the required intention concerning the prohibited result or offence;
3. conduct moving sufficiently beyond preparation into direct execution of that offence;
4. non-completion of the intended prohibited result, or incomplete satisfaction of the intended offence.

The difficult boundary is item 3.

A future test should focus on whether the participant has begun execution of the prohibited interference rather than merely creating conditions from which they could later choose to execute it.

Candidate principle:

> **Attempt Begins When Conduct Has Entered Execution Of The Intended Prohibited Interference, Not Merely Preparation For A Future Choice To Execute It.**

This remains provisional and requires scenario testing.

---

## 8. Worked hammer boundary

### State A — possession
A owns a hammer.

No attempt.

### State B — hostile thought
A thinks about striking B.

No attempt.

### State C — formed intention
A decides privately to strike B tomorrow.

Intent exists; attempt not yet established.

### State D — preparation
A carries the hammer to a location expecting B to arrive.

Strong evidence of preparation and perhaps intent; attempt not automatically established.

### State E — execution
A deliberately swings the hammer at B intending serious injury.

Execution has begun.

If B ducks and is unharmed:

**Unlawful Physical Harm + ATTEMPTED + Intended H3/H4 candidate + Actual H0**

If the hammer strikes and causes H3:

the physical-harm offence is completed as to H3, while any independently established higher intended result remains preserved.

---

## 9. Attempt and actual lesser harm can coexist

Attempt should not erase completed lesser harm.

Example:

A intends to kill B and performs an executory attack. B survives with H3 injury.

The legal state may include:
- completed unlawful physical harm H3;
- attempted unlawful killing;
- intended result death/H5;
- actual result H3.

Therefore:

> **Attempted Greater Offence May Coexist With Completed Lesser Offence.**

Sentencing architecture must prevent unjustified double counting while preserving both factual/legal findings.

---

## 10. Impossibility

An actor may begin execution believing the offence can succeed where success is factually impossible.

Example:
A performs an executory act intended to harm B, but unknown circumstances make harm impossible.

The failure may still reveal the same authored prohibited interference.

Candidate principle:

> **Unknown Factual Impossibility Does Not Necessarily Defeat Attempt Where Intent And Executory Conduct Are Otherwise Established.**

However, imaginary or legally impossible offences require separate analysis: a participant should not be punished for attempting conduct that is not actually prohibited merely because they mistakenly believe it is illegal.

> **Belief That Law Prohibits Conduct != Creation Of A Prohibition.**

---

## 11. Voluntary abandonment

A participant may begin an attempt and then voluntarily stop.

The attempt already undertaken should not be falsified out of the historical/legal record.

However voluntary abandonment may be strongly relevant to:
- mitigation;
- present risk;
- prevention of further harm;
- rehabilitation;
- proportionality.

> **Voluntary Abandonment != Historical Non-Occurrence Of Attempt.**

Whether exceptionally early abandonment can constitute a formal defence is left open for later testing.

---

## 12. Prevention by others

If an attempt fails because another participant intervenes, the intervention does not reduce the factual completion state of the actor's conduct below attempt.

> **External Prevention != Voluntary Abandonment.**

The reason for failure is relevant to evidence and consequence but does not convert executory conduct back into preparation.

---

## 13. Attempt and mental state

Attempt ordinarily requires intention concerning the prohibited offence/result.

One cannot straightforwardly “attempt an accident.”

Likewise the concept of attempted negligence is generally incoherent because negligence concerns failure to satisfy a duty rather than intentional execution of a prohibited result.

> **Attempt Classifier Does Not Automatically Combine With Every Mental-State Classifier.**

Reckless endangerment or risk creation may instead be a completed offence/state in its own right where law validly defines it.

---

## 14. Attempt liability must be declared by the base law

Each offence should state:

**Attempt Liability: YES / NO / CONDITIONAL**

This prevents a general attempt doctrine from silently expanding every legal prohibition.

Where CONDITIONAL, the law should identify the relevant conditions.

---

## 15. Attempt and sentencing

Attempt can justify sanction even where actual harm is low or absent because culpable execution of the prohibited interference occurred.

But failure to achieve the result remains relevant.

Sentencing should preserve:
- intended result;
- actual result;
- degree of execution;
- reason for non-completion;
- voluntary cessation;
- capacity;
- aggravation;
- mitigation.

> **Attempted Offence != Automatically Same Consequence As Completed Offence.**

and:

> **No Actual Injury != No Culpability.**

Exact sentencing treatment remains for the general sentencing architecture.

---

## 16. Protection is separate

Evidence of a serious attempt may support a present-risk assessment, but protective intervention requires its own authority basis.

> **Past Attempt != Unlimited Future Protective Authority.**

Risk must remain evidence-based, current and reviewable.

---

## 17. Candidate completion states

A reusable schema may include:

**NOT EXECUTED** — thought/intention/preparation only; no attempt liability under this classifier.

**ATTEMPTED** — required intent plus sufficiently direct executory conduct; intended offence/result incomplete.

**COMPLETED** — required elements of the base offence satisfied.

Some events can contain both a completed lesser offence and an attempted greater offence.

## 19. Development result

Attempt is viable as a reusable appended classifier rather than a duplicate family of offences.

The architecture preserves four facts that conventional shorthand can blur:

**what offence was intended; what execution occurred; what result actually occurred; why completion failed.**

The remaining major development question is calibration of the preparation/execution boundary through adversarial scenarios.
