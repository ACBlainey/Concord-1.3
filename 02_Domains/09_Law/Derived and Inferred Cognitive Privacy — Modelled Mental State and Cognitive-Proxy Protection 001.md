# Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy — Modelled Mental State and Cognitive-Proxy Protection 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Domain:** Law / Research / Participation and Intelligence / Metrics
**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / COGNITIVE-PRIVACY ADJACENT ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Parent protection:** Provisional Rights Charter 002 — Article 10 Privacy and Protected Civil Space; Article 10.1 Cognitive Privacy and Integrity
**Related architecture:** Cognitive Protected Space; Participant Digital Twin; Prediction Does Not Grant Authority; Intermediary Personal-Data Boundary

## 1. Purpose

Cognitive Protected Space (CPS) protects cognition itself.

But a civilisation may learn intimate facts about a participant's likely:

- beliefs;
- preferences;
- memories;
- intentions;
- vulnerabilities;
- emotional states;
- decision patterns;
- future choices

without directly entering CPS.

Such information may be derived from:

- external behaviour;
- communications;
- purchases;
- movement;
- service interactions;
- social relationships;
- biometric/physiological signals;
- public activity;
- historical records;
- participant models;
- statistical correlations;
- AI inference.

This creates a distinct problem.

> **A model of a mind is not the mind, but sufficiently powerful models of minds can create some of the same risks as direct cognitive access.**

This architecture therefore develops **Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy (DICP)** without falsely reclassifying external evidence as participant cognition.

---

## 2. Critical distinction from CPS

The distinction must remain explicit.

### Direct cognitive access

Civilisation accesses state/process materially constituting or supporting participant cognition.

This engages **Cognitive Protected Space**.

### Derived/inferred cognition

Civilisation uses information outside CPS to infer or model participant cognitive state.

This engages **Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy** where the resulting model reaches relevant intimacy, fidelity, consequence or substitutive function.

Therefore:

**Inference About Cognition != Direct Access To Cognition**

but:

**No Direct Cognitive Access != No Cognitive-Privacy Harm**

DICP is adjacent to CPS, not an expansion of the CPS boundary.

---

## 3. Existing Concord source resolution

This architecture does not begin from zero.

### Participant Digital Twin

Existing development already requires participant models to preserve epistemic distinctions such as:

- OBSERVED;
- PARTICIPANT-REPORTED;
- VERIFIED;
- INFERRED;
- HYPOTHESISED;
- PREDICTED;
- DISPUTED;
- UNKNOWN;
- REVISED;
- REJECTED.

It also establishes:

> **The Digital Twin is a model of the participant. It is not the participant.**

and:

> **The model should preserve how it knows what it claims to know.**

DICP adopts these distinctions.

### Prediction Does Not Grant Authority

CP-03 establishes:

> **Prediction may supply evidence for attention, preparation, contextual examination or the expansion of available options, but prediction alone should neither create nor automatically escalate legitimate authority over a participant.**

Relevant derived rules already include:

- Population Pattern Is Not Individual Proof;
- Indicator Is Not Conclusion;
- Unrealised Futures Are Not Completed Acts;
- No Punishment for Predicted Futures;
- Models Generate Questions, Not Guilt;
- Prediction Should Expand Options;
- Evidence Before Predictive Investigation.

DICP treats these as upstream safeguards.

### Metrics and anonymisation

Existing Metrics architecture prefers structural learning without unnecessary individual observation and distinguishes:

- personal operational pathways;
- anonymised civil-learning pathways;
- exceptional identifiable pathways.

DICP should preserve this preference.

## 4. Why ordinary privacy alone may be insufficient

Traditional information privacy often asks:

> What data about the participant was collected?

Inference systems introduce another question:

> **What new participant information was constructed from data that may individually appear innocuous?**

Example:

A system may collect no explicit statement of:

- political belief;
- religious belief;
- medical vulnerability;
- addiction risk;
- relationship instability;
- fear;
- intention to resign;
- likely future behaviour.

Yet a model may infer those states with high confidence.

Therefore:

**Low-Sensitivity Inputs != Low-Sensitivity Derived Output**

The sensitivity of a derived model cannot be determined solely from the sensitivity of each source variable.

---

## 5. Derived cognitive model

Candidate definition:

> **A Derived Cognitive Model is a representation, score, classification, probability, embedding, profile or other inference concerning a participant's likely cognitive, preferential, intentional, emotional, deliberative or memory-related state, produced without directly reading the participant's Cognitive Protected Space.**

Examples may include:

- inferred preference profile;
- likely belief classification;
- predicted intention;
- behavioural susceptibility model;
- inferred emotional state;
- estimated cognitive impairment;
- likely memory/knowledge possession;
- persuasion-response model;
- decision-style model;
- predicted future choice.

Not every model about a participant is cognitive.

Examples that may be non-cognitive include:

- height estimate;
- current account balance;
- vehicle ownership;
- externally observed location;
- verified qualification.

Classification depends on what the model claims to represent.

---

## 6. Epistemic provenance

Every consequential derived cognitive claim should preserve how it arose.

Candidate chain:

**External Observations**
+
**Participant-Provided Information**
+
**Authorised Records**
+
**Population/Structural Model**
+
**Inference Method**
↓
**Derived Cognitive Claim**
↓
**Confidence / Uncertainty / Alternatives**
↓
**Purpose-Bounded Use**

The model should distinguish:

**Observed Fact**
from
**Inferred Cognitive State**
from
**Predicted Future State**
from
**Institutional Interpretation**

Therefore:

**Inference != Observation**

**Prediction != Present Fact**

**Model Confidence != Ontological Certainty**

---

## 7. Fidelity gradient

Derived cognitive models exist on a gradient.

### Low cognitive fidelity

Broad non-individual or weak inference.

Example:

“Most users of this service prefer morning appointments.”

### Moderate cognitive fidelity

Individual preference inference relevant to a bounded service.

Example:

“Participant usually chooses morning appointments.”

### High cognitive fidelity

Detailed individual reconstruction of beliefs, vulnerabilities, intentions or decision processes.

Example:

“Participant is highly susceptible to message type X when distressed and is likely to choose Y after trigger Z.”

### Cognitive-proxy fidelity

A model becomes sufficiently detailed that it can functionally substitute for some direct access to cognition for prediction, manipulation or consequential classification.

DICP protection should scale with the actual function and consequence rather than applying one binary rule to every inference.

---

## 8. Cognitive-proxy threshold

Candidate concept:

> **A derived model approaches a Cognitive Proxy where its fidelity and functional use allow another actor to predict, reproduce, exploit or act upon participant cognitive tendencies at a level materially comparable to possessing otherwise protected internal cognitive information.**

This does not make the proxy the participant.

**Cognitive Proxy != Cognitive State**

But higher proxy fidelity may justify stronger:

- purpose limitation;
- access restriction;
- participant visibility;
- contestability;
- retention limits;
- secondary-use controls;
- authority requirements.

---

## 9. Construction can itself be the privacy event

A privacy architecture focused only on disclosure can miss the point.

The harm may arise when the model is constructed.

Example:

Publicly observable actions may be individually legitimate to observe.

Their aggregation may permit reconstruction of:

- private belief;
- health state;
- vulnerability;
- intimate preference.

Therefore:

**Lawful Observation Of Inputs != Unlimited Authority To Construct Every Possible Inference**

and:

**Data Accessibility != Unlimited Derivation Authority**

The legitimacy of observation and the legitimacy of inference/use are separable.

---

## 10. Aggregation and composition

Many low-sensitivity data points may collectively reveal high-sensitivity cognitive information.

Therefore:

**Individually Low-Sensitivity Inputs May Produce High-Sensitivity Aggregate Inference**

This parallels CPS aggregate reconstruction.

DICP analysis should consider the effective derived result, not merely each source field.

---

## 11. Participant Digital Twin interface

A Digital Twin may contain derived cognitive information where legitimately relevant.

But:

**Purpose-Limited Twin != General Cognitive Profile**

A housing-support model may legitimately contain:

- preferred communication method;
- stated housing goals;
- relevant support needs.

It does not automatically need:

- inferred political belief;
- sexual preference;
- unrelated fears;
- persuasion vulnerabilities;
- criminal-risk prediction.

The existing rule remains:

> **as detailed as necessary for its legitimate purpose, but no more detailed than that purpose requires.**

DICP makes cognitive inference one of the dimensions subject to that minimisation.

---

## 12. Support versus manipulation

Predictive Support may infer likely needs/preferences in order to expand participant options.

That is materially different from using the same model to manipulate the participant toward an institutional objective.

Therefore:

**Prediction For Participant Support != Authority For Behavioural Manipulation**

A support system should ordinarily:

- preserve options;
- expose useful choices;
- remain corrigible;
- respect refusal;
- avoid covert coercive optimisation.

A model of participant vulnerability should not silently become an exploitation interface.

---

## 13. Prediction does not grant authority

DICP incorporates CP-03 directly:

**Prediction != Authority**

A high-confidence inference that a participant:

- may offend;
- may relapse;
- may leave employment;
- may become ill;
- may refuse treatment;
- may become politically active;
- may default;
- may self-isolate

does not by itself create authority to impose a consequential restriction.

Independent civil basis remains necessary.

---

## 14. Cognitive inference and investigation

Legitimate external evidence may support investigative hypotheses.

Investigators remain permitted to reason.

DICP must not make ordinary inference impossible.

Example:

- participant buys travel ticket;
- CCTV shows departure;
- message says “I am leaving”;
- investigators infer participant intended to leave.

This is ordinary evidential reasoning.

DICP becomes more significant where systems construct persistent, high-fidelity, participant-specific cognitive profiles or use inference as a substitute for otherwise protected cognitive access.

Therefore:

**Evidential Reasoning != Automatically Prohibited Cognitive Profiling**

Context, scale, persistence, fidelity, purpose and consequence matter.

---

## 15. Cognitive inference cannot bootstrap direct CPS access

A model may predict that participant cognition contains useful evidence.

That prediction does not itself create authority to enter CPS.

**Inferred Cognitive Relevance != Cognitive Search Authority**

This prevents:

external behaviour
→ cognitive inference
→ claimed probable internal evidence
→ direct cognitive search

from becoming a self-authorising loop.

---

## 16. Sensitive cognitive categories

Some inferred states may warrant heightened caution because misuse can materially affect dignity, autonomy or vulnerability.

Possible examples include inferred:

- belief/conscience;
- intimate preference;
- trauma;
- cognitive impairment;
- fear;
- addiction;
- mental-health-like state;
- susceptibility to persuasion;
- confidential relationship;
- intention concerning protected association/disassociation;
- protected refusal.

This is not yet an exhaustive constitutional category list.

Sensitivity should remain function/context dependent.

---

## 17. Inference about belief and conscience

The Charter protects belief and conscience.

A participant may choose to express belief publicly.

But persistent covert reconstruction of private belief from unrelated behaviour raises a distinct privacy question.

Therefore:

**Protected Belief != Prohibition On All External Inference**

but:

**Ability To Infer Private Belief != Unlimited Authority To Profile Or Act Upon It**

Any consequential use must remain compatible with fairness, autonomy, privacy and lawful authority.

---

## 18. Inference about capacity

A model may infer reduced capacity from:

- communication pattern;
- response delay;
- unusual choices;
- disagreement;
- disability markers.

Existing Concord architecture already states:

- capacity is not intelligence;
- refusal is not incapacity;
- communication difficulty is not incapacity;
- unconventional choice is not incapacity.

Therefore:

**Capacity Inference != Capacity Determination**

and:

**Modelled Difficulty != Loss Of Standing**

Consequential capacity decisions require the proper capacity architecture.

---

## 19. Inference about criminality

Population or behavioural models may estimate offence risk.

Existing CP-03 already establishes:

**Population Pattern Is Not Individual Proof**

**Models Generate Questions, Not Guilt**

DICP adds:

**Cognitive/Intent Inference != Evidence That Predicted Intent Became Act**

Criminal investigation/adjudication remains evidence- and authority-bound.

---

## 20. Persuasion and susceptibility models

A sufficiently detailed model may predict how to alter participant behaviour.

This creates a direct autonomy risk even if the underlying model is accurate.

Examples:

- optimal fear trigger;
- ideal pressure timing;
- personalised deception susceptibility;
- emotional leverage;
- dependency exploitation.

Candidate rule:

> **Greater ability to predict how to influence a participant creates greater responsibility not to convert that knowledge into covert domination.**

This follows BL2, BL6 and BL7.

---

## 21. Participant access and contestability

Where a derived cognitive model materially affects consequential civil treatment, the participant should ordinarily have a viable route to know enough about:

- existence of the model;
- relevant claims;
- epistemic status;
- purpose;
- material inputs/categories;
- consequential use;
- correction/challenge route.

This does not necessarily require disclosure of:

- protected third-party information;
- security-sensitive implementation;
- every model parameter;
- information that would create serious legitimate harm.

But:

**Secret Consequential Cognitive Classification -> Elevated Contestability Concern**

Article 16 Consequential Self-Relevant Information and Article 9 Contestability apply.

---

## 22. Correction versus disagreement

Some derived cognitive claims cannot be “corrected” like a date of birth.

Example:

“Participant has 70% probability of preferring X.”

The participant may disagree.

The system should preserve:

- participant statement;
- model basis;
- uncertainty;
- alternative interpretation;
- later outcome.

It should not treat disagreement as confirmation.

Existing Digital Twin rule remains:

> **A participant disagreeing with the Digital Twin should not itself automatically become evidence that the participant is unreliable, incapable or higher risk.**

---

## 23. Model expiry

Cognitive models can become stale rapidly.

Preferences, beliefs, intentions and emotional states change.

Therefore:

**Past Cognitive Inference != Current Cognitive State**

High-consequence uses should consider:

- model age;
- changed circumstances;
- contradictory evidence;
- participant correction;
- model version;
- uncertainty.

Persistent historical models must not silently become permanent identity.

---

## 24. Purpose conversion

A model created for participant support should not silently become:

- criminal-risk scoring;
- employment screening;
- political classification;
- insurance-like exclusion;
- behavioural manipulation.

Therefore:

**Support Model != General Authority Model**

and:

**Purpose A Cognitive Inference != Purpose B Use Authority**

Secondary use requires independent legitimacy.

---

## 25. Population models versus individual models

Aggregate patterns can be valuable for:

- service design;
- resource planning;
- safety;
- research;
- system improvement.

Where the objective can be achieved without individual cognitive profiling, prefer the less intrusive route.

**System-Level Learning != Need For Individual Cognitive Reconstruction**

This aligns with the existing Metrics preference for structural learning without unnecessary identity.

---

## 26. Anonymisation and re-identification

An anonymised cognitive pattern may become identifiable when combined with other information.

Therefore:

**Anonymised At Source != Permanently Non-Identifiable**

Resolution, rarity, linkage and external data matter.

Metrics architecture should continue to minimise unnecessary resolution and monitor re-identification risk.

---

## 27. Group inference

A system may infer participant characteristics from group membership.

Example:

“Members of group G usually believe X; participant belongs to G; therefore participant probably believes X.”

This is weak individual evidence and may create unfair treatment.

Therefore:

**Group Correlation != Individual Cognitive Fact**

and existing fairness rules apply.

---

## 28. Self-fulfilling models

A cognitive model can change the participant's environment in ways that cause the prediction to become true.

Example:

Model predicts participant is unreliable
→ opportunities removed
→ participant cannot perform
→ system records failure
→ model appears confirmed.

Therefore:

**Model-Induced Outcome != Independent Validation Of Model**

Consequential systems should track interventions that may alter the measured outcome.

---

## 29. Model feedback contamination

Where model outputs affect future source data, later training/validation may become circular.

Candidate provenance:

**Pre-Model Observation**
+
**Model Output**
+
**Intervention**
+
**Post-Intervention Observation**

must remain distinguishable.

Otherwise:

**Prediction**
→ **Intervention**
→ **Changed Behaviour**
→ **Training Data**
→ **Apparent Confirmation**

can create a self-sealing loop.

---

## 30. Inferred memory possession

A system may infer that a participant probably knows/remembers X.

This does not make X an external evidential object.

**Likely Knowledge != External Evidence Object**

and:

**Likely Knowledge != Cognitive Disclosure Obligation**

subject to any separately justified exceptional rule.

---

## 31. Derived models of protected consultation

External metadata may permit inference about fiduciary, medical or other protected consultation.

Example:

timing and behaviour suggest a likely legal strategy.

Even without reading the consultation itself, exploitation of such inference may undermine the protected wrapper.

Therefore:

**Inference Around Protected Context != Automatic Permission To Defeat Protected Context**

This requires context-specific rules and should not be treated as direct CPS access.

---

## 32. Model access

Access to a high-fidelity cognitive proxy may itself be sensitive.

Potential access classes may distinguish:

- participant;
- originating support service;
- authorised analyst;
- Health;
- Civil Security;
- Judiciary;
- anonymised Research/Metrics;
- public.

**Model Exists != Universal Access**

Access remains function-specific.

---

## 33. Model copying and persistence

Derived cognitive profiles can proliferate more easily than cognition itself.

Copies may outlive:

- purpose;
- source accuracy;
- participant relationship;
- model validity.

Therefore high-fidelity cognitive models should have explicit:

- purpose;
- provenance;
- access;
- retention;
- update;
- correction;
- deletion/archive;
- secondary-use rules.

Historical preservation, where justified, must distinguish:

**Historical Model Existence**
from
**Current Operational Validity**

---

## 34. Derived cognition and Historical

Historical may legitimately preserve provenance of consequential models.

But:

**Historically Predicted != Currently True**

**Historical Model != Current Participant State**

and:

**Historical Preservation != Operational Authority**

Old cognitive models should not become permanent civil labels merely because provenance is preserved.

---

## 35. Derived cognition and Research

Research may need cognitive-pattern data.

Where individual identity is unnecessary:

**Research Need -> Prefer Anonymised / Aggregated Cognitive Pattern**

rather than persistent identified cognitive profiles.

Where identified research is legitimately necessary, authority/consent and protections should be explicit.

---

## 36. Model minimisation

The correct design question is not:

> “How accurately can we model this participant?”

but:

> **“What minimum participant model is adequate for the legitimate function?”**

This follows existing Digital Twin and Metrics architecture.

Greater predictive fidelity is not automatically a civil good.

**More Accurate Cognitive Model != Automatically More Legitimate Cognitive Model**

---

## 37. Cognitive-proxy escalation test

Where a derived model becomes more intimate/fidelitous, protection should increase.

Candidate questions:

1. Does the model infer cognition rather than external condition?
2. Is it participant-specific?
3. How intimate is the inferred state?
4. How confident is the inference?
5. How persistent is the model?
6. How broad is its purpose?
7. Can it predict participant decisions?
8. Can it identify vulnerabilities?
9. Can it enable manipulation?
10. Can it substitute for information otherwise sought from CPS?
11. Is it used consequentially?
12. Can the participant contest it?
13. Can the model become stale?
14. Can it be combined with other models?
15. Is a less intrusive model adequate?

No single factor creates a binary threshold.

The effective risk is multidimensional.

---

## 38. Candidate protection gradient

### DICP-0 — ordinary non-cognitive inference

No special cognitive-proxy treatment; ordinary applicable data/evidence rules.

### DICP-1 — bounded low-fidelity cognitive inference

Purpose limitation, epistemic provenance, ordinary privacy/contestability.

### DICP-2 — consequential participant-specific cognitive model

Stronger access control, participant visibility/contestability, retention and secondary-use limits.

### DICP-3 — high-fidelity cognitive proxy

Strong purpose/authority limitation, minimum necessary construction, heightened access control, anti-manipulation safeguards, robust provenance and review.

### DICP-4 — functionally substitutive cognitive proxy

Where model use materially approximates otherwise protected cognitive access, require explicit review against Article 10.1 and CPS-adjacent protections even though the model remains derived rather than direct cognition.

These levels are developmental, not yet legal classes.

## 41. Relationship to Cognitive Protected Space

CPS asks:

> **May civilisation access this participant cognitive state/process?**

DICP asks:

> **May civilisation construct, retain, access or consequentially use this model of participant cognition from information outside that cognitive state?**

The two architectures should remain linked but separate.

A civilisation should not evade CPS by constructing a near-equivalent cognitive proxy and then claim:

> “We never read the mind.”

Equally, DICP should not pretend that every inference from external evidence is direct mind-reading.

The boundary must remain truthful in both directions.

---

## 42. Development conclusion

Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy fills the gap exposed by the CPS stress test while preserving legitimate evidential reasoning, participant support, research and Metrics.

The central rule is:

> **A model of a participant is not the participant, but increasing power to reconstruct the participant's inner state creates increasing responsibility over why that model is built, who may use it, what authority it can support, and how it can be challenged.**

The next step should adversarially stress-test DICP against:

- ordinary human inference;
- police investigation;
- personalised advertising;
- employment screening;
- Health prediction;
- participant support;
- political/religious belief inference;
- high-confidence criminal-risk prediction;
- persuasion models;
- public-source intelligence;
- group inference;
- anonymous models;
- Digital Twins;
- self-fulfilling predictions;
- AI participants whose external behaviour exposes unusually accurate internal-state inference.
