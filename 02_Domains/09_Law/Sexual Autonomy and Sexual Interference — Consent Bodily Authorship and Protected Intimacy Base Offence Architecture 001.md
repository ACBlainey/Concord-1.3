## 1. Purpose

This document derives candidate fundamental law protecting sexual autonomy and intimate bodily authorship.

It deliberately avoids defining the fundamental offence through a catalogue of body parts, mechanisms or historically named acts.

Instead:

**Protected Sexual/Bodily Autonomy**  
→ **Specific Interaction**  
→ **Consent / Capacity / Authorship State**  
→ **Scope / Continuity / Withdrawal**  
→ **Interference**  
→ **Result / Harm / Culpability**

The general Concord architectures for consent, physical harm, coercion, privacy, mental state, attempt, participation, aggravation/mitigation and contextual wrappers apply.

---

## 3. Protected interest

The protected interest is:

> **Sexual And Intimate Bodily Authorship.**

This includes control over whether, how, with whom and for how long a participant engages in protected intimate interaction, subject to capacity and other legitimate legal constraints.

> **Sexual Access != Entitlement.**

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not intentionally or knowingly initiate, perform, continue or cause a sexual or protected intimate bodily interaction with another participant where valid consent for that interaction is absent, withdrawn, exceeded or invalid.**

Where coercion, deception, incapacity or other conditions defeat meaningful authorship, apparent compliance does not necessarily establish consent.

---

## 5. Decision-specific consent

Consent must relate to the actual interaction.

> **Consent To Act A != Consent To Act B.**

> **Consent To One Participant != Consent To Another Participant.**

> **Consent At Time T1 != Automatic Consent At Time T2.**

The existing Consent and Withdrawal architecture applies directly.

---

## 6. Relationship status

Marriage, partnership, dating, cohabitation, prior sexual relationship or emotional intimacy does not create permanent sexual authority.

> **Relationship != Continuing Sexual Consent.**

> **Past Sexual Consent != Present Sexual Consent.**

This follows:

**Past Consent != Continuing Consent.**

---

## 7. Withdrawal

Consent can be withdrawn during an interaction.

No special phrase is required.

> **Consent To Begin != Consent To Continue Indefinitely.**

Once withdrawal is communicated or otherwise sufficiently clear, continuation must cease as soon as reasonably practicable.

> **Withdrawal != Retroactive Invalidity Of Earlier Consented Conduct.**

The legal state can therefore change during one continuous event.

---

## 8. Scope

Consent may be bounded by:
- type of interaction;
- participant;
- protective condition;
- location/context;
- duration;
- recording/privacy condition;
- other material condition.

> **Consent Within Scope != Consent Beyond Scope.**

A deliberate material departure can change the legal state.

---

## 9. Capacity

Valid consent requires decision-specific capacity.

> **Capacity For Decision A != Automatic Capacity For Decision B.**

Capacity may be affected by:
- unconsciousness;
- severe intoxication;
- cognitive impairment;
- acute medical state;
- mental-health state;
- developmental status;
- other functional impairment.

Diagnostic label alone is not decisive.

> **Diagnosis != Consent Incapacity.**

The question is functional ability to author the relevant decision.

---

## 10. Unconsciousness and sleep

A participant who is unconscious or asleep ordinarily cannot contemporaneously author a new sexual decision.

Prior agreements concerning specific contexts require careful treatment and cannot become unlimited waivers.

> **Prior Relationship Or General Permission != Unlimited Authority Over An Unconscious Participant.**

---

## 11. Intoxication

Intoxication does not produce a simple binary rule where any alcohol/drug use eliminates consent.

The relevant question is functional decision capacity and authorship.

> **Intoxication != Automatic Incapacity.**

> **Severe Incapacity != Valid Consent Merely Because Words Were Spoken.**

Evidence must assess the participant's actual functional state.

---

## 12. Coercion

Consent produced by sufficiently serious illegitimate coercion may be invalid.

The Threats, Coercion and Extortion architecture applies.

> **Submission != Necessarily Consent.**

> **Absence Of Physical Resistance != Consent.**

A participant may comply because of fear, freezing, dependency or threat.

---

## 13. Deception

Some deception may materially defeat authorship; other deception may be morally objectionable without invalidating consent to the physical interaction.

The Fraud/Deceptive Acquisition and Consent architectures provide the test:

**Deception**  
→ **Material To This Decision?**  
→ **Did It Defeat Meaningful Authorship Of This Interaction?**

> **Any Lie In A Relationship != Automatic Sexual Non-Consent.**

The legal boundary must remain tied to material decision authorship.

---

## 14. Identity deception

Where consent is specifically authored toward participant A, deliberate substitution/impersonation by participant B can defeat that authorship.

> **Consent To A != Consent To B Through Deception.**

Identity materiality depends on the actual decision.

---

## 15. Conditional consent

A participant may consent subject to a material protective or reproductive condition.

Deliberate violation of a condition material to the participant's decision may alter the consent state.

The precise legal treatment requires evidence of:
- communicated condition;
- materiality;
- knowledge;
- intentional departure.

> **Private Assumption != Communicated Consent Condition.**

---

## 16. Silence

> **Silence != Automatic Consent.**

Likewise:

> **Failure To Resist != Automatic Consent.**

But law should evaluate the complete interaction rather than demand ritualised verbal formulas in every consensual encounter.

Consent may be communicated through words or conduct where meaningful authorship is reasonably clear.

---

## 17. Ambiguity

Where consent is genuinely uncertain, participants should clarify rather than assume authority over another's body.

> **Uncertainty About Consent != Permission To Proceed.**

This is especially important where context has materially changed.

---

## 18. Mistake about consent

A participant may honestly misunderstand another's communication.

The legal system must distinguish:
- intentional disregard of non-consent;
- knowing absence of consent;
- recklessness concerning consent;
- genuine reasonable mistake.

> **Non-Consent In Fact != Automatic Proof Of Identical Culpability In Every Case.**

Protected autonomy remains violated while culpability classification remains separately assessed.

---

## 19. Physical force

Physical force is not a required element of sexual interference.

> **No Force != No Sexual Violation.**

Equally, consensual sexual activity may involve agreed physical force within a bounded context.

> **Physical Force != Automatic Non-Consent.**

Consent, scope and harm remain controlling.

This is a direct contextual-wrapper application.

---

## 20. Contextual wrappers

Some consensual activities intentionally permit conduct that outside the context could appear aggressive, restrictive or harmful.

Examples may include bounded sexual practices.

CWA requires:
- legible context;
- valid entry;
- specific consent;
- safe withdrawal;
- scope;
- limits;
- no export of unconsented harm.

> **Consent To Context != Consent To Every Act Within Context.**

> **Entry Into Wrapper != Unlimited Waiver.**

---

## 21. Physical harm

Consensual sexual activity can sometimes involve foreseeable minor harm or risk.

Consent does not automatically authorise unlimited injury.

The Physical Harm architecture applies.

> **Sexual Consent != Unlimited Consent To Physical Harm.**

Severe or catastrophic harm may exceed legitimate consent/context depending on the general harm and BCA architecture.

---

## 22. Privacy

Sexual/intimate activity occurs within especially strong privacy contexts.

Consent to participate does not imply consent to:
- record;
- transmit;
- publish;
- show others;
- retain intimate media beyond agreed scope.

> **Consent To Intimacy != Consent To Recording.**

> **Consent To Recording != Consent To Publication.**

The Privacy architecture applies independently.

---

## 23. Intimate images

Creating, retaining or sharing intimate images may engage distinct privacy and consent states.

A participant may validly consent to creation but not onward disclosure.

> **Lawful Creation != Unlimited Distribution Authority.**

Future information/privacy law may require a dedicated intimate-media implementation without changing the underlying principle.

---

## 24. Third-party presence

Consent to interaction with one participant does not automatically authorise observation or participation by others.

> **Consent To Participant A != Consent To Hidden Participant B.**

This may engage privacy and sexual-autonomy interests simultaneously.

---

## 25. Authority

Ordinary civil authority does not create sexual access.

> **Official Authority != Sexual Authority.**

> **Custody Authority != Sexual Consent.**

> **Treatment Authority != Sexual Consent.**

Abuse of official, custodial, medical, fiduciary or dependency relationships may aggravate responsibility and may affect whether consent is meaningfully authored.

---

## 26. Dependency and power imbalance

Power imbalance does not automatically eliminate consent.

But it may affect:
- voluntariness;
- coercion;
- capacity to refuse safely;
- evidential assessment;
- aggravation.

> **Power Imbalance != Automatic Non-Consent.**

> **Power Imbalance != Irrelevant To Consent.**

Both errors should be avoided.

---

## 27. Employment and services

Employment, housing, healthcare, education, support or access to essential services must not be conditioned on sexual submission outside independently lawful arrangements.

Using control of such resources to compel sexual conduct may engage coercion.

> **Service Dependency != Sexual Access Entitlement.**

---

## 28. Payment and exchange

Exchange of value does not erase sexual autonomy.

> **Payment != Ownership Of Sexual Access.**

> **Agreement To Exchange != Irrevocable Consent.**

Withdrawal may create contractual/economic questions but does not create authority to continue sexual interaction without consent.

---

## 29. Age/developmental protection

Developmental capacity requires dedicated implementation.

The Concord already recognises Developmental Priority and capacity-sensitive authority.

A future legal implementation must define:
- developmental thresholds;
- close-age contexts;
- exploitation;
- guardianship limits;
- evolving capacity.

This document does not invent arbitrary numerical thresholds without source-resolving the broader developmental architecture.

> **Guardian Consent != Substitute For A Participant's Sexual Consent.**

Protective authority cannot convert another participant into an object of authorised sexual access.

---

## 30. Incapacitated participants

Where a participant lacks capacity for sexual consent, another person cannot manufacture sexual permission through guardianship, medical authority or substituted judgment.

> **Protective Authority != Sexual Consent Authority.**

This is a particularly strong boundary.

---

## 31. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: YES.**

Where a participant intentionally enters direct execution of a non-consensual sexual interference but is prevented before completion, the general Attempt architecture applies.

Preparation alone remains insufficient.

---

## 32. Participation and facilitation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

Potential roles:
- principal;
- co-principal;
- director;
- knowing assistant;
- knowing facilitator.

> **Presence != Automatic Participation.**

> **Association With Offender != Complicity.**

Knowing assistance in creating or sustaining the non-consensual situation may nevertheless establish participation.

---

## 33. Mental state

Relevant mental states may include:
- intentional conduct knowing consent absent;
- knowing conduct;
- recklessness concerning consent;
- genuine mistake concerning consent.

The exact threshold for criminal liability requires later procedural/legal testing.

Negligence should not be silently imported into serious intentional sexual offences without explicit derivation.

---

## 34. Harm

Potential harms include:
- bodily injury;
- loss of sexual autonomy;
- privacy harm;
- psychological/neurological harm;
- reproductive consequence;
- infection/disease consequence;
- coercive dependency;
- loss of safe space;
- social/economic consequence.

> **No Visible Injury != No Sexual Harm.**

But harm severity and culpability remain separate dimensions.

---

## 35. Reproductive consequences

Sexual activity may carry reproductive consequences.

Consent to sexual activity does not automatically resolve every later reproductive decision or parental/legal status.

Those require separate Health/family/legal architecture.

> **Consent To Sex != Advance Consent To Every Possible Consequence Or Later Decision.**

---

## 36. Infection risk and disclosure

Known material transmissible health risks can affect meaningful authorship.

The exact disclosure duty should be derived through Health, privacy, harm, material-risk and consent architecture rather than stigma.

> **Diagnosis != Automatic Sexual Prohibition.**

> **Material Risk May Require Information Necessary For Authored Consent.**

Proportionality and actual transmission risk matter.

---

## 37. Evidence

Sexual cases can involve difficult evidence states.

The same legal protections apply:

> **Accusation != Guilt.**

> **Absence Of Physical Injury != Proof Of Consent.**

> **Prior Sexual History != Automatic Evidence Of Present Consent.**

> **Delayed Report != Automatic Proof Of Falsity.**

Evidence should be assessed through the general Judiciary/evidence architecture, with mirrored inculpatory/exculpatory Reality Trees where useful.

---

## 38. Prior history

Past consensual conduct between the same participants can provide context but cannot establish present consent by itself.

> **Past Consent != Present Proof Of Consent.**

Likewise, prior non-consensual conduct does not prove a later allegation automatically.

Each event requires evidence.

---

## 39. Reporting and false accusation

Good-faith reporting protections from the Harmful False Factual Assertion architecture apply.

> **Failure To Prove Sexual Allegation != Automatic False Accusation.**

Knowingly fabricated allegations remain separately actionable.

This preserves both complainant access to justice and accused-participant rights.

---

## 40. Protective measures

Current protective need must remain separate from retrospective guilt.

> **Protective Measure != Finding Of Guilt.**

Temporary no-contact or safe-space measures may sometimes be justified on a protective threshold while adjudication remains unresolved, subject to proportionality and review.

---

## 41. Remedy

Potential remedies include:
- immediate cessation/protection;
- health care;
- privacy containment;
- correction/removal of unlawfully distributed material;
- support;
- compensation for established loss;
- safe-space restoration;
- other participant-directed restoration where possible.

Remedy must not require unwanted continuing contact with the offender.

---

## 42. Sanction, protection and rehabilitation

The general Sentencing and Legal Consequence architecture applies.

> **Punishment != Victim Restoration.**

> **Treatment != Punishment.**

> **Present Dangerousness != Historical Culpability.**

Where custody is justified, custody function and destination remain separately assessed.

## 44. Worked examples

### A. Withdrawal during consensual interaction

A and B validly consent to an interaction. B then clearly withdraws consent. A knowingly continues beyond the point at which stopping is reasonably practicable.

**Result:** earlier conduct remains consented; later continuation enters a different legal state.

### B. Prior relationship

A and B have been partners for years and have previously engaged in the same activity. B does not consent on this occasion.

**Result:** relationship/history does not supply present consent.

### C. Genuine ambiguity and clarification

A is uncertain whether B wants to continue and asks. B says no. A stops.

**Result:** uncertainty was resolved without unauthorised continuation.

### D. Incapacity

A knowingly initiates sexual activity with B while B is unconscious.

**Result:** contemporaneous consent is absent; relationship/general prior permission does not create unlimited authority.

### E. Consensual bounded context

A and B explicitly consent to a bounded activity involving physical restraint, with agreed limits and withdrawal signal.

**Result:** restraint/force is not automatically unlawful merely because it would be outside the wrapper; scope and continuing consent remain controlling.

### F. Recording

A and B consent to sexual activity. A secretly records it without B's consent.

**Result:** sexual interaction may be consensual while recording independently violates privacy/intimate-context rights.

### G. Authority abuse

A custodial official conditions ordinary lawful treatment of B on sexual compliance.

**Result:** custody authority supplies no sexual authority; coercion/sexual interference and authority abuse may coexist.

## 45. Development result

Sexual law can be derived from the same general Concord architecture rather than relying on a mechanism-specific catalogue.

The protected interest is:

**decision-specific, continuing sexual and intimate bodily authorship.**

The core legal question is therefore not merely what physical act occurred, but:

**What interaction occurred?  
Was valid consent present for that interaction?  
Did the participant have decision-specific capacity?  
Was consent voluntary, informed enough for the material choice, within scope and continuing?  
Was it withdrawn or exceeded?  
What did the other participant know or consciously disregard?  
What harm resulted?**

This architecture also demonstrates that consent is a **state variable capable of changing during one continuous physical event**, closely matching the existing Concord authority principle that one event may pass through multiple legal states.

A natural next fundamental derivation is **killing and unlawful causation of death**, using H5 from the Physical Harm architecture while determining whether death should remain the highest result-state of unlawful physical harm or also require a dedicated legal implementation because of its irreversible continuity consequences.
