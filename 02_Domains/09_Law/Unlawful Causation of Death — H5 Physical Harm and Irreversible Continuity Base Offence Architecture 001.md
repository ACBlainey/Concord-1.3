## 1. Purpose

This document derives the legal treatment of death from the existing Unlawful Physical Harm architecture.

Death is already represented as:

**H5 — Death**

The purpose of this document is therefore not to invent an unrelated fundamental offence.

Instead it provides a dedicated implementation because death has:
- irreversible consequences for biological participants;
- uniquely severe continuity consequences;
- difficult causation questions;
- important distinctions between intent, knowledge, recklessness, negligence and accident;
- special interaction with self-defence, protective authority, medicine and end-of-life autonomy.

Core structure:

**Unlawful Physical Harm**  
+ **H5 Result**  
+ **Mental State**  
+ **Causation**  
+ **Consent / Context / Authority / Justification**  
+ **Capacity / Authorship**  
= **Legal Classification Of Unlawful Causation Of Death**

## 3. Death as result, not mental state

The central distinction is:

> **Death != Intent To Kill.**

The same H5 result may arise from:
- deliberate intention to kill;
- intention to cause serious harm with known fatal consequence;
- knowing conduct;
- recklessness;
- negligence where a legally sufficient duty/standard exists;
- non-culpable accident;
- justified defensive conduct;
- medical/end-of-life processes.

Therefore:

> **Outcome Severity != Mental State.**

This preserves the general Legal Mental State architecture.

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not, without lawful justification or other independently legitimate authority, intentionally, knowingly, recklessly or where specifically established negligently cause the death of another participant.**

The mental-state classifier determines the relevant responsibility state.

The H5 result does not erase those distinctions.

---

## 5. Intentional killing

Candidate classification:

**Unlawful Physical Harm**  
+ **H5 Death**  
+ **Intent To Cause Death**

This is the clearest intentional fatal-harm state.

Premeditation may aggravate but is not required for intentional killing.

> **Intentional Killing != Necessarily Premeditated Killing.**

---

## 6. Intentional serious harm resulting in death

A participant may intend severe physical harm without specifically intending death.

If death results, the legal state should preserve both:
- intended result;
- actual result.

Example:

**Intended H3/H4**  
+ **Actual H5**

This should not be falsely rewritten as proven intent to kill merely because death occurred.

> **Death Result != Retroactive Proof Of Intent To Kill.**

---

## 7. Knowing causation

A participant may act knowing death will result even where death is not their ultimate purpose.

Purpose and knowledge remain distinguishable.

> **Purpose != Knowledge Of Consequence.**

Both may establish serious culpability, but legal reasoning should preserve the actual state.

---

## 8. Reckless causation of death

Reckless fatal harm requires conscious disregard of a substantial and unjustified fatal risk under the general mental-state architecture.

> **Dangerous Outcome != Automatic Recklessness.**

Foreseeability must be assessed from the information reasonably available at the relevant time, without hindsight back-propagation.

---

## 9. Negligent causation of death

Negligent fatal harm requires:
- legitimate duty/standard;
- materially foreseeable risk;
- failure to meet the required standard;
- causation;
- death.

> **Death != Automatic Negligence.**

Bad outcomes alone do not prove breach.

Specialised contexts may include:
- professional duties;
- transport;
- safety-critical systems;
- custodial duties;
- infrastructure;
- care relationships.

---

## 10. Non-culpable accident

Death may occur without legally sufficient culpability.

> **Death != Automatic Criminal Responsibility.**

A tragic result does not justify inventing fault.

Civil support, insurance, investigation, systemic learning or other consequences may still be appropriate.

---

## 11. Causation

H5 responsibility requires a legally sufficient causal relationship between conduct and death.

Candidate questions:
1. Did the conduct materially contribute to the death?
2. Was the causal chain sufficiently connected?
3. Were there intervening causes?
4. Was the relevant result within the legally attributable risk?
5. Did an independent later act materially change the chain?

> **Temporal Sequence != Causation.**

> **Contribution != Automatically Sole Cause.**

Multiple causes may coexist.

---

## 12. Multiple contributors

Death can result from several participants' conduct.

Responsibility should track actual contribution and mental state.

> **Multiple Causes != No Individual Responsibility.**

But:

> **Presence In Causal Chain != Automatic Equal Responsibility.**

Participation and complicity remain separately analysed.

---

## 13. Medical treatment after injury

A participant who unlawfully inflicts serious injury should not automatically escape fatal causation because ordinary medical treatment later fails.

Conversely, a genuinely independent grossly abnormal intervening event may alter attribution.

This requires future causation doctrine.

> **Medical Intervention != Automatic Break In Causation.**

---

## 14. Pre-existing vulnerability

A victim's vulnerability does not erase causation merely because a healthier participant might have survived.

> **Victim Vulnerability != Permission To Cause The Harm.**

However causation must still establish that the conduct materially contributed to death.

---

## 15. Self-defence and defence of others

Fatal force can be lawful only where the independent defensive BCA supports that level of force.

Candidate:

**Immediate Threat**  
→ **Legitimate Protective Function**  
→ **Necessity**  
→ **Proportionality**  
→ **Minimum Sufficient Defensive Capability**  
→ **Fatal Result**

> **Death Caused != Automatically Unlawful Killing.**

But:

> **Past Attack != Continuing Licence To Kill.**

Threat-derived authority ends when the relevant threat ends.

---

## 16. Defence of property

Property interests do not automatically justify lethal force.

> **Property Ownership != Automatic Authority To Kill.**

Any exceptional case must derive independently from protection of life/safety rather than from ownership alone.

---

## 17. Official lethal force

Official role does not create general lethal authority.

> **Official Status != General Authority To Kill.**

Any fatal use of force requires:
- legitimate function;
- current necessity;
- proportionality;
- minimum sufficient capability;
- bounded temporal scope;
- independent review.

> **Lethal Capability != Lethal Authority.**

---

## 18. War and defence context

Defence architecture may create specialised contexts, but it does not suspend the Ethical Kernel.

> **War Context != Ethical Vacuum.**

Rules governing combatants, civilians, surrender, retreat and defensive authority require dedicated Defence/Law development.

This document does not manufacture missing Defence doctrine.

---

## 19. Consent to fatal harm

Self-ownership creates difficult boundaries.

A competent participant may make decisions that foreseeably result in their own death.

But:

> **Autonomous Choice To Die != Automatic Authority For Another Participant To Kill.**

This matches the existing Concord end-of-life position:

- a fully informed, autonomous, capacitated choice may be respected;
- Concord has no general authority to enforce existence;
- others have a duty where appropriate to alleviate suffering;
- that duty does not automatically extend to assisting death;
- death should not be celebrated, encouraged or assisted merely because the choice is autonomous.

---

## 20. Assisted death

The current Concord position distinguishes non-interference from assistance.

> **Respecting A Competent Decision != Participating In Its Execution.**

Therefore:

> **Consent Of The Person Who Dies != By Itself Create Authority For Another To Cause Death.**

Any future exception would require explicit ethical/legal derivation and cannot be inferred from autonomy alone.

At present, no such general assistance authority is derived here.

---

## 21. Refusal of treatment

A competent participant may refuse treatment even where refusal is expected to result in death.

> **Refusal Of Treatment != Request To Be Killed.**

Respecting a valid refusal is not automatically causation of unlawful death.

The Health architecture should govern:
- capacity;
- information;
- voluntariness;
- treatment withdrawal;
- palliative care;
- clinical duties.

---

## 22. Withdrawal of treatment

Ceasing treatment in accordance with valid participant authorship and legitimate clinical/legal process is not automatically equivalent to intentionally killing the participant.

> **Allowing Underlying Condition To Progress != Automatically Causing Death Unlawfully.**

Causation, purpose, duty and authority require careful classification.

---

## 23. Palliative treatment

Treatment intended to alleviate suffering may carry foreseeable mortality risk.

> **Foreseeable Risk Of Death != Automatically Intent To Kill.**

Purpose, dose/action, medical necessity, proportionality and standard of care matter.

This protects legitimate alleviation while preserving accountability for disguised intentional killing.

---

## 24. Suicide and self-caused death

A participant's self-caused death does not automatically create criminal responsibility in others.

Potential third-party questions include:
- coercion;
- intentional assistance;
- deception;
- unlawful supply/control;
- custodial duty;
- failure of a specific protective duty.

> **Knowledge Of Another's Distress != Automatic Responsibility For Their Death.**

A legitimate duty must be independently established.

---

## 25. Protective authority and diminished capacity

Where capacity is materially diminished and serious self-harm is imminent, Mental Health Exceptional Authority may create temporary protective authority.

That authority is:
- protective;
- bounded;
- current-state dependent;
- reviewable.

> **Protective Authority != Ownership Of Life.**

When the threshold ends, authority ends or requires new justification.

---

## 26. Duty to rescue

There should not be an unlimited general criminal duty to save every person merely because assistance was physically possible.

> **Capability To Rescue != Automatic Legal Duty To Rescue.**

A duty may arise from:
- custodial responsibility;
- professional role;
- created danger;
- contractual/legal responsibility;
- specific relationship;
- other independently derived duty.

Failure then requires omission analysis under the Mental State architecture.

---

## 27. Creating the danger

A participant who culpably creates a dangerous condition may acquire duties to mitigate/rescue.

> **Creating A Risk May Create Responsibilities That Mere Observation Does Not.**

Exact scope requires later duty architecture.

---

## 28. Attempted killing

**Attempt Liability: YES.**

Attempted intentional killing requires:
- intent to cause death;
- direct executory conduct;
- non-completion of death.

> **Attempted Killing Requires Intent Appropriate To The Attempted Result.**

Reckless or negligent attempted killing is conceptually incoherent under the current attempt architecture.

A participant can instead complete a reckless endangerment or lesser harm offence if such law exists.

---

## 29. Attempted greater offence plus completed lesser harm

Example:
A intentionally tries to kill B, causes H2 injury, but B survives.

Candidate classification:

**Attempted H5 Intentional Killing**  
+ **Completed H2 Physical Harm**

> **Survival != Erasure Of Fatal Intent.**

But consequence assessment must avoid double punishment for the same conduct without justification.

---

## 30. Preparation

Planning a killing does not by itself establish attempt.

> **Premeditation != Attempt.**

The general progression remains:

**Thought / Desire → Intention → Preparation → Executory Attempt → Completion**

Separate offences concerning conspiracy or specific dangerous preparation, if desired, require independent derivation.

---

## 31. Participation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

Potential roles:
- principal;
- co-principal;
- director;
- knowing assistant;
- facilitator.

> **Physical Delivery Of Fatal Act != Sole Form Of Responsibility.**

Equally:

> **Association With Killer != Responsibility For Killing.**

Authorship and contribution must be established.

---

## 32. Remote and automated causation

Death may be caused through:
- autonomous systems;
- remote devices;
- software;
- infrastructure control;
- delayed mechanisms.

> **Physical Distance != Absence Of Causation Or Authorship.**

Mechanism remains evidentially relevant but does not define the fundamental offence.

---

## 33. AI and digital participants

For biological humans, H5 death has familiar biological meaning.

For digital/hybrid participants, death requires continuity and identity analysis.

> **Hardware Destruction != Automatically Participant Death.**

Potential states may include:
- temporary interruption;
- recoverable shutdown;
- loss of active instance;
- loss of continuity;
- irreversible destruction of identity-critical state.

The Continuity Domain must define when an H5-equivalent participant death has occurred.

> **Substrate Difference != Absence Of Protection Against Participant Destruction.**

---

## 34. Proof of death

A legal finding of death requires reliable evidence.

> **Missing != Dead.**

> **Loss Of Communication != Death.**

This is particularly important for digital/distributed participants.

Presumptive-death rules may later be required for unresolved disappearance.

---

## 35. Identity and continuity

Where restoration from backup is possible, the legal question is not merely whether hardware stopped.

Relevant questions include:
- continuity of identity;
- state loss;
- divergence;
- authorised backup;
- recoverability;
- participant's own continuity model.

The Law domain should consume, not invent, the canonical continuity definition.

---

## 36. Harm to others

Death can create consequential harm to:
- dependants;
- family;
- contractual relationships;
- enterprises;
- communities.

These harms may inform remedy but should not be used to assign different intrinsic worth to different lives.

> **Social Status != Value Of Life.**

---

## 37. Equal protection

The protection against unlawful death applies irrespective of:
- wealth;
- occupation;
- popularity;
- political power;
- dependency;
- disability;
- productivity;
- substrate, where participant status applies.

> **Participant Value != Economic Output.**

---

## 38. Aggravation and mitigation

Potential aggravators may include:
- premeditation;
- torture/prolonged deliberate suffering;
- abuse of authority;
- multiple victims;
- exploitation of vulnerability;
- concealment where independently relevant.

Mitigation may include:
- diminished capacity/authorship;
- extreme coercion;
- other recognised factors.

> **Premeditation != Full Capacity.**

> **Fatal Result != Automatic Maximum Responsibility.**

The mirrored Aggravation/Mitigation architecture applies.

---

## 39. Mental health

Mental illness does not automatically remove responsibility for fatal harm.

The existing Mental Health as General Legal Factor architecture applies.

> **Diagnosis != Automatic Exoneration.**

Where custody is justified, mental-health state may affect custodial destination/treatment.

Sentence expiry remains distinct from any later independent Mental Health Exceptional Authority.

---

## 40. Remedy

Death cannot ordinarily be restored for a biological participant.

This changes the remedy landscape.

Potential responses include:
- support for affected dependants/participants;
- financial/material restoration where appropriate;
- correction of records;
- return/restoration of property;
- accountability;
- systemic correction;
- prevention of recurrence;
- memorial/historical accuracy where appropriate.

> **Irreversible Harm != Unlimited Punitive Authority.**

Severity strengthens the need for proportional accountability, not unbounded punishment.

---

## 41. Investigation

Because H5 is irreversible and highly consequential, investigation should preserve both inculpatory and exculpatory hypotheses.

Mirrored Reality Trees are particularly appropriate.

Questions include:
- did death occur?
- what caused it?
- who contributed?
- what was each participant's mental state?
- was there justification?
- were duties present?
- did intervening causes exist?

> **Death + Suspect != Proof Of Killing.**

## 43. Worked examples

### A. Deliberate fatal attack

A intentionally performs conduct for the purpose of killing B. B dies.

**Result:** unlawful physical harm + H5 + intent to kill, absent justification.

### B. Intended serious injury, unexpected death

A intentionally causes what A intends to be H3 serious injury. B dies from the injury.

**Result:** actual H5 and intended H3 must both remain visible. Death does not automatically rewrite historical intent as intent to kill.

### C. Reckless fatal conduct

A consciously disregards a substantial unjustified fatal risk and B dies as a result.

**Result:** H5 + reckless mental state if all elements are established.

### D. Non-culpable accident

A acts with reasonable care and an unforeseeable event nevertheless causes B's death.

**Result:** H5 outcome exists; criminal culpability does not automatically follow.

### E. Defensive fatal force

B presents an immediate lethal threat to A. A uses the minimum reasonably available defensive force and B dies.

**Result:** fatal causation occurred, but defensive BCA may establish lawful justification.

### F. Threat ended

B attacks A, then is disarmed and safely restrained. A deliberately kills B afterward in retaliation.

**Result:** prior defensive authority does not continue after the threat ends.

### G. Treatment refusal

Competent B knowingly refuses life-sustaining treatment after being sufficiently informed. Clinicians respect the refusal and B dies from the underlying condition.

**Result:** refusal is not a request to be killed; lawful respect for authored refusal is not automatically unlawful causation of death.

### H. Attempted killing with injury

A intentionally tries to kill B. B survives with H2 injury.

**Result:** attempted H5 intentional killing + completed H2 harm may coexist.

### I. Digital system destruction

A destroys hardware hosting digital participant B, but B is immediately restored with legally recognised continuity from an intact authorised state.

**Result:** hardware/property damage may be severe, but participant H5 death is not established merely from hardware destruction.

## 44. Development result

Death should remain **H5 of the general Physical Harm architecture**, preserving a single ethical root.

A dedicated legal implementation is nevertheless justified because death requires additional treatment of:
- causation;
- irreversible continuity;
- lethal defensive authority;
- medical/end-of-life boundaries;
- attempt;
- omission/duty;
- digital/hybrid participant continuity.

This avoids creating disconnected historical offence labels while preserving familiar distinctions through classifiers.

The resulting model is:

**Base Protected Interest: Embodied/Participant Integrity**  
+ **Actual Result: H5 Death**  
+ **Intended Result**  
+ **Mental State**  
+ **Causation**  
+ **Consent / Authority / Justification**  
+ **Capacity / Authorship**  
+ **Aggravation / Mitigation**  
= **Fatal-Harm Legal State**

A natural next derivation is **endangerment and creation of serious risk**, because the current architecture now exposes conduct that culpably creates a major danger but, through chance or intervention, causes neither completed harm nor an intentional attempt.
