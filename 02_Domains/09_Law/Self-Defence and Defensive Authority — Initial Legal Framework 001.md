# Self-Defence and Defensive Authority — Initial Legal Framework 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / INITIAL LEGAL FRAMEWORK / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Purpose

The Constitutional Core now contains a provisional derivation of self-ownership, self-defence and defensive authority.

Law must make that protection usable without converting it into an unlimited permission to harm.

This note establishes the initial legal questions and boundaries only. It is not a completed self-defence code.

## 2. Basic legal relationship

Candidate rule:

> A participant may take necessary and proportionate action to defend themselves or another against an imminent or ongoing wrongful threat of harm where the circumstances reasonably support defensive action.

The exact tests remain to be developed and adversarially examined.

## 3. Bounded Contextual Authority

Defensive authority should be represented as:

**Legitimate Defensive Context + Protective Function + Necessity -> Bounded Defensive Authority**

The authority is limited by:
- context;
- function;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- reasonable perception;
- time;
- affected rights;
- applicable duties;
- functional sunset.

It does not require case-specific institutional permission where the immediacy of the threat makes such permission impracticable.

## 4. Proportionality

Proportionality is a boundary on defensive authority.

It should not be reduced to mechanical equivalence between the aggressor's action and the defender's response.

Future Law must determine what response was reasonably necessary and proportionate to stop or avoid the relevant threatened harm under the circumstances.

**Proportionality != Mechanical Equality Of Force**

**Defensive Necessity != Unlimited Force**

## 5. Cessation

Defensive authority exists for protection, not punishment.

**Threat / Defensive Necessity Ends -> Defence-Derived Authority Ends**

Any continuing restraint, detention, investigation, punishment or adjudication requires its own legitimate legal basis.

## 6. Retreat

A participant ordinarily retains the right to avoid or withdraw from danger rather than engage.

**Right To Defend != Duty To Fight**

Law should not infer a general duty to risk the self merely from citizenship, proximity or capacity to assist.

A separate reasonable duty to assist may be justified in some circumstances, particularly where assistance can be provided without disproportionate danger, but its scope remains unresolved.

## 7. Defence of others

A participant may possess defensive authority to protect another participant where the relevant conditions exist.

**Right To Defend Another != General Duty To Sacrifice Self**

Future Law must address:
- mistaken intervention;
- consent where relevant;
- vulnerable or incapacitated persons;
- conflicts between apparent defender and protected participant;
- proportionality;
- reasonable perception;
- when intervention becomes unlawful.

## 8. Voluntarily accepted protective duty

A participant may accept a role carrying additional legal obligations to act or remain in danger.

Candidate form:

**Voluntary Agreement + Legitimate Role + Activation Context -> Bounded Duty To Act**

This may alter the participant's ordinary freedom to retreat while the accepted duty is legitimately active.

It does not create unlimited obligation.

**Accepted Protective Duty != Duty Of Futile Sacrifice**

## 9. Reasonable perception

Legal review should assess defensive conduct using the circumstances and information reasonably available at the relevant time, not only facts discovered afterward.

This does not validate self-declared threat automatically.

**Claimed Threat != Established Defensive Context**

Evidence and review must distinguish reasonable defensive error from fabricated, reckless or unjustified claims.

## 10. Review and accountability

Serious defensive action may be reviewed without presuming wrongdoing.

Relevant questions include:
1. Did a defensive context exist or reasonably appear to exist?
2. What harm was threatened?
3. Was intervention necessary?
4. What alternatives were reasonably available?
5. Was the action proportionate to the protective function?
6. Did the participant remain within the authority envelope?
7. When did the threat end?
8. Did defensive action continue after its justification ended?
9. What continuing duties arose from the event?

## 11. Scale

The same legal mechanism should remain recognisable from individual defence through organised Concord Defence.

**Self-Defence -> Defence Of Others -> Collective Defence -> Coordinated Civilisational Defence**

Scale may change coordination, delegation and capability. It does not itself create broader moral or legal permission to harm.

## 12. Questions requiring later legal development

This note does not yet settle:
- exact imminence threshold;
- defence against continuing but non-immediate coercion;
- defence of property;
- proportionality tests;
- lethal-force thresholds;
- retreat expectations where retreat is available;
- defence of another against that person's expressed wishes;
- mistaken threats;
- provocation;
- mutual aggression;
- defence during unlawful institutional action;
- automated/AI defensive action;
- defence across jurisdictions;
- arrest/detention following defence;
- evidential burdens;
- civil liability/remedy;
- special duties attached to professional or contracted roles.

These require dedicated Law development rather than assumption.

## 13. Initial legal principle

> **Law should preserve a participant's practical ability to protect self and others from wrongful harm while ensuring that defensive authority remains necessary, proportionate, contextual, temporary and accountable.**
