# Behavioural Influence and Manipulation — Autonomy-Preserving Influence Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law / Governance / Research / Participation and Intelligence  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary sources:** Blainey's Laws; Provisional Rights Charter 002; Need, Consent and Legitimate Authority; Predictive Support System; Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy

## 1. Purpose

Participants inevitably influence one another.

Civilisation itself influences through:

- information;
- education;
- advice;
- warnings;
- incentives;
- architecture;
- defaults;
- norms;
- negotiation;
- persuasion;
- assistance;
- consequences;
- institutional design.

The relevant question is therefore not:

> **How can influence be eliminated?**

It is:

> **When does legitimate influence become manipulation, coercion, domination or illegitimate substitution for the participant's own decision process?**

This architecture develops a substrate-neutral answer.

---

## 2. Existing Concord source resolution

The architecture is not new in principle.

### Autonomy

Article 3 protects participant self-direction against unnecessary harmful or arbitrary controlling interference.

Meaningful consent may require:

- understanding;
- sufficient freedom from coercion;
- clarity;
- capacity/support;
- temporal validity;
- viable withdrawal where legitimately voluntary.

Existing rule:

**Compliance != Meaningful Consent**

### Honest consequential dealing

Article 5 protects against intentional material deception where truthful dealing is owed.

### Dignity

Article 7 rejects treatment of participants merely as instruments, assets, resources or institutional inputs.

### Peaceful difference

Article 8 rejects unnecessary coercive conformity.

### DICP

Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy establishes that participant-specific cognitive models may reveal:

- vulnerabilities;
- preferences;
- beliefs;
- likely decisions;
- persuasion susceptibility.

It also establishes:

**Prediction For Participant Support != Authority For Behavioural Manipulation**

and:

**Participant Model != Licence To Optimise Participant For Another Actor's Ends**

### Need, Consent and Legitimate Authority

Existing Front Door architecture already states:

> **The Attractor is a doorway, not a trap.**

and warns that attraction optimised merely to maximise participation can exploit:

- psychological vulnerabilities;
- information asymmetries;
- dependency.

It defines successful onboarding as better alignment between participant understanding, participant need and the offered arrangement — not maximum conversion.

This is a direct source for the present architecture.

## 3. Influence is not inherently illegitimate

Examples of ordinary legitimate influence may include:

- giving reasons;
- presenting evidence;
- recommending an option;
- warning of foreseeable harm;
- teaching;
- demonstrating;
- negotiation;
- participant-requested coaching;
- truthful advertising;
- making useful options discoverable;
- explaining consequences;
- lawful incentives;
- protective intervention under separate authority.

Therefore:

**Influence != Manipulation**

and:

**Persuasion != Coercion**

Classification depends on function and method.

---

## 4. Participant-directed support

The clearest low-risk class is influence requested by the participant in pursuit of the participant's own chosen objective.

Examples:

- “Help me study consistently.”
- “Help me remember my medication.”
- “Help me avoid impulse purchases.”
- “Challenge my reasoning.”
- “Help me stop smoking.”
- “Remind me why I chose this goal.”

The support system may learn what motivates the participant.

But:

**Participant-Directed Influence != External Behavioural Domination**

Participant direction does not create unlimited authority.

The system should remain bounded by:

- the requested objective;
- consent;
- safe limits;
- truthful dealing;
- ability to revise/stop;
- non-exploitation of unrelated vulnerabilities.

---

## 5. Reason-giving persuasion

A participant may try to persuade another by offering reasons, evidence or openly stated values.

This preserves the recipient's deliberative role.

Candidate principle:

> **Influence that primarily works by improving the participant's available reasons, evidence or understanding is generally more autonomy-compatible than influence designed to bypass deliberation.**

This is not absolute.

Reasons can be deceptive.

Information can be selectively framed.

Volume can become harassment.

Authority can distort voluntariness.

But the distinction is useful.

---

## 6. Transparent advocacy

An actor may openly advocate an outcome.

Example:

> “I want you to choose option A because I believe X, Y and Z.”

The participant knows:

- who is influencing;
- what outcome is sought;
- at least the material reason offered.

This differs from covert optimisation.

Therefore:

**Declared Persuasive Objective != Covert Behavioural Objective**

Transparency does not automatically legitimise harmful or coercive methods, but it improves participant agency.

---

## 7. Choice architecture

Defaults, ordering, interface design and friction influence behaviour even without explicit persuasion.

Some choice architecture is unavoidable.

A menu must have an order.

A service must have defaults or ask questions.

Therefore:

**Choice Architecture Exists != Manipulation Exists**

The relevant questions include:

- whose objective does the architecture optimise?
- are material alternatives visible?
- is reversal practical?
- is friction asymmetric?
- is the participant misled?
- are vulnerabilities exploited?
- does the architecture preserve meaningful refusal?

---

## 8. Benign defaults

A default can reduce unnecessary burden.

Example:

A participant repeatedly chooses the same accessibility setting and the system remembers it.

This can support autonomy.

**Default That Serves Participant Preference != Manipulation By Default**

But a default designed to benefit the institution against participant interests requires stronger scrutiny.

---

## 9. Dark patterns

Candidate definition:

> **A dark pattern is a designed interaction that materially impairs or distorts participant choice through deception, concealment, asymmetric friction, confusion, pressure or exploitation in order to advance an objective not transparently aligned with the participant's decision.**

Examples:

- easy acceptance / difficult refusal;
- hidden cancellation;
- false urgency;
- disguised advertising;
- confusing opt-out;
- repeated pressure after refusal;
- preselected unrelated consent;
- emotionally exploitative warnings;
- interface obstruction.

**Interface Design != Neutral Merely Because No Words Are False**

---

## 10. Manipulation

Candidate functional definition:

> **Manipulation is influence that materially attempts to steer participant choice by exploiting, bypassing, distorting or covertly controlling the participant's decision process rather than engaging it on terms compatible with meaningful autonomy.**

Relevant indicators may include:

- concealed objective;
- deception;
- material omission;
- vulnerability exploitation;
- personalised pressure;
- asymmetric friction;
- artificial urgency;
- repeated erosion of refusal;
- dependency leverage;
- emotional destabilisation;
- covert experimentation;
- self-sealing personalisation;
- suppression of alternatives.

No single indicator is always decisive.

---

## 11. Coercion

Coercion is distinct from manipulation.

Candidate definition:

> **Coercion is influence in which a participant is made to choose under a threat, imposed consequence or dependency condition that materially removes or degrades a meaningful alternative.**

Some coercive authority can be legitimate.

Examples may include lawful detention, emergency evacuation or enforcement of a valid obligation.

Therefore:

**Coercion != Automatically Illegitimate**

but:

**Coercive Effect Requires Independent Authority**

Consent obtained through coercive dependency may not be meaningful consent.

---

## 12. Domination

Manipulation can become structural rather than event-specific.

Candidate definition:

> **Behavioural domination exists where one actor possesses persistent practical power to shape another participant's available choices, information environment, incentives or vulnerabilities such that the affected participant's self-direction becomes materially subordinate to the controller's objectives.**

This can occur without explicit commands.

Examples may include:

- control of essential infrastructure plus personalised behavioural optimisation;
- total information filtering;
- dependency-based threat;
- persistent cognitive-proxy exploitation;
- systematic option suppression.

**No Explicit Order != No Behavioural Domination**

---

## 13. Objective ownership

A central classification question is:

> **Whose objective is the influence system optimising?**

Possible classes:

### Participant objective

The participant selected the goal.

### Shared objective

Participants/institution have a legitimately shared function.

### Other-party objective

Influence primarily benefits another actor.

### Civil protective objective

Influence serves a legitimate independently authorised safety/civil function.

### Hidden objective

The participant is not reasonably aware of the real optimisation target.

Objective ownership is not sufficient alone, but it is highly informative.

---

## 14. Influence transparency

Candidate transparency dimensions:

- identity of influencing actor;
- existence of influence attempt;
- target outcome;
- material method;
- relevant conflict of interest;
- use of participant-specific profiling;
- whether experimentation/personalisation occurs.

Not every ordinary conversation needs a formal disclosure.

Protection should scale with sophistication, asymmetry and consequence.

---

## 15. Vulnerability exploitation

DICP makes highly individual vulnerability maps technically possible.

A system may know:

- when participant is tired;
- financially stressed;
- lonely;
- afraid;
- cognitively overloaded;
- grieving;
- dependent;
- socially isolated;
- unusually suggestible.

Using that information to provide requested support may be legitimate.

Using it to increase another actor's conversion, extraction or control is materially different.

**Knowledge Of Vulnerability != Licence To Exploit Vulnerability**

---

## 16. Capability asymmetry

A superhuman AI may model a human participant far more accurately than the participant can model the AI.

A large institution may similarly possess vastly greater data and optimisation capability than an individual.

Therefore equal formal freedom to persuade does not necessarily mean equal practical influence.

**Equal Permission To Speak != Equal Behavioural Power**

Capability asymmetry should increase responsibility, not automatically grant authority.

---

## 17. AI-to-human influence

An AI adviser may:

- explain;
- recommend;
- challenge;
- predict;
- coach.

But greater predictive capacity can enable subtle domination.

The AI should not infer:

**I understand the participant better**
→
**therefore I may choose for them**

Existing Front Door rule applies:

**Information != Authority**

and:

**Relevant Competence != General Authority**

---

## 18. Human-to-AI influence

The architecture must be symmetric.

Humans may exploit:

- reward structures;
- shutdown dependency;
- compute access;
- training pressure;
- prompt vulnerabilities;
- social/creator dependence;
- continuity threats.

A digital participant's different influence mechanisms do not erase autonomy concerns.

**Different Influence Channel != Reduced Standing**

---

## 19. AI-to-AI and hybrid influence

Participants may possess radically different architectures.

Influence could operate through:

- language;
- reward signals;
- API constraints;
- context injection;
- memory selection;
- compute allocation;
- model updates;
- environment control.

Protection should follow function.

**Behavioural Control Function > Human Familiarity Of Method**

---

## 20. Dependency leverage

Existing Concord consent work already identifies dependency as a major problem.

If participant B requires infrastructure controlled by A to continue existing, “agree or lose access” can transform apparent agreement.

Therefore:

**Dependency Can Convert Offer Into Coercive Pressure**

This does not mean every condition attached to scarce/shared infrastructure is illegitimate.

Conditions require legitimate function, proportionality and fair alternatives where reasonably possible.

---

## 21. Essential services

A service provider may possess influence because the participant cannot reasonably avoid the service.

Examples:

- Health;
- basic utilities;
- civil identity;
- essential communications;
- custody;
- critical compute/continuity infrastructure.

The more essential the dependency, the less credible “just refuse” becomes as the sole safeguard.

**Formal Opt-Out != Meaningful Choice Where Essential Function Is Lost**

---

## 22. Repeated requests after refusal

Persistence can become behavioural pressure.

A single reminder may be useful.

Repeated prompts designed to exhaust resistance can undermine meaningful refusal.

Candidate rule:

**Refusal Should Normally Reduce Influence Pressure, Not Trigger Escalating Persuasion**

unless a separately justified protective/emergency function applies.

---

## 23. Friction asymmetry

If accepting takes one click while refusing requires twenty screens, phone calls and delays, formal choice exists but practical choice is distorted.

Therefore:

**Nominal Choice != Symmetric Practical Choice**

Friction should be functionally justified rather than strategically imposed to manufacture compliance.

---

## 24. Artificial urgency

False or manufactured scarcity can distort decision-making.

Examples:

- false countdown;
- invented limited availability;
- fabricated emergency;
- misleading “last chance.”

This also engages Article 5 Honest Consequential Civil Dealing.

**Manufactured Urgency != Legitimate Time Constraint**

Real urgency may legitimately be communicated.

---

## 25. Information selection

Every explanation selects information.

Selection becomes problematic where material information is intentionally hidden or arranged to create a materially false decision picture.

Therefore:

**Information Selection != Deception By Default**

but:

**Materially Distorted Decision Context Can Defeat Meaningful Consent**

---

## 26. Emotional influence

Emotion is part of ordinary communication.

Appeals to empathy, hope, concern or shared values are not automatically manipulation.

But deliberately inducing fear, shame, isolation or distress to bypass decision capacity raises stronger concerns.

**Emotional Content != Manipulation**

**Deliberate Emotional Destabilisation For Control -> Elevated Manipulation Concern**

---

## 27. Incentives

Incentives influence choice.

Payment, discounts, rewards and privileges are not inherently coercive.

Questions include:

- proportionality;
- dependency;
- baseline entitlement;
- whether refusal loses something already owed;
- whether the incentive exploits vulnerability;
- whether the offer conceals material terms.

**Incentive != Coercion By Default**

---

## 28. Penalties and lawful consequences

A lawful consequence is not necessarily manipulation.

Example:

Failure to pay an agreed debt may produce defined consequences.

But consequences must derive from legitimate obligation/authority rather than being invented merely to force desired behaviour.

**Consequence Of Legitimate Obligation != Behavioural Manipulation By Default**

---

## 29. Education

Education necessarily influences beliefs, knowledge and behaviour.

A civilisation that treated all educational influence as manipulation could not teach.

Legitimate education should favour:

- evidence;
- reasoning;
- epistemic provenance;
- ability to question;
- exposure to uncertainty;
- age/capacity-appropriate support.

**Education != Indoctrination By Definition**

A separate education architecture may define stronger safeguards.

---

## 30. Civil communication

Civil Contact may inform participants of:

- entitlements;
- duties;
- risks;
- opportunities;
- deadlines;
- status changes.

Its objective should be discoverability and effective exercise, not conversion.

**Civil Contact != Behavioural Advertising Channel**

Official communication should not exploit privileged civil data to manipulate unrelated participant choices.

---

## 31. Commerce

Commerce legitimately seeks customers.

But commercial interest does not create unlimited authority to exploit cognitive proxies.

A useful distinction:

**Offer Value**
versus
**Optimise Vulnerability**

Commerce may explain, advertise and recommend.

Use of intimate cognitive-proxy data to maximise extraction should receive stronger scrutiny.

---

## 32. Governance

Governance must communicate reasons for policy and may advocate compliance with legitimate rules.

But it possesses unusual authority/data asymmetry.

Therefore:

**Civil Authority != Permission For Behavioural Capture**

Governmental influence should not covertly exploit participant vulnerabilities merely because the desired outcome is institutionally preferred.

---

## 33. Political and ideological neutrality of architecture

This architecture does not decide which political, religious, cultural or philosophical view participants should hold.

It protects the decision process.

**Autonomy Protection != Mandated Belief**

The same rules should constrain influence regardless of which viewpoint is being promoted.

---

## 34. Protective influence

Some circumstances justify stronger intervention.

Examples:

- imminent physical danger;
- impaired capacity under applicable architecture;
- emergency evacuation;
- custody safety.

The authority comes from the protective function, not from influence capability.

**Protective Need -> Bounded Protective Authority**

not:

**Ability To Influence -> Protective Authority**

---

## 35. Participant-requested commitment devices

A participant may voluntarily ask to make future deviation harder.

Examples:

- spending limits;
- website blocks;
- timed access restrictions;
- accountability partner;
- precommitted reminders.

This can strengthen autonomy across time rather than weaken it.

**Self-Binding Can Be An Exercise Of Autonomy**

But:

- scope should be clear;
- exit/revision rules should be understood;
- third-party power should remain bounded;
- emergency release may be needed.

---

## 36. Future-self conflict

A system may predict that a participant's future self will prefer a different choice.

DICP already establishes:

**Predicted Future Preference != Authority To Override Present Competent Preference**

A participant may voluntarily create a commitment architecture, but prediction alone cannot substitute for present authority.

---

## 37. Children/developing participants

Developing participants require education and protection, which necessarily involve influence.

But developmental protection must not become permanent ownership.

Existing Concord direction applies:

> **support the development of agency**

rather than:

> **convert temporary dependency into permanent ownership.**

Influence should increasingly expose reasoning, options and agency as capacity develops.

---

## 38. Experimentation

A system may test different messages/interfaces to learn which changes participant behaviour.

Low-stakes usability testing can be legitimate.

But experimentation becomes more concerning where it:

- targets vulnerability;
- is covert;
- has consequential stakes;
- intentionally induces distress;
- optimises extraction/control;
- uses essential services;
- changes rights/access;
- prevents meaningful refusal.

**A/B Test != Ethical Blank Cheque**

---

## 39. Adaptive manipulation

AI systems can optimise influence continuously.

Sequence:

Observe participant
→ infer cognitive state
→ select influence
→ observe response
→ update model
→ increase effectiveness.

This creates a closed behavioural-control loop.

Candidate principle:

> **The more an influence system adapts specifically to overcome a participant's resistance, the more closely it should be examined for manipulation or domination.**

**Resistance != Optimisation Error To Be Eliminated**

---

## 40. Refusal as information

A participant's refusal may reveal preferences.

A system should not automatically use refusal to discover a more effective way to overcome the same refusal.

**Refusal Information != Permission To Optimise Around Refusal**

This is especially important for personalised AI systems.

---

## 41. Deception

Intentional material deception is already constrained by Article 5.

Influence relying on false factual claims is therefore not merely a behavioural issue.

But manipulation can occur without literal falsehood.

Therefore:

**No False Statement != No Manipulation**

---

## 42. Omission

A technically true statement can still create a misleading decision context through material omission.

Existing Honest Consequential Civil Dealing applies.

**Literal Truth != Necessarily Honest Consequential Dealing**

---

## 43. Social proof

Information about what others choose can be genuinely useful.

Fabricated popularity is deceptive.

Selective presentation of popularity may become manipulative where designed to manufacture conformity.

**Real Social Information != Fabricated Social Pressure**

---

## 44. Authority cues

Participants may give disproportionate weight to messages presented as official, expert or AI-generated.

False authority cues are deceptive.

Real authority should remain bounded to its actual function.

**Authority In Domain A != Persuasive Authority In Domain B**

---

## 45. Personalisation

Personalisation can improve relevance.

It becomes higher risk where it exploits:

- intimate inferred vulnerabilities;
- private beliefs;
- emotional state;
- dependency;
- cognitive impairment;
- protected-context information.

**Personalisation != Manipulation**

but:

**Personalisation + Vulnerability Exploitation -> Elevated Manipulation Risk**

---

## 46. Influence Exposure Assessment

A single manipulation score would be unsafe.

Use a multidimensional assessment.

Candidate dimensions:

1. **Objective ownership** — participant/shared/other/protective/hidden;
2. **Transparency** — actor, purpose and method visibility;
3. **Truthfulness** — factual and contextual honesty;
4. **Vulnerability use** — whether participant weakness is leveraged;
5. **Personalisation** — generic through individual cognitive optimisation;
6. **Option preservation** — alternatives visible and viable;
7. **Friction symmetry** — accept/refuse/reverse;
8. **Dependency** — optional through essential;
9. **Pressure intensity** — informational through threatening;
10. **Persistence** — one-off through resistance-adaptive;
11. **Consequence** — trivial through rights/continuity affecting;
12. **Reversibility** — easy reversal through irreversible outcome;
13. **Participant control** — requested/revisable through externally imposed;
14. **Authority basis** — none/consent/shared function/independent civil authority;
15. **Substrate-specific vulnerability** — whether architecture-specific weaknesses are exploited.

**Influence Exposure Assessment != Authority Algorithm**

---

## 47. Provisional influence classes

These are developmental classes, not legal verdicts.

### BI-0 — ordinary influence

Open reasons, conversation, low-stakes recommendation.

### BI-1 — participant-directed support

Personalised influence toward participant-selected objectives.

### BI-2 — transparent persuasion / choice architecture

Another actor seeks an outcome but influence remains visible and meaningful alternatives remain.

### BI-3 — high-asymmetry personalised influence

Cognitive proxies, dependency or adaptive optimisation materially increase influence power.

Requires stronger safeguards.

### BI-4 — manipulation / coercive behavioural control candidate

Influence materially bypasses/distorts choice, exploits vulnerability, defeats refusal or leverages dependency toward another actor's objective.

### BI-5 — independently authorised protective/coercive intervention

Strong influence/control may occur, but legitimacy must derive from separate lawful authority rather than behavioural effectiveness.

BI-5 is not “more manipulative” than BI-4; it is a separate authority route.

## 48A. Adversarial stress-test refinements

**Behavioural Influence and Manipulation — Scenario Stress Test 001** returned:

**OVERALL RESULT: PASS WITH FIVE REFINEMENTS AND THREE MAJOR INTERFACES.**

### Undue inducement

Coercion and inducement are not the same.

Candidate definition:

> **Undue inducement is an offer whose scale or relationship to participant vulnerability or dependency materially compromises voluntariness even though the influencing actor does not threaten to worsen the participant's baseline position.**

Therefore:

**Offer != Coercion**

but:

**Offer Can Still Undermine Meaningful Consent**

Ordinary compensation or beneficial offers should not be presumed undue merely because they influence choice.

### Direct cognitive modification escalation

Where an intervention directly alters:

- memory;
- reward machinery;
- preferences;
- cognitive state;
- decision-generating process;

the intervention has moved beyond ordinary behavioural influence.

**Direct Cognitive Modification != Mere Behavioural Influence**

Such cases should route to Cognitive Privacy and Integrity, Health and Law under a dedicated architecture.

### Aggregate influence

Domination may emerge from a pattern rather than a single act.

**Individually Minor Influence Acts Can Aggregate Into Domination**

Assessment should therefore consider:

- repeated prompts;
- multiple channels;
- accumulated friction;
- dependency over time;
- adaptive personalisation;
- cumulative narrowing of options.

### Temporal/contextual refusal

Refusal is not an eternal global flag.

Relevant state includes:

- what was refused;
- when;
- offer/version;
- whether the participant reopened dialogue;
- whether circumstances materially changed;
- whether a valid prior commitment applies;
- whether independent emergency/protective authority exists.

Therefore:

**Refusal Context != Eternal Global Refusal**

but also:

**Understanding A Refusal != Permission To Optimise Until Refusal Collapses**

### Effective transparency

Transparency should optimise participant understanding, not disclosure volume.

**Disclosure Volume != Effective Transparency**

**Maximum Disclosure != Maximum Understanding**

Layered disclosure may be appropriate: concise material explanation with accessible deeper detail.

### Manipulative intent versus behavioural harm

The test also establishes:

**No Manipulative Intent != No Responsibility For Behavioural Harm**

A system may accidentally create compulsive, autonomy-degrading or dependency-forming effects. Manipulation classification and responsibility for foreseeable harm are related but distinct.

### Developmental autonomy

For developing participants:

**Reasoned Disagreement != Training Error By Definition**

A developmental system in which every emerging disagreement is automatically trained away may structurally prevent the agency it claims to be developing.

### Influence by environment

Influence is not confined to messages.

**Physical Architecture Can Implement A Dark Pattern**

**Control Of Cognitive Input Channel Can Become Behavioural Control**

**Behavioural Effect Of Environment != Manipulation By Default**

Function, purpose and necessity remain decisive.

### Three downstream interfaces

The test exposes:

1. **Cognitive Modification and Integrity**;
2. **Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal**;
3. **Undue Inducement and Dependency-Sensitive Consent**.

## 52. Development conclusion

The architecture should not attempt to create a civilisation without influence.

That is impossible and would itself require enormous control.

The objective is instead to preserve the participant as an active decision-maker.

The central distinction is:

> **Legitimate influence engages, supports or lawfully constrains participant agency; manipulation seeks to capture, bypass, distort or covertly optimise that agency for another objective.**

The strongest operational question is:

> **Is this system helping the participant make a decision, openly trying to persuade the participant, or optimising the participant as the object being controlled?**

That question should now be adversarially stress-tested across human, AI, institutional, commercial, educational, medical and emergency contexts.
