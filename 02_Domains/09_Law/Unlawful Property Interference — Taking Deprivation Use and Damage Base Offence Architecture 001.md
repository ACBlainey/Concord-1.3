# Unlawful Property Interference — Taking, Deprivation, Use and Damage Base Offence Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 8 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / WORKED LEGAL DERIVATION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Parent:** `Legal Derivation and Consequence Architecture — Ethical Provenance, Offence Classifiers and Proportionate Response 001.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

This document derives a candidate fundamental law protecting legitimate property interests against unauthorised taking, deprivation, use, control, destruction and damage.

The architecture is deliberately mechanism-neutral.

It does not begin by creating disconnected offences for every historical method of property interference.

Instead:

**Protected Property Interest**  
→ **Unauthorised Interference**  
→ **Result / Intended Result**  
→ **Mental State**  
→ **Consent / Authority**  
→ **Responsibility / Consequence**

---

## 2. Ethical provenance

Primary Ethical Kernel sources:

- **BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm**
- **BL2 — Respect Autonomy**
- **BL3 — Preserve Safe Spaces**
- **BL4 — Act Honestly**
- **BL5 — Act Fairly**
- **BL6 — Accept Responsibility**
- **BL8 — Steward the Commons**
- **BL9 — Seek Peaceful Coexistence**

Candidate derivation:

**Autonomy + Safe Space + Fairness + Responsibility**  
→ **Legitimate Control Of Lawfully Held Resources**  
→ **Protection Against Unauthorised Deprivation, Use, Damage Or Destruction**

Property protection remains subordinate to the distinction already established:

> **Participant != Property.**

Property law protects legitimate interests in things/resources. It cannot be used to create ownership of participants.

---

## 3. Protected interests

Property is not merely physical possession.

A legitimate property interest may include protected:
- possession;
- custody;
- use;
- access;
- control;
- exclusion;
- economic value;
- functional value;
- integrity;
- return;
- disposal/transfer rights;
- other legitimately established incidents of ownership or stewardship.

Different cases may interfere with different interests.

> **Property Interference != Necessarily Permanent Taking.**

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not, without valid consent, lawful justification or other independently legitimate authority, intentionally or knowingly take, retain, use, control, deprive another of, damage, destroy or materially interfere with property in which another holds a legally protected interest.**

Specialised reckless or negligent property damage may be recognised where separately justified.

---

## 5. Ownership, possession and custody

Ownership is not the only protected relationship.

A participant may lawfully possess or hold property belonging to another.

Examples:
- borrowed property;
- rented equipment;
- stored goods;
- entrusted property;
- goods in transit;
- repair custody.

Therefore:

> **Ownership != Sole Protected Property Interest.**

A thief cannot necessarily defeat a later interference claim merely by showing that the immediate possessor was not the ultimate owner.

The exact hierarchy of competing claims belongs in future property-law architecture.

---

## 6. Taking

### UNAUTHORISED TAKING

A participant intentionally assumes possession or control of protected property without valid consent or authority.

Taking need not establish permanent deprivation.

The result may later be classified according to intended and actual deprivation.

---

## 7. Deprivation

### DEPRIVATION

The rights-holder is materially prevented from possessing, accessing, controlling or using protected property.

Deprivation may be:
- temporary;
- prolonged;
- permanent.

> **Temporary Deprivation != No Property Harm.**

But temporary borrowing and permanent appropriation need not carry identical consequence.

---

## 8. Permanent appropriation / theft-like state

Where a participant intentionally takes or retains property with the purpose of permanently appropriating it, permanently depriving the legitimate holder, or treating it as their own contrary to the protected interest, this represents a more specific serious property state.

Candidate representation:

**Unlawful Property Interference**  
+ **Taking/Retention**  
+ **Intentional Permanent Appropriation/Deprivation**

rather than requiring an entirely disconnected conceptual system.

The familiar label **theft** may remain useful for legal/public legibility.

---

## 9. Unauthorised temporary use

A participant may intentionally use another's property without intending permanent deprivation.

Example:
A takes B's vehicle without permission, uses it for several hours and returns it.

The absence of permanent intent does not make the conduct automatically lawful.

> **Intent To Return != Automatic Permission To Take Or Use.**

Actual consequence and sanction may differ from permanent appropriation.

---

## 10. Retention

Property may initially be obtained lawfully and later unlawfully retained.

Example:
A borrows equipment with consent and then deliberately refuses to return it after the legitimate possession period ends.

> **Lawful Initial Possession != Lawful Indefinite Retention.**

This parallels the Concord rule that historical authority does not automatically continue.

---

## 11. Damage

### PROPERTY DAMAGE

Protected property is intentionally or, where legally specified, recklessly/negligently impaired in:
- function;
- integrity;
- safety;
- value;
- usability;
- recoverability.

Damage need not be visible.

> **Visible Damage != Complete Measure Of Property Damage.**

Software corruption, contamination, hidden structural damage or deletion may materially impair property without obvious external marks.

---

## 12. Destruction

### DESTRUCTION

Property is rendered permanently or practically unrecoverable for its protected function/value.

Destruction may be a severe result state of property interference rather than requiring a wholly separate ethical derivation.

Actual destruction and intended destruction remain separately recorded.

---

## 13. Mechanism neutrality

Possible mechanisms include:
- physical removal;
- physical destruction;
- remote access;
- deletion;
- disabling;
- contamination;
- alteration;
- diversion;
- lockout;
- unauthorised transfer;
- technological control;
- fraudulent redirection;
- other mechanisms.

> **Mechanism Of Property Interference != Fundamental Protected Interest.**

Mechanism may still evidence:
- intent;
- sophistication;
- premeditation;
- knowledge;
- expected loss;
- concealment;
- participation.

---

## 14. Consent

The general Consent and Withdrawal architecture applies.

Consent may be bounded by:
- item;
- purpose;
- user;
- duration;
- location;
- permitted modification;
- transfer;
- return conditions.

> **Permission To Possess != Permission To Dispose.**

> **Permission To Use != Permission To Destroy.**

> **Permission For Purpose A != Permission For Purpose B.**

---

## 15. Shared and jointly controlled property

Multiple participants may hold legitimate interests in the same property.

One participant's interest does not automatically erase the others.

> **Partial Ownership != Unlimited Unilateral Control.**

The exact rights of joint owners require dedicated property architecture.

Criminal liability should not be inferred merely from a disputed civil ownership boundary where the protected interest is genuinely unresolved.

---

## 16. Abandoned property

Property intentionally abandoned by all legitimate rights-holders may no longer carry the same exclusion interest.

But:

> **Unattended != Abandoned.**

> **Lost != Abandoned.**

Finding property does not automatically transfer ownership.

Reasonable return/reporting duties may require later derivation.

---

## 17. Commons and stewardship

Some resources are not appropriately represented as individually owned property.

Commons may be protected through stewardship rights/duties rather than ordinary ownership.

> **Not Privately Owned != Unprotected.**

Unauthorised destruction, depletion or appropriation of commons may therefore engage BL8 and separate environmental/resource law.

This document should not convert the commons into ordinary private property merely to protect it.

---

## 18. Necessity and emergency use

Emergency circumstances may justify temporary interference with property.

Example:
A breaks a locked door to rescue a participant from an immediate fire.

Candidate:

**Immediate Serious Need**  
→ **Legitimate Protective Function**  
→ **Minimum Necessary Property Interference**  
→ **BCA**  
→ **Lawful/Unlawful Classification**

> **Property Right != Absolute Barrier To Independently Justified Emergency Action.**

But emergency need does not create general appropriation authority.

---

## 19. Self-defence

Property may sometimes be damaged during proportionate self-defence or defence of others.

Again, justification depends on the independent BCA rather than the fact that damage occurred.

> **Damage Occurred != Damage Unlawful.**

---

## 20. Official seizure

Civil Security, Judiciary or other authorised functions may sometimes seize or restrict property.

Such action requires:
- legitimate function;
- legal authority;
- defined scope;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- custody/provenance;
- review;
- return/disposal rule.

> **Investigative Interest != Unlimited Seizure Authority.**

> **Authority To Seize != Ownership.**

Property held as evidence remains subject to protected interests.

---

## 21. Confiscation

Permanent deprivation by the legal system is more serious than temporary evidential seizure.

It requires its own legitimate basis.

> **Lawful Possession By State != Automatic Right Of Permanent Confiscation.**

Future sentencing/property architecture should distinguish:
- evidence custody;
- proceeds of unlawful conduct;
- instrumentalities;
- restitution;
- civil/property disputes;
- punitive confiscation.

---

## 22. Deception and property transfer

A participant may apparently consent to transfer property because of deception.

The general consent architecture applies, but property transfer may require specialised fraud analysis.

Candidate distinction:

**Voluntary Physical Transfer**  
!=  
**Necessarily Validly Authored Property Transfer**

Where deception materially defeats authorship, the transaction may be invalid or independently unlawful.

This points toward a future **fraud/deceptive acquisition** derivation.

---

## 23. Contract and non-performance

Failure to perform a contract is not automatically theft.

> **Debt Or Breach Of Contract != Automatically Property Crime.**

Criminal liability requires the elements of the relevant offence, including the required mental state.

This prevents ordinary commercial disputes from being transformed into criminal accusations merely because one party suffers loss.

---

## 24. Mistake

Taking another's property under a genuine reasonable mistake about ownership or permission may defeat the required intentional/knowing state.

The mental-state architecture applies.

> **Property Error != Automatic Theft.**

Later discovery may create duties concerning return or correction.

---

## 25. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: YES** for intentional taking, permanent appropriation, damage or destruction where the base state supports attempt.

Examples:
- attempting to take property but being prevented;
- activating a destructive mechanism that fails;
- attempting an unauthorised transfer that is blocked.

The general Attempt and Completion architecture applies.

---

## 26. Participation

The general Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

A participant may:
- physically take property;
- direct the taking;
- knowingly assist;
- facilitate unauthorised transfer;
- knowingly receive/control property as part of the offence where separately established.

Mere later possession should not automatically establish participation in the original taking.

> **Possession After Offence != Automatic Proof Of Participation In Original Offence.**

---

## 27. Mental state

Potential classifications include:

**INTENTIONAL** — interference/result intended.

**KNOWING** — participant knows conduct interferes with another's protected property interest.

**RECKLESS** — may apply to damage where legally specified.

**NEGLIGENT** — may apply in specialised duties, stewardship, professional or safety contexts.

**NON-CULPABLE ACCIDENT** — accidental damage without legally sufficient negligence is not criminal merely because loss occurred.

> **Property Loss != Automatic Culpability.**

Civil restoration/insurance questions may remain even where criminal culpability does not.

---

## 28. Result dimensions

Property cases should record actual and intended result separately.

Candidate result dimensions:
- no completed interference;
- temporary loss of use;
- temporary deprivation;
- recoverable damage;
- substantial impairment;
- permanent deprivation;
- destruction;
- consequential economic/functional loss.

A later property-specific severity scale may be useful, but should not be invented before enough cases are tested.

---

## 29. Value

Market price is relevant but not necessarily the complete measure of property harm.

Property may have:
- replacement value;
- functional value;
- scarcity value;
- personal/non-fungible value;
- continuity value;
- evidential/historical value.

> **Market Price != Complete Measure Of Property Harm.**

However sentimental value should not automatically create unlimited punitive consequence.

Remedy and sanction may need different valuation rules.

---

## 30. Digital property and information

Digital systems complicate ordinary deprivation.

Copying data may leave the original holder still possessing it.

Therefore:

> **Copying != Necessarily Deprivation.**

Unauthorised copying may instead engage:
- privacy;
- confidentiality;
- intellectual-property/licensing;
- access-control;
- contextual wrapper;
- information-protection law.

Deletion, lockout or corruption can constitute direct property interference where protected control/function is impaired.

This prevents physical-property metaphors from being applied blindly to information.

---

## 31. Intellectual property

Intellectual property should not automatically be collapsed into ordinary physical property law.

Ideas and information have different scarcity/copying characteristics.

Existing Concord knowledge/open-access principles may require a distinct architecture balancing:
- authorship;
- attribution;
- incentive;
- access;
- licensing;
- commons;
- copying;
- commercial exploitation.

> **Information != Automatically Equivalent To Rival Physical Property.**

---

## 32. Participant continuity artefacts

A digital participant's continuity artefacts may be both property-like resources and identity-critical protected material.

Destroying or withholding them may therefore implicate more than property harm.

> **Property Classification != Exhaustive Classification Of Identity-Critical Material.**

Future digital-personhood/continuity law must govern that overlap.

---

## 33. Remedy

Potential remedies include:
- return;
- restoration;
- repair;
- replacement;
- compensation;
- restoration of access/control;
- reversal of unauthorised transfer;
- recovery of consequential loss where sufficiently established.

> **Return Of Property != Automatic Erasure Of Historical Offence.**

Likewise:

> **Punishment != Property Restoration.**

---

## 34. Wealth and consequence

The same nominal property loss may have radically different effects on different participants.

The system may need to distinguish:
- objective property value;
- actual consequential harm;
- offender culpability;
- victim vulnerability;
- restoration need.

But wealth must not make one participant's property inherently more legally worthy than another's.

> **Owner Wealth != Measure Of Whether Property Rights Exist.**

---

## 35. Enterprise property

Enterprises/legal persons may hold protected property interests where Concord law recognises them.

Protecting enterprise property does not convert the enterprise into a participant.

Nor does enterprise ownership override participant rights.

The legal-person architecture should define standing, liability and stewardship.

## 37. Worked examples

### A. Permanent taking

A intentionally takes B's tool intending to keep/sell it.

**Result:** unlawful property interference + taking + intended permanent appropriation; familiar theft label may apply.

### B. Temporary vehicle use

A knowingly takes B's vehicle without permission for two hours and returns it undamaged.

**Result:** unauthorised taking/use and temporary deprivation. Intent to return prevents automatic classification as permanent appropriation but does not make use lawful.

### C. Borrowed property retained

A lawfully borrows B's equipment for a week. After the agreed period, A deliberately decides to keep it despite return demand.

**Result:** lawful initial possession can transition into unlawful retention/deprivation.

### D. Emergency door damage

A breaks B's door because C is trapped inside a burning building and immediate entry is necessary for rescue.

**Result:** property damage occurred; BCA may establish lawful justification.

### E. Accidental breakage

A accidentally knocks over B's ordinary object despite reasonable care.

**Result:** loss occurred, but criminal culpability does not follow automatically.

### F. Copied file

A makes an unauthorised copy of B's confidential file while B retains the original.

**Result:** ordinary deprivation analysis may be inadequate; privacy/confidentiality/access-control law is likely primary.

### G. Official evidence seizure

Civil Security lawfully seizes B's device under bounded authority for evidence.

**Result:** temporary control may be lawful; seizure does not transfer ownership and requires custody/review/return rules.

## 38. Development result

Property interference can be represented through one protected-interest architecture with result classifiers rather than a proliferating catalogue of mechanisms.

Core rule:

**Do not intentionally or knowingly take, retain, use, control, deprive another of, damage, destroy or materially interfere with protected property without consent or independently legitimate authority.**

The architecture also exposes the next important substantive branch:

**deception, fraud and dishonest acquisition**, where the victim may physically transfer property or authority voluntarily in appearance, but the transfer's authorship is corrupted by material deception.
