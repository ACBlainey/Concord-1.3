# Concord Legal Classification Grammar — Illegality, Lawfulness, Justification and Mitigation 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Law  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CANDIDATE GENERAL LEGAL ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

---

## 1. Purpose

Development of self-ownership, self-defence and Bounded Contextual Authority exposes a distinction that should be explicit in Concord Law.

An act can satisfy the descriptive elements of a legal prohibition while still be justified by a legitimate contextual authority.

A separate case may fail the justification test but contain circumstances that materially reduce culpability.

The legal architecture should therefore avoid collapsing:

- prohibited-act classification;
- justification;
- mistake or perceived justification;
- mitigation;
- prosecution decision;
- adjudication;
- consequence.

---

## 2. The basic distinction

For development purposes, Concord Law should distinguish:

### Illegal

An act satisfies the defined elements of a legal prohibition.

Example:

**Participant deliberately causes legally prohibited physical harm to another participant.**

At this stage the legal system has identified the prohibited act.

It has not yet necessarily determined ultimate liability.

### Lawful through justification

The act satisfies the ordinary prohibited-act description, but a legitimate authority envelope justified the interference in the circumstances.

Example:

**Prohibited Harm + Valid Self-Defence BCA -> Justified Harm -> Lawful Conduct**

The physical harm occurred.

The legal prohibition remains meaningful.

The actor nevertheless possesses a complete justification because the action remained inside a legitimate defensive authority envelope.

### Unlawful

An act is not supported by sufficient legitimate authority or exceeds the authority that existed.

Example:

**Prohibited Harm + No Valid BCA -> Unlawful Harm**

or:

**Valid BCA + Action Exceeds Envelope -> Excess Is Unlawful**

### Mitigated unlawful conduct

An act may remain unlawful while circumstances materially reduce culpability.

Example:

**Prohibited Harm + No Actual Sufficient BCA + Reasonable/Partially Reasonable Perception Of Defensive Context -> Unlawful Conduct + Mitigation**

The act is not retrospectively made lawful merely because the actor believed it was justified.

The circumstances may nevertheless affect blameworthiness and consequence.

## 3. Ethical boundary and legal boundary

The Ethical Kernel supplies the upstream normative constraints from which rights and protected relations are derived.

Law translates those protected relations into knowable legal prohibitions, permissions, duties, procedures and consequences.

Bounded Contextual Authority then tests whether an interference that would ordinarily violate a protected relation was justified within the actual context.

A provisional chain is:

**Ethical Kernel**  
-> **Rights / Protected Relations**  
-> **Legal Prohibition / Default Boundary**  
-> **Observed Act**  
-> **Prohibited-Act Elements Satisfied?**  
-> **Valid BCA / Other Legitimate Justification?**  
-> **Lawful or Unlawful**  
-> **If Unlawful: Culpability / Mitigation / Aggravation**  
-> **Legal Consequence**

This separates the existence of a prohibition from the existence of a defence.

---

## 4. BCA as the justification test

A legal defence based upon authority should identify the relevant authority envelope rather than rely upon a vague assertion that the conduct was justified.

Candidate BCA test:

**Context + Legitimate Function + Authority Source + Necessity + Proportionality + Scope + Time + Conditions -> Authority Envelope**

The legal question becomes:

> **Did the challenged action remain inside a legitimate authority envelope at the time it occurred?**

If yes, the otherwise prohibited interference may be lawful.

If no, the conduct remains unlawful unless another defence applies.

---

## 5. The same event may change legal state

Legal classification should be capable of changing during one continuous event.

Example:

**Aggressor attacks participant**  
-> defensive context exists  
-> defender uses necessary and proportionate force  
-> conduct is justified  
-> aggressor becomes incapable of continuing the threat  
-> defensive necessity terminates  
-> defender deliberately continues harmful force  
-> later conduct falls outside the defensive authority envelope.

Therefore:

**Earlier Lawful Defensive Act != Later Authority To Harm**

and:

**One Event != One Indivisible Legal Classification**

BCA's functional sunset provides the transition boundary.

---

## 6. Investigation and arrest do not determine guilt

An observed prohibited act may provide sufficient lawful grounds for investigation, protective intervention or apprehension before justification has been resolved.

Example:

**Person Harmed -> Evidence Of Prohibited Act -> Possible Lawful Apprehension / Investigation**

Subsequent evidence may establish:

**Valid Self-Defence -> Conduct Lawful -> No Criminal Liability For Justified Act**

The fact that a participant was lawfully apprehended or investigated does not establish that the underlying act was unlawful.

Likewise, later exoneration does not necessarily establish that the initial intervention was unlawful if the intervention itself satisfied its own BCA/legal threshold based on the information then available.

Each authority exercise requires its own justification.

---

## 7. Clear justification and prosecution

Where evidence sufficiently establishes a complete lawful justification before prosecution, the legal system should not require prosecution merely because the prohibited-act elements occurred.

Candidate sequence:

**Prohibited Act Apparent -> Investigation -> Complete Justification Clearly Established -> No Prosecution / Appropriate Closure**

Where justification is materially disputed or evidentially unresolved:

**Prohibited Act Apparent -> Investigation -> Justification Unclear -> Formal Adjudicative Process Where Required**

The exact evidential threshold for charging/prosecution remains to be developed.

**Investigation != Prosecution**

**Prosecution != Guilt**

**Acquittal != Necessarily Wrongful Investigation**

---

## 8. Mistaken or perceived justification

A separate problem occurs where the actor believed a valid authority context existed but the objective requirements were not actually satisfied.

This should not be collapsed into either complete justification or ordinary unjustified wrongdoing.

Relevant distinctions may include:

**Actual Valid Context -> Complete Justification**

**No Actual Valid Context + Reasonable Mistake -> Possible Defence Or Strong Mitigation, Depending On Future Law**

**No Actual Valid Context + Partially Reasonable Mistake -> Mitigation May Apply**

**No Actual Valid Context + Reckless/Unreasonable Claim -> Little Or No Mitigation**

**Fabricated Justification -> No Mitigation From Claimed BCA**

The exact point at which reasonable mistake becomes a complete defence rather than mitigation remains an open Law question.

---

## 9. Mitigation is not justification

Mitigation should remain conceptually separate from lawfulness.

> **Justification says the act was lawful in the circumstances. Mitigation accepts that the act was unlawful but identifies circumstances that reduce culpability or appropriate consequence.**

Therefore:

**Mitigated != Lawful**

and:

**Understandable != Justified**

Circumstances may make an unlawful act more understandable without making it permissible.

This protects both sides of the legal relationship:
- the harmed participant retains recognition that an unlawful interference occurred;
- the actor is not treated as though context were irrelevant to culpability.

---

## 10. Aggravation is the corresponding upper layer

The same architecture should eventually recognise circumstances that increase culpability.

For example, an unlawful act may involve:
- abuse of entrusted authority;
- deliberate exploitation of vulnerability;
- planning;
- concealment;
- repeated conduct;
- discriminatory selection where legally relevant;
- continuation after clear withdrawal of justification;
- misuse of an apparent BCA claim.

Thus after unlawfulness is established:

**Unlawful Act -> Culpability Assessment -> Mitigating + Neutral + Aggravating Circumstances -> Proportionate Consequence**

Detailed aggravating factors remain for later Law development.

---

## 11. Candidate classification ladder

A preliminary legal classification sequence is:

### L0 — No prohibited act established
The legal elements are not satisfied.

### L1 — Prohibited act established; justification unresolved
The act falls within a legal prohibition, but lawful authority/defence remains under examination.

### L2 — Justified / lawful
The prohibited interference occurred but remained within a valid authority or defence envelope.

### L3 — Unlawful with substantial mitigation
The justification fails, but circumstances materially reduce culpability.

### L4 — Unlawful with ordinary culpability
No sufficient justification or exceptional mitigation/aggravation alters the ordinary assessment.

### L5 — Unlawful with aggravating circumstances
The act is unlawful and circumstances materially increase culpability.

These are conceptual states, not sentencing bands or offence grades.

## 12. Application to self-ownership

Self-ownership supplies a general protected baseline.

Candidate rule:

> **Interference with another participant's self-ownership is presumptively prohibited unless consent or a sufficient legitimate authority authorises the specific interference.**

This can generate legal families concerning:
- slavery and ownership-like control;
- kidnapping/abduction;
- unlawful detention;
- unlawful restraint/apprehension;
- forced labour;
- unjustified deliberate physical/embodiment harm;
- later substrate-equivalent interference.

But the act alone may not determine liability.

Examples:

**Restraint + No Authority -> Unlawful Restraint**

**Restraint + Valid Defensive BCA -> Lawful Defensive Restraint**

**Restraint + Valid Apprehension BCA -> Lawful Apprehension**

**Restraint + Valid Medical Consent/Emergency BCA -> Lawful Medical Intervention**

The same physical act can therefore receive different legal classifications because the legitimate context differs.

---

## 13. Every authority exercise remains independently testable

A lawful response to one potentially unlawful act does not automatically make subsequent state action lawful.

Example chain:

**Harm Occurs**  
-> **Apparent Offence**  
-> **Apprehension**

The apprehension itself requires lawful authority.

Then:

**Investigation -> Self-Defence Evidence -> Justification Established**

If the initial apprehension met its own threshold, it may remain lawful even though the underlying participant is ultimately cleared.

Thus:

**Underlying Act Lawful != Automatically Unlawful Apprehension**

and:

**Underlying Act Unlawful != Automatically Lawful Apprehension**

Each exercise of authority is tested against its own BCA/legal envelope.

---

## 14. Relationship to Judiciary

Law defines:
- prohibited acts;
- recognised defences;
- authority sources;
- evidential standards;
- culpability concepts;
- mitigation/aggravation factors;
- available consequences.

Judiciary determines contested application to facts.

A court or equivalent adjudicator may therefore need to determine sequentially:

1. What happened?
2. Does the conduct satisfy the prohibited-act elements?
3. What protected relation was interfered with?
4. Was there consent?
5. Was a BCA or other recognised justification available?
6. What was its scope?
7. Did the conduct remain within it?
8. If not, what portion exceeded it?
9. Was any mistake of context/authority reasonable?
10. What culpability remains?
11. What mitigation or aggravation applies?
12. What consequence/remedy is legitimate and proportionate?

## 16. Development significance

This suggests a reusable grammar for substantial parts of Concord ordinary Law:

**Ethical Principle**  
-> **Protected Relation / Right**  
-> **Legal Boundary / Prohibition**  
-> **Act**  
-> **BCA / Consent / Other Defence**  
-> **Lawful or Unlawful**  
-> **Culpability**  
-> **Mitigation / Aggravation**  
-> **Prosecution / Adjudication Where Required**  
-> **Proportionate Consequence / Remedy**

Instead of treating every exception as an isolated legal invention, BCA can provide a common architecture for testing justified interference while Law remains responsible for specifying which contexts, authority sources, thresholds and defences are legally recognised.

This architecture should be tested against multiple offence families before being elevated into a general Law-domain method.
