## 1. Purpose

This document derives candidate law protecting the integrity of entrusted authority, fiduciary responsibility and purpose-bound civil function.

It addresses conduct conventionally described as:
- corruption;
- bribery;
- abuse of office;
- self-dealing;
- misuse of entrusted assets/information/capability;
- corrupt favour;
- some conflicts of interest;
- fiduciary exploitation.

The protected interest is not "obedience to officials" or preservation of institutional prestige.

It is:

> **Integrity Of Entrusted Function And The Protected Interests For Which That Function Exists.**

## 3. Entrustment

Entrustment occurs where a participant receives bounded control, discretion, custody, access, authority or responsibility over interests they do not wholly own for a defined legitimate function.

Examples:
- public office;
- guardianship;
- trusteeship;
- advocacy;
- enterprise fiduciary role;
- custody;
- professional discretion;
- control of communal resources;
- authorised access to protected information;
- delegated purchasing/contracting authority.

---

## 4. Foundational boundary

> **Entrusted Power != Personal Property.**

The holder may exercise it only for the legitimate function and within its authority envelope.

Likewise:

> **Control != Ownership.**

> **Access != Personal Entitlement.**

> **Discretion != Permission To Substitute Private Purpose.**

---

## 5. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not intentionally or knowingly use, redirect, sell, exchange, conceal, withhold, distort or otherwise exercise an entrusted authority, discretion, asset, access right, capability or fiduciary function for an illegitimate purpose materially inconsistent with the function, protected interest or duty for which it was entrusted.**

Reckless or negligent fiduciary breach may require specialised civil/regulatory treatment rather than the same criminal classification.

---

## 6. Function substitution

The central corrupt state is often:

**Legitimate Entrusted Function**  
→ **Private / Illegitimate Purpose Substituted**  
→ **Entrusted Capability Used To Serve New Purpose**

Therefore:

> **Valid Authority Source != Valid Use For Every Purpose.**

An official may possess a real capability and still act unlawfully by repurposing it.

---

## 7. Personal benefit

Personal benefit may include:
- money;
- property;
- employment;
- status;
- gifts;
- sexual access;
- favours;
- political advantage;
- benefit to family/associates;
- reciprocal treatment;
- concealment of wrongdoing;
- future opportunity.

But:

> **Personal Benefit != Necessary Element Of Every Abuse Of Entrusted Authority.**

A participant may misuse authority maliciously or ideologically without receiving material benefit.

---

## 8. Bribery

Candidate bribery structure:

**Offer / Request / Give / Receive / Agree To Receive Benefit**  
+ **Purpose Of Improperly Influencing Entrusted Function**  
+ **Required Mental State**

The wrong is not the gift itself.

> **Gift != Automatically Bribe.**

The legally significant state is the intended corruption of entrusted function.

---

## 9. Bribe offer without acceptance

An attempted corrupt influence can exist even if the entrusted participant refuses.

> **Refused Bribe != No Corrupt Offer.**

The offeror's conduct can be independently actionable.

The entrusted participant who refuses and appropriately reports it has not thereby committed corruption.

---

## 10. Accepted benefit without corrupt purpose

Not every accepted benefit is bribery.

Relevant questions include:
- Was the benefit permitted?
- Was it connected to a decision/function?
- Was there an expectation/exchange?
- Was disclosure required?
- Could it materially distort impartial function?

> **Benefit Received != Automatic Proof Of Corruption.**

---

## 11. Quid pro quo and influence

Corruption may involve explicit exchange, but sophisticated corruption may avoid explicit words.

Evidence can include:
- timing;
- concealed transfers;
- repeated favourable decisions;
- communications;
- conflicts;
- unusual deviations;
- intermediary conduct.

But:

> **Suspicious Pattern != Automatic Proof Of Corrupt Agreement.**

Mirrored Reality Trees should test innocent and culpable explanations.

---

## 12. Self-dealing

A fiduciary may direct entrusted assets/opportunities toward themselves.

> **Authority To Manage Asset != Authority To Appropriate Asset.**

Self-dealing may overlap with:
- property interference;
- fraud;
- false records;
- fiduciary breach.

---

## 13. Conflict of interest

A conflict exists where a participant's private interests could materially compete with entrusted function.

> **Conflict Of Interest != Automatically Corruption.**

Conflicts may be managed through:
- disclosure;
- recusal;
- independent review;
- divestment;
- reassignment;
- other safeguards.

The offence arises from culpable misuse/concealment where legally specified, not merely from human participants having multiple interests.

---

## 14. Disclosure

Disclosure can make a conflict legible but does not automatically legitimise conduct.

> **Disclosure != Permission.**

If the conflict is incompatible with the function, recusal or other action may still be required.

---

## 15. Recusal

Where impartial performance cannot reasonably be maintained, recusal can preserve function.

> **Recusal != Admission Of Corruption.**

It is often a preventative integrity mechanism.

---

## 16. Public office

Public authority is especially clearly entrusted.

> **Public Office != Ownership Of Public Power.**

A public official acts as holder of bounded civil function, not sovereign owner of authority.

Use of official powers for private retaliation, enrichment or favour can therefore violate the protected interest even where the official technically possesses the relevant capability.

---

## 17. Political disagreement

Disagreement with an official decision is not evidence of corruption.

> **Unpopular Decision != Corrupt Decision.**

> **Policy Difference != Abuse Of Office.**

The analysis must identify illegitimate purpose, conflict, duty breach, deception or other relevant state.

---

## 18. Discretion

Some entrusted roles legitimately require judgment.

> **Discretion != Corruption.**

> **Different Reasonable Decision != Proof Of Improper Purpose.**

Law must preserve genuine decision space.

---

## 19. Error

Mistakes occur.

> **Bad Decision != Automatic Corruption.**

Negligence, incompetence, error and corruption are distinct states.

Correction and accountability may still follow.

---

## 20. Favouritism

Entrusted decisions should use legitimate criteria.

Deliberately substituting irrelevant private favour may violate fiduciary integrity and fair comparable treatment.

This interfaces with the future discrimination/fair-treatment derivation.

> **Personal Preference != Legitimate Criterion Merely Because Decision-Maker Holds Authority.**

---

## 21. Nepotism

Benefit to relatives/associates is not automatically corrupt where they independently satisfy legitimate criteria and process is protected.

But undisclosed or improper preference can be evidence of function substitution.

> **Relationship != Automatic Disqualification.**

> **Relationship != Automatic Permission For Preferential Treatment.**

---

## 22. Fiduciary duty outside government

The architecture applies beyond public officials.

Examples:
- trustee;
- guardian;
- executor;
- enterprise director;
- advocate;
- professional handling another's assets;
- AI guardian/guarantor role.

> **Private Institution != Absence Of Entrusted Duty.**

---

## 23. Advocacy

An advocate may legitimately favour the represented participant within the role.

Therefore:

> **Fiduciary Loyalty != Universal Impartiality.**

The protected function determines the duty.

A defence advocate serving their client is not corrupt merely because they do not neutrally balance prosecution interests.

---

## 24. Judicial/prosecutorial function

Some roles require stronger impartiality.

Judges/adjudicators and other neutral decision-makers must not substitute personal interest for adjudicative function.

Prosecution/defence fiduciaries may have different legitimate role duties.

> **Same Institution != Same Fiduciary Function.**

---

## 25. Custody

Custodial authority exists for lawful custody/protection/process.

Using custody to extract:
- money;
- labour;
- sexual compliance;
- unrelated information;
- personal favours

is outside the custodial function.

> **Custody Authority != General Leverage Over Detainee.**

Other base offences may additionally apply.

---

## 26. Information access

Entrusted access to private information is purpose-bound.

> **Authorised Access For Function A != Personal Use For Purpose B.**

Misuse may combine:
- fiduciary breach;
- privacy intrusion;
- fraud;
- harassment.

---

## 27. Resources

Control of public/common/enterprise resources does not convert them into personal property.

> **Budget Authority != Personal Ownership Of Funds.**

Misappropriation may combine fiduciary breach and property interference.

---

## 28. Procurement

Procurement decisions should serve legitimate procurement function.

Bribes, kickbacks or undisclosed self-dealing can corrupt:
- price;
- quality;
- competition;
- resource stewardship.

But:

> **Lowest Price != Automatically Required Fiduciary Decision.**

Legitimate criteria can include quality, resilience, stewardship, risk and lifecycle value.

---

## 29. Gifts and hospitality

A mature implementation may define:
- permitted low-risk gifts;
- disclosure thresholds;
- prohibited benefits;
- role-specific restrictions.

This is regulatory implementation.

The fundamental rule remains purpose/function integrity.

---

## 30. Post-role benefits

A benefit offered after a decision may still be corrupt if it was promised/expected as part of the prior decision.

Conversely:

> **Later Benefit != Automatic Proof Of Earlier Corruption.**

Evidence must establish connection.

---

## 31. Future employment

Promises of future employment can create conflicts or corrupt incentives.

This may require disclosure/recusal rules in sensitive roles.

But ordinary career mobility should not be criminalised.

---

## 32. Intermediaries

Bribes/corrupt benefits can be routed through third parties.

Participation/complicity applies.

> **Indirect Benefit != Automatically Unrelated Benefit.**

But relationship alone is insufficient; knowledge/authorship must be established.

---

## 33. Organisational corruption

Corrupt states can become systemic.

Potential indicators:
- routine kickbacks;
- concealed decision criteria;
- retaliation against reporting;
- manipulated records;
- captured oversight.

> **Systemic Corruption != Automatic Guilt Of Every Member.**

Individual and organisational responsibility must be source-resolved.

---

## 34. Enterprise/legal person responsibility

An enterprise may itself bear responsibility through authorised decision structures, while individual participants bear responsibility according to role/authorship.

> **Corporate Liability != Automatic Employee Liability.**

> **Employee Liability != Automatic Exoneration Of Enterprise.**

---

## 35. AI decision-makers

An AI participant/system may exercise entrusted function.

The same rules apply conceptually:
- function;
- authority;
- purpose;
- constraints;
- conflicts;
- provenance;
- audit.

> **Algorithmic Decision != Absence Of Entrusted Function.**

Where the AI is not an independent participant, responsibility must trace to deployers/operators/designers according to actual authorship and duty.

---

## 36. Hidden optimisation objectives

An automated decision system may appear to perform function A while secretly optimising for private objective B.

This is a direct function-substitution risk.

> **Declared Function != Actual Decision Objective.**

Auditability and provenance matter.

---

## 37. Bribery of AI

If an AI participant independently holds entrusted discretion, attempts to improperly influence it may be functionally equivalent to bribery.

If it is merely a tool, manipulation may instead be fraud, unauthorised access or interference with the responsible institution.

Participant status matters.

---

## 38. Coercion of entrusted participant

An entrusted participant may be coerced into misuse.

The coercion architecture and mitigation apply.

> **Coerced Misuse != Automatically Fully Authored Corruption.**

Protection of the underlying function remains necessary even where individual culpability is reduced.

---

## 39. Capacity

Mental-health/capacity architecture applies.

> **Entrusted Role != Presumption Of Permanent Full Capacity.**

But diminished capacity may also require suspension/reassignment to protect the entrusted interest.

Protection and culpability remain separate.

---

## 40. Duty to disclose material conflicts

Some entrusted functions may create positive disclosure duties.

This uses the Positive Duties/Omissions grammar:

**Entrusted Role**  
→ **Material Conflict**  
→ **Disclosure/Recusal Duty**  
→ **Knowing Failure**  
→ **Responsibility**

The duty must be promulgated and scoped.

---

## 41. Duty to preserve records

Sensitive entrusted functions may require decision records/provenance.

This supports:
- audit;
- correction;
- contestability;
- anti-capture.

But:

> **Audit Need != Unlimited Surveillance Of Office Holder.**

Record requirements should track function.

---

## 42. Whistleblowing

Participants should be able to report suspected corruption in good faith.

> **Good-Faith Corruption Report != False Accusation Merely Because It Is Not Proven.**

False-assertion protections remain applicable to knowing fabrication.

Anti-retaliation may require separate employment/governance implementation.

---

## 43. Oversight

Oversight itself is entrusted authority.

> **Anti-Corruption Authority != Immunity From Corruption Rules.**

Recursive oversight applies.

Investigators/auditors must remain bounded by their own function.

---

## 44. Entrapment and testing

Integrity testing may be legitimate in some contexts but risks manufacturing wrongdoing or exceeding investigative authority.

Future Civil Security/Judiciary procedure must derive its boundaries.

> **Desire To Detect Corruption != Unlimited Authority To Manufacture It.**

---

## 45. Benefit to protected function

An entrusted participant may make decisions that incidentally benefit themselves while legitimately serving the entrusted function.

> **Incidental Benefit != Automatic Corruption.**

The analysis concerns purpose, conflict, process and material distortion.

---

## 46. Mixed motives

Human decisions can have multiple motives.

A private motive does not automatically invalidate an otherwise legitimate decision.

Candidate question:

**Did the illegitimate interest materially distort or substitute for the entrusted function?**

> **Mixed Motive != Automatic Corruption.**

This avoids impossible purity tests.

---

## 47. Appearance versus actuality

Public confidence matters but cannot substitute for proof.

> **Appearance Of Conflict != Proof Of Corrupt Conduct.**

Appearance may justify disclosure, recusal or review.

Punitive liability requires established elements.

---

## 48. Harm

Corruption can cause:
- financial loss;
- unequal treatment;
- rights interference;
- unsafe systems;
- degraded services;
- wrongful legal outcomes;
- institutional capture;
- public distrust;
- opportunity loss.

Some corruption may be complete before quantifiable downstream harm occurs because the entrusted function itself has been intentionally corrupted.

> **No Proven Financial Loss != No Fiduciary Harm.**

---

## 49. Attempt

**Attempt Liability: YES / CONDITIONAL BY FORM.**

Examples:
- offering a bribe that is refused may itself be a completed corrupt-offer offence;
- direct execution of planned self-dealing prevented before transfer may be attempt;
- mere private desire to misuse office remains insufficient.

---

## 50. Participation

General Participation/Complicity architecture applies.

Potential participants:
- benefit giver;
- benefit receiver;
- intermediary;
- directing participant;
- knowing facilitator;
- record falsifier.

> **Transaction Proximity != Automatic Complicity.**

---

## 51. Aggravation

Potential aggravators:
- high office/trust;
- vulnerable beneficiary;
- major public/common resources;
- repeated/systemic conduct;
- concealment;
- retaliation;
- interference with oversight;
- severe downstream harm.

> **Higher Authority Can Increase Responsibility Because Entrusted Capability And Consequence Increase.**

But status alone should not mechanically determine sentence.

---

## 52. Mitigation

Potential mitigation:
- coercion;
- diminished capacity;
- minor peripheral role;
- limited knowledge;
- prompt voluntary correction/cooperation where genuine.

Correction does not erase completed corruption.

---

## 53. Remedy

Potential remedies:
- return/restitution;
- reversal/review of corrupted decision;
- recusal/removal from specific function;
- re-procurement/re-adjudication;
- correction of records;
- compensation;
- protective reassignment;
- audit/system redesign;
- sanction.

> **Removing Office Holder != Automatically Repairing Corrupted Decision.**

The function/output may need restoration separately.

---

## 54. Disqualification

Loss of a role may sometimes be justified where trust/function has been materially compromised.

But:

> **Misuse Of Authority A != Automatic Permanent Disqualification From Every Authority B.**

Restrictions must be function-related, proportionate and reviewable.

---

## 55. Sentence versus civil protection

Punishment and protection of entrusted function remain distinct.

A participant may complete a criminal sentence yet remain temporarily or permanently unsuitable for a specific fiduciary role if an independently justified competence/trust condition applies.

> **Sentence Expiry != Automatic Qualification For Every Entrusted Role.**

Conversely:

> **Role Disqualification != Additional Punitive Custody.**

---

## 56. Correction of corrupted decisions

Where corruption affected a decision, the legal system should identify dependent outputs.

Potential chain:

**Corrupted Decision Found**  
→ **Identify Dependent Decisions / Transfers / Permissions**  
→ **Review Dependency**  
→ **Correct Where Legally Required**

This resembles KCS change propagation.

> **Correction Of Decision Source May Require Review Of Dependent Legal States.**

Not every dependent state automatically reverses; reliance/finality/third-party rights may matter.

---

## 57. Historical record

Historical should preserve:
- entrusted function;
- authority state;
- conflict;
- evidence;
- decision;
- finding;
- correction.

But:

> **Past Corruption != Permanent Identity Of Participant.**

Records should preserve truth without converting history into unlimited future authority.

## 59. Worked examples

### A. Bribe offered and refused

A offers official B money to approve an application regardless of legitimate criteria. B refuses and reports it.

**Result:** A's corrupt offer may be complete; B is not corrupt merely for receiving the offer.

### B. Permitted gift

B receives a disclosed low-value ceremonial gift permitted by applicable rules, with no decision exchange.

**Result:** receipt alone does not establish bribery.

### C. Self-dealing

Trustee A directs an entrusted asset to a company secretly owned by A on deliberately disadvantageous terms for the beneficiary.

**Result:** fiduciary function has been substituted by private purpose; property/fraud issues may additionally apply.

### D. Unpopular decision

Official A makes a controversial decision using legitimate published criteria and receives no improper benefit.

**Result:** disagreement or unpopularity is not evidence of corruption.

### E. Custodial leverage

Officer A threatens to withhold B's ordinary lawful access to food unless B performs a personal favour.

**Result:** custodial authority is repurposed as private leverage; coercion and entrusted-authority abuse may coexist.

### F. Conflict and recusal

Decision-maker A discovers that a close relative is bidding for a contract, discloses the conflict and recuses according to procedure.

**Result:** conflict existed; corruption is not thereby established. Integrity mechanism functioned.

### G. AI procurement system

An automated procurement system is secretly configured to favour a supplier linked to its operator rather than apply the declared procurement criteria.

**Result:** algorithmic mediation does not remove the entrusted-function problem; responsibility traces through actual authorship/control.

## 60. Development result

Entrusted authority creates a distinct protected legal relation not fully reducible to property, fraud or coercion.

The protected object is:

# **The Integrity Of Purpose-Bound Power Exercised For Interests The Holder Does Not Own.**

This directly expresses the wider Concord authority model:

**Legitimacy → Function → Need → Authority**

Corruption reverses that relationship:

**Authority / Capability → Private Purpose → Function Rationalised Afterward**

The legal architecture must reject that reversal.

The next major distinct protected-interest gap identified by the Compact Basic Law Coverage Audit is **Fair Comparable Treatment and Illegitimate Discriminatory Exclusion**, deriving BL5 into operational law while preserving legitimate differentiation, competence requirements, contextual eligibility, private association and safe spaces.
