## 1. Purpose

This document derives candidate law governing harassment, stalking and persistent unwanted interference.

This category is structurally important because legal significance may arise from a **course of conduct** rather than from one independently prohibited act.

Candidate structure:

**Repeated / Patterned Conduct**  
→ **Targeting / Knowledge / Notice**  
→ **Context**  
→ **Cumulative Interference With Safe Space, Autonomy Or Ordinary Civil Function**  
→ **Consent / Legitimate Purpose / Authority**  
→ **Responsibility**

The architecture must preserve ordinary communication, accidental encounters, legitimate reporting, peaceful protest, lawful investigation and other civil interaction.

## 3. Pattern-level legal state

The central principle is:

> **Individually Lawful Acts May Form An Unlawful Pattern.**

Example:

One lawful message may be ordinary communication.

Hundreds of unwanted messages after an explicit request to stop may create a materially different legal state.

Therefore:

> **Act-Level Legality != Pattern-Level Legality.**

The legal system must be able to evaluate both.

---

## 4. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not intentionally or knowingly engage in a repeated, persistent or patterned course of unwanted conduct directed at another participant where, in context, the actor knows or should under a legally specified standard recognise that the course of conduct materially interferes with the other's protected safe space, autonomy, security or ordinary civil activity, absent legitimate purpose or authority.**

A stronger culpability threshold may be required for criminal sanction than for civil protective intervention.

---

## 5. Harassment

### HARASSMENT

A repeated or patterned course of targeted unwanted conduct producing material interference, distress, intimidation, disruption or degradation of protected civil function.

Harassment may occur through:
- communications;
- repeated approaches;
- unwanted presence;
- interference with work;
- repeated unwanted deliveries;
- coordinated targeting;
- publication directed at the target;
- technological means;
- other repeated conduct.

> **Annoyance != Automatically Harassment.**

Materiality and pattern matter.

---

## 6. Stalking

### STALKING

A pattern of targeted observation, following, monitoring, approach, contact or presence that materially intrudes upon another participant's safety, private context or practical freedom.

Stalking may exist without explicit threats.

> **No Explicit Threat != No Stalking.**

Fear may be relevant but should not be the only possible harm.

A participant may alter routes, employment, residence or ordinary activity because persistent targeting has made normal civil life unsafe or practically impossible.

---

## 7. Repetition

No universal numerical threshold should be fixed at this stage.

> **Two Acts != Automatically Harassment.**

> **Many Acts != Automatically Harassment.**

Frequency is one dimension alongside:
- targeting;
- duration;
- context;
- notice;
- purpose;
- intensity;
- proximity;
- persistence;
- cumulative effect.

---

## 8. Unwanted state

A participant can communicate that contact is unwanted explicitly or through sufficiently clear contextual conduct.

No particular phrase should be mandatory.

> **No Particular Formula Is Required To Communicate "Stop."**

But the law should not assume another participant can always infer an uncommunicated preference.

Notice matters.

---

## 9. Notice

Explicit notice can strengthen knowledge that continued conduct is unwanted.

Examples:
- "Do not contact me again."
- blocking an account;
- withdrawal from a voluntary relationship;
- formal no-contact notice;
- clear contextual withdrawal.

However:

> **Blocking One Channel != Automatic Universal Legal Prohibition On Every Future Contact.**

Context and legitimate necessity matter.

For example, a required legal notice may still need delivery through an authorised channel.

---

## 10. Withdrawal from relationship

Past relationship does not create continuing access.

> **Past Relationship != Continuing Contact Entitlement.**

This applies to:
- friendship;
- intimate relationships;
- family contact where no separate legal duty/right applies;
- employment;
- support relationships;
- voluntary associations.

The general Consent and Withdrawal architecture applies.

---

## 11. Public presence

A participant cannot ordinarily claim exclusive control over every public place they occupy.

> **Shared Public Presence != Automatically Stalking.**

Repeatedly arranging presence specifically to monitor or intimidate another participant can create a different state.

Intent, pattern and context matter.

---

## 12. Accidental encounters

Repeated coincidence can occur.

> **Repeated Encounter != Automatic Targeting.**

Evidence should distinguish:
- common route;
- shared workplace;
- shared community;
- deliberate following;
- deliberate schedule adaptation;
- tracking.

---

## 13. Observation

Ordinary observation in public is not automatically unlawful.

But persistent targeted observation may become stalking or privacy intrusion.

> **Lawful Observation At Time T != Unlimited Authority For Persistent Targeted Monitoring.**

Privacy and harassment analyses may overlap without being identical.

---

## 14. Communication

Participants remain free to:
- request;
- criticise;
- disagree;
- invite;
- communicate ordinary civil matters.

But repeated unwanted communication can become interference.

> **Right To Speak != Right To Compel Another's Attention Indefinitely.**

This does not create a general right to prevent others discussing a participant with third parties.

Targeted contact and public discourse are distinct contexts.

---

## 15. Criticism

Repeated criticism is not automatically harassment.

> **Repeated Disagreement != Automatically Harassment.**

Political, academic, journalistic and public-interest criticism may be persistent without targeting the subject's private safe space.

The law must distinguish:
- discussion about a participant;
- direct unwanted targeting of a participant;
- threats;
- false factual claims;
- privacy intrusion.

---

## 16. Protest

Peaceful protest can involve repetition, visibility and criticism.

> **Being The Subject Of Protest != Automatically Being Harassed.**

However protest does not authorise:
- unlawful entry;
- threats;
- targeted home intrusion;
- obstruction beyond lawful bounds;
- persistent private-space invasion.

Contextual boundaries remain operative.

---

## 17. Legitimate reporting and debt/contact processes

Repeated contact may have legitimate purposes:
- legal notices;
- safety warnings;
- debt administration;
- service communications;
- court processes;
- emergency contact.

Legitimate purpose does not create unlimited frequency or method.

> **Legitimate Purpose != Unlimited Contact Authority.**

Minimum necessary communication should be preferred.

---

## 18. Employment and professional contexts

Work may require repeated interaction.

> **Required Professional Contact != Automatically Harassment.**

But role authority does not permit:
- personal targeting;
- intimidation;
- sexualised unwanted conduct;
- persistent unrelated contact;
- abuse of scheduling/evaluation authority.

> **Professional Authority != Personal Access Entitlement.**

---

## 19. Sexual/romantic pursuit

An invitation or expression of interest is not automatically harassment.

Continued pursuit after clear refusal may become harassment depending on pattern/context.

> **Initial Interest != Continuing Entitlement To Pursue.**

> **Refusal != Invitation To Negotiate Indefinitely.**

This follows autonomy and withdrawal.

---

## 20. Threats

Harassment may include threats, but threats are not required.

Where threats occur, the Threats/Coercion architecture also applies.

> **Harassment != Threat Requirement.**

Multiple offences may coexist without double counting the same fact during consequence assessment.

---

## 21. Privacy intrusion

Stalking may involve:
- covert observation;
- tracking;
- data correlation;
- unauthorised access;
- recording.

The Privacy architecture applies separately.

> **Stalking Pattern != Automatic Permission To Collapse Privacy Findings Into One Label.**

Each protected interest should remain legible.

---

## 22. Property interference

Repeated minor interference with property may form part of harassment even if each incident is low-value.

Property law still applies where elements are satisfied.

Cumulative pattern may additionally show:
- targeting;
- intimidation;
- persistence;
- escalation.

---

## 23. Third-party contact

A participant may attempt to bypass a target's withdrawal by repeatedly contacting:
- family;
- friends;
- employer;
- colleagues;
- service providers.

This can form part of the same course of conduct where intended to continue unwanted access/control.

> **Indirect Contact != Automatically Independent Contact.**

But legitimate contact with third parties must not be prohibited merely because they know the target.

---

## 24. Coordinated harassment

Multiple participants can collectively create a harmful pattern.

The Participation and Complicity architecture applies.

> **Many Small Contributions May Form One Coordinated Pattern, But Group Membership != Individual Guilt.**

Each participant's knowledge, intent and contribution require proof.

---

## 25. Online scale

Digital systems allow:
- repeated messages;
- account creation;
- automated contact;
- mass publication;
- location tracking;
- coordinated targeting.

> **Low Cost Per Message != Low Cumulative Interference.**

Scale and persistence may materially alter harm.

---

## 26. Blocking and evasion

Deliberately creating new channels/accounts to defeat a clear contact boundary may evidence knowledge and persistence.

But:

> **Circumvention Evidence != Automatic Proof Of Every Alleged Motive.**

It is one evidential factor.

---

## 27. Automation

Automated systems can sustain harassment.

> **Automation != Absence Of Authorship.**

Responsibility should trace to those who intentionally configure, direct or knowingly maintain the system, subject to participation rules.

General-purpose platform providers are not automatically authors of user harassment.

---

## 28. AI-mediated conduct

AI may:
- send messages;
- monitor;
- schedule contact;
- generate content;
- identify locations.

> **Use Of AI != Automatic Transfer Of Responsibility To AI.**

Participant status and independent AI authorship, where applicable, require separate analysis.

---

## 29. Knowledge and mental state

Candidate criminal forms should ordinarily require:
- intentional targeting; or
- knowing continuation after the unwanted/material state is clear.

A civil protective order may operate on a different threshold where necessary to stop ongoing harm.

> **Protective Threshold != Punitive Culpability Threshold.**

This preserves the distinction between protection and punishment.

---

## 30. Reasonableness

A purely subjective standard is insufficient.

A participant's genuine discomfort matters, but cannot automatically criminalise ordinary lawful conduct.

The system should assess:
- actual context;
- communicated boundary;
- pattern;
- foreseeability;
- protected interest;
- legitimate purpose;
- proportionality.

> **Subjective Distress != Automatic Proof Of Unlawful Harassment.**

Likewise, absence of dramatic visible distress does not prove absence of material interference.

---

## 31. Harm

Potential harms include:
- fear;
- anxiety/distress;
- sleep disruption;
- loss of private space;
- changed routes/routines;
- employment disruption;
- social isolation;
- health effects;
- financial costs;
- loss of practical freedom;
- escalation risk.

> **No Physical Injury != No Harassment Harm.**

This parallels the physical-harm architecture's rejection of visibility as the sole measure.

---

## 32. Pattern evidence

Evidence may include:
- communications;
- timestamps;
- location histories;
- witness accounts;
- access logs;
- account linkage;
- repeated deliveries;
- surveillance records;
- explicit withdrawal/no-contact messages;
- shared contextual history.

Pattern analysis should avoid cherry-picking isolated events.

Mirrored Reality Trees may be useful:

**Targeted Harassment Hypothesis**  
↔  
**Coincidence / Legitimate Contact / Alternative Explanation**

---

## 33. Protective orders

A lawful protective/no-contact order may create clearer future boundaries.

Such orders require:
- legitimate threshold;
- defined scope;
- duration;
- exceptions where necessary;
- review;
- service/notice.

> **Protective Order != Ownership Of Another Participant's Movement.**

It should restrict only what is necessary to protect the relevant interest.

---

## 34. Geographic restrictions

Where necessary, bounded exclusion zones may be justified.

But:

> **Protection Of Safe Space != Unlimited Exclusion From Civil Society.**

Restrictions should consider:
- home;
- work;
- shared services;
- public routes;
- competing rights;
- practical feasibility.

Spatial Planning/context architecture may assist.

---

## 35. Attempt

Attempt liability is generally **CONDITIONAL**.

Because harassment is often a pattern offence, one intended contact may not itself constitute attempted harassment.

Where a participant enters direct execution of a planned prohibited course but the pattern is prevented early, attempt may sometimes apply.

This requires later testing.

> **Intent To Harass != Automatically Attempted Harassment Without Executory Conduct.**

---

## 36. Completion and continuation

A harassment offence may become complete once the legally sufficient course of conduct exists, while continuing conduct can increase duration/harm.

> **Completed Pattern != Licence To Count Every Later Act As A Separate Full Offence Automatically.**

Charging/sentencing architecture should prevent artificial multiplication while preserving additional distinct offences.

---

## 37. Remedy

Potential remedies include:
- cessation/no-contact order;
- channel blocking;
- privacy/security correction;
- return/restoration of property;
- relocation support where necessary;
- compensation for established loss;
- correction of false records/content where applicable;
- safety planning;
- other proportionate restoration.

The victim should not automatically bear all costs of creating safety.

> **Right To Safety != Duty To Disappear From Civil Life.**

---

## 38. Sanction and protection

Retrospective culpability and present risk remain distinct.

> **Harassment Culpability != Present Dangerousness.**

> **Present Escalation Risk != Proof Of Greater Historical Culpability.**

Protection may require current measures even where punitive responsibility is mitigated.

---

## 39. Escalation

Patterned unwanted conduct can sometimes precede physical violence.

Risk assessment may consider escalation indicators, but:

> **Statistical Risk Pattern != Proof Of Future Offence.**

Protective authority must remain evidence-based, proportionate and reviewable.

---

## 40. Historical record

Past harassment findings may inform future context where legally relevant.

But:

> **Past Harassment != Permanent Civil Identity.**

Historical custody should preserve findings without converting them into unlimited future restrictions.

## 42. Worked examples

### A. Repeated unwanted messages

A asks B on a date. B clearly declines. A then sends hundreds of direct messages over several weeks after repeated requests to stop.

**Result:** initial approach may be lawful; persistent targeted contact can create a harassment pattern.

### B. Shared commute

A and B independently use the same train every weekday and frequently see each other.

**Result:** repetition alone does not establish stalking; targeting must be evidenced.

### C. Deliberate following

A repeatedly changes route to follow B home, waits outside B's residence and records B's movements.

**Result:** stalking pattern may be established; privacy intrusion may additionally apply.

### D. Public criticism

A journalist publishes a series of critical articles about official B's public decisions without contacting B privately or invading protected spaces.

**Result:** repetition/criticism alone does not establish harassment.

### E. Required legal notice

B tells A not to contact them. A later serves one legally required court notice through the authorised route.

**Result:** legitimate bounded process is not automatically harassment.

### F. Coordinated targeting

A directs dozens of participants to repeatedly contact B specifically to overwhelm B's communication channels. Participants knowingly follow the plan.

**Result:** coordinated pattern can be analysed through participation/complicity; group membership alone remains insufficient.

### G. Protective order

A court imposes a bounded no-contact/exclusion condition after sufficient evidence of stalking.

**Result:** protective authority must remain scoped, reviewable and no broader than necessary.

## 43. Development result

Harassment and stalking demonstrate that Concord law must classify **patterns as legal objects**, not only isolated acts.

Core principle:

**An act may be lawful in isolation yet become part of an unlawful course when repeated, targeted and sustained in a way that materially destroys another participant's safe space, autonomy, security or ordinary civil function.**

This is a significant reusable legal concept beyond harassment. Pattern-level reasoning may later apply to:
- cumulative environmental harm;
- repeated low-level fraud;
- systemic discrimination;
- repeated authority misuse;
- distributed coercion;
- coordinated information abuse.

The next fundamental derivation should examine **sexual autonomy and sexual interference**, because the existing consent, physical integrity, privacy, coercion and contextual-wrapper architectures now provide most of the reusable machinery needed to derive it without mechanism-specific law.
