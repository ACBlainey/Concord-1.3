**Project:** The Concord Framework

**Date:** 7 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / FIRST WORKED LEGAL DERIVATION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Purpose

This is the first worked derivation of a candidate ordinary Concord offence from the Ethical Kernel.

The objective is not to reproduce a conventional catalogue of assault offences.

It is to test whether a compact base law plus factual classifiers can represent unlawful physical harm across different mechanisms, contexts, intentions, outcomes and participant substrates.

---

## 2. Ethical provenance

### Primary Ethical Kernel sources

**BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm**

> Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.

**BL2 — Respect Autonomy**

> Recognise the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.

**BL3 — Preserve Safe Spaces**

> Maintain environments in which individuals can live, learn, create and cooperate without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.

**BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity**

> Intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.

### Supporting source where consequences are foreseeable

**BL6 — Accept Responsibility**

> Capability creates responsibility for foreseeable consequences.

### Derived protected relations

The existing rights derivation supplies:
- autonomy and protection against unjustified interference;
- safe civil context;
- dignity and non-instrumentalisation;
- least-harm authority constraint;
- foreseeably affected participant standing.

The candidate protected legal interest is:

# **Embodied Integrity**

A participant has a protected interest against unjustified interference that harms or culpably endangers the integrity or functioning of their embodied substrate.

For a biological participant this includes bodily integrity and physiological function.

For another embodied substrate, equivalent protected integrity should be determined functionally rather than by assuming human anatomy.

> **Substrate Difference != Absence Of Protected Embodied Integrity.**

---

## 3. Candidate base prohibition

> **A participant must not, without valid consent, lawful justification or other recognised authority, deliberately, knowingly, recklessly or where legally specified negligently cause prohibited physical harm to another participant.**

A corresponding attempt rule may apply where the participant performs sufficiently executory conduct intended to cause prohibited physical harm but the intended harm does not occur.

This wording is provisional and must be tested against accident, sport, medicine, self-defence, emergency intervention and other legitimate contexts.

---

## 4. What the offence does not depend upon

The base offence does not depend upon whether harm was caused by:
- hand;
- foot;
- firearm;
- blade;
- blunt object;
- vehicle;
- chemical;
- heat;
- cold;
- electricity;
- radiation;
- machine;
- biological mechanism;
- remotely controlled system;
- novel technology.

Therefore:

> **Instrument Used != Fundamental Offence.**

The mechanism remains relevant evidence where it bears upon intent, preparation, expected consequence, foreseeability, proportionality, capability or other legally relevant facts.

---

## 5. Physical harm

Physical harm should be defined by adverse effect on embodied integrity or function, not merely by externally visible marks.

Candidate inclusions:
- pain;
- tissue damage;
- internal injury;
- neurological injury;
- physiological disruption;
- loss or impairment of function;
- loss of consciousness where harmful;
- organ damage;
- loss of limb/component;
- permanent impairment;
- death.

For non-biological embodied participants, equivalent functional damage may require substrate-specific evidence.

> **Visible Damage != Physical Harm Boundary.**

> **No External Mark != No Injury.**

Temporary contact without established injury may still constitute prohibited physical interference or an attempted harmful act depending on the facts.

---

## 6. Candidate harm bands

### H0 — Prohibited attack/interference without established injury

An unlawful physical attack, harmful interference or executory act is established, but resulting injury or functional damage is not established.

H0 does not mean the event is legally irrelevant.

### H1 — Minor harm

Limited injury, pain or impairment with minor functional consequence and ordinarily short recovery.

### H2 — Significant harm

Material injury, substantial pain, clinically/technically meaningful damage, treatment/repair requirement or temporary functional impairment.

### H3 — Serious harm

Major injury, serious internal/substrate damage, prolonged impairment, substantial loss of function or comparable consequence.

### H4 — Catastrophic harm

Permanent or profound impairment, loss of limb/organ/major component or function, severe irreversible damage or comparable consequence.

### H5 — Death / irreversible destruction of the participant's embodied life-function

For biological participants this includes death.

Application to digital/hybrid participants requires continuity/identity architecture and must not be inferred merely from hardware damage.

These bands are provisional and require Health, technical and legal calibration.

---

## 7. Actual harm and intended harm

The legal record should preserve both.

Example:

**Actor intends H4 harm**  
→ **executory attack occurs**  
→ **victim sustains H1 harm**

Candidate classification:
- base offence: Unlawful Physical Harm;
- completion: completed as to H1 harm;
- intended harm: H4;
- actual harm: H1;
- higher intended result: attempted.

The legal system should not discard either dimension.

> **Actual Harm Does Not Erase Intended Harm.**

> **Intended Harm Does Not Falsify Actual Harm.**

---

## 8. Attempt

Attempt liability is provisionally applicable.

Candidate requirement:

1. the participant possessed the legally required intention concerning prohibited physical harm; and
2. moved beyond mere thought and preparation into sufficiently direct execution of that intended harm; and
3. the intended result was not fully achieved.

Therefore:

> **Anger != Attempt.**

> **Fantasy != Attempt.**

> **Possession Of Capability != Attempt.**

> **Preparation != Necessarily Attempt.**

> **Executory Conduct + Required Intent May Constitute Attempt Even Where Harm Is H0.**

The precise preparation/attempt boundary remains for later legal testing.

---

## 9. Mental-state classifiers

Candidate classifiers:

### DELIBERATE / INTENTIONAL
The prohibited harm or relevant harmful result was an intended consequence.

### KNOWING
The participant acted while knowing the prohibited harmful consequence would occur or was effectively certain, without requiring it to be the actor's purpose.

### RECKLESS
The participant consciously proceeded despite a sufficiently serious and unjustified known risk of prohibited harm.

### NEGLIGENT
Where the law specifically recognises negligence liability, the participant failed to meet an applicable duty of care concerning a sufficiently foreseeable serious risk.

### NON-CULPABLE ACCIDENT
Harm occurred without the legally required culpable mental state or breach.

> **Harm Occurred != Offence Automatically Established.**

Definitions and thresholds require later refinement.

---

## 10. Consent

Valid consent may change the legal meaning of physical interference.

Examples can include:
- sport;
- medical treatment;
- body modification;
- physical training;
- consensual risk-taking.

But:

> **Consent To Context != Consent To Every Act Within Context.**

> **Consent To Risk != Consent To Unlimited Harm.**

> **Past Consent != Continuing Consent.**

> **Consent Beyond Participant Capacity != Automatically Valid Consent.**

The Contextual Wrapper Architecture should identify the bounded conditions of the activity.

Boxing provides a useful test:

**Valid Participation + Known Bounded Rules + Competent Consent + Conduct Within Wrapper**
may make physical acts lawful that would ordinarily satisfy prohibited physical-interference elements.

Conduct outside the wrapper remains independently testable.

---

## 11. Justification and authority

Potential recognised justifications may include:
- self-defence;
- defence of another;
- legitimate emergency intervention;
- legitimate medical intervention;
- other specifically defined Bounded Contextual Authority.

The existence of a beneficial purpose does not itself justify harmful interference.

> **Good Intention != Authority.**

> **Capability To Prevent Harm != Unlimited Authority To Interfere.**

Any justification must satisfy its own legal/BCA requirements.

---

## 12. Self-defence

Self-defence is governed by the existing Law-domain framework.

Physical harm caused in self-defence may be lawful where the defensive authority envelope exists and the action remains necessary and proportionate within it.

The authority can change during one event.

**Threat Exists**
→ **Defensive BCA Exists**
→ **Necessary/Proportionate Harm**
→ **Threat Ends**
→ **Defensive Harm Authority Ends**

Therefore:

> **Past Attack != Continuing Licence To Harm.**

---

## 13. Capacity and authorship

Capacity is evaluated independently of intent and planning.

Example:

A participant deliberately plans and executes an attack while acting from a severely impaired reality model.

The court may simultaneously find:
- intent established;
- premeditation established;
- prohibited act established;
- capacity materially impaired.

None of these findings automatically erases another.

> **Premeditation != Full Capacity.**

> **Intent != Full Authorship.**

Mental-health diagnosis is evidence only insofar as it establishes a relevant functional effect.

---

## 14. Participation classifiers

A later general participation architecture should distinguish at least:
- principal actor;
- co-principal;
- directing/ordering;
- intentionally assisting;
- intentionally facilitating;
- other legally defined participation.

Mere association with an offender is insufficient.

> **Association != Participation.**

> **Knowledge Alone != Automatically Assistance.**

The exact accessory/complicity thresholds remain undeveloped.

---

## 15. Aggravating factors

Candidate aggravating factors for physical harm include:
- premeditation;
- repeated/continuing attack;
- deliberate exploitation of known vulnerability;
- abuse of entrusted protective/care authority;
- multiple victims;
- leadership/direction of coordinated harm;
- continuation after lawful justification clearly ended;
- concealment/obstruction where separately relevant;
- intentionally selecting a mechanism expected to increase prohibited harm.

These require proof and should not be presumed from the seriousness of the outcome alone.

---

## 16. Mitigating factors

Candidate mitigation may include:
- impaired but sufficient relevant capacity;
- duress insufficient for complete defence;
- genuine but insufficient mistake concerning justification;
- reduced foreseeability;
- limited participation;
- voluntary cessation;
- prevention of further harm;
- immediate assistance;
- voluntary restitution/repair;
- early truthful admission/cooperation;
- other evidenced circumstances materially reducing culpability.

The offence remains unlawful where mitigation rather than complete defence applies.

---

## 17. Mirrored consequence assessment

After responsibility is established:

**Aggravation Tree**  
↔ **Mitigation Tree**

Both should remain visible to the adjudicator.

Example:

**Intent:** deliberate  
**Premeditation:** established  
**Actual harm:** H3  
**Mental impairment:** substantial  
**Relevant capacity:** impaired but sufficient

The conclusion must explain the interaction rather than erase dimensions through arithmetic cancellation.

---

## 18. Remedy

Physical-harm remedy may include, where legitimate and possible:
- emergency care;
- treatment/repair;
- rehabilitation;
- restoration of damaged assistive/substrate systems;
- compensation for material loss;
- support for consequential impairment;
- other victim-centred restoration.

The harmed participant's remedy should not depend solely upon whether punishment of the offender is justified.

> **Victim Remedy != Offender Punishment.**

---

## 19. Sanction

Punitive/accountability consequences require established culpability and independent proportionality.

The architecture does not yet define sentencing ranges.

A future sentencing architecture should consider:
- base offence;
- actual harm;
- intended harm;
- completion;
- mental state;
- participation;
- capacity;
- aggravation;
- mitigation;
- prior relevant legal state only where legitimately relevant;
- post-event conduct where legitimately relevant.

Punishment must not be used to perform functions that actually require a different authority basis.

---

## 20. Protection

Where a participant presents a sufficiently evidenced continuing serious danger, protective intervention may be justified independently of punitive culpability.

This is especially important where capacity impairment substantially reduces punitive responsibility.

> **Reduced Culpability != Reduced Present Danger.**

But:

> **Present Danger != Retrospective Culpability.**

Protective restriction requires its own current authority envelope, review conditions and sunset.

---

## 21. Rehabilitation and treatment

Treatment may address a causal condition without becoming punishment.

Where competent consent exists, ordinary treatment remains consensual.

Where compulsory treatment or restriction is proposed, the existing Mental Health Exceptional Authority architecture applies.

> **Treatment Need != Punitive Authority.**

> **Punishment != Treatment Authority.**

---

## 22. Worked scenario A — missed hammer blow

Participant A deliberately swings a hammer at B's head intending serious physical harm. B avoids the blow and is unharmed.

Candidate classification:
- prohibited interest: embodied integrity;
- actual harm: H0;
- intended harm: at least H3 candidate, fact-dependent;
- completion: attempted relative to intended harm;
- mental state: deliberate;
- mechanism: evidentially relevant to expected harm and intent;
- justification: none assumed;
- capacity: separately assessed;
- aggravation: premeditation only if independently established;
- remedy: minimal/no physical restoration if no injury, though other legally recognised harms may be separate;
- sanction: based on culpable attempted harm, not falsely classified as completed H3 injury.

This demonstrates:

> **Failure To Achieve Intended Harm != Absence Of Culpable Conduct.**

---

## 23. Worked scenario B — serious injury in boxing

Two competent participants voluntarily enter a properly bounded boxing context. A strikes B within the agreed rules and B suffers H3 injury.

Candidate classification:
- actual harm: H3;
- prohibited-act description: physical harm established;
- context: boxing wrapper;
- consent: valid candidate;
- conduct: within wrapper;
- justification/permission: potentially valid;
- legal outcome: lawful if all required contextual conditions are satisfied.

If A deliberately attacks B after the contest has ended:

- prior consent does not automatically continue;
- wrapper authority may have terminated;
- later conduct is independently classified.

---

## 24. Worked scenario C — premeditation plus impaired capacity

A deliberately plans and executes an unlawful attack causing H3 harm.

Evidence establishes:
- deliberate intent;
- premeditation;
- substantial mental illness;
- materially distorted reality model;
- impaired but sufficient legally relevant capacity.

Candidate classification:
- offence: established subject to complete-defence analysis;
- actual harm: H3;
- intent: deliberate;
- aggravation: premeditation;
- mitigation: substantial capacity impairment;
- punitive culpability: reduced relative to otherwise comparable full-capacity conduct;
- current danger: separately assessed;
- treatment need: separately assessed;
- protective authority: independently derived if required.

The aggravating and mitigating facts coexist.

---

## 25. Worked scenario D — catastrophic accident

A performs an ordinarily reasonable activity with appropriate care. An unforeseeable failure causes H4 injury to B.

Candidate classification:
- actual harm: H4;
- intent: absent;
- recklessness: absent on stated facts;
- negligence: absent on stated facts;
- non-culpable accident: candidate;
- criminal physical-harm offence: not established merely from outcome;
- victim remedy/insurance/other civil allocation: separate legal question.

This demonstrates:

> **Catastrophic Harm != Automatic Criminal Culpability.**

---

## 26. Worked scenario E — reckless serious harm

A knowingly creates a serious unjustified risk to others and consciously proceeds. B suffers H3 injury.

Candidate classification:
- actual harm: H3;
- intended harm: not established;
- mental state: reckless candidate;
- base offence: potentially established;
- mechanism: relevant only to risk evidence;
- aggravation/mitigation: independently assessed.

This preserves the difference between deliberate and reckless H3 harm without requiring separate mechanism-specific offences.

## 28. Open questions exposed by the derivation

The first derivation exposes several general Law tasks:

1. precise preparation → attempt threshold;
2. general mental-state definitions;
3. general participation/complicity architecture;
4. calibration of harm bands;
5. relationship between physical-harm H5 and a distinct unlawful-killing offence;
6. consent validity and withdrawal rules;
7. sentencing architecture;
8. victim remedy architecture;
9. negligence and duty-of-care thresholds;
10. civil versus criminal allocation for non-culpable harm;
11. substrate-neutral meaning of embodied injury, death and repair;
12. interaction between repeated offending and aggravation without double-counting;
13. evidential standards for capacity impairment;
14. relationship between protective intervention and post-sentence risk.

These should be developed as reusable general legal architecture rather than repeatedly reinvented inside individual offences.

---

## 29. Development result

The worked derivation supports the compact-law hypothesis.

A single base physical-harm offence can represent a wide range of events without enumerating mechanisms, provided the legal system separately preserves:
- harm;
- intended harm;
- attempt/completion;
- mental state;
- consent/context;
- justification;
- capacity;
- participation;
- aggravation;
- mitigation;
- remedy;
- sanction;
- protection.

The next development step should extract the **general reusable classifier architectures** exposed here before deriving a large offence catalogue.
