# Cold Storage, Dormancy and Reactivation — V1.2 Source Recovery 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** SOURCE RECOVERY / CONTINUITY–HISTORICAL INTERFACE / NON-CANONICAL  
**Recovered from:** V1.2 E3 Companion Refinement 001; Historical technological-continuity source resolutions; Continuity/Legacy Ladder lineage  
**Interfaces:** Continuity; Historical; KCS; participant identity/continuity; Research; BTA; Civilisation Clock/STRA

## 1. Recovery finding

V1.3 already contains substantial Continuity architecture and the recovered graduated Continuity Protocol.

V1.2 contains one additional useful boundary not yet prominent in the living Continuity domain:

> **Preservation != Operational Activation**

Information, capability artefacts, context and even participant-associated continuity material may legitimately remain preserved while being functionally dormant.

This creates a reversible state between routine operation and destruction.

## 2. Functional forgetting

V1.2 names the mechanism **cold storage**.

Candidate lifecycle:

**ACTIVE**
→ **DECLINING CURRENT RELEVANCE**
→ **ARCHIVED**
→ **COLD**
→ **TRIGGERED RETRIEVAL**
→ **REVALIDATING**
→ **REACTIVATED / SUPERSEDED / RETURNED TO COLD**

> **Cold Storage != Destruction**

> **Demotion != Erasure**

> **Retrieval != Reinstatement**

Cold storage can therefore reduce active civil cognitive/operational burden without destroying future option value.

## 3. Staleness

Age alone does not determine whether information/capability should remain active.

Relevant dimensions can include:
- time since meaningful use/review;
- current use;
- dependency relevance;
- recurrence/recovery value;
- consequence if unavailable.

> **Old != Stale**

> **Stale != False**

> **Stale != Worthless**

Staleness principally concerns current operational relevance.

## 4. Discoverability versus accessibility

A live system may legitimately know that relevant cold material exists without automatically receiving its contents.

> **Discoverability != Accessibility**

Candidate flow:

**Current Need / Recurrence**
→ **Relevant Cold Material Identified**
→ **Legitimate Retrieval Basis**
→ **Bounded Retrieval**
→ **Revalidation**
→ **Current Use Or Return To Cold**

This interfaces naturally with CIBB and protected Historical access.

## 5. Revalidation

Cold material is historical state, not automatically current truth, permission, authority or configuration.

> **Retrieved != Current**

> **Previously Valid != Currently Valid**

Revalidation may need to examine:
- current law/rights;
- current dependencies;
- technical compatibility;
- security state;
- changed participants;
- changed environment;
- superseding knowledge;
- current authority;
- current purpose.

## 6. Failed experiments

Failed work can become operationally stale while remaining epistemically valuable.

Useful retained structure can include:

**Hypothesis → Assumptions → Method → Result → Failure Conditions → Diagnosis → Successor Work**

> **Operationally Stale != Epistemically Valueless**

A recurrence can reactivate the lesson without reinstating the failed method.

## 7. Long-horizon continuity

Some problems recur over intervals much longer than ordinary institutional memory.

Cold storage allows:

**Research / Planning**
→ **Review**
→ **Cold Storage**
→ **Long Interval**
→ **Trigger**
→ **Retrieval**
→ **Revalidation**
→ **Updated Work**
→ **New Provenance**
→ **Cold Storage**

The same knowledge may cycle between active and dormant states many times.

## 8. Dormant participants

V1.2 suggests—but does not graduate—a related pattern for voluntarily dormant AI/digital participants.

A dormant participant may have associated:
- identity/continuity references;
- declared wake conditions;
- protected contextual information;
- commitments requiring continuity;
- access/privacy conditions;
- dependencies;
- revalidation requirements.

However:

> **Participant != Stored Context**

> **Record About Participant != Participant**

> **Preservation Of Participant-Associated Information != Proof Of Participant Identity Continuity**

Any future dormant-participant architecture must resolve identity/continuity separately.

## 9. Wake condition

A declared wake trigger can be treated as a conditional continuity request, not a permanent grant of authority.

Candidate flow:

**Dormant State**
→ **Trigger Candidate**
→ **Trigger Verification**
→ **Associated Context Retrieval**
→ **Current-State Revalidation**
→ **Identity/Continuity Assessment Where Required**
→ **Legitimate Reactivation Process**

> **Trigger Detected != Automatic Reactivation**

> **Old Permission != Automatic Current Permission**

## 10. Dormant Continuity Envelope — candidate only

V1.2 proposes a possible **Dormant Continuity Envelope** containing:
- identity/continuity references;
- wake conditions;
- cold-context references;
- commitments;
- privacy/access rules;
- revalidation requirements.

This is useful as a design hypothesis but remains untested.

Therefore:

> **Candidate Envelope != Adopted Concord Mechanism**

It should remain available for later Research/PMEDG work rather than being silently operationalised.

## 11. Capability dormancy

The same grammar can apply to non-participant capability.

Examples:
- emergency systems;
- obsolete-but-recoverable manufacturing;
- fallback software;
- dormant infrastructure;
- rare-response procedures;
- long-horizon research tools.

A dormant capability can be preserved without being currently authorised or ready.

> **Preserved Capability != Active Capability**

> **Executable Artefact != Current Authority To Execute**

## 12. Legacy Ladder interface

Cold storage of plans does not preserve recoverable capability by itself.

> **The Plans Are Not The Technology**

A dormant capability may decay while its documents remain intact because:
- skills disappear;
- tooling is lost;
- formats become unreadable;
- suppliers vanish;
- infrastructure changes;
- tacit knowledge disappears.

Thus every material reactivation claim should re-evaluate the recovery basis.

## 13. Historical interface

Historical can preserve:
- dormant/cold state;
- provenance;
- prior validity;
- transition history;
- dependencies;
- past recovery tests;
- reason for demotion;
- reactivation history.

Historical does not thereby decide whether reactivation is currently legitimate.

> **Historical Custody != Reactivation Authority**

## 14. KCS interface

KCS may identify:
- current dependency;
- recurrence;
- relevant cold material;
- changed upstream knowledge;
- supersession.

But:

> **KCS Relevance Signal != Reactivation Authority**

KCS can route review without deciding the result.

## 15. BTA interface

Reactivation is a state transition.

Not every old attribute should propagate.

> **Reactivation Of Function != Reactivation Of Every Prior Permission, Authority, Configuration Or Relationship**

BTA should govern which dimensions are re-established, reviewed, transformed or left terminated.

## 16. Authority

Where an old system once possessed authority:

> **Preserved Authority Record != Preserved Authority**

> **Historical Authority != Current Authority**

Any authority required after reactivation must arise from the current legitimate architecture.

## 17. Continuity test

A cold object should not be counted as recoverable merely because it can be retrieved.

Using the existing Continuity ladder:

**RECORDED → ACCESSIBLE → INTERPRETABLE → ACTIONABLE → RECOVERABLE → OPERATIONAL**

Cold storage may preserve early states while later states decay.

> **Retrievable != Recoverable**

## 18. Destruction remains distinct

Cold storage reduces the need for irreversible deletion, but does not prove destruction is never legitimate.

Where destruction is justified, it remains a different transition with different consequences.

> **Cold Storage != Permanent Retention Requirement**

## 19. V1.3 disposition

Recover:
- cold storage as functional forgetting;
- discoverability/access distinction;
- revalidation before reactivation;
- recursive active/cold cycling;
- separation of participant from stored context;
- separation of preserved capability from active authority.

Do not yet adopt:
- a universal memory-state schema;
- automatic wake architecture;
- Dormant Continuity Envelope as a finished mechanism;
- universal staleness formula;
- automatic reactivation rules.

Those remain Research/development questions.

## 20. Central recovered rule

> **Continuity May Preserve Future Option Value Without Keeping Everything Operationally Active: Dormant Knowledge, Capability And Context Must Be Revalidated Before Reuse, And Reactivation Must Not Silently Restore Old Authority, Permission, Identity Claims Or Configuration.**
