# 1. Purpose

This review determines whether Historical should remain in open-ended exploratory development or move into a candidate graduated state.

Graduation does not mean:

- complete;
- canonical forever;
- implementation finished;
- immune to correction;
- constitutionally self-authorising;
- free of unresolved questions.

Graduation means that the domain has acquired a coherent purpose, stable boundaries, tested interfaces, internally consistent objects and sufficient adversarial validation that further change should normally be driven by explicit evidence, implementation experience or newly discovered failure rather than continued speculative expansion.

# 3. Domain Purpose Stability

Historical's purpose has remained stable through successive tests:

> **Historical is the Concord's bounded temporal-custody, provenance and reconstruction layer.**

Its function is temporal interpretability.

It preserves enough civilisational state to reconstruct what existed, happened, was represented, remained unknown, changed, was authorised or purportedly authorised, and was later corrected, without inheriting the authority of the systems it remembers.

No adversarial or interface test required replacement of this core purpose.

**Assessment: STABLE.**

---

# 4. Governing Principle Stability

The governing principle remains:

> **Preserve the Record; Preserve Its Provenance; Preserve Its Historical State; Preserve the Limits of Its Evaluation Space; Do Not Inherit the Authority of the System That Produced It.**

Testing strengthened rather than displaced it.

The Humanity Machine constraint also survived:

> **Present inability to identify value is not proof of future irrelevance.**

Later privacy/destruction testing added the balancing principle:

> **Historical Value ≠ Unlimited Retention Authority.**

These principles are complementary rather than contradictory.

**Assessment: STABLE WITH BALANCED RETENTION BOUNDARY.**

# 8. Authority Model Stability

The original static-looking authority-state model was materially improved through Bounded Contextual Authority.

Current architecture represents:

**Standing Eligibility → Context → Justification → Authority Activation → Bounded Exercise → Termination → Changed Context → Fresh Justification**

Historical preserves the authority event but not the authority itself.

The glass-hammer model survived cross-domain and end-to-end testing.

Core rules:

> **Historical Evidence of Authority ≠ Preserved Authority.**

> **Past Legitimate Authority ≠ Reusable Authority.**

> **Historical Retrieval ≠ Operational Reactivation.**

**Assessment: STABLE AFTER MATERIAL REFINEMENT.**

# 9. Epistemic Architecture Stability

ESCP proved central rather than peripheral.

Historical now preserves:

- contemporary evaluation context;
- known unknowns;
- known missing dimensions;
- later-recognised unrepresented dimensions;
- search boundaries;
- negative space;
- retrospective evidence;
- scoped completeness claims.

Two reconstruction modes remain necessary:

- Contemporary-State Reconstruction;
- Present-Best Reconstruction.

The architecture survived the end-to-end case without contaminating one with the other.

**Assessment: STRONGLY STABLE.**

---

# 10. Privacy, Forgetting and Destruction Stability

The domain no longer relies on a binary retain/delete model.

It supports:

- reduced discoverability;
- access restriction;
- correlation restriction;
- pseudonymisation;
- anonymisation;
- aggregation;
- sealing;
- cryptographic inaccessibility;
- destruction.

It distinguishes source state, derivative residual state, replica execution and minimum necessary residue.

Important rules include:

> **ESCP Is Not a Universal-Retention Licence.**

> **Source Deletion ≠ Automatic Derivative Deletion.**

> **Destruction Provenance ≠ Reconstruction of Destroyed Content.**

> **Deletion Completeness Must Be Scoped.**

**Assessment: STABLE ENOUGH FOR GRADUATION; IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS REMAIN.**

---

# 11. Contextual-State Architecture

Adversarial schema testing produced the most important late-stage refinement:

> **State Is Often Relational and Contextual, Not Merely Object-Intrinsic.**

HCSA-1 allows privacy, access, secrecy, retention, classification, discoverability, correlation, disclosure and publication states to depend on actor, purpose, jurisdiction, context, authority source and time.

This aligns structurally with BCA and Contextual Wrapper Architecture.

Cross-object testing found no contradiction with the earlier architecture.

**Assessment: MATERIAL IMPROVEMENT / STABLE AFTER TESTING.**

This finding should later be evaluated as a possible cross-Concord architectural pattern, but Historical graduation does not depend on generalising it.

# 17. Candidate Graduation State

Recommended state:

**CANDIDATE GRADUATED DOMAIN — ARCHITECTURE STABLE / IMPLEMENTATION OPEN**

Meaning:

- open-ended conceptual expansion stops by default;
- new architecture changes require an identified failure, new evidence, unresolved interface or implementation result;
- implementation work may proceed without reopening the whole domain;
- prior development remains available for provenance and future reconstruction;
- future changes should be versioned rather than silently inserted into the baseline;
- cross-Concord discoveries may trigger targeted re-review.

---

# 18. Change-Control Trigger

After graduation, Historical architecture should reopen when at least one of the following occurs:

1. implementation cannot represent a required invariant;
2. a real or synthetic case produces silent state loss;
3. a cross-domain interface proves contradictory;
4. a new constitutional rule changes Historical obligations;
5. privacy/destruction requirements cannot be reconciled with existing semantics;
6. a new substrate cannot represent required provenance/context;
7. a migration loses material relationships or evaluation state;
8. a new ESCP failure reveals a missing dimension;
9. a security or surveillance failure arises from Historical architecture;
10. a newly developed Concord module changes the relevant dependency topology.

Change should be proportional to the demonstrated failure.

---

# 19. Graduation Safeguard

Graduation must not become a new form of epistemic closure.

Therefore:

> **Graduated ≠ Complete.**

> **Stable ≠ Unchangeable.**

> **Validated Within Current Evaluation Space ≠ Proven Complete Across All Future Evaluation Spaces.**

Historical's own graduation state must itself be historically preserved and open to later correction.
