# Historical Domain — End-to-End Conformance Test 001 — Complete Consequential Event Lifecycle

**Project:** The Concord
**Domain:** Historical
**Status:** END-TO-END DEVELOPMENT TEST / NOT CANONICAL
**Date:** 25 September 2026
**Target:** Formal Domain Specification v0.3 + Formal Schema Set 002

## 1. Purpose

Previous tests examined Historical components and boundaries separately.

This test asks a harder question:

> Can one consequential civilisational event pass through the full Historical lifecycle without losing authority context, epistemic state, privacy, correction history, dependency structure, information-state constraints or reconstructability?

The scenario is synthetic. It is designed to exercise the architecture, not to prescribe policy.

---

## 2. Scenario

A citizen submits a Civil Attention problem report concerning repeated failures in a municipal autonomous food-distribution system.

The report alleges that deliveries are systematically failing in one district and that vulnerable residents are being missed.

The report contains:

- the citizen's identity;
- addresses of affected residents;
- delivery logs;
- photographs;
- an initial causal hypothesis;
- several statements from neighbours.

The Civil Attention system creates an Issue and routes Domain Actions to relevant operational domains.

The operational system investigates.

KCS records relationships between:

- the complaint;
- delivery-system configuration;
- logistics data;
- maintenance history;
- weather conditions;
- supplier changes;
- prior complaints;
- health-impact reports.

An authorised official temporarily accesses restricted participant-level data to determine whether immediate intervention is required.

The official concludes that a routing software update is the likely cause and authorises a bounded emergency correction.

The correction improves deliveries.

Three months later, new evidence shows that the software update was only one factor. A warehouse staffing change and a road-access restriction had also contributed.

Five years later, a researcher studies historical food-supply failures. Most participant-identifying raw material has by then been anonymised or destroyed, but aggregate and institutional records survive.

This lifecycle exercises creation, routing, knowledge state, authority, action, correction, privacy, destruction, derivatives, reconstruction and later pattern analysis.

---

## 3. Stage A — Operational Creation

Civil Attention creates:

- Submission S1;
- Issue I1;
- Domain Actions DA1...DAn.

Historical does not need immediate ownership of every source object.

The appropriate initial handoff may be DUAL_CUSTODY or REFERENCE_PRESERVATION.

HCM-1 records custody mode where Historical qualification occurs.

HSO-1 can represent the initial Historical state without replacing the operational objects.

**Conformance:** PASS.

Key rule:

> **Historical Qualification ≠ Operational Removal.**

---

## 4. Stage B — Original evidence and epistemic typing

The citizen's claim that the software update caused the failures is preserved as a claim, not a finding.

Neighbour statements are testimony.

Delivery logs are observations generated by a technical system.

Photographs are evidential objects with provenance.

The schema must not flatten these into generic "facts."

HPR-1 and HSO-1 preserve source/provenance and epistemic type.

HEC-1 begins representing the contemporary evaluation space.

**Conformance:** PASS.

---

## 5. Stage C — Privacy and contextual access

S1 contains identifying information.

Different actors have different legitimate access:

- Civil Attention intake can process the submission;
- logistics investigators can access operationally necessary details;
- unrelated researchers cannot access identities;
- public reporting may receive only aggregate information.

HCSA-1 represents these contextual states.

No single global AccessState can accurately describe S1.

HSO-1 may expose a summary such as MULTIPLE_CONTEXTUAL_STATES.

**Conformance:** PASS.

This confirms the necessity of HCSA-1.

---

## 6. Stage D — KCS relationship formation

KCS connects the complaint to configuration, maintenance, supplier, weather and prior-complaint objects.

Historical does not copy the live KCS graph into a competing graph.

It preserves relevant state at T and provenance-bearing relationships needed for later reconstruction.

HPR-1 records Historical relationship state where required.

**Conformance:** PASS.

---

## 7. Stage E — Authority activation

An official needs restricted participant-level data.

Standing role eligibility is not enough.

The BCA sequence is represented through HAE-1:

C0
→ observed service failure
→ rights-relevant risk
→ legitimate civil objective
→ investigation function
→ need for restricted data
→ minimum proportionate access
→ A1 activated
→ bounded inspection
→ A1 terminates
→ C1.

HCSA-1 records the resulting temporary access state.

HAE-1 remains the authority-event record.

**Conformance:** PASS.

Key consistency rule confirmed:

> **State Produced by Authority ≠ Source of Authority.**

---

## 8. Stage F — Initial causal conclusion

The investigation concludes that routing software is the likely primary cause.

HEC-1 records:

- evidence available;
- observed evidence;
- represented variables;
- uncertainty;
- known unknowns;
- method;
- scope.

The warehouse staffing factor is not represented.

The road-access factor exists but is underweighted.

Historical records the conclusion without converting it into timeless truth.

**Conformance:** PASS.

---

## 9. Stage G — Bounded corrective action

A legitimate authority authorises a temporary routing correction.

HAE-1 records a second authority event A2.

A2 is not a continuation of A1 merely because the same official is involved.

A1 changed the context by producing evidence.

The relationship is explicit:

A1 → produces E1 → E1 contributes to justification for A2.

A2 is then exercised and terminates.

**Conformance:** PASS.

The glass-hammer model survives an end-to-end operational case.

---

## 10. Stage H — Operational outcome

Delivery performance improves.

This is evidence that the correction affected the system.

It does not prove the original causal model was complete.

Historical Event Spine view:

Complaint
→ Investigation
→ Restricted Access
→ Initial Finding
→ Routing Correction
→ Delivery Improvement.

At this stage the spine is valid but incomplete.

HES-1 must expose source coverage and epistemic limits.

**Conformance:** PASS.

---

## 11. Stage I — Civil Attention resolution

Operational domains report completion.

Civil Attention evaluates the parent Issue and marks it RESOLVED.

Historical preserves:

- Domain Action completion;
- central Issue resolution;
- participant feedback state.

It does not collapse these into one event.

**Conformance:** PASS.

> **Domain Completion ≠ Issue Resolution.**

---

## 12. Stage J — Later evidence

Three months later, further evidence shows:

- warehouse staffing contributed;
- road-access restriction contributed;
- the routing update amplified both effects.

The original software-only interpretation is corrected.

HCR-1 creates typed correction/contextual-addition relationships.

HEC-1 preserves the original evaluation context.

A new evaluation context records the expanded represented space.

The original conclusion is not erased.

**Conformance:** PASS.

This produces:

**Contemporary Reconstruction(T1):** software update appeared to be the primary cause within the represented evaluation space.

**Present-Best Reconstruction(T2):** failure arose from interacting software, staffing and access factors.

Both can coexist without contradiction.

---

## 13. Stage K — Reopening

Because the causal understanding changed, Civil Attention reopens I1 for additional remedial work.

HIST-1 records:

RESOLVED → REOPENED.

The earlier RESOLVED state remains historically true at T1.

**Conformance:** PASS.

> **Later Reopening ≠ Earlier Resolution Never Occurred.**

## 14. Stage L — Participant-data minimisation

After operational need ends, participant-level data is reviewed.

Suppose the legitimate outcome is:

- direct identifiers destroyed;
- some testimony anonymised;
- address-level data aggregated to district;
- institutional decision records retained;
- authority-event records retained with minimum necessary participant references;
- public reporting remains aggregate.

HIST-1 records each authorised transition.

HCSA-1 changes applicable access/privacy states.

HMHR-1 records only justified residue.

**Conformance:** PASS.

---

## 15. Stage M — Derivative review

A statistical model trained during the investigation remains.

Source participant data has been destroyed.

HDRS-1 records:

- derivation type;
- contribution state;
- extractability;
- reconstruction risk;
- identity leakage risk;
- independent legitimate basis;
- required review/action.

Historical does not infer either "model must survive" or "model must be destroyed."

It exposes the residual state for legitimate resolution.

**Conformance:** PASS / EXTERNAL DECISION MAY BE REQUIRED.

## 16. Stage N — Replica reconciliation

One archival replica was offline during the destruction transition.

HIST-1 records the authorised transition.

HRI-1 records:

PENDING_REPLICA.

The system cannot yet claim reconciled deletion.

When the replica reconnects and executes the transition:

PENDING_REPLICA → RECONCILED.

The completion claim remains scoped to known legitimate replicas.

**Conformance:** PASS.

## 19. Stage Q — Historical Event Spine after correction

The five-year Event Spine becomes:

Problem Reports / Delivery Failures
→ Civil Attention Issue
→ KCS Relationship Formation
→ Bounded Investigation Access
→ Initial Software-Cause Finding
→ Routing Correction
→ Partial Operational Improvement
→ Later Staffing + Road Evidence
→ Causal Model Correction
→ Issue Reopened
→ Additional Remediation
→ Data Minimisation
→ Later Historical Reconstruction.

Alternative branches and negative space include:

- earlier complaints not linked at T1;
- inaccessible/deleted participant material;
- initial unrepresented staffing interaction;
- underweighted road-access evidence.

**Conformance:** PASS.

The Event Spine has become more informative without pretending the later model existed earlier.

## 20. Stage R — Pattern analysis

The researcher compares this case with other food-supply disruptions.

Historical can expose bounded derived pattern objects if authorised.

The pattern might suggest that failures often emerge from interactions between software changes, workforce changes and physical-access constraints.

That pattern is not a historical fact merely because it was computed from historical records.

It must carry:

- derivation method;
- source scope;
- excluded cases;
- uncertainty;
- correlation/causation status;
- version.

**Conformance:** PASS.

## 21. Stage S — Future operational use

A current logistics system asks to use the historical pattern to alter present routing policy.

Historical can supply authorised evidence.

It cannot itself mandate the policy.

The current operational/governance/research architecture must evaluate present conditions.

> **Historical Insight ≠ Present Operational Mandate.**

**Conformance:** PASS.

## 23. Lifecycle conformance

The v0.3 lifecycle:

Operational Creation
→ Operational Custody
→ Historical Qualification/Handoff
→ Historical Custody State
→ Access/Derivation/Reconstruction
→ Review/Reclassification
→ Cold Storage / Continued Archive / Authorised Destruction

is sufficient to represent the scenario.

However, the test suggests that "Review/Reclassification" should be understood broadly as repeated state transition, not a single late lifecycle phase.

Historical objects may cycle through review, correction, access, restriction and reconstruction many times.

Candidate refinement:

> **Historical Lifecycle Is Stateful and Recurrent, Not Strictly Linear.**

This is not a structural failure; it is a clarification.

## 25. End-to-end ESCP result

The scenario demonstrates all three ESCP dimensions:

**Known Unknown:** investigators knew some causal uncertainty remained.

**Known-Missing Dimension:** some operational data was known to be unavailable or incomplete.

**Later-Recognised Unrepresented Dimension:** the staffing interaction was not represented in the initial causal model.

Historical can preserve these without claiming the initial actors should necessarily have known the later result.

At the same time, the preserved evaluation context permits later examination of whether reasonable evidence was ignored.

**Conformance:** PASS.

The architecture preserves both:

It does not require either permanent identifiable retention or historical amnesia.

The key mechanisms are:

- contextual state assertions;
- bounded access;
- minimum necessary residue;
- derivative review;
- scoped deletion reconciliation;
- aggregate historical preservation.

**Conformance:** PASS.

## 27. End-to-end authority result

No authority persisted merely because Historical preserved evidence of it.

A1 investigation access terminated.

A2 corrective authority terminated.

Later researchers received separate permissions.

Present operational systems require fresh present authority.

The glass-hammer model therefore survives the complete lifecycle.

**Conformance:** PASS.**

## 28. Defects found

No architectural contradiction was found.

Three minor formal refinements emerged:

1. Historical lifecycle language should explicitly describe recurrent state transitions rather than imply a one-way archival pipeline.
2. HSO-1 should have a standard summary value for MULTIPLE_CONTEXTUAL_STATES rather than forcing one state where HCSA-1 contains several.
3. Event Spine outputs should have an explicit distinction between HISTORICAL_SEQUENCE and DERIVED_CAUSAL_INTERPRETATION so temporal sequence cannot silently imply causation.

These are formalisation refinements, not redesigns.
