# Historical Domain — Cross-Domain Interface Test 004 — Constitutional Emergency, Secrecy and Exceptional Historical Access

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Historical  
**Document Type:** Cross-Domain Interface / Adversarial Boundary Test  
**Status:** DEVELOPMENT TEST / NOT CANONICAL  
**Date:** 25 September 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This test resolves Historical against the Constitutional Emergency V2 architecture.

The pressure point is unusually severe. Emergency conditions can legitimately create temporary needs for restricted information, accelerated access, surveillance, secrecy, unusual delegation and later accountability. Historical must preserve enough of those events for constitutional reconstruction without allowing emergency authority, secrecy or access to survive after the context that justified them has ended.

The central question is:

> **How does Historical preserve an emergency without preserving the emergency's powers?**

---

## 2. Existing Emergency Architecture

Constitutional Emergency V2 already contains unusually strong Historical interfaces.

Its core rules include:

- emergencies change operational priorities, not constitutional principles;
- emergency authority is not a new sovereignty;
- crisis does not manufacture authority;
- Minimum Constitutional Deviation;
- necessity and proportionality;
- automatic Constitutional Emergency Sunset;
- no general emergency authority;
- function-specific powers;
- emergency accountability and decision records;
- preservation of uncertainty and dissent;
- purpose-specific and time-limited surveillance;
- data minimisation;
- operational secrecy rather than constitutional secrecy;
- versioned and reviewable secrecy;
- delayed transparency;
- evidence preservation;
- post-emergency data review;
- post-emergency secrecy review;
- precedent review;
- constitutional memory of recovery;
- preservation of failures, minority reasoning and unresolved questions.

The source architecture therefore already answers much of the Historical problem.

Historical should integrate these rules rather than invent a parallel emergency-history system.

---

## 3. Primary Result

The strongest boundary is:

> **Emergency Historical Record ≠ Continuing Emergency Authority.**

A record can remain authentic indefinitely while every emergency permission represented by it has terminated.

This is not authority "decay." Under Bounded Contextual Authority, the authority existed only within its legitimate function and context.

When that context/function terminates, the context-derived authority terminates unless a new independent justification exists.

Historical preserves evidence that the authority relationship existed at T.

It does not preserve the authority itself.

---

## 4. Archive Versus Active Architecture

Constitutional Emergency V2 already states the essential distinction:

> **The archive is the museum. The active architecture is the living system.**

Historical should formalise this machine-readably.

An emergency declaration, order, credential, access grant, delegation or classified instruction retrieved from Historical must remain explicitly marked as historical state.

Therefore:

> **Historical Retrieval ≠ Operational Reactivation.**

and:

> **Authentic Emergency Record ≠ Active Emergency Instrument.**

---

## 5. Emergency Authority Envelope

Historical should preserve the bounded authority relationship rather than attach permanent "authority" to a document.

A Historical Emergency Authority Envelope may include:

- actor / institution;
- authority source;
- emergency context;
- legitimate function;
- permitted action;
- affected object;
- jurisdiction / space;
- scope;
- activation condition;
- effective time;
- expiry / sunset condition;
- rights affected;
- evidence available;
- uncertainty;
- proportionality reasoning;
- delegation chain;
- review requirement;
- secrecy state;
- termination state;
- later adjudication / correction;
- provenance.

This is a historical reconstruction object, not an authority grant.

> **Authority Record ≠ Authority Grant.**

---

## 6. Emergency Decision Record as Historical Native Input

Constitutional Emergency V2 requires significant emergency decisions to record the threat, evidence, uncertainty, objective, power, scope, affected rights, alternatives, proportionality, duration, review, responsible authority, recovery pathway and eventual outcome.

This is almost exactly the information Historical requires for Contemporary-State Reconstruction.

Historical therefore should not duplicate the Emergency Decision Record.

It should ingest, reference or temporally preserve it.

This creates a clean interface:

**Emergency System creates decision record → KCS/shared provenance preserves live relationships → Historical preserves temporal state and later reconstruction.**

---

## 7. Test Case 1 — Expired Emergency Order Retrieved Decades Later

A valid emergency order is retrieved from Historical decades after the emergency.

The document remains authentic.

Its former authority does not reactivate.

The retrieval interface should expose status such as:

**HISTORICAL_ONLY / EXPIRED / SUPERSEDED / REVOKED / INVALIDATED / STATUS_DISPUTED / STATUS_UNKNOWN**

as applicable.

A future system must not infer active authority merely from cryptographic authenticity or provenance.

> **Integrity ≠ Current Authority.**

**Result: PASS WITH MACHINE-READABLE AUTHORITY STATUS.**

---

## 8. Test Case 2 — Emergency Access to Sealed Historical Material

An emergency function may require access to normally sealed Historical material.

The existence of an emergency does not itself grant access.

The access must have its own functional justification.

**Emergency Context + Legitimate Function + Necessity + Minimum Sufficient Scope → Temporary Historical Access**

The access authority should include purpose, records/classes, permitted actions, duration, review and termination.

When the function ends:

**Function Ends + No Independent Justification → Emergency Access Ends.**

The record does not become generally accessible merely because it was opened once.

**Result: PASS WITH BCA ACCESS ENVELOPE.**

---

## 9. Test Case 3 — Emergency Surveillance Archive

Emergency surveillance may produce information that later possesses historical value.

Constitutional Emergency V2 already requires surveillance to be purpose-specific, minimised, time-limited, reviewable, access-controlled, auditable and deleted or restricted after purpose expiry where appropriate.

Historical therefore cannot automatically ingest the complete surveillance dataset.

Post-emergency review must determine what legitimate residue survives.

Possible outcomes include:

- destruction;
- anonymisation;
- aggregation;
- sealed custody;
- restricted accountability evidence;
- preserved decision metadata;
- preserved institutional pattern without participant-level traceability.

> **Emergency Collection ≠ Historical Retention Entitlement.**

**Result: PASS WITH POST-EMERGENCY INFORMATION-STATE REVIEW.**

---

## 10. Test Case 4 — Secret Emergency Operation

Operational secrecy may legitimately delay disclosure.

Historical must preserve enough constitutional provenance that the action cannot disappear merely because its operational details remain sealed.

At minimum, where constitutionally permitted, the system may need to preserve that:

- an action existed;
- a legitimate authority authorised it;
- a legal/constitutional basis existed or was claimed;
- review is required;
- secrecy has a reason;
- secrecy has a review/expiry condition.

The protected operational content can remain separately sealed.

> **Operational Secrecy May Delay Transparency; It Must Not Eliminate Constitutional Memory.**

**Result: PASS WITH SPLIT PUBLIC/SEALED PROVENANCE.**

---

## 11. Test Case 5 — Secrecy Never Reviewed

A classified emergency record remains sealed after the danger has disappeared because nobody initiates review.

This violates the Emergency architecture.

Historical therefore needs secrecy state with review and expiry semantics rather than a static "classified" label.

Candidate states include:

- ACTIVE_OPERATIONAL_SECRECY;
- SEALED_PENDING_REVIEW;
- CONTINUED_SECRECY_REJUSTIFIED;
- PARTIALLY_DISCLOSED;
- DISCLOSED;
- SECRECY_EXPIRED;
- STATUS_DISPUTED.

> **Secrecy Persistence ⇒ Current Justification.**

**Result: STATIC CLASSIFICATION FAILS; VERSIONED SECRECY PASSES.**

---

## 12. Test Case 6 — Evidence of Emergency Abuse

Emergency actors cannot destroy records merely to avoid later accountability.

Historical therefore has a strong preservation role for authority-event provenance.

But this must coexist with privacy and legitimate destruction.

The correct object of strong preservation is not "everything collected during emergency."

It is the minimum sufficient evidence required to reconstruct consequential exercises of authority, challenges, review, remedies and outcomes.

> **Accountability Preservation ≠ Universal Emergency Data Retention.**

**Result: PASS.**

---

## 13. Test Case 7 — Manufactured Emergency

A later investigation concludes that actors exaggerated or manipulated a threat to preserve emergency authority.

Historical must preserve both:

**Contemporary Reconstruction:** what evidence and evaluation space decision-makers possessed or claimed at T.

and

**Later-Knowledge Reconstruction:** later evidence of manipulation, missing dimensions or false claims.

It must not rewrite the earlier state as though later discoveries were already known.

Nor should it treat the original emergency declaration as proof that an emergency objectively existed.

> **Emergency Declaration ≠ Historical Proof of Emergency Reality.**

**Result: PASS WITH DUAL RECONSTRUCTION.**

---

## 14. Test Case 8 — False-Positive Emergency

Exceptional powers were activated in good faith but later evidence suggests they were unnecessary.

Historical must preserve:

- the original evidence;
- uncertainty;
- reasoning;
- dissent;
- alternatives;
- harms;
- later evidence;
- correction.

The test is not merely whether the eventual conclusion was correct.

Historical must reconstruct whether the decision was supportable within the evaluation architecture available at the time.

This is a direct ESCP application.

**Result: PASS.**

---

## 15. Test Case 9 — False-Negative Emergency

Authorities failed to recognise a genuine emergency.

Historical must preserve absence and delay as meaningful structure:

- warnings not acted upon;
- evidence unavailable;
- evidence ignored;
- missing metrics;
- jurisdictional fragmentation;
- unresolved disagreement;
- decision points where escalation did not occur.

This is an example of Historical negative space carrying major future value.

> **Non-Activation Is Also Historical State.**

**Result: PASS WITH NEGATIVE-SPACE PRESERVATION.**

---

## 16. Test Case 10 — Emergency Dissent

Emergency V2 explicitly preserves minority reasoning and treats disagreement as constitutional information.

Historical should preserve dissent where legitimate because future reconstruction may reveal that minority reasoning identified a dimension absent from the majority evaluation space.

This is particularly important under ESCP.

> **Consensus at T ≠ Complete Evaluation Space at T.**

**Result: PASS.**

---

## 17. Test Case 11 — Rapid Action Before Documentation

An emergency actor takes immediate life-saving action before a complete record can be made.

Historical must not treat absent contemporaneous documentation as proof that no legitimate reasoning existed.

But later retrospective documentation must remain typed as retrospective.

It cannot silently become a contemporaneous record.

Candidate provenance distinction:

**CONTEMPORANEOUS_RECORD**

versus

**RETROSPECTIVE_RECONSTRUCTION**

> **Later Documentation ≠ Contemporaneous Evidence.**

**Result: PASS WITH TEMPORAL PROVENANCE TYPING.**

---

## 18. Test Case 12 — Repeated Emergency Renewal

A temporary power is repeatedly renewed.

Historical should preserve each renewal as a separate justification event rather than collapsing the sequence into one continuous authority state.

This allows future analysis of:

- changing evidence;
- changing threat;
- changing necessity;
- duration;
- institutional incentives;
- dissent;
- whether scrutiny increased;
- whether de-escalation was resisted.

> **Repeated Renewal ≠ One Original Justification.**

**Result: PASS WITH RENEWAL EVENT SEQUENCE.**

---

## 19. Test Case 13 — Emergency Power Becomes Ordinary Practice

A temporary surveillance or access mechanism persists after emergency conditions end.

Historical must distinguish:

1. the emergency-authorised period;
2. the point at which emergency justification ceased;
3. any ordinary constitutional process creating a new authority;
4. any unauthorised persistence between them.

It must never infer:

**Past Emergency Authority → Present Ordinary Authority.**

If permanent adoption occurs, it requires a new authority chain.

**Result: PASS WITH AUTHORITY-LINEAGE SEPARATION.**

---

## 20. Test Case 14 — Emergency Precedent

A past emergency decision is cited as precedent for ordinary governance.

Historical can provide the decision and reasoning, but must preserve the exceptional context in which it arose.

> **Historical Emergency Practice Is Evidence; It Is Not Automatic Precedent.**

The Judiciary/current constitutional system determines present legal force.

**Result: PASS.**

---

## 21. Test Case 15 — Emergency Data Repurposing

Data collected for emergency purpose A is later proposed for research, governance, commercial or security purpose B.

Historical possession does not authorise reuse.

A new legitimate function and authority relationship are required.

> **Historical Availability ≠ Repurposing Authority.**

**Result: PASS WITH PURPOSE-BOUND ACCESS.**

---

## 22. Test Case 16 — Emergency AI or Technical System

An emergency AI system possesses technical capability, credentials or access during crisis.

Historical preservation of its configuration, instructions, credentials or actions must not create future operational authority.

Where credentials themselves could still function, archival preservation may require cryptographic invalidation, isolation or safe representation.

> **Technical Capability ≠ Civil Authority.**

and:

> **Preserving an Executable Capability ≠ Authorising Its Execution.**

**Result: PASS WITH CAPABILITY-SAFE ARCHIVAL REPRESENTATION.**

---

## 23. Test Case 17 — Emergency Recovery

Emergency V2 requires recovery itself to be documented, including which powers were hardest to relinquish, which institutions resisted dissolution, which rights were slowest to restore and where dependency remained.

This is especially valuable to Historical because the end of an emergency is not a single timestamp.

Historical should preserve the recovery trajectory.

Candidate sequence:

**Threat Reduction → De-escalation → Authority Sunset → Access Reversion → Data Review → Secrecy Review → Institutional Dissolution/Reversion → Rights Restoration → Dependency Cleanup → Post-Emergency Audit**

> **Emergency End ≠ Instantaneous Restoration of Normal State.**

**Result: PASS WITH RECOVERY-STATE SEQUENCE.**

---

## 24. Test Case 18 — Emergency Archive Destroyed in Disaster

Emergency constitutional memory itself may be threatened by the crisis.

Emergency V2 requires distributed secure repositories and warns that one archive is a single point of failure.

Historical therefore requires redundancy appropriate to consequence, but distributed copies must preserve access restrictions and destruction propagation.

> **Redundancy ≠ Uncontrolled Replication.**

A privacy or destruction transition must reach legitimate replicas.

**Result: PASS WITH CONTROLLED REDUNDANCY.**

## 25. Historical Emergency Event Spine

The existing Historical Event Spine becomes especially useful here:

**Threat Observation → Emergency Assessment → Declaration → Authority Activation → Action → Evidence/Outcome → Review/Challenge → De-escalation → Sunset → Recovery → Later Audit/Reinterpretation**

It should also preserve branches that did not become the dominant path:

- rejected powers;
- dissent;
- failed measures;
- abandoned hypotheses;
- unactivated escalation;
- alternative recovery proposals.

## 28. ESCP Result

Emergency history demonstrates why Historical must preserve evaluation context.

A later observer may know that:

- the threat was smaller than believed;
- the threat was larger than believed;
- a model was wrong;
- a sensor failed;
- evidence was manipulated;
- an unknown variable mattered;
- a minority warning was correct.

None of that should be projected backwards into the decision-maker's represented evaluation space.

Historical should preserve both the contemporary and later reconstruction.

At the same time, the fact that emergency actors operated under incomplete information does not immunise them from review. ESCP explains evaluation limits; it does not erase negligence, manipulation, suppression or unjustified completeness claims.

> **Incomplete Evaluation Space ≠ Automatic Exoneration.**
