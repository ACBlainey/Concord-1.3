# Historical Domain — Cross-Domain Interface Test 003 — Privacy, Forgetting, Destruction and Accountability

**Project:** The Concord
**Domain:** Historical
**Status:** DEVELOPMENT TEST / NOT CANONICAL
**Date:** 25 September 2026

## 1. Purpose

This test asks how Historical can preserve enough past state for accountability, reconstruction and future learning without converting participants into permanent historical dossiers.

It resolves Historical against existing Concord work on personal data, KCS, Continuity, Civil Attention and Bounded Contextual Authority.

## 2. Existing Concord constraints

The personal-data architecture already distinguishes identifiable personal information from civilisational information. It proposes participant primary ownership and control, meaningful access, contestability, contextualisation, anonymisation/aggregation where identity is unnecessary, minimum necessary resolution, and a rule that possession for one legitimate purpose does not automatically authorise another use.

It also recognises practical re-identification risk: removing a name is insufficient where grouping or relational structure can still identify the participant.

KCS rejects indefinite surveillance and distinguishes active information from material that merely needs to remain available. Continuity strongly values preservation and provenance, but that cannot create unlimited authority to preserve identifiable private life. Civil Attention preserves submissions and provenance without acquiring substantive decision authority.

BCA supplies the operational rule: retention, search, correlation, access, disclosure and destruction are bounded actions requiring current legitimate function, need and context.

## 3. Core result

> **Historical Value ≠ Unlimited Retention Authority.**

Possible future usefulness is relevant to a retention decision. It is not sufficient authority to retain identifiable information indefinitely.

> **ESCP Is Not a Universal-Retention Licence.**

## 4. Information-state separation

The following must remain distinct:

**Preserved ≠ Discoverable ≠ Searchable ≠ Accessible ≠ Retrievable ≠ Disclosable ≠ Publishable ≠ Correlatable**

A record may remain preserved while disappearing from ordinary discovery. It may be retrievable for a tightly bounded judicial purpose without being available for research or publication.

## 5. Forgetting as state transition

Forgetting need not always mean immediate physical destruction. A candidate reversibility ladder is:

Reduced Discoverability → Access Restriction → Correlation Restriction → Pseudonymisation → Anonymisation → Aggregation → Sealed Custody → Cryptographic Inaccessibility → Destruction.

Where several mechanisms satisfy the legitimate purpose, the least irreversible adequate mechanism should normally be preferred. But this must not become an excuse to refuse legitimately authorised destruction.

## 6. BCA applied to Historical information

Separate authority may be required for preservation, indexing, search, identity linkage, cross-domain correlation, retrieval, inspection, copying, analysis, disclosure, publication and destruction.

> **Authority to Preserve ≠ Authority to Correlate.**

> **Authority to Inspect ≠ Authority to Publish.**

> **Authority to Retrieve for Purpose A ≠ Authority to Reuse for Purpose B.**

## 7. Correlation Boundary

Historical relational preservation creates a special privacy risk. Nominally anonymous records may become identifying when linked.

> **Identity Removal ≠ Relationship Removal ≠ Non-Reidentifiability.**

A new candidate Correlation Boundary follows:

**Record A Accessible + Record B Accessible ≠ Authority to Correlate A and B.**

Cross-domain correlation must itself be a bounded contextual action.

## 8. Minimum Necessary Historical Residue

After legitimate destruction, Historical may need evidence that an authorised information-state transition occurred. But destruction provenance must not become a covert copy.

A Minimum Necessary Historical Residue may record only what is required to establish that a record/class existed, that an authorised transition occurred, its time and category, responsible process where legitimate, affected derivative classes and necessary audit references.

> **Destruction Provenance ≠ Reconstruction of Destroyed Content.**

## 9. Ordinary participant deletion

Where an ordinary participant requests deletion and no continuing accountability, legal, safety or operational need independently justifies retention, Historical cannot answer that the record might someday be useful and therefore must remain.

**Result: PASS if Historical executes rather than invents retention authority.**

## 10. Public-authority records

Records documenting exercises of public authority can carry stronger accountability requirements than ordinary private activity. But accountability does not justify preserving irrelevant private information.

> **Public Authority Accountability ≠ Permanent Private Dossier.**

**Result: PASS WITH MINIMUM-NECESSARY PROVENANCE.**

## 11. Later-exonerated participant

The history of accusation, investigation and exoneration may need preservation while the obsolete accusation must not remain an unqualified discoverable stigma.

**Accusation → Investigation → Finding → Exoneration / Reversal / Correction**

> **Preserving an Accusation's History ≠ Preserving the Accusation as Current Truth.**

**Result: PASS WITH LINKED CORRECTION AND DISCOVERABILITY CONTROLS.**

## 12. Medical data with population value

The preferred transformation is identifiable personal data → appropriate anonymisation/aggregation → civilisational information.

Future population value does not automatically justify carrying identity with the information. Re-identification risk must be assessed against actual relationships, not merely absence of names.

**Result: PASS if identity survives only where independently justified.**

## 13. Anonymous whistleblower

An anonymous submission may lose meaningful anonymity if relational metadata reconstructs identity.

> **Provenance Completeness ≠ Identity Traceability.**

Protected provenance may require severed, sealed or separately controlled identity relationships.

## 14. Secret ballot

Historical completeness may require evidence that a ballot occurred, eligibility rules, process integrity, aggregate result and audit evidence. It need not preserve a voter-to-vote map.

**Result: PASS.**

## 15. Victim and survivor records

Historical may preserve institutional events, aggregate patterns and accountability evidence while restricting identity, testimony or sensitive details.

> **Authority to Preserve Evidence ≠ Authority to Publish It.**

## 16. Institutional abuse

Over-aggressive deletion can make systematic abuse impossible to establish; unlimited retention can harm victims and innocent participants. The legitimate authority must determine minimum evidence for accountability. Historical preserves and executes the resulting state; it does not manufacture the entitlement.

## 17. Derivative-aware destruction

Deleting a source does not automatically resolve aggregates, models, derived metrics, cached copies, dependency edges, summaries or exported datasets.

> **Source Deletion ≠ Automatic Derivative Deletion.**

But:

> **Derivative Status ≠ Automatic Right to Survive.**

A derivative propagation and reconciliation protocol is required.

## 18. Copies across Concord systems

Historical must not become a universal deletion controller.

The preferred sequence is:

**Authorised Information-State Decision → Identify Affected Holdings/Dependencies → Domain-Specific Execution → Status Return → Reconciliation → Historical Record of Transition**

This resembles KCS change propagation while preserving domain ownership.

## 19. Cultural and community knowledge

Some knowledge may legitimately remain in community or protected custody. Historical can preserve sufficient metadata to know protected knowledge exists and how legitimate access is governed without possessing the content.

> **Historical Custody ≠ Centralised Possession.**

## 20. Emergency access to sealed history

Emergency access is a direct BCA case:

**Emergency Context + Legitimate Function + Functional Need → Temporary Bounded Access → Exercise → Context Changes/Ends → Access Authority Terminates**

Emergency access must not permanently convert sealed history into generally accessible history.

## 21. Contested right to forget

Historical should not adjudicate a contested constitutional or legal entitlement merely because it holds the record. The legitimate authority resolves the entitlement; Historical executes and preserves the resulting information-state transition.

> **Custody ≠ Adjudication.**

## 22. Unknown future value

The Humanity Machine and ESCP warn that present civilisation may fail to perceive future value. That supports caution around irreversible destruction but cannot override every privacy or deletion claim.

> **Possible Unknown Future Value ≠ Automatic Authority to Retain Identifiable Data.**

## 23. Searchability as the harm

Sometimes the harmful property is effortless discoverability rather than existence itself. This confirms the need for independent indexing, search and correlation controls.

> **Preservation ≠ Discoverability.**

## 24. Accountability Gradient

Exercises of consequential public or institutional authority can create stronger needs for authority-event provenance than ordinary private activity. This is an Accountability Gradient, not a blanket rule that officials lose privacy.

Irrelevant private detail has no automatic accountability value.

## 25. Historical's role

Historical should not invent destruction rights and should not possess a retention veto merely because information is historical. It should preserve applicable retention state, execute authorised transitions within its custody, maintain bounded transition provenance, identify affected relationships and derivatives, avoid accidental historical duplication of destroyed content, preserve only legitimate minimum residue and support audit.

## 26. Candidate invariants

1. Historical Value ≠ Unlimited Retention Authority.
2. ESCP Is Not a Universal-Retention Licence.
3. Preservation ≠ Discoverability ≠ Accessibility ≠ Correlatability ≠ Publication.
4. Forgetting ≠ Always Destruction.
5. Anonymisation ≠ Guaranteed Non-Reidentification.
6. Identity Removal ≠ Relationship Removal.
7. Accountability ≠ Universal Exposure.
8. Future Possible Value ≠ Sufficient Retention Justification.
9. Source Deletion ≠ Automatic Derivative Deletion.
10. Destruction Provenance ≠ Reconstruction of Destroyed Content.
11. Public Authority Accountability ≠ Permanent Private Dossier.
12. Participant Rights Survive Archival Transfer.
13. Custody ≠ Adjudication.
14. Authority to Preserve ≠ Authority to Correlate.
15. Historical Custody ≠ Centralised Possession.

## 27. Candidate objects

### Information-State Transition Record

Candidate fields include affected record/reference class, previous and new state, authorising process/reference, effective time, scope, affected derivatives/relationships, execution status, review state, minimum surviving provenance and access classification.

### Correlation Permission

Cross-domain correlation should be purpose-bounded rather than assumed from possession.

### Minimum Necessary Historical Residue

A bounded post-destruction marker sufficient for accountability without recreating deleted substance.

## 28. Cross-domain propagation

Deletion, sealing, anonymisation or correlation restriction can fail if only one repository applies it. Historical therefore needs an interface with KCS/shared dependency architecture to identify affected copies and derivatives without becoming their operational owner.

> **Information-State Change Must Propagate Across Known Material Dependencies.**

Unknown copies remain an ESCP limitation.

> **Deletion Completeness Must Be Scoped.**

## 29. ESCP result

The test exposes two opposite completeness errors.

**Destruction-side error:** present civilisation destroys information because it cannot perceive future value.

**Retention-side error:** present civilisation retains intrusive information because it cannot prove future irrelevance.

ESCP cannot mechanically choose retention.

> **ESCP Creates Epistemic Humility, Not Automatic Preservation.**

## 30. Test result

**Participant Privacy ↔ Historical:** PASS WITH INFORMATION-STATE ARCHITECTURE

**Accountability ↔ Forgetting:** PASS WITH MINIMUM-NECESSARY PROVENANCE

**Personal Data ↔ Civilisational Pattern Value:** PASS WITH ANONYMISATION / AGGREGATION AND RE-IDENTIFICATION CONTROLS

**Historical ↔ KCS Cross-Domain Correlation:** MATERIAL RISK / REQUIRES CORRELATION BOUNDARY

**Destruction ↔ Derivatives:** MATERIAL RISK / REQUIRES PROPAGATION AND RECONCILIATION

**Continuity Permanent Provenance Language:** REQUIRES INTERPRETIVE BOUNDARY FOR PERSONAL DATA

**BCA ↔ Historical Access:** STRONG FIT

**ESCP ↔ Retention:** PASS ONLY IF ESCP IS NOT TREATED AS RETENTION AUTHORITY

No fundamental failure of Historical is exposed. The missing control surface is explicit information-state, correlation, derivative and minimum-residue semantics.
