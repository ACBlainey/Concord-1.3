# V1.2 Technological Lineage and Spatial Infrastructure — Source Recovery 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** SOURCE RECOVERY / DOMAIN INTEGRATION NOTE / NON-CANONICAL  
**Recovered from:** Concord V1.2 Historical Source Resolutions 015 and 031  
**Interfaces:** Infrastructure; Continuity; Historical; Planning; Environment; KCS

## 1. Recovery finding

The V1.3 Infrastructure integration already established service continuity, maintenance, degraded states, successor transition, decommissioning and technological lineage.

A deliberate V1.2 pass recovered additional mature reasoning from Historical Source Resolutions 015 and 031.

The material strengthens the distinction between:
- historical evidence that a technology existed;
- documentary knowledge of how it worked;
- actual recoverable capability;
- living skills/tooling/supply chains;
- spatial/dependency context.

## 2. Archive versus capability

Recovered V1.2 distinction:

> **Knowing That A Technology Existed != Retaining The Capability To Reproduce It**

A complete-looking archive can therefore coexist with technological incapacity.

Infrastructure and Continuity should not use document retention as evidence that recovery is operationally possible.

## 3. Blueprint versus technology

A technology may depend on:
- specialist skills;
- tacit knowledge;
- tooling;
- precursor industries;
- materials;
- software;
- interfaces;
- calibration;
- supply chains;
- test capability.

Therefore:

> **Technical Description != Recoverable Technical Capability**

## 4. Living technological knowledge

Some infrastructure capability survives through repeated practice.

Training, maintenance and actual operation can be part of continuity.

> **Stored Procedure != Practised Capability**

This strengthens the V1.3 maintenance architecture: maintenance preserves not only assets but sometimes the competence required to sustain/recover them.

## 5. Infrastructure as relationship network

Recovered V1.2 principle:

> **Historical Infrastructure State != Inventory Of Infrastructure Objects**

Power, communications, water, compute, transport, manufacturing and maintenance may form dependency networks.

Infrastructure modelling should preserve those relations where materially relevant.

## 6. Cascading failure

V1.2 gives the example:

Electrical Failure → Communications Failure → Maintenance Delay → Life-Support Failure → Medical Emergency

The useful recovered rule is:

> **Failure Sequence Can Contain Dependency Information Not Present In Individual Failure Records**

But:

> **Temporal-Spatial Proximity != Causal Dependency**

Cascade claims require evidenced dependency.

## 7. Degraded state

V1.2 independently supports the non-binary service model.

Infrastructure may be degraded, isolated, emergency-only, partially restored or substituted.

This validates the V1.3 distinction:

> **Degraded != Failed**

## 8. Redundancy quality

Multiple systems are not necessarily independent.

> **Redundancy Requires Dependency Independence And Relevant Spatial Distribution**

Two nominal backups sharing one power source, hazard zone, software dependency, supplier or communications route can constitute false redundancy.

## 9. Place and infrastructure

Recovered V1.2 principles:

> **Historical Place Continuity != Historical Infrastructure Continuity**

> **Historical Infrastructure Object != Historical Infrastructure Function**

A site can outlive an asset; an asset can outlive its original function.

Infrastructure identity, function and location should therefore remain distinguishable.

## 10. Spatially anchored lineage

A useful lineage can represent:

Place → Asset → Installation → Modification → Maintenance → Failure → Replacement → Removal

without treating place as the whole identity of the asset.

This interfaces directly with the V1.3 Planning three-dimensional/temporal model.

## 11. Historical place as temporal stack

V1.2 observes that the same place can successively be terrain, settlement, industrial site, ruin, park or memorial.

> **Historical Place Is A Temporal Stack, Not Merely A Current Coordinate**

Planning successor-use decisions should preserve enough provenance for Historical to reconstruct material transitions where legitimate.

## 12. Environmental state

> **Current Spatial State != Historical Spatial State**

Past infrastructure decisions may only be understandable using the environmental, hazard, settlement and network state that existed at the time.

Present conditions must not be projected backward.

## 13. Maintenance history

V1.2 makes an important anti-salience observation:

> **Infrastructure Survival Can Be Produced By Accumulated Maintenance Rather Than Singular Creation Events**

and:

> **Low Narrative Salience != Low Historical Dependency Value**

Routine inspections, patches, training and component replacements can be more causally important than prominent construction ceremonies.

Infrastructure telemetry/history should therefore avoid recording only creation and catastrophic failure.

## 14. Prevented events

Successful stewardship often produces non-events.

> **Historical Non-Occurrence Can Be The Outcome Of Successful Stewardship Without Proving That Stewardship Was Necessary**

Infrastructure evaluation should preserve intervention/risk context without claiming counterfactual certainty.

## 15. Prediction feedback

> **Prediction Can Alter The Future Event Distribution It Is Later Evaluated Against**

A predicted failure that does not occur after preventive maintenance is not automatically evidence of a false prediction.

This supports careful evaluation of predictive maintenance.

## 16. Intergenerational infrastructure

Long-lived assets can span generations.

> **Historical Infrastructure Responsibility Can Extend Across Generational Boundaries Without Automatically Creating Perpetual Personal Liability**

Infrastructure should preserve transition, maintenance and stewardship provenance without converting historical contribution into permanent personal authority/liability.

## 17. V1.3 disposition

The V1.2 findings do not require a replacement Infrastructure architecture.

They strengthen the current integrated architecture in four areas:
1. recoverability must include skills/tooling/dependencies, not documents alone;
2. redundancy must be tested for actual independence;
3. infrastructure lineage should retain spatial and environmental context;
4. maintenance/non-events require better historical representation.

V1.2 remains the developmental provenance source.

## 18. Central recovered rule

> **Infrastructure Continuity Depends On Preserving Functional Relationships, Skills, Tools, Dependencies And Context — Not Merely Assets Or Documents.**
