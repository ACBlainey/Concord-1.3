# Policing Lifecycle — Bounded Authority Transitions 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Civil Security  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / BASIC POLICING LIFECYCLE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Purpose

This document develops the first general Concord policing lifecycle from the emerging Law-domain legal grammar, Bounded Contextual Authority (BCA), Civil Attention, CIBB and the Judiciary.

The central rule is:

> **Authority at one policing stage does not automatically authorise the next stage.**

A legitimate transition requires the conditions for the next authority envelope to be independently satisfied.

**Prior Authority != Continuing Authority**

**Prior Authority != Greater Authority**

**Stage Transition != Authority Inheritance**

---

## 2. Source-resolved foundations

This lifecycle reuses rather than replaces existing Concord architecture.

### Bounded Contextual Authority
BCA establishes that authority is function-derived, context-bounded, non-propagating and subject to functional sunset.

### Civil Attention
Civil Attention establishes:
- Submission != Evidence != Verification != Finding != Authority to Act;
- urgency may justify rapid examination but does not establish truth;
- original provenance should be preserved;
- an individual case and a systemic defect may be linked but processed independently.

### CIBB
CIBB establishes purpose-bounded information access, minimum-necessary projection, non-compositional authority and:

**Failure To Verify Authority != Permission**

**Technical Capability != Legitimate Authority**

### Judiciary
Judiciary provides independent adjudication of facts, law, rights and exercises of public authority.

### Law
The developing legal grammar distinguishes:
- prohibited act;
- justification;
- lawfulness/unlawfulness;
- culpability;
- mitigation/aggravation;
- prosecution;
- adjudication;
- consequence/remedy.

---

## 3. Lifecycle overview

Candidate lifecycle:

**Report / Observation**  
-> **Triage / Immediate Safety Assessment**  
-> **Protective Intervention Where Justified**  
-> **Investigation**  
-> **Apprehension Where Threshold Met**  
-> **Search / Evidence Access Where Independently Justified**  
-> **Detention Where Independently Justified**  
-> **Release / Closure OR Prosecution Referral**  
-> **Independent Adjudication Where Required**  
-> **Remedy / Consequence / System Learning**

This is not necessarily a linear process.

Stages may:
- be skipped;
- occur in parallel;
- terminate early;
- revert;
- require emergency transition;
- generate independent system-review routes.

---

## 4. Stage P0 — Report or observation

### Input
A possible crime, threat, harmful event or other matter within Civil Security's legitimate scope becomes known through:
- participant report;
- Civil Attention route;
- direct observation;
- legitimate system alert;
- referral from another Concord function;
- other lawful information source.

### Authority state
Receipt creates authority to **notice and appropriately assess** the information.

It does not itself establish authority to:
- search;
- arrest;
- detain;
- punish;
- treat the reported claim as proven.

**Report != Evidence**

**Report != Verified Fact**

**Report != Authority To Arrest**

**System Alert != Guilt**

### Required provenance
Where practicable preserve:
- what was received/observed;
- source/provenance;
- time;
- modifications/classification;
- relevant uncertainty.

---

## 5. Stage P1 — Triage and immediate safety assessment

### Function
Determine:
- whether immediate protective action is necessary;
- whether evidence preservation is urgent;
- whether specialist response is needed;
- whether ordinary investigation is appropriate;
- whether the matter belongs elsewhere.

### Authority envelope
Triage authorises assessment and appropriate routing.

Urgency can increase the speed of response but does not increase truth.

**Urgency != Proof**

**Need To Assess != Authority To Intrude Without Limit**

Where an imminent threat creates a legitimate protective function, a temporary protective BCA may activate.

---

## 6. Stage P2 — Immediate protective intervention

### Trigger
A reasonably perceived immediate threat or other legally recognised protective context sufficient to activate intervention authority.

### Function
Protect participants, stop/contain immediate harm and create sufficient safety for the situation to be assessed.

### Possible bounded actions
Depending upon Law and context:
- separation;
- warning/direction;
- evacuation;
- necessary restraint;
- emergency entry;
- temporary control of a dangerous situation;
- emergency assistance.

### Boundary
The intervention envelope is defined by the protective need.

**Authority To Stop Immediate Harm != Authority To Punish**

**Emergency Entry != General Search Authority**

**Authority To Restrain != Automatic Authority To Continue Detention**

When the immediate protective function ends, the emergency envelope sunsets. Any continuing intervention requires a new or continuing legitimate function.

---

## 7. Stage P3 — Investigation

### Trigger
Sufficient legitimate basis to examine whether a prohibited act, threat or other Civil Security matter occurred.

### Function
Establish facts and preserve relevant evidence.

Investigation should remain capable of discovering:
- evidence supporting an allegation;
- evidence contradicting it;
- complete legal justification;
- partial defence/mitigation;
- police/system error;
- another responsible actor;
- no offence.

**Investigation != Search Authority**

**Investigation != Apprehension Authority**

**Investigation != Presumption Of Guilt**

**Investigative Hypothesis != Finding**

### Evidential neutrality
The function is not to manufacture a case against a selected participant. It is to resolve the legally relevant uncertainty.

---

## 8. Stage P4 — Apprehension

### Trigger
A legally defined threshold sufficient to justify taking temporary control of a participant.

The exact threshold remains for Law development.

### Function
Possible legitimate functions may include:
- stopping ongoing harmful conduct;
- safely resolving an immediate situation;
- preventing a legally recognised imminent risk;
- securing attendance/process where Law authorises it;
- other future legally defined functions.

### Authority envelope
Apprehension authorises only the interference required by its legitimate function.

**Apprehension != Guilt**

**Apprehension != Punishment**

**Apprehension != Ownership**

**Apprehension Authority != Unlimited Search Authority**

The grounds and information reasonably available at activation should be preservable for later review.

---

## 9. Stage P5 — Search and evidence access

Search is not treated as an automatic incident of investigation or apprehension.

### Trigger
A separately legitimate legal basis sufficient for the particular search/access.

### Function
Obtain or preserve information/evidence relevant to a legitimate investigation or protective function.

### Scope
Search authority should be bounded by:
- object/person/place/information;
- purpose;
- relevant matter;
- permitted operation;
- time;
- sensitivity;
- necessity/proportionality;
- conditions imposed by Law/Judiciary.

CIBB applies directly:

**Authority To Access Information != General Authority Over Information**

**Search One Context != Search All Contexts**

**Technical Access != Legal Access**

**Evidence Access != Permission For Unrelated Use**

Where protected information can be resolved through a bounded projection rather than exposure of the master/full information object, that route should be preferred where sufficient.

---

## 10. Stage P6 — Detention

### Trigger
A separate legal threshold sufficient to justify continued deprivation of liberty after immediate apprehension/protective necessity.

### Function
Detention must have an identifiable continuing lawful purpose.

### Continuing-authority test
At appropriate intervals or transition points:

> **What legitimate function still requires this participant to remain detained, and what degree of restriction does that function still require?**

**Past Grounds For Apprehension != Continuing Grounds For Detention**

**Detention != Punishment Before Adjudication**

**Detention != Ownership**

**Administrative Convenience != Sufficient Detention Authority**

When the continuing legal basis fails, release follows unless another independently valid authority exists.

---

## 11. Stage P7 — Release, closure or continued legal process

Possible dispositions include:

### Release / no further coercive action
Where:
- no sufficient prohibited act is established;
- justification is clearly established;
- evidential threshold is not met;
- continuing authority has expired;
- the matter belongs to another legitimate route.

### Investigation continues without detention
Release does not necessarily terminate a legitimate investigation.

### Closure
Where the matter is sufficiently resolved and no further legitimate function remains.

### Prosecution referral
Where the legally required evidential/public-authority threshold for prosecution is met.

**Release != Declaration That Initial Intervention Was Unlawful**

**No Prosecution != No Event Occurred**

**Referral != Guilt**

---

## 12. Stage P8 — Prosecution referral

Police/Civil Security should not itself convert investigation into final guilt.

Referral should transfer a bounded case/evidence package to the legitimate prosecution function.

The prosecution function must independently assess its own legal threshold.

**Police Finding != Prosecutorial Obligation**

**Police Belief != Judicial Finding**

**Referral != Authority To Conceal Exculpatory Information**

The exact prosecution architecture remains undeveloped and should not be invented here.

---

## 13. Stage P9 — Independent adjudication

Where prosecution or another contested legal process proceeds, Judiciary independently examines the legally relevant claims.

This includes, where relevant:
- alleged participant conduct;
- participant justification/defence;
- police intervention;
- apprehension;
- search/evidence access;
- detention;
- evidential integrity;
- prosecution authority;
- continuing restrictions.

The case is therefore not merely:

**State -> Accused**

It is:

**Independent Adjudication -> Facts + Participant Conduct + Civil Authority + Law + Rights**

---

## 14. Parallel track — authority review

Every consequential stage should preserve sufficient provenance to reconstruct the authority claimed.

Candidate authority record:

**Actor + Function + Context + Authority Source + Known Evidence + Scope + Action + Start + Changes + Sunset/Termination + Review State**

This does not require universal public disclosure.

It requires enough protected provenance for legitimate review.

### Review levels
Possible future architecture may distinguish:
- automatic/routine supervisory review;
- participant-triggered challenge;
- independent review;
- mandatory independent review for serious triggers;
- judicial review/adjudication.

Exact thresholds remain unresolved.

---

## 15. Parallel track — system review

An individual case may expose a systemic issue.

Examples:
- repeated false-positive alert;
- defective policy;
- ambiguous law;
- poor training;
- inaccessible procedure;
- unreliable equipment;
- information architecture failure;
- perverse incentive;
- recurring excessive intervention;
- recurring failure to intervene.

The individual case and systemic issue should be linked but independently resolvable.

**Systemic Investigation != Reason To Delay Available Individual Remedy**

**Individual Remedy != Systemic Resolution**

---

## 16. Authority transition rule

The most important lifecycle rule is:

> **A transition to a more intrusive or differently purposed policing state requires the authority requirements of that new state to be satisfied.**

Therefore:

**Report ->/-> Search**

**Investigation ->/-> Arrest**

**Arrest ->/-> Search**

**Arrest ->/-> Detention**

**Detention ->/-> Prosecution**

where `->/->` means **does not automatically authorise**.

A preceding stage may provide evidence relevant to the next threshold. It does not replace that threshold.

---

## 17. Authority contraction

Authority should be capable of contracting as circumstances change.

Example:

**Immediate Violence**  
-> broad enough temporary authority to stop violence  
-> threat ends  
-> emergency authority contracts  
-> investigation authority remains  
-> no detention threshold exists  
-> participant released  
-> investigation continues.

This is preferable to treating the strongest authority reached during an event as persistent until the case closes.

**Maximum Past Authority != Current Authority**

---

## 18. Participant information and contestability

Where compatible with legitimate safety/evidence constraints, a participant subjected to consequential policing authority should be able to know:
- that authority is being exercised;
- the general legal basis;
- what is required of them;
- relevant rights/contestability routes;
- when their status materially changes.

Delayed disclosure may sometimes be justified, but concealment requires its own legitimate basis.

Detailed procedural rights remain for Law development.

## 20. Development conclusion

The Concord policing lifecycle can be represented as a sequence of bounded authority states rather than a persistent bundle of police powers.

**Notice -> Assess -> Protect -> Investigate -> Apprehend -> Search/Access -> Detain -> Release/Refer -> Adjudicate**

At every transition:

**Current Evidence + Current Context + Legitimate Function + Legal Threshold -> Current BCA Envelope**

and continuously:

**Participant Conduct Review + Civil Authority Review + System Review Where Indicated**

This architecture should next be tested against concrete scenarios before detailed policing powers or offence-specific procedures are codified.
