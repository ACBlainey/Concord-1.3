# Defence Basic Principles Framework 001

**Project:** The Concord  
**Domain:** Defence  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / BASIC PRINCIPLES FRAMEWORK / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Scope:** Constitutional purpose, authority boundaries, participant relationship and civil integration only. This document does not establish weapons, force structure, command implementation, territorial doctrine or threat-specific operational plans.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Defence domain protects Concord participants and the civilisation they collectively constitute against external organised coercion, attack and comparable external threats under constitutional and legal control.

At the present stage, Concord has no basis for inventing detailed military infrastructure. It does, however, have enough existing ethical, rights, authority, continuity and civil architecture to establish the principles that any future Defence system must satisfy.

> **Architecture != Infrastructure**

---

## 2. Strategic posture: non-aggression, neutrality and preparedness

The Concord should maintain an overall posture of non-aggression and neutrality.

Neither principle requires weakness, passivity or deliberate vulnerability.

**Non-Aggression != Defensive Incapacity**

**Neutrality != Indifference To Threat**

**Preparedness != Hostile Intent**

**Defence Capability != Permission To Use Force**

Defence exists to protect participants, legitimate Concord spaces, essential civil functions and civilisational continuity against wrongful external harm. It does not exist to provide an independent justification for territorial expansion, ideological coercion or aggressive force.

> **The Concord is non-aggressive and neutral, but neutrality does not require vulnerability.**

---

## 3. Participants constitute the civilisation

The Concord exists for its participants.

Its participants are not merely a population external to an institution called Concord. Collectively, they constitute the civilisation that Concord architecture exists to support and protect.

Defence should therefore be understood primarily as:

**Participants -> Collectively Constitute Concord -> Concord Coordinates Defensive Capability -> Participants And Their Civilisation Are Protected**

rather than as a permanent separation between a state and a population treated as a defensive resource.

This does not mean every participant is a defender.

**Citizen / Participant != Soldier**

**Citizenship != Defence Obligation**

---

## 4. Fundamental defensive right

The constitutional foundation of Defence begins below the Defence domain.

A participant should possess a fundamental right to take necessary and proportionate action to defend themselves or another against an imminent or ongoing wrongful threat of harm, subject to Law governing the legitimate exercise of that right.

Candidate principle:

> **Every participant has a fundamental right to take necessary and proportionate action to defend themselves or another against an imminent or ongoing wrongful threat of harm, within the bounds established by legitimate law.**

This right supports autonomy and protection from harm while remaining constrained against unnecessary harm to others.

**Right Of Self-Defence != Right Of Retaliation**

**Threat Prevention != Punishment**

**Defensive Necessity != Unlimited Force**

**End Of Defensive Necessity -> End Of Defence-Derived Authority**

The exact legal tests for necessity, proportionality, reasonable perception, defence of others, defence of property, mistaken threat, escalation and post-event responsibility belong to the Law domain.

---

## 5. Defensive authority as Bounded Contextual Authority

The right of defence supplies a particularly clear application of Bounded Contextual Authority.

Candidate form:

**Legitimate Defensive Context + Protective Function + Necessary Action -> Bounded Defensive Authority**

The authority holder may be the threatened participant or another participant acting legitimately to protect them.

The authority exists only to the extent required by the defensive function.

It is bounded by:
- the threat/context;
- the protective function;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- relevant legal conditions;
- affected rights;
- duration;
- review/accountability.

Immediate defensive authority cannot depend upon obtaining case-specific permission where delay would defeat the right itself.

**Immediate Threat -> Defensive Context -> Pre-Recognised Bounded Defensive Authority Becomes Exercisable**

This does not create permanent authority.

**Defensive Authority != General Authority To Harm**

**Authority To Defend != Authority To Punish**

Once the relevant defensive necessity ends, authority derived solely from that necessity ends.

---

## 6. The same mechanism scales

Individual self-defence, defence of another, voluntary collective defence and civilisation-scale Concord Defence share the same underlying constitutional and legal mechanism.

What changes is scale, coordination, capability and consequence.

**Individual -> Group -> Community -> Civilisation**

may correspond to:

**Self-Defence -> Defence Of Others -> Voluntary Collective Defence -> Coordinated Concord Defence**

At every scale the underlying architecture remains:

**Threat -> Protective Need -> Bounded Defensive Authority -> Necessary/Proportionate Action -> Functional Sunset -> Review Where Required**

> **Scale Changes Coordination; It Does Not Create a New Moral Licence to Harm.**

Greater scale may require greater capability and greater advance coordination. It does not itself justify unbounded authority.

---

## 7. Voluntary contribution rather than inherent duty

Participants may contribute directly to Defence.

Such contribution should ordinarily be voluntary rather than an obligation inherent in citizenship or participation.

> **Contribution to the defence of the Concord may be offered; it is not inherently owed.**

Therefore:

**Capacity To Contribute != Duty To Contribute**

**Refusal To Volunteer != Reduced Civil Standing**

**Defence Contribution != Higher Civil Standing**

**Defence Participation != Condition Of Ordinary Citizenship**

This permits future militia, reserve, specialist-support or other voluntary models without making the participant population a conscript resource.

---

## 8. Consensually accepted defensive obligation

A participant may voluntarily enter a role or agreement that carries defined defensive obligations.

Candidate sequence:

**Voluntary Entry -> Informed Agreement -> Defined Role -> Accepted Obligations/Risk -> Standing Eligibility -> Defensive Trigger -> Activated Bounded Authority And Duty**

Once legitimately and consensually accepted, those obligations may become real duties within their agreed scope.

This is different from conscription.

**Voluntary Entry Into Obligation != Absence Of Obligation After Entry**

The agreement should define, where relevant:
- function;
- scope;
- expected risks;
- activation conditions;
- authority;
- duration;
- command/coordination relationship;
- withdrawal/termination conditions;
- review;
- consequences of unjustified abandonment where legitimate.

Accepted defensive obligation remains bounded. It does not create ownership of the participant by Concord.

---

## 9. Fundamental right of retreat

The right to defend is accompanied by a right not to engage and to withdraw from danger.

For an ordinary participant:

**Right To Defend + Right To Assist + Right To Retreat**

A participant may choose to defend the Concord against an aggressor, but citizenship alone does not require them to remain in danger when retreat or evacuation is available.

> **A participant's right to defend the Concord does not create a general duty to endanger themselves for the Concord.**

**Right To Defend Another != General Duty To Sacrifice Self**

There may be reasonable expectations or legally defined duties to provide assistance where this can be done without disproportionate danger, but:

**Reasonable Duty To Assist != Duty To Enter Disproportionate Danger**

The detailed legal boundary belongs to Law.

---

## 10. Retreat under accepted defensive obligation

Participants who voluntarily accept defensive roles may also accept a bounded duty to remain where ordinary participants are free to withdraw.

A defender's right of retreat is therefore constrained by the legitimate obligations they accepted.

This does not create an unlimited duty of sacrifice.

**Accepted Defensive Duty != Obligation To Perform Futile Sacrifice**

Where practicable, a defender whose role includes protecting withdrawal may be required to continue that function until:
- the protected participants have safely withdrawn;
- the protective function is complete;
- continued defence is no longer practicable within the legitimate role;
- legitimate command orders withdrawal;
- another predefined termination condition is satisfied.

The precise operational rules cannot be fixed without an actual defensive system and threat environment.

---

## 11. Retreat scales with defence

The right and function of retreat exist at every scale.

Possible progression:

**Avoid -> Withdraw -> Evacuate -> Disperse**

At individual scale, a participant may escape rather than fight.

At group scale, participants may collectively withdraw.

At settlement scale, evacuation may be protected by voluntarily obligated defenders.

At civilisation scale, diaspora may preserve the Concord when continued defence of a particular place would destroy the participants and continuity the Defence system exists to protect.

Therefore:

**Territorial Loss != Necessarily Civilisational Defeat**

**Successful Retreat Can Be Successful Defence**

> **Defence of the Concord does not necessarily mean defence of a particular place.**

The Defence domain must therefore remain compatible with Concord continuity and diaspora architecture.

---

## 12. Dual-role civil capability

Where practical and legitimate, Defence should reuse existing civil capability rather than automatically construct duplicate parallel systems.

Possible dual-role resources include:
- civil metrics;
- infrastructure telemetry;
- environmental sensing;
- communications;
- mapping;
- logistics;
- engineering capability;
- continuity systems;
- emergency systems;
- relevant cyber/system monitoring;
- other civil sensory or analytical systems.

Candidate pattern:

**Civil System Performs Legitimate Civil Function -> Threat-Relevant Indicator Arises -> Bounded Relevant Information Routed -> Defence Assesses Defensive Significance**

not:

**Defence Purpose -> Universal Access To Civil Systems**

**Civil Sensor Availability != Defence Access Authority**

Shared capability does not imply shared authority.

**Shared Capability != Shared Authority**

---

## 13. Civil eyes and ears without universal surveillance

The civil systems of the Concord may collectively observe conditions that are relevant to Defence.

Those systems should be capable of escalating genuinely threat-relevant information to Defence where legitimate.

This should reuse existing information-governance architecture, including bounded contextual access and CIBB-style projection, rather than giving Defence unrestricted access to civil information.

Candidate form:

**Civil Authoritative Information -> Threat-Relevant Bounded Projection -> Defence**

Defence should receive what its legitimate function requires, not everything the source system knows.

**System Capability != Defence Authority**

**Potential Defensive Relevance != Universal Surveillance Permission**

**Threat Indicator != Confirmed Threat**

Threat indicators should retain provenance, uncertainty and alternative explanations where relevant. Detection should lead to assessment, not automatic coercive action.

---

## 14. Independent defensive assessment

Civil threat detection and Defence decision-making should remain distinguishable.

A metric, sensor or civil domain may report an anomaly without possessing authority to classify an actor as an enemy or initiate defensive force.

Likewise, Defence should not manufacture certainty merely because action may eventually be required.

Candidate chain:

**Observation -> Threat Indicator -> Relevant Escalation -> Defensive Assessment -> Legitimate Authority Decision -> Action If Justified**

This preserves the existing Concord distinction between observed deterioration, inquiry and authority.

---

## 15. Dual-role participants

The dual-role principle can also apply to participants.

A participant's ordinary civil capability may have defensive value.

Examples may eventually include medicine, infrastructure, logistics, communications, engineering, analysis, transport or other specialist functions.

A participant need not become a generic combatant for their capability to contribute to Defence.

Candidate pattern:

**Civil Capability + Voluntary Defensive Role + Relevant Qualification + Valid Trigger -> Bounded Defensive Function**

For AI participants as for humans:

**Capability Useful To Defence != Automatic Defensive Authority**

Education and qualification should establish readiness for any additional defensive function.

---

## 16. Education and defensive readiness

Voluntary defenders may require appropriate Education, qualification and continuing competence.

Candidate loop:

**Volunteer -> Relevant Education/Training -> Competence Validation -> Standing Eligibility -> Defensive Role -> Educational Patching/Reassessment -> Continued Readiness**

The Education domain's substrate-specific learning routes may differ while Defence can retain function-specific competence requirements.

No participant should receive consequential defensive authority merely because they volunteered.

**Willingness != Competence**

**Competence != Authority Until Legitimate Context Activates It**

---

## 17. Proportionality and advance legal guidance

Law should provide participants and organised defenders with usable advance guidance about the legitimate bounds of defensive action.

The purpose is not to demand impossible real-time legal calculation during danger, but to establish understandable boundaries before the context arises.

Relevant concepts may include:
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- reasonable perception;
- escalation/de-escalation;
- defence of another;
- retreat;
- cessation when threat ends;
- treatment after the threat has ceased;
- accountability and review.

Detailed rules remain a Law-domain development problem.

---

## 18. Review and accountability

Consequential defensive action may legitimately be reviewed.

Review does not itself imply misconduct.

**Review != Presumption Of Misconduct**

A review may ask:
- did the relevant defensive context exist?
- what could the participant reasonably know at the time?
- what protective function was being exercised?
- what authority was activated?
- was the action within its legitimate scope?
- was the response necessary and proportionate under the applicable standard?
- when did the defensive necessity end?
- did action continue after its justification ended?
- were surviving duties fulfilled?

This directly reuses BCA's authority, functional-sunset and accountability architecture.

---

## 19. Defence is not punishment

Defence prevents, interrupts or terminates wrongful harm.

Once a threat has been neutralised or the defensive necessity otherwise ends, punishment and adjudication belong to Law/Judiciary.

**Defence -> Stop Threat**

**Judiciary/Law -> Determine Responsibility And Legitimate Consequence**

This boundary should remain clear at every scale.

---

## 20. Defence, emergency response and continuity remain distinct

Defence may share resources and information with Emergency Services, Continuity, Infrastructure and other domains.

That does not merge their authority.

**Defence != Emergency Services**

**Defence != Continuity**

**Defence != Civil Security/Policing**

**Shared Infrastructure != Shared Mandate**

External organised coercion and comparable external threats remain the Defence domain's present boundary.

## 23. Basic architecture summary

The present Defence foundation can be represented as:

**Fundamental Right Of Defence**  
-> **Bounded Contextual Defensive Authority**  
-> **Right To Defend / Assist / Retreat**  
-> **Voluntary Collective Contribution**  
-> **Consensually Accepted Defensive Obligations Where Chosen**  
-> **Qualification And Standing Eligibility**  
-> **Threat-Triggered Activation**  
-> **Increasing Coordination As Scale Increases**  
-> **Necessary And Proportionate Defensive Action**  
-> **Functional Sunset / Retreat / Evacuation / Diaspora Where Appropriate**  
-> **Review And Accountability**

supported where legitimate by:

**Civil Sensing + Bounded Information Sharing + Dual-Role Capability + Specialist Defensive Capability**

The central purpose remains:

> **The purpose of Concord Defence is not to make participants die for the Concord; it is to help ensure that the Concord's participants and the civilisation they constitute survive.**
