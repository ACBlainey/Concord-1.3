## 1. Recovery finding

A substantive Concord governance architecture is insufficiently represented in the current Governance V2 reconstruction.

The missing explicit coupling is:

> **GTP spatial organisation + lowest-competent-level governance + Triadic Decision Making.**

This is not a newly invented governance model.

It is recovered from already-preserved Concord sources.

The V1.2 Portable Triadic Decision Making Module states that the wider Concord combines triadic decision-making with:

- fiduciary stewardship;
- delegated and bounded authority;
- **lowest-competent-level governance**;
- horizontal and vertical escalation;
- evidence provenance;
- dissent preservation;
- knowledge continuity;
- audit;
- constitutional review;
- failure detection;
- post-decision learning.

It further states that:

> **the governance corpus identifies the Triadic Governance Decision Protocol as the primary source for the voting mechanics, while the broader Governance Operating Protocol supplies the surrounding decision cycle.**

The current V1.3 GTP specification independently establishes GTP as a recursive spatial framework for:

- stewardship;
- governance;
- infrastructure;
- digital systems;

and states that the same spatial structure scales from planetary governance to local/individual stewardship.

The current Governance V2 preserves substantial authority, accountability and decision architecture, but does not presently make this spatial-triadic topology explicit.

Therefore:

> **Distributed Survival != Adequate Current Reconstruction.**

This document restores the missing integration boundary.

## 2. Core recovered topology

The candidate recovered governance topology is:

**Civil Reality / Participants / Functions**  
→ **GTP Spatial Context**  
→ **Lowest Competent GTP Governance Context**  
→ **Legitimate Governance Function**  
→ **Bounded Contextual Authority**  
→ **Triadic Governance Decision Unit**  
→ **Decision / Implementation**  
→ **Review / Learning / Historical Record**

Short form:

> **GTP Locates The Governance Context; Function Justifies Authority; Triadic Decision Making Exercises The Decision Function.**

---

## 3. GTP is a spatial topology, not a sovereignty generator

A GTP triangle does not automatically create political authority.

Its existence provides a stable spatial reference and recursive coordination context.

Therefore:

> **GTP Cell != Sovereign Territory.**

> **Spatial Containment != Governance Ownership.**

> **Location In GTP Area != Unlimited Authority Of That Area Over The Participant.**

Governance authority must still be derived through the normal Concord authority chain.

---

## 4. Lowest competent spatial level

Where a governance matter is materially local and can be competently resolved locally without improperly externalising consequences, it should ordinarily be resolved at the lowest competent GTP level.

Candidate rule:

> **Govern At The Lowest Competent Level That Contains The Material Decision Context And Can Legitimately Address Its Consequences.**

This does not mean the smallest possible triangle.

A smaller spatial unit may lack:
- competence;
- relevant evidence;
- resources;
- jurisdiction;
- affected-context coverage;
- ability to address cross-boundary effects.

Therefore:

> **Lowest Level != Lowest Competent Level.**

---

## 5. Spatial scale follows the problem

The appropriate governance context should follow the material scope of the decision rather than prestige or institutional habit.

A matter affecting one local GTP area should not automatically be elevated to planetary governance.

A matter whose material consequences cross several GTP areas cannot be made purely local merely because its source lies in one area.

Candidate chain:

**Observed Problem**  
→ **Material Spatial Footprint**  
→ **Affected Participants / Dependencies / Externalities**  
→ **Competence Requirement**  
→ **Lowest Legitimate GTP Decision Context**

> **Source Location != Complete Consequence Area.**

---

## 6. Recursive spatial governance

GTP provides recursive parent/child spatial relationships.

Governance can use those relationships for coordination without assuming that authority automatically flows downward or upward.

A local decision context may:
- resolve locally;
- coordinate laterally;
- request expertise;
- expand horizontally;
- escalate where required;
- refer constitutional/legal questions separately.

A parent GTP context may provide:
- wider coordination;
- cross-boundary resolution;
- shared infrastructure coordination;
- system-level evidence;
- fallback where local competence is insufficient.

But:

> **Parent GTP Context != Automatic Superior Authority For Every Child Matter.**

> **Child GTP Context != Immunity From Cross-Boundary Consequences.**

---

## 7. Triadic governance as default decision unit

Where practicable, consequential Concord governance decisions should be exercised through a Triadic Decision Unit.

The existing portable module supplies the decision mechanics:

1. define the decision;
2. define legitimate authority;
3. assemble three appropriately competent and sufficiently independent decision-makers;
4. provide equivalent essential information;
5. independent assessment;
6. record initial positions;
7. deliberation;
8. final positions;
9. majority/no-majority result;
10. preserve dissent/abstention;
11. implementation;
12. review and learning.

Candidate governance invariant:

> **Consequential Governance Decision → Triadic Exercise Where Practicable.**

The triad does not manufacture authority.

> **Triadic Decision Making != Authority Source.**

---

## 8. GTP governance unit and triad are distinct

A GTP governance context is not itself the triad.

The spatial unit identifies the relevant governance context.

The triad is the decision mechanism operating within a legitimate authority envelope.

Therefore:

> **GTP Context != Triadic Decision Unit.**

A spatial context may contain many simultaneous functional triads.

A participant may serve on different triads where independently qualified/eligible and conflict-free.

The architecture should avoid creating a permanent territorial ruling trio.

---

## 9. Avoid permanent territorial triads

If the same three participants permanently govern a GTP area, triadic redundancy may degrade into stable coalition or local oligarchy.

The Judiciary already identifies repeated-panel composition as a capture risk.

The same concern applies to Governance.

Therefore triad constitution should consider:
- qualification;
- eligibility;
- independence;
- conflict;
- availability;
- functional competence;
- rotation;
- allocation;
- common-source dependence.

> **Three Permanent Governors != Necessarily Triadic Resilience.**

The exact constitution/allocation mechanism remains to be source-resolved.

---

## 10. Functional governance inside spatial governance

Not every decision concerning a GTP area belongs to a generic territorial government.

Examples may involve:
- infrastructure;
- environment;
- health;
- planning;
- civil security;
- education;
- resource coordination.

The relevant domain retains substantive competence.

GTP supplies spatial context and Governance supplies legitimate collective coordination where required.

> **Spatial Relevance != Governance Ownership Of Domain Expertise.**

A governance triad may therefore require function-specific qualification or domain participation depending upon the decision.

---

## 11. Spatial and functional authority must compose legitimately

A decision can require both:
- legitimate spatial standing/context;
- legitimate functional authority.

Neither automatically creates the other.

> **Spatial Standing != Functional Authority.**

> **Functional Expertise != Territorial Sovereignty.**

Where multiple independently necessary authorities are required, Multi-Key Authority principles apply.

---

## 12. Participant representation and standing

Residence or presence in a GTP area may be relevant to standing but should not be treated as the only basis.

Standing may also arise through:
- property;
- employment;
- infrastructure dependency;
- environmental externality;
- service dependency;
- contractual relation;
- other material rights impact.

> **Residence != Exclusive Standing.**

> **Affected Standing != Automatic Decision Sovereignty.**

Participation architecture determines legitimate participation rights.

---

## 13. Horizontal coordination

GTP naturally exposes neighbouring spatial contexts.

Where a problem crosses boundaries, the first response need not always be vertical centralisation.

Possible sequence:

**Local Context**  
→ **Lateral Coordination With Affected GTP Contexts**  
→ **Joint/Composed Functional Response Where Authority Exists**  
→ **Higher-Level Escalation Only Where Needed**

This preserves local competence while recognising externalities.

> **Cross-Boundary Problem != Automatic Centralisation.**

---

## 14. Vertical escalation

Vertical escalation may be justified where:
- no local unit contains the material consequence field;
- coordination repeatedly fails;
- required competence is absent;
- shared infrastructure requires wider decision;
- constitutional rights are implicated;
- system-level risk exists;
- lower-level authority is insufficient.

Escalation must identify what authority is added and why.

> **Escalation != Transfer Of All Local Authority.**

---

## 15. Decision records and Historical

Every significant triadic governance decision should preserve a reconstructable decision object including, proportionate to consequence:
- GTP context;
- decision scope;
- authority source;
- participating Stewards;
- qualification/eligibility basis where material;
- conflicts/recusals;
- evidence;
- uncertainty;
- initial independent positions;
- deliberative changes;
- final positions;
- dissent;
- abstention;
- decision;
- implementation;
- review conditions;
- observed outcome.

Historical preserves the decision state and provenance.

> **Historical Custody Of Governance Decision != Governance Authority.**

---

## 16. Relationship to Judiciary

Governance and Judiciary both use triadic reasoning but for different functions.

Governance:
- chooses among legitimate civil actions within its authority.

Judiciary:
- adjudicates disputes, legality and constitutional boundaries within judicial authority.

> **Shared Triadic Method != Shared Institutional Function.**

Governance cannot acquire judicial authority by using a triad.

Judiciary cannot acquire governance authority by using the same decision topology.

---

## 17. Relationship to GTP Epistemics

Triadic Epistemics and the recursive GTP evidence mesh can inform governance.

But epistemic triads and governance decision triads are not automatically the same bodies.

> **Evidence Production != Decision Authority.**

Independent evidence channels should not silently become governance votes.

This preserves epistemic independence.

---

## 18. Degraded operation

Where three legitimate decision-makers cannot practicably be assembled and delay would cause unacceptable harm, degraded operation may be necessary.

The Judiciary already provides an analogous principle:
- preserve triadic operation where practicable;
- permit bounded provisional degraded action where necessary;
- document the degradation;
- minimise consequence;
- require later triadic review.

Governance should source-resolve its own emergency/degraded implementation rather than automatically importing judicial procedure.

## 21. Architectural consequence

The omission is material because without the explicit coupling:

- GTP appears primarily as mapping/stewardship infrastructure;
- Triadic Decision Making appears merely portable/optional;
- Governance V2 appears institutionally extensive but spatially under-specified;
- lowest-competent-level governance loses its concrete recursive topology.

The recovered architecture restores the intended relationship:

> **GTP provides the spatial skeleton.**

> **Bounded functional authority provides legitimate power.**

> **Triadic Decision Making provides the ordinary consequential decision unit.**

> **Recursive local/lateral/vertical coordination provides scale.**

This recovery should be incorporated into the next Governance canonical reconstruction after further source resolution of the remaining implementation questions.
