## 2. Persistent function

Governance provides legitimate collective direction, stewardship, delegation, administration, coordination and civil decision-making within constitutional bounds.

It exists to discharge legitimate civil functions.

It does not exist to perpetuate governing institutions for their own sake.

> **Governance Function != Institutional Self-Preservation**

## 3. Stewardship

Governance V2 frames authority as constitutional trust rather than personal property.

> **Authority Is A Responsibility, Not An Entitlement**

A steward exercises authority for a defined purpose.

Election, appointment, expertise, reputation, contribution or office may help select an authority-holder; none independently creates unlimited authority.

> **Selection Into Office != Creation Of Unlimited Authority**

## 4. Direction of justification

V1.2's Authority Justification Chain supplies the clearest compact operating grammar:

**Observed Condition**
→ **Rights-Relevant Problem**
→ **Legitimate Civil Objective**
→ **Required Function**
→ **Functional Need For Authority**
→ **Minimum Proportionate Authority**
→ **Bounded Contextual Authority**

The direction is:

> **Legitimacy → Function → Need → Authority**

not:

> **Authority → Function → Legitimacy**

## 5. Anti-circularity

> **Authority Exists != Function Is Legitimate**

> **Authority Exists != Authority Is Necessary**

> **Historical Authority != Present Justification**

> **Declared Function != Legitimate Function**

> **Institutional Interest != Civil Objective**

Existing power cannot cite its own existence as sufficient evidence for continuation.

## 6. Legitimate function does not automatically require authority

A legitimate function may be performed through:
- voluntary interaction;
- information;
- coordination;
- ordinary consent;
- public access;
- market/community mechanisms;
- technical interoperability.

Therefore:

> **Legitimate Function != Special Authority Required**

Exceptional/coercive authority requires the additional necessity step.

## 7. Minimum Necessary Capability

V1.2 graduated Minimum Necessary Capability at specification level after blind transfer testing.

Its stable chain is:

**Purpose → Function → Need → Minimum Sufficient Capability → Bounded Exercise → Review → Termination Or Re-Justification**

It protects both sides:

> **Minimum Necessary != Minimum Possible**

Under-capability can prevent legitimate function.

Over-capability creates unnecessary power.

## 8. Capability and authority

Capability and authority remain distinct.

> **Capability != Permission != Authority**

A system may technically be capable of an action without legitimate authority to perform it.

Likewise an office may possess legitimate authority in principle while lacking the practical capability to discharge the function.

Governance must track both.

## 9. Bounded Contextual Authority

Once a legitimate function and need are established, authority should be bounded by the actual context.

Candidate authority envelope:
- actor;
- purpose;
- function;
- permitted actions;
- affected object/resource;
- spatial scope;
- information scope;
- time;
- activation conditions;
- delegation;
- revocation/termination;
- review;
- provenance.

> **Authority != Single Boolean Permission**

## 10. Inherent sunset

Where authority derives solely from a function:

> **Function Ends → Function-Derived Authority Ends**

unless a separately justified successor function exists.

This is stronger than a policy preference for sunset: continued authority requires continued justification.

> **Past Justification != Permanent Authority**

## 11. Successor function

A successor function can legitimately preserve some authority after an original function ends.

But:

> **Successor Function != Automatic Continuation Of All Original Authority**

The new function must justify its own scope.

This interfaces directly with BTA.

## 12. Dynamic authority

Context may change.

**Context Change → Function Change → Authority Reassessment**

Authority may expand if new necessity is independently justified.

It should contract when necessity contracts.

> **Earlier Authority != Automatic Authority For Later State**

## 13. Delegation

Ordinarily:

> **Delegated Authority <= Delegator's Legitimate Delegable Authority**

Delegation carries bounds.

> **Delegation != Unlimited Role Transfer**

Where authority originates independently in law/constitution rather than from the delegator, that separate source should remain explicit.

## 14. Aggregate authority/capability

Local grants can combine into a dangerous aggregate.

Minimum Necessary Capability's blind test showed the importance of checking overlapping credentials/roles.

> **Minimum Local Capability != Minimum Systemic Capability**

The same applies to authority.

Several individually bounded permissions may compose into de facto general control.

Aggregate state therefore matters.

## 15. Effective versus formal authority

Formal rules are not the whole governance state.

Economic dependence, infrastructure control, information monopoly, advisory dominance, reputation, complexity or technical capability can create effective influence/control.

> **Formal Authority != Effective Power**

But:

> **Effective Power != Legitimate Authority**

Governance should detect the divergence rather than legitimise it by observation.

## 16. Articulability

Governance V2 requires significant authority to be explainable.

A decision should be able to state:
- question;
- authority basis;
- evidence;
- relevant rights/principles;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- reasoning;
- consequences;
- justification.

> **Model Recommendation != Authority**

Use of AI or complex systems does not transfer responsibility from the legitimate decision-holder.

## 17. Proportionality

The existence of authority does not justify maximal use.

> **Available Power != Necessary Exercise**

Proportionality asks what intervention is reasonably sufficient for the legitimate purpose while respecting affected rights and consequences.

It applies to action and inaction.

## 18. Accountability and correction

Governance V2 distinguishes accountability from punishment.

Review should distinguish:
- reasonable adverse outcome;
- good-faith error;
- inadequate evidence;
- implementation failure;
- negligence;
- recklessness;
- conflict;
- corruption;
- deliberate misconduct;
- systemic failure;
- unforeseeable failure.

> **Bad Outcome != Automatic Misconduct**

> **Correction != Institutional Disloyalty**

Loyalty to the Concord includes loyalty to correction.

## 19. Authority should not manufacture its own evidence

V1.2 identifies recursive escalation:

**Authority Exercise → Condition Produced/Detected → Condition Used To Justify More Authority**

Where authority materially contributes to the evidence used for its own expansion, independent examination is required.

> **Authority-Generated Evidence Of Need Requires Independent Review**

## 20. Optimisation boundary

Civil objectives remain rights-bounded.

> **Legitimate Objective != Unlimited Optimisation Of One Metric**

“Maximise safety”, “maximise stability” or “maximise efficiency” cannot erase the rest of the constitutional evaluation space.

## 21. Routine low-friction authority

The architecture must remain usable.

Repeated ordinary functions can use pre-legitimated patterns where appropriate:

**Recognised Routine Function + Valid Context/Consent → Low-Friction Bounded Authority**

This avoids requiring constitutional re-derivation for every routine act while preserving the upstream basis.

## 22. High-impact authority

As consequence, rights impact and irreversibility rise, stronger justification/review may be required.

Candidate controls include:
- stronger evidence;
- multiple independent authorisers;
- predefined triggers;
- short functional windows;
- judicial review;
- provenance;
- contestability;
- after-action review.

The exact mechanism remains domain-specific.

## 23. Governance interfaces

Governance does not absorb neighbouring domains.

- Constitutional Core supplies superior constitutional/ethical bounds.
- Judiciary independently adjudicates/reviews where applicable.
- Law supplies legal rules and offences.
- Civil Security exercises bounded protective/investigative authority.
- Emergency architecture governs exceptional constitutional states.
- Research tests/develops candidate mechanisms.
- Historical preserves provenance.
- Domains define their substantive functions.
- Portable Modules provide reusable mechanisms but do not create authority merely by adoption.

## 26. Central rule
