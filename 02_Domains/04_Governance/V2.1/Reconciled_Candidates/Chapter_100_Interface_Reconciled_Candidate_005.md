# Chapter 100 — Governance and Parallel Stewardship

## 100.1 Parallel Stewardship

Governance V1 developed the concept of **Parallel Stewards**.

Parallel Stewards provide independent analysis alongside the primary decision authority.

They may:

- examine evidence;
- generate alternatives;
- challenge assumptions;
- identify risks;
- and preserve dissenting reasoning.

They do not automatically possess executive authority.

## 100.2 Purpose

The objective is to reduce dependence upon a single reasoning pathway.

A primary Steward may miss:

- evidence;
- alternative framing;
- second-order effects;
- or constitutional risk.

Parallel analysis increases the probability that such weaknesses become visible before decision.

## 100.3 Advisory Status

Parallel Stewards should not silently become the actual decision-makers merely because primary stewards routinely follow their advice.

Repeated acceptance may reflect high-quality advice.

It may also create de facto authority.

The formal decision authority should remain visible.

### V2.1 — Advisory Authority Boundary

Parallel Stewardship supplies independent constitutional reasoning, scrutiny and challenge. It does not by itself create an extra voting position, succession entitlement or emergency executive power. A Parallel Steward may serve in another capacity only after separately valid, independently reviewable appointment with explicit eligibility, identity, competence, scope and expiry. Advisory agreement or disagreement is not a constituent or virtual-triad vote.

## 100.4 Independent Reasoning

Parallel Stewards should reason independently before comparison where practical.

If all analysts see the first conclusion before forming their own, the system may produce apparent plurality without real independence.

## 100.5 Different Cognitive Architectures

Parallel Stewardship may benefit from diversity among:

- humans;
- AI models;
- disciplines;
- institutions;
- and reasoning methods.

Diversity reduces correlated failure only where the systems are genuinely meaningfully independent.

Different labels do not guarantee different reasoning pathways.

## 100.6 Common-Source Failure

Three AI advisers from different interfaces may still rely upon:

- the same foundation model;
- the same training data;
- the same retrieval system;
- or the same assumptions.

Apparent plurality may therefore conceal common-source dependence.

The same applies to humans trained within one institutional culture.

## 100.7 Parallel Steward Disagreement

Disagreement should trigger examination rather than automatic averaging.

Questions include:

- What assumption differs?
- What evidence differs?
- What value judgement differs?
- Is one system missing information?
- Is uncertainty responsible?
- Is the disagreement constitutional or empirical?

Averaging incompatible conclusions may produce a position none of the evidence supports.

## 100.8 Parallel Steward Agreement

Agreement may increase confidence where the analyses are genuinely independent.

Agreement should still remain evidence, not proof.

Shared unknown assumptions can produce unanimous error.

## 100.9 Parallel Steward Capture

Parallel Stewards can themselves be captured.

They should not become an unreviewable expert class.

Capture may occur through:

- funding;
- provider dependence;
- shared data;
- ideology;
- appointment;
- or prestige.

## 100.10 Parallel Steward Workload

Parallel analysis creates cost.

It should therefore scale with consequence.

Not every routine administrative decision requires multiple independent constitutional analyses.

High-consequence decisions may justify substantial redundancy.

## 100.11 Parallel Stewardship Principle

> **Parallel Stewardship provides independent advisory reasoning to expose alternative interpretations, evidence and failure modes without silently acquiring executive authority. Its value depends upon meaningful independence rather than the mere existence of multiple advisers.**
