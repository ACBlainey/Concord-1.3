# Chapter 132 — Governance and GTP

## 132.1 Geodesic Triangular Partitioning — Conceptual Mesh

GTP is Concord's adopted conceptual multiresolution triangular spatial reference and coordination mesh. The tessellation exists at every level of geometric subdivision. Selecting a scale for a particular task does not create, abolish or activate the existence of a triangle; it selects a level of description and a corresponding operational coverage.

Spatial parent and child describe geometric containment and subdivision only. They do not describe superior and subordinate governments or offices.

The mesh can provide a consistent reference for:

- territorial and overlapping jurisdiction analysis;
- local and cross-cell coordination;
- representation and stewardship coverage;
- resources and infrastructure;
- geographic scaling;
- and simultaneous operations at different spatial resolutions.

GTP does not itself appoint Stewards or create constitutional competence.

## 132.2 Constitutional Independence and Spatial Continuity

GTP is foundational to Concord's **current spatial governance implementation**, but it is not the source of constitutional rights, ethical standing, consent, lawful authority or legitimacy.

Those principles must remain effective if a future properly authorised constitutional process modifies or replaces the spatial implementation. Such a change requires explicit approval, reference migration, rights-preserving continuity, provenance and review. Neither the adopted status of GTP nor its potential replacement may be silently inferred from convenience.

## 132.3 Spatial Architecture and Constitutional Authority

GTP describes spatial relationships, not the distribution of lawful power. It cannot determine constitutional worth, rights, legitimacy, authority or ethical principle by geometry alone.

A larger triangle is not automatically a superior jurisdiction. A legally established constitutional priority rule, federated competence or delegated office may exist, but must derive from an independently valid constitutional source.

Spatial architecture operates beneath constitutional legitimacy.

## 132.4 Concurrent Resolution and Operational Coverage

Every tessellation level exists continuously. Multiple levels may be operational at once for different tasks, including overlapping coordination fields and wider-area common projects.

Workload, available capacity, externalities, geography and functional competence may justify operational subdivision or consolidation. Operational change is a change of coverage, not a change to the conceptual mesh. Hysteresis must prevent unnecessary oscillation as workload or population varies.

Each operation must identify its lawful jurisdiction, responsible Stewards, resource authority, implementation responsibility, review and termination.

GTP cannot alone settle representation for unequal populations, mobile populations, cross-cell infrastructure or non-territorial affiliations.

## 132.5 Mesh Coordination and Legitimate Shared Decisions

GTP supports coordination at any relevant scale without presuming a governance command hierarchy. Local decisions remain with the lowest capable legitimate decision field. Wider coordination may pool evidence, logistics or genuinely shared resources but does not itself override independent local competence.

A binding cross-cell decision requires separately established shared jurisdiction or lawful delegation, with bounded scope and accountability.

Where expressly authorised, an expanded triadic decision may use originating triangle R and edge-adjacent A, B and C:

- **Origin-excluded:** three authorised constituent triads A, B and C each yield one resultant F/A/U vote.
- **Origin-included:** three mixed constituent triads, each using two authorised positions from A, B or C respectively and one from R, each yield one resultant F/A/U vote.
- The three constituent results form the final virtual triad. At least two F results yield F; at least two A yield A; otherwise the result is U (Unresolved).
- The nine individual votes must not be pooled; R receives no fourth resultant vote.
- **Unique Steward positions:** all nine Steward-occupied positions in either three-constituent configuration require nine distinct eligible Stewards. In the origin-included arrangement, the three origin positions R_A, R_B and R_C must be occupied by three distinct eligible Stewards; no Steward may also occupy a neighbouring position or any other position in the same decision.
- **Authority conservation:** each constituent triad produces one resultant occupying only its receiving decision slot. When an extended triad substitutes for one position of a larger triad, its three internal votes jointly determine that single position's weight, never three additional original-level votes. This is a voting-weight constraint, not a transfer of jurisdiction.
- **Bounded voting context:** a Steward's vote authority exists only for the specific authorised decision event and position. A new constituent label, session or retry does not establish a fresh independent decision. All occupied positions in the event graph must pass protected identity-uniqueness checks; missing event-boundary proof withholds consequential commit.
- **Validity and consequence:** F/A/U arithmetic remains non-operative until independent competence, forum, participation, quorum, and commit checks pass. A valid committed decision may have enduring legal consequences after vote authority expires, but implementation requires separate lawful authority.

At a shared abstract vertex of six GTP triangles, the six associated Steward positions belong to **six different triangles**. They are not one six-Steward governing body or shared office. Each triangle has its own three-Steward triad and jurisdiction. Two adjacent triangles can share two geometric vertex locations without their Stewards sharing offices, voting positions, or authority. A Steward's abstract vertex does not prescribe a physical workstation: the triangle's Stewards may work together in a governance building elsewhere **inside their own triangle**, separately from neighbouring triangles.

The geometry creates no standing cross-triangle interaction. A Steward may take a role in another triangle's **specifically authorised extended vote**, with only the lawful bounded vote-specific authority for that event; no general authority over that triangle follows.

Individual eligibility for such extended voting, appointment, quorum and authoritative decision-event boundary rules still require separate formal specification. There is **no six-Steward vertex interoperation procedure** to reconstruct.

**Vertex-adjacent substitute pool.** When a triangle-owned Steward is lawfully recused from a particular decision for a material conflict of interest, the five other triangle-owned Stewards whose triangles share that Steward's **abstract vertex** provide the geographically defined pool of alternate candidates. Following independent eligibility and decision-event identity checks, a fair, auditable, tamper-resistant random draw may select one eligible Steward to fill the original **single bounded decision position**. The five alternates do not constitute a governing body, and their geometric relationship supplies a candidate pool, not automatic authority. The chosen person must be independently authorised for the decision, cannot already occupy another Steward voting position in the complete event, and receives no continuing jurisdiction over the originating triangle. An abstention due to insufficient information instead triggers evidence gathering, not replacement on that ground. The detailed recusal, random-draw and quorum interfaces are developed provisionally in Chapter 12 Candidate 010 and Integration notes 022–023.

## 132.6 Population Variation

Equal geographic area does not mean equal population.

Governance should therefore avoid assuming that identical cells necessarily deserve identical representation in every institution.

Representation may need to consider:

- population;
- territory;
- resource function;
- constitutional equality;
- and institutional purpose.

No final formula is established.

## 132.7 Geographic Neutrality

A mathematically generated boundary may reduce some forms of politically motivated district manipulation.

It does not remove all political consequence.

Geography still contains:

- communities;
- transport;
- resources;
- language;
- ecosystems;
- and historical relationships.

Neutral geometry can therefore reduce one bias while creating other mismatches.

## 132.8 Boundary Revision

Territorial systems change.

Population moves.

Infrastructure grows.

Habitats expand.

New settlements appear.

A spatial system should therefore consider whether and how boundaries can be revised.

Revision should not become an uncontrolled political mechanism for manipulating representation.

## 132.9 GTP and Digital Communities

Digital communities may not occupy conventional territory.

GTP therefore cannot by itself define all future jurisdiction.

A plural civilisation may require both:

- spatial Governance;
- and non-spatial constitutional affiliation.

## 132.10 GTP and Interplanetary Settlement

GTP may be applied differently on:

- Earth;
- Mars;
- moons;
- orbital habitats;
- or artificial structures.

The geometry may need adaptation.

The constitutional principles should remain independent of exact geometric implementation.

## 132.11 Geometry Does Not Create Legitimacy

A mathematically neutral territorial division does not automatically produce fair governance.

Population, culture, infrastructure and real-world geography remain relevant.

The principle is:

> **Geometry may structure jurisdiction. Geometry does not create constitutional legitimacy.**

## 132.12 GTP Principle

> **GTP is Concord's adopted multiresolution spatial coordination mesh, not a hierarchy of governing authority. Every spatial level exists conceptually, and multiple levels may be used concurrently. Geometry can organise the location and scale of governance activity but cannot create constitutional legitimacy, rights, jurisdiction or command authority.**

Its use must preserve subsidiarity, representation, cultural context, practical rights, lawful delegation and the possibility of properly authorised constitutional evolution.
