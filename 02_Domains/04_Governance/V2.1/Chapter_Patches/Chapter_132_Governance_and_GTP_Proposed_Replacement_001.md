# Chapter 132 — Governance and GTP
## V2.1 provisional replacement chapter — 001

**Status:** Temporary patch for Section 14 (Chapters 131–140). Replaces the interpretation of GTP as optional or inherently hierarchical in V2 §132.1–132.5; retain and reconcile remaining provisions from original chapter during assembly.

### 132.1 — Geodesic Triangular Partitioning as the spatial mesh
Geodesic Triangular Partitioning (GTP) is a foundational spatial reference architecture for Concord's geographically distributed governance and coordination. It provides a persistent, multiresolution tessellation and stable spatial addressing for triangles and vertices. The spatial mesh exists conceptually at all resolutions, regardless of which resolutions are active in a particular task.

### 132.2 — Spatial abstraction does not confer authority
GTP spatial parent/child relationships identify geometric containment and resolution only. They do **not** confer superior or subordinate governance authority. Governance authority remains contextual, bounded, separately justified and subject to constitutional constraints. A larger spatial area may coordinate smaller ones without automatically commanding them.

### 132.3 — Concurrent resolutions and adaptive workload
Different tasks may operate at different GTP resolutions simultaneously; overlapping coordination fields are permitted when scope and jurisdiction are explicitly specified. Governance workload may justify choosing finer or broader operational resolution. Dynamic subdivision or consolidation of *operational coverage* should use hysteresis to avoid repeated oscillation, without implying that spatial child triangles are created or destroyed: they always exist in the underlying tessellation.

### 132.4 — Edge adjacency and triadic decision geometry
An originating triangle R has three edge-adjacent triangles A, B and C in the basic four-triangle local arrangement. The two author-confirmed expanded voting configurations are:
- **Origin excluded:** three neighbouring triads A, B, C each produce one vote.
- **Origin included:** three mixed triads (2 A + 1 R), (2 B + 1 R), (2 C + 1 R) each produce one vote.
The three resultant votes form the virtual final triad. Constituent votes are not pooled. The GTP vertex is represented by six Stewards, creating further potential configurations subject to authority and independence rules.

### 132.5 — Coordination, jurisdiction and externalities
The lowest capable legitimate decision level remains the default. Larger geographic scope may require information sharing, operational coordination, shared decisions for inseparable externalities, or a combination. Geographic aggregation does not automatically override valid independent local decisions. Expansion should be exceptional and proportionate, with defined authority, scope and termination.

### 132.6 — GTP's constitutional interface
GTP's foundational spatial role does not mean geometry creates rights, ethical standing, constitutional legitimacy or unlimited power. Constitutional safeguards govern all spatially expressed decisions. Technical refinements to tessellation or implementation may be made through proper change control without silently making the entire spatial coordination concept optional.
