## Compatibility findings

**C-01 — Emergency levels vs GTP mesh: compatible.** Constitutional Emergency V2 Chapter 41 expressly describes five levels as operational classifications, not constitutional hierarchy. A wider emergency can require more coordination without creating a higher spatial government. The examples of local, regional, civilisation-wide and planetary declaration in Chapter 21 are illustrative; the actual declaring institutions remain open design.

**C-02 — Threat recognition vs legal power: compatible.** Chapters 19–22 distinguish threat, declaration, classification and specific power activation. The GTP mesh may support sensing and coordination across triangles, but those observations do not confer binding jurisdiction.

**C-03 — Succession vs power: compatible.** Emergency V2 Chapters 82 and 92 and Governance Chapter 28 distinguish delegated succession from independent activation of exceptional power. Emergency classification does not raise a Steward's constitutional rank.

**C-04 — Restoration: compatible.** Emergency V2 Chapters 169–171 end temporary delegation, dissolve emergency coordination structures and restore ordinary jurisdiction. This matches bounded overlapping GTP coordination, provided cessation of one emergency mandate does not erase separate standing lawful commitments.

## Missing Civilisational Clock interface — propose integration, not a new authority

For every emergency event, record distinct timestamps or cycle references:
1. **Observed:** when threat evidence is first received; observation alone does not grant authority.
2. **Recognised/classified:** when the threat is assessed and level assigned; classification is operational.
3. **Declared:** when a legally competent actor issues the declaration; abbreviated records may be completed later as Emergency V2 Chapter 20 allows.
4. **Activated:** when each separately authorised power meets its valid trigger and starts.
5. **Decided:** when the relevant lawful forum determines the decision; a two-matching-vote majority may already be arithmetically settled under G-03, subject to all other validity gates.
6. **Effective:** when that decision lawfully takes effect, normally at the applicable clock transition unless an existing urgent-effect rule permits earlier effect.
7. **Expired/restored:** when each exceptional power and restriction ends, including automatic sunset, rights restoration and post-event review.

These are **distinct event fields**, not seven mandatory sequential delays. One action may lawfully occur immediately after activation when its independently valid urgency rules allow. An ordinary majority does not itself activate extraordinary emergency power.

## Specific integration guardrails

- Do not infer a universal emergency clock bypass. The operative timing rule belongs to the actual mandate and urgency class.
- A high emergency level does not imply a larger GTP office, a superior Steward, more votes, or a shared command jurisdiction.
- Local protective measures, cross-area information coordination, shared-competence commitments and exceptional compulsory directions have different authorisation paths.
- A later clock transition cannot retrospectively legitimise an unauthorised action.
- Restoration must consider individually continuing restrictions and rights, not merely the disappearance of the emergency label.
