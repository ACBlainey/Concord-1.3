# Chapter 132 — Source-resolved targeted amendment proposal 006

**Status:** working chapter patch, not adopted. Original V2 canonical remains untouched.
**Exact V2 source:** `02_Domains/04_Governance/Governance V2 Canonical.md`, Chapter 132, lines 37720–37892 in blob `c65a269fdc95b79a8b815bb6ec7381529a6c151f`.

## Identified source conflict
- §132.1 describes GTP as a **neutral hierarchical territorial partitioning** and says it remains an optional architectural module.
- §§132.2 and 132.12 say Governance can operate without GTP and that GTP is not a constitutional foundation.
- §132.5 describes **nested jurisdiction** using larger and smaller spatial layers.
- §132.11 correctly states that geometry cannot create constitutional legitimacy.
- The later GTP recovery document and author-confirmed V2.1 terminology distinguish **permanent conceptual tessellation** from the selected operational resolution; GTP parent/child is geometric only, never governance rank. Multiple resolutions can operate concurrently and governance is a mesh, not a territorial command hierarchy.
- The conceptual availability of GTP at all resolutions is compatible with the separate constitutional principle that geometry does not confer legitimacy. However, §132's optional-module framing and hierarchical terminology require explicit source reconciliation, not silent reinterpretation.

## Proposed replacement for §132.1 (working proposal)
> **Geodesic Triangular Partitioning as a spatial reference mesh.** GTP is a persistent conceptual tessellation in which all geometric resolutions remain available simultaneously. Selecting a resolution for a particular task does not create or remove triangles. Governance may coordinate across neighbouring, overlapping, finer-resolution, coarser-resolution or wider consequence contexts without treating spatial containment as political command. Physical offices and Stewards need not occupy a triangle's mathematical vertex.

## Proposed replacement for §132.5 (working proposal)
> **Cross-resolution coordination, not nested command.** GTP's geometric parent/child relationships describe spatial subdivision only. Governance responsibilities depend on independent competence, mandate, consequences and consent; no finer/coarser context inherits authority from spatial containment. Multiple resolutions may operate at the same time. Workload may justify activating finer-grained coordination or consolidating operational coverage, with hysteresis to avoid excessive oscillation, but the underlying conceptual tessellation does not change.

## Proposed supplement to §132.12 (working proposal)
> **Constitutional separation.** GTP may be the standard Concord spatial reference architecture while rights, legitimacy and bounded authority remain independent of geometry. Functional, digital and non-territorial governance contexts continue to exist alongside spatial contexts. GTP coordinates; it does not rule.

## Unresolved question
Whether §132.2's optional-module framing should be replaced by a normative GTP-default requirement needs explicit constitutional and source-owner review. The amendment above deliberately does **not** silently remove that source clause.

## Preservation and review
Keep §§132.3–132.4 and §§132.6–132.11 unless a subsequent clause-level review identifies a justified change. No automatic replacement of V2; reconcile with authoritative GTP specification and original triadic governance source before Section 14 assembly.
