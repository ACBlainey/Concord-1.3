## Editorial decisions and open points
1. **Do not equate non-hierarchical GTP geometry with abolition of all constitutional hierarchy.** A constitutional priority rule may exist independent of spatial scale.
2. **Do not delete V2 §132.2 by default.** The user's clarification establishes GTP's foundational role as a spatial abstraction; it does not necessarily repeal the principle that constitutional rights survive a change in spatial implementation. Proposed Chapter 132 patch must be narrowed accordingly.
3. **Do not create a fourth vote for R** in origin-included expanded voting.
4. **Do not count all nine individual ballots** to decide the final virtual triad.
5. **Do not equate U with Against**, or abstention with absence, or Parallel Steward with appointed substitute.
6. **Do not infer common decision authority** merely because a hurricane or other event spans multiple jurisdictions.
7. **Do not imply a geometric parent triangle can command a child triangle.** Institutional delegation and jurisdictional precedence require separate lawful sources.

## Blocking unresolved details
- Six-Steward vertex-to-office mapping and permissible repeated R participation across mixed constituent triads.
- Recusal/abstention/absence substitution triggers, voting thresholds and exceptional expiry.
- Exact jurisdictional competence for mixed triads and common infrastructure decisions.
- Whether V2.1's final Chapter 132 should describe GTP as a default required Concord spatial reference while preserving constitutional portability.
- Cross-resolution conflict resolution and workload hysteresis numerical rules.
