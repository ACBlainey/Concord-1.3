## New explicit authorial clarification

An extended triad's three internal members **jointly determine one decision position** in the receiving original or virtual triad. That position remains one of three equally weighted positions. It has the same **decision weight**, not three times the decision weight. No internal votes are pooled into the receiving triad's count.

This is **not** a claim that three members share a divisible third of all constitutional authority. The substituted position is still bounded by the mandate of the original position, and the substitute forum needs independent lawful authorisation.

## Formal interface

For a receiving triad P=[P1,P2,P3], let `S(P2)=[Q1,Q2,Q3]` be a valid authorised substitution. Define `resolve(Q)` as the ordinary two-of-three resultant, with no-majority recorded as U. Then `resolve(P)` uses exactly `[P1, resolve(Q), P3]`, not `[P1,Q1,Q2,Q3,P3]`. The substitution preserves the number of receiving positions, **three**.

For a three-constituent virtual triad, use `[resolve(QA),resolve(QB),resolve(QC)]`. This is three decision positions, not nine. Recursion does not change receiving-level position count, but this mathematical fact does **not** license recursive escalation or confer jurisdiction.

## Critical separation of mechanisms

1. **Information expansion:** invite additional evidence, expertise or neighbouring perspectives; no additional decision position is created.
2. **Position substitution:** an authorised constituent triad resolves one original decision position; decision weight is conserved.
3. **Jurisdictional expansion/coordination:** affected authorities may need a new jointly competent forum or lawful agreement; vote arithmetic cannot supply the missing jurisdiction.
4. **Escalation:** referral to a separately constituted competent institution, not automatic rule by a geometrically larger GTP cell.

These mechanisms can coexist, but one does not imply the others.
