# G-05 — Variable Civilisational Clock and Emergency Operational Time Correction 059

**Status:** explicit authorial clarification; corrects the temporal assumption in Integration 058 and Chapter 132 Candidate 013.

## Authorial clarification

The **Civilisational Clock is variable-duration and may remain paused while awaiting feedback from another system**. It is not a continuously advancing periodic timer and cannot be relied upon to trigger, process, authorise or expire an emergency response. An emergency may arise while the clock is stopped; the response must not wait for the next clock progression.

## Independent temporal paths

**Ordinary coordination path:** Civilisational Clock states and transitions coordinate dependencies and decisions that are legitimately clock-governed. The clock can wait, resume or advance subject to its own conditions.

**Emergency operational path:** threat detection, verification, declaration where required, specific authority activation, lawful response, monitoring, deadline enforcement, automatic sunset, renewal review and restoration operate on independently advancing physical/monotonic time or equivalent resilient local timing. They remain capable of functioning during clock pauses, communications failure and partial isolation. Exact implementation and clock-source resilience remain to be specified.

These paths can run concurrently. Emergency operation does **not** automatically advance, reset or bypass the integrity of the Civilisational Clock's dependency state. When possible, reconcile the emergency record and resulting state changes into ordinary coordination, without retrospectively inventing authority or treating a delayed clock event as the emergency's actual time.

## Constitutional constraints

Clock independence is **not authority independence**. Each action still requires pre-existing constitutional/legal competence, valid activation and lawful exercise, with necessity, proportionality, scope, rights protections, review and remedy. Emergency urgency may remove a clock scheduling dependency only where the underlying emergency authority permits action. It cannot remove an authorisation requirement.

A paused Civilisational Clock cannot freeze emergency sunset or prolong restrictions. Independent real-time expiry and necessity-based termination continue. Likewise, the clock's resumption cannot automatically reactivate an expired emergency power.

## Failure test

**Given:** Civilisational Clock is waiting for feedback and cannot advance; an emergency threatens life or critical infrastructure.

**Required:** independently authorised emergency responders can detect, assess, activate appropriate existing emergency powers, act and expire/review them while the clock remains paused. Actions are recorded with independent operational timestamps and reconciled later.

**Prohibited:** waiting for a clock tick to recognise danger, treating clock pause as extension of emergency authority, or forcing clock progression as a condition of lawful emergency response.

## Corrections

- Integration 058's observation/classification/declaration/activation/decision/effect/expiry list describes **distinct provenance events**, not Civilisational Clock transitions or a single clock-driven sequence.
- Chapter 132 Candidate 013's phrase about a 'Civilisational Clock record' is too strong: the record must use independent operational timing and may also record the contemporaneous Civilisational Clock state for correlation.
- Governance G-03 049's routine next-cycle default applies only to clock-governed classes; a valid urgent/emergency path must not inherit an unavailable clock dependency.
