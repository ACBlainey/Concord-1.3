# GTP Vertex Representation — Authorial Clarification 020

**Status:** Authorial clarification recorded 2026-10-11; provisional V2.1 integration, not canonical enactment.

## Clarified meaning of the six Stewards at a GTP vertex

At an abstract GTP vertex where six triangular cells meet, the six associated Steward positions **belong separately to the six different triangles**. The coincidence is geometric and representational, not an office-sharing, governing-body or jurisdiction-sharing arrangement.

Each triangle has its own three-Steward governance triad. The three abstract vertex positions of triangle A represent its own three Stewards; triangle B has its own three. Even where A and B share two geometric vertex locations, their respective Steward positions remain distinct offices associated with their respective triangles. They do not become co-holders of one official office, and one triangle's Steward gains no authority over another triangle merely through geometric coincidence.

## Physical location versus abstract position

A triangle's vertex is an abstract reference on the tessellation, **not** the mandated physical location of its Steward. Its three Stewards may work from a governance facility physically situated anywhere appropriate **within their own triangle**. The neighbouring triangle's Stewards may similarly work together within that neighbouring triangle. Physical co-location across triangles, or direct physical contact between their Stewards, is not required or presumed.

## Authority and exceptional interaction

Ordinarily, a Steward's governance office is related to their own triangle and the lawful scope of its governance. There is no standing authority, chain of command, shared office, or automatic joint decision arising from a common GTP vertex.

The identified official cross-triangle interaction is **specific authorised extended voting**: a Steward from triangle A may be called to contribute to an extended vote concerning triangle B, and vice versa. Any such role is confined to the independently lawful, bounded decision-specific vote. Adjacency is a possible selection relation, not itself an appointment, mandate or jurisdiction grant.

A Steward must still occupy at most one Steward-voting position in the complete authorised decision event; origin-included three-constituent voting requires nine distinct eligible Steward holders, with no duplication. Constituent resultants preserve their single receiving-position weight.

## Effect on the earlier unresolved-mapping question

The **representational mapping** is now clarified: six separate triangle-owned Steward positions coincide at one abstract geometric vertex. There is **no intended six-Steward interoperation mechanism at that vertex** to recover or reconstruct.

This does **not** by itself resolve specific legal eligibility, appointment, recusals, quorum, the selection of the three distinct origin holders in an extended vote, or decision-event boundaries. Those remain separate governance procedures and must not be invented from the geometry.
