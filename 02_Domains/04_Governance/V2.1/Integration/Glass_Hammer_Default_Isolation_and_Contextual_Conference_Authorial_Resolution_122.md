# Glass Hammer — Default Isolation and Contextual Conference — Authorial Resolution 122

**Status:** authorial clarification and source reconciliation; developmental integration, not a new constitutional authority or deployed implementation.  
**Date:** 13 October 2026  
**Scope:** ordinary triadic Steward voting, contextual conference, irreversible ballot commitment, and related information boundaries.

## 1. Authorial clarification

**Official Steward voting ordinarily occurs in isolation. Conference may nevertheless be allowed in legitimately established contexts, including circumstances not presently foreseeable.** Concord must preserve provision for such contextual exceptions rather than declaring conference categorically forbidden.

This resolves the apparent version conflict between the earlier *Triadic Decision Making Module* (independent preliminary reasoning followed, where appropriate, by collective deliberation) and the later glass-hammer isolation and single-use commitment architecture. The earlier deliberative capability survives as a **contextual possibility**, while isolated decision-making remains the **ordinary operational default**.

This clarification does not itself grant anyone permission or authority to convene a conference.

## 2. Core invariants

1. **Default isolation.** Each Steward ordinarily reasons and commits independently, without access to other Stewards' votes, reasoning, or decision status beyond expressly authorised minimal projections.
2. **Contextual conference exception.** Conference can occur only through an independently legitimate contextual procedure, with its own activation basis, permitted participants, scope, information-sharing boundaries, termination conditions, and provenance. Its eligible circumstances are **open-ended**, not an exhaustive enumerated list.
3. **No manufactured authority.** Neither conference nor agreement during conference creates jurisdiction, mandate, participation eligibility, collective sovereignty, additional votes, or host execution authority. CAP ≠ PERM ≠ AUTH.
4. **Independent official positions.** Conference does not fuse the three official Steward positions. Each official ballot remains a separate, authenticated, bounded, single-use exercise of its position's authority.
5. **Immutable commitment.** A valid committed ballot cannot be revoked, revised, replaced, or resubmitted under a new event identity. Conference after one or more commitments cannot reopen them. Concerns and review are separate append-only events with independently authorised pathways.
6. **Information controls.** CWA specifies the conference context; CIBB controls access and projections. A conference must not silently expose sealed ballots or protected reasoning, including by indirect disclosure or aggregation.
7. **Outcome and implementation separation.** Two matching independently valid official F/A ballots determine the substantive outcome. Contextual early acceptance and downstream execution remain separately authorised. Conference cannot change that arithmetic or re-enable a consumed vote.
8. **Historical continuity.** Record the contextual basis, authority, relevant participants, activation/termination, and consequential provenance under applicable privacy controls. Historical custody does not confer present authority.

## 3. Procedure boundary

### 3.1 Before any official ballot

Where an independently legitimate context permits conference, eligible Stewards may exchange only the information allowed by that context. The conference must not be treated as a binding preliminary vote or a substitute for each Steward's final individual ballot. The eventual official ballot remains single-use and individually attributable.

The source corpus does **not** yet establish a universal rule for whether, or how, a conference may disclose preliminary nonbinding positions. This must be controlled by the specific authorised context and its safeguards, not assumed permitted by this resolution.

### 3.2 After a ballot has been committed

A committed ballot is immutable. A later conference cannot confer authority to change it. A Steward may separately report a concern through the existing concern/review route. Any replacement decision requires a fresh decision identity and independently established authority, with independence safeguards as applicable.

### 3.3 Exceptional timing and partial participation

Whether conference may be initiated after one Steward has committed but before the others have voted is **not settled** by the authorial clarification. Such a case presents heightened risks of anchoring, coercion, and inference about sealed ballots. Do not implement it as an automatic permission. Preserve as an unresolved contextual-authority and information-flow question.

### 3.4 Failure to establish a legitimate conference context

Where the exception's legitimacy or safeguards cannot be established, ordinary isolated voting remains the default. This is not a general power to prevent an independently legitimate decision from proceeding.

## 4. Existing architecture owns the exception

- **Governance / Bounded Contextual Authority:** verifies the legitimate civil function, mandate, eligible participants, and authority lifecycle.
- **Contextual Wrapper Architecture:** declares the exceptional conference context, boundaries, activation, permissible conduct, exit and review.
- **Concord Information Black Box:** protects ballot, reasoning, concern and conference records; provides only authorised projections and enforces disclosure boundaries.
- **Triadic decision state machine:** retains three independent positions, single-use immutable commitments, majority determination and separate acceptance.
- **Judiciary / STRA:** independently handle authorised challenge, concern routing and review, without turning conference into a revocation mechanism.
- **BTA / CBPR / host owners:** retain ownership of consequential transitions and execution; conference is not an execution grant.
- **Historical:** preserves protected provenance and supersession links without rewriting committed history.

No new independent conference authority owner, universal exception list, or parallel voting protocol is created.

## 5. Source precedence and version reconciliation

The older portable module's provision for discussion **does not override** the later ordinary-isolation default. The later glass-hammer description of isolation **does not categorically prohibit** legitimate conference. Read them together as:

> **Independent isolated official voting is the default; legitimately authorised contextual conference remains possible; every official ballot is still individually committed and irreversible.**

This is an authorial source reconciliation, not a claim that either original document has been silently rewritten or that every historical version was already consistent.

## 6. Open questions, without premature rules

- Who is the competent owner for authorising conference in each decision class?
- Which pre-commit information, including provisional positions, may be exchanged?
- Can a conference lawfully occur once some but not all official ballots are committed, and with what information restrictions?
- How should conferences involving substitute, expanded, or virtual triads preserve constituent independence and avoid correlated influence?
- Which material conference events require Historical provenance, and what protections apply to private deliberation?

These questions are to be resolved through existing contextual and domain authority, not by an invented universal hierarchy or a closed catalogue of exceptions.

## 7. Validation boundary

This is an **architectural source resolution**, not a test fixture. Do not test the room, case, lock or hammer analogy. Any later validation should address an actual implementation of conference authorisation, independent vote commitment, disclosure restrictions, immutable history, and downstream authority separation, using existing components where possible.
