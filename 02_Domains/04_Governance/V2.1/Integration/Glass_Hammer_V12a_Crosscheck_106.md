## Findings

**EKC-02 comparison:** explicitly reopened the V1.2 Authority Justification Chain and Bounded Contextual Authority as *active-development evidence*, not canonical governance law. It classified authority legitimacy, minimum proportionality, function-bounded scope and functional sunset as strong architectural convergence (C1), with functional variation and non-kernel dependencies. No direct tension was identified. Its counterfactual is not an independent implementation of glass-hammer consumption.

**U1–U8 derivation:** governance authority is derivative from legitimate cooperative function, not status or office; consequential intervention needs a defensible harm/burden relationship, proportionality, evidence, correction and non-domination. Institutional function may persist while its implementing institution is replaceable.

**CRSTL:** separates coarse Civilisation Clock cycles from consequence-relevant civil transitions and material ordering/dependency relations. A transition record can include ID, domain/function, prior state, prerequisites, authority/source, local time evidence, consequence, branch, provenance, uncertainty and reconciliation. Material ordering can be unknown; legitimate concurrent branches can coexist. Clock timestamps do not establish causal/authority precedence. The Clock **does not acquire governance authority** and domain owners retain authority for domain-specific consequences.

**State Map 001A:** current state, domain ownership, dependency, evidence, uncertainty, correction and provenance are distinct fields; B1 expressly says state description is not governance authority.

## Interface with glass-hammer section 30A

- Authority legitimacy and boundedness: BCA / Authority Justification Chain / competent domain and ethical rights constraints.
- **Consumption event:** the exact context-guarded decision/act changes its authoritative state, preventing re-use of the same enabling situation. This is the user's current clarified requirement; V1.2 section 30A supports one-use exercises and changed context, but does not prescribe a specific atomic transaction implementation.
- **Transition history and cross-domain ordering:** CRSTL may represent the resulting event and material dependencies, but it **must not become a gate that can delay an emergency while the variable Civilisation Clock is paused**. Immediate operational state-machine handling remains independent; CRSTL consumes/preserves consequences and ordering evidence.
- **State Map:** descriptive synthesis of the current and prior state, not an issuer of authority or a substitute for the operational state machine.
- **ACKS:** transport/custody confirmation does not regenerate authority or establish a second execution.
- **External effects:** a committed local state transition is not proof of an irreversible external host effect. Reconciliation and unknown-commit handling remain CBPR/BTA/host-owned.

## Nuance / unresolved boundary

V1.2 section 30A.4 allows a legitimately justified continuous *bounded authority envelope* for some functions and cautions against bureaucratic re-authorisation for trivial movements. The user's current clarification stresses single-use exercise and mandatory context change. These can be compatible **if** the envelope is treated as a defined activity context while each materially consequential exercise has its own non-replayable state transition; that reconciliation is a proposal requiring validation, not an assertion that the historical text already resolved every case.

## Implications for Governance fixtures

Stages 100–102 test time-bounded prerequisite evidence and an unfenced evidence-check/effect race; they do not validate glass-hammer authority. A new fixture should test an atomic, context-guarded state transition and distinguish same-event replay from a new event ID attempting to reuse a consumed enabling state. CRSTL records must be emitted as provenance without turning the Civilisation Clock into a synchronous execution dependency.
