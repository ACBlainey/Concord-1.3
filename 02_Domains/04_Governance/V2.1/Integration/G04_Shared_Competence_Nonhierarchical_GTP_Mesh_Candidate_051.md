# G-04 — Shared Competence in a Non-Hierarchical GTP Mesh 051

**Status:** PROVISIONAL design candidate for authorial review. Derived from Integration 006 and 045, and Chapter 132 Candidate 011; does not amend original Governance V2 Canonical or create new constitutional jurisdiction.

## Existing architectural constraints

GTP is a continuously existing, multiresolution spatial reference and coordination mesh. Several levels can operate concurrently. Spatial parent/child is a **geometric** relation only, not governance hierarchy. Adjacency, workload, scale, a shared node or majority agreement among neighbours never grants authority over a non-consenting independent jurisdiction. Triadic decisions operate within their own legitimate mandates; an expanded virtual triad is not itself a new cross-jurisdiction legislature.

## Distinguish four activities

1. **Information coordination:** independently competent actors share evidence, forecasts, alerts and plans. No new binding authority is required merely to communicate.
2. **Voluntary joint execution:** participants agree to contribute specified resources or services under their own lawful mandates. Each participant's authority remains bounded by its own contribution.
3. **Binding shared competence:** affected parties create or invoke an **independently lawful common mandate** covering an identified common question, assets, obligations, limits, decision procedure, duration and review. Its decisions bind **only to the extent of that mandate**.
4. **Emergency preservation:** independently established temporary authority may be needed where urgent harm crosses jurisdictions; this is G-05, not an implied shortcut through G-04.

## Proposed common-competence instrument

A minimal `SharedCompetenceRecord` should specify:

- **Question and common interest:** exactly what joint matter requires decision, with evidence of common effect.
- **Affected participants and rights:** whose assets, jurisdiction, safety or obligations are implicated, including nonparticipants and dissenters.
- **Mandate provenance:** each participant's lawful source of consent/delegation, or another independently established constitutional basis; silence and geographic adjacency are not consent.
- **Bounded scope:** which decisions may bind whom, which resources can be committed and what rights cannot be overridden.
- **Decision forum:** eligible triadic/virtual triadic or other authorised decision process; distinct participant positions and provenance.
- **Temporal interface:** start, Civilisational Clock effective cycle if relevant, review intervals, expiry and withdrawal/continuity obligations.
- **Audit and remedy:** challenge, conflicts, record retention, responsibility for consequences and lawful termination.

This is a **candidate interface record**, not a claim that a particular consent threshold or institution is already adopted.

## Worked discriminating case

Five triangles A–E share a river basin. A–D authorise a joint monitoring and maintenance scheme for equipment and funding they legitimately control. Their shared decision may bind those contributions. Triangle E declines to join. A–D may still share observations and operate their own assets; they **cannot** bind E's independent budget, compel E's Steward positions, or regulate E's unrelated resources merely because four of five agree. If downstream harm or rights issues arise, the response requires an independently valid legal basis or the separately authorised emergency-preservation process, not an invented fifth-triangle vote.

## Distinguish shared effects from shared jurisdiction

An effect crossing a boundary does not, by itself, transfer governance jurisdiction. Conversely, where an independently lawful mandate already covers a shared asset or service, a participant cannot automatically veto every action by invoking geometric locality. The actual legal scope must be examined; do not invent a universal unanimity rule or a universal majority-over-all rule.

## Open authorial choices

1. How are common-competence mandates created or recognised: explicit reciprocal delegation, standing pre-authorised agreements, constitutional competence, or a combination?
2. Which affected rights require affirmative consent, which can be lawfully constrained under general law, and how are nonparticipants protected?
3. How do withdrawal, Civilisational Clock timing, shared obligations and infrastructure continuity interact?

## Provisional recommendation

**Adopt mandate-bounded shared competence rather than territorial hierarchy.** Require explicit provenance for every binding scope; allow overlapping mesh coordination at multiple scales without multiplying authority. Do not infer the legal mandate merely from a triadic majority or GTP topology.
