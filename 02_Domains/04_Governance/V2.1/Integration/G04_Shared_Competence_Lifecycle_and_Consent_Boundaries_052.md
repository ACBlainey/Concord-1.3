# G-04 — Shared Competence Lifecycle and Consent Boundaries 052

**Status: PROVISIONAL development refinement, not an adopted constitutional rule.** Extends Candidate 051 without presuming that voluntary agreement is the *only* lawful origin of shared competence.

## Operating distinction

A GTP area can participate in overlapping coordination fields without becoming subordinate to a spatial parent. Shared governance is a **bounded mandate relation**, not a new territorial hierarchy. A triangle's agreement is not a transferable blank cheque; it only commits matters within that participant's existing lawful competence.

## Minimal lifecycle

1. **Identify the shared question:** map common assets, service dependencies, affected rights, and participants at whatever GTP scales are appropriate. A shared impact is evidence of interdependence, **not itself a grant of jurisdiction**.
2. **Resolve authority:** for each binding obligation, identify an existing constitutional competence, lawful standing agreement, or explicit bounded delegation/consent. If none exists, coordinate nonbindingly or seek a legitimate mandate.
3. **Constitute the shared forum:** identify the authorised decision procedure, independent eligibility, triadic vote-resultant composition where used, decision-event boundary and audit. Do not count neighbouring triangles as votes merely because they touch.
4. **Decide and time effect:** distinguish substantive majority from legal validity and Civilisational Clock timing. An urgent effect requires authority under existing rules; urgency does not generate new common competence.
5. **Operate and review:** each contribution remains accountable to its lawful holder. Record execution responsibility, consequences, periodic review and contestation.
6. **Change or end:** changes of scope, participation or rights require renewed lawful authority. Withdrawal should preserve existing lawful commitments and necessary continuity arrangements, without making membership irrevocable by default.

## Consent boundary

**Normal candidate pathway:** reciprocal voluntary agreement or a standing pre-authorised agreement between independently competent parties.

**Important exception:** existing constitutional or legal competence may already authorise decisions affecting multiple areas; do not require fresh unanimous territorial consent where the applicable lawful mandate does not. Equally, neither majority participation nor geometry can commandeer an independently protected third party's assets or rights.

A party may opt out of an otherwise voluntary new undertaking, but opting out does not extinguish independently applicable legal duties or permit harm to others. Those duties must be established through a **separate lawful source**, not invented by the shared forum.

## Small example: inter-area water service

Triangles A, B and C may each lawfully contribute equipment, staff or funding to a shared pumping station. Their authorised shared forum may determine maintenance and scheduling within those contributions. D may use downstream water without joining the funding agreement. The agreement alone cannot impose new charges on D or seize D's resources; any duties concerning pollution, safety or access must arise from an independently valid rights/legal instrument.

## Non-goals and remaining authorial decisions

This note does **not** specify a universal unanimity threshold, territorial veto, voting weight by population, or a standing central governing office. It does not grant emergency authority. Open questions: precise consent/delegation instruments, third-party rights boundaries, termination liabilities and continuity across Civilisational Clock cycles.

**Provisional engineering recommendation:** make the source of competence explicit on each shared decision record, and allow voluntary, standing or independently constitutional mandates as distinct pathways. This is an implementation framing awaiting authorial approval.
