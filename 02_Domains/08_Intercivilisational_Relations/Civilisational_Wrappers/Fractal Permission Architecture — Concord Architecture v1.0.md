## 1. Purpose

Fractal Permission Architecture (FPA) represents contextual permission without reducing permission to binary access, physical capability, ownership, role or a universal context-independent rule.

It asks:

> **Who may do what, where, why, when, under which conditions, by which legitimate basis, and until when?**

## 2. Core principle

> **Represent permission as a bounded contextual relationship among participant, action, target/context, function, legitimate basis, conditions and time; grant no more permission than the legitimate basis requires; and terminate context-derived permission when that basis ends.**

## 3. Minimum Necessary Permission

**Legitimate Function → Required Interaction → Required Access/Action → Minimum Necessary Permission → Function-Bounded Activity → Function Completion → Permission Termination**

> **Function → Need → Minimum Necessary Permission**

Permission should extend no further than reasonably necessary for the legitimate function, remain subject to higher-order rights/rules, and terminate when its basis ends unless another legitimate basis persists.

## 4. Core separations

> **Spatial Classification ≠ Access Classification**

> **Permission to Enter ≠ Permission to Use**

> **Access ≠ Single Permission**

> **Physical/Technical Capability ≠ Normative Permission**

> **Contextual Permission ≠ General Permission**

> **Permission for One Function ≠ Permission for Unrelated Functions**

> **Parent-Space Permission ≠ Automatic Child-Space Permission**

> **Shared Context ≠ Shared Consent**

> **Destination Permission ≠ Universal Route Permission**

> **Ownership ≠ Unlimited Contextual Sovereignty**

> **Permission to Act ≠ Permission to Delegate**

> **Credential Transfer ≠ Delegation Authority**

> **Machine-Readable Permission ≠ Machine-Determined Legitimacy**

> **Permission Representation ≠ Permission Justification**

## 5. Permission claim

A consequential permission claim may be represented as:

**PermissionClaim = <Participant, Action, Target/Context, Function, Basis, RuleSource, Consent, Path, State, Conditions, Start, Expiry/Termination, DelegationScope, Provenance, Status>**

The representation is extensible.

Unknown fields remain UNKNOWN where material.

## 6. Multidimensional action scope

Permission may separately concern approach, traverse, enter, remain, occupy, observe, interact, use, inspect, record, modify, remove, deliver, retrieve, supervise, exclude or authorise.

> **One Granted Action ≠ All Actions Granted**

Permission may also be path-, purpose-, participant-, role-, relationship-, consent-, state-, time- and rule-source-specific.

## 7. Rule provenance and legitimacy

Record the source of a material permission basis where practicable: law, steward rule, contract, consent/relationship, local convention, emergency necessity or another legitimate source.

> **Rule Content ≠ Rule Source**

FPA can represent a declared rule without deciding that the rule is legitimate.

Where legitimacy or precedence is disputed, route externally.

## 8. Explicit and implied permission

Explicit permission may be represented directly.

Implied permission may be represented only as a bounded contextual claim supported by an applicable local source/evaluation space.

Candidate evidence may include:
- published local rule;
- stable practice;
- communicated convention;
- prior consistent interaction;
- another externally recognised source.

For consequential implied claims, preserve provenance and confidence.

Explicit markings/restrictions may narrow an otherwise applicable convention subject to external legitimacy.

> **Local Convention ≠ Universal Permission**

## 9. Nested and path-specific permission

> **Parent Permission ≠ Automatic Child Permission**

A participant may be permitted to reach a destination without permission to use every possible route or enter every nested context.

Permission can narrow as privacy, sensitivity, risk or functional specificity increases.

## 10. Function boundary

Function-derived permission depends on the legitimate function actually supplied.

Where consequential or contested, preserve the function definition/source and uncertainty.

> **Actor Assertion ≠ Self-Authorised Function Expansion**

Materially unresolved function or competence predicates should produce UNKNOWN/REVIEW rather than automatic expansion.

Competence/qualification definitions remain external domain inputs.

## 11. State, time, revocation and termination

Permission may change with context state.

Past permission does not establish present permission.

A permission record should identify expiry, revocation or termination conditions where material.

> **Function Completion Should Normally Terminate Function-Derived Permission**

> **Context Termination Should Normally Terminate Context-Derived Permission Unless Another Legitimate Basis Persists**

Technical credentials remaining active after termination are stale capability, not continuing permission.

## 12. Delegation

Permission to act does not automatically include permission to delegate.

Delegation requires its own legitimate basis and scope.

Credential sharing, account sharing or impersonation cannot itself create delegation authority and may independently violate security rules.

## 13. Emergency-bounded permission

A credible emergency may justify minimum necessary emergency access where supported by legitimate emergency architecture.

> **Emergency Need ≠ Unlimited Emergency Authority**

Record the emergency basis and termination/transition condition where practicable.

Authorised handover may terminate or transform the original emergency permission. Continued action requires a continuing or new basis.

Emergency access does not create later ordinary permission.

## 14. Permission conflict

FPA represents conflict but does not manufacture universal precedence.

Minimum conflict record should preserve:
- conflicting claims;
- rule/basis provenance;
- affected action/context;
- uncertainty;
- known legitimate resolution route;
- interim state.

If no legitimate route is known, preserve UNKNOWN/CONFLICT.

For unresolved high-consequence or irreversible action, do not treat the most permissive claim as controlling merely because it enables action. Avoid irreversible action where reasonably possible while routing legitimate review.

This is a bounded safety posture, not a universal precedence rule.

## 15. Machine/normative divergence

Technical ACLs, credentials, badges or physical accessibility may diverge from supported normative permission.

> **More Permissive Technical State ≠ Authority**

Where divergence is material, flag and route remediation to the legitimate owner. Interim restriction should be proportionate to consequence and confidence rather than automatically maximally restrictive.

## 16. Proportional formalisation

FPA must not turn ordinary social life into continuous permission administration.

Use lightweight contextual judgement where consequence and contestability are low.

Richer formal records become more justified as consequence, contestability, automation, cross-context reach, persistence, irreversibility or audit need increases.

No universal numerical threshold is created.

## 17. Multidimensional outputs

Permission states may coexist across different dimensions/actions.

Candidate outputs include PERMITTED, PERMITTED-WITH-CONDITIONS, NOT-PERMITTED, EXPLICITLY-PROHIBITED, EXPIRED, REVOKED, EMERGENCY-BOUNDED, CONFLICTING-CLAIMS, REQUIRES-ADDITIONAL-CONSENT, REQUIRES-HIGHER-ORDER-REVIEW and UNKNOWN/INSUFFICIENT-BASIS.

These are not a universal precedence ladder.

## 18. Relationship to Contextual Wrapper Architecture

CWA asks what rules/expectations/interfaces apply in a bounded context.

FPA asks who may perform which action there and on what legitimate basis.

> **Context Description ≠ Permission Relationship**

CWA may supply context; it does not decide FPA permission merely by describing it.

## 19. Relationship to Minimum Necessary Capability

MNC asks what capability is justified by function.

FPA asks whether a participant is normatively permitted to perform a particular action in context.

> **Capability ≠ Permission**

Capability minimisation and permission minimisation may reinforce one another without becoming the same architecture.

## 20. Rights, privacy, safe spaces and consent

FPA consumes higher-order rights, privacy, safe-space and valid-consent constraints from their legitimate owners.

It does not manufacture consent or make ownership unlimited sovereignty.

Nested private/protected contexts may require stronger or more specific permission than their parent context.

## 21. Emergency and authority boundary

FPA does not create emergency powers, jurisdiction or general authority.

It represents the permission consequence of a legitimate externally supplied basis.

## 22. ESCP

> **No Permission Basis Found ≠ Proof No Basis Exists**

> **Represented Permission State ≠ Complete Normative Reality**

Where incomplete evaluation could materially change a consequential decision, preserve uncertainty or route further resolution.

## 23. Failure modes

Guard against:
- capability laundering;
- ownership/role/function laundering;
- consent laundering;
- implied-permission laundering;
- permission creep;
- emergency creep;
- inheritance/path errors;
- stale permission;
- delegation laundering;
- credential/delegation conflation;
- rule-source collapse;
- machine-legibility laundering;
- excessive bureaucracy;
- hidden discrimination;
- provenance loss;
- actor-defined function expansion.

FPA should be weakened, revised or rejected where evidence shows it creates impractical bureaucracy, reduces legitimate autonomy, encodes one culture as universal, creates irresolvable nested ambiguity, encourages excessive control, or is outperformed by simpler domain mechanisms without loss of required safeguards.
