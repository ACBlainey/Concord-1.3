# Protected Information Transfer Across Civilisational Boundaries — Protocol Development 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / INTERCIVILISATIONAL PROTOCOL / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Parent:** Concord Civilisational Wrapper procedural architecture  
**Interfaces:** Rights Translation and Recognition; CIBB; CWA; Non-Transitive Contextual Dependency Chains; Protection-State Transition Authority; MKA; MNC; BTA

## 1. Purpose

Two civilisations may classify the same information differently.

Civilisation A may treat object D as protected participant information.

Civilisation B may regard equivalent information as public or unrestricted.

Interoperability requires a route that does not silently impose A's entire internal regime on B or erase A's protection at the boundary.

## 2. Core principle

> **Cross-Boundary Transfer Requires A Legitimate Interface State; It Does Not Automatically Select Either Side's Entire Protection Regime.**

This follows the wrapper principle:

> **Standardise the interface; preserve contextual sovereignty.**

## 3. Transfer is a new decision object

Permission to use D inside A does not automatically include permission to transfer D to B.

Permission to receive D inside B does not establish A's authority to send it.

Therefore:

**Domestic Use Authority != Export Authority**

**Import Permission != Source Disclosure Authority**

## 4. Transfer envelope

A proposed protected-information transfer should represent, proportionate to consequence:
- source object/projection;
- sending context;
- receiving context;
- purpose;
- sender authority;
- recipient authority/standing;
- minimum necessary payload;
- recipient protection commitment where required;
- onward disclosure;
- retention;
- correlation/recombination conditions;
- permitted decisions/uses;
- expiry/review;
- dispute route;
- provenance;
- unresolved classification differences.

## 5. Minimum common interface

The parties need not agree on the philosophical basis of privacy.

They may agree operationally:

`D may be transferred for Purpose P under Conditions C`

while preserving:

`A believes protection basis X`

`B believes protection basis Y`

`X/Y remain unresolved`

This is the information analogue of wrapper non-inference.

> **Operational Information Agreement != Convergence Of Rights Theory**

## 6. Recipient-side lower protection

If B normally permits broader use than A, transfer can still be conditioned on a bounded receiving wrapper.

If B will not or cannot satisfy the minimum conditions required for legitimate export, A may:
- refuse transfer;
- send a narrower projection;
- use PLE/query-to-data;
- provide aggregate output;
- use a trusted intermediary;
- negotiate a different bounded protocol.

> **Interoperability Need != Duty To Export Protected Source Data**

## 7. Recipient-side higher protection

If B applies stronger protection than A, B may impose its own legitimate receiving conditions.

A's lower domestic classification does not entitle A to demand that B lower its internal protection.

## 8. Onward transfer

B's legitimate receipt does not automatically authorise transfer B -> C.

> **Cross-Civilisational Receipt != Transitive Export Authority**

Each consequential onward hop requires its own legitimate basis or an explicitly authorised bounded chain.

## 9. Purpose change

Transfer for research does not automatically authorise policing, commercial profiling, employment classification or unrelated intelligence use.

Material purpose change requires renewed legitimate basis.

## 10. Correlation

A may safely disclose D in isolation while B possesses auxiliary datasets that make D identifying or more sensitive.

Therefore transfer review should consider recipient-context recombination where reasonably foreseeable.

> **Source-Safe Projection != Necessarily Destination-Safe Projection**

## 11. Local query alternative

Where B needs an answer rather than the source:

`B Query -> A Protected Evaluation -> Minimum Result -> B`

may be preferable to source transfer.

This preserves sovereignty and reduces disclosure.

## 12. Protection disagreement

If A and B disagree whether D is protected, the wrapper should preserve the disagreement rather than fabricate consensus.

Candidate states:
- MUTUALLY_ACCEPTED_TRANSFER;
- CONDITIONAL_TRANSFER;
- PROJECTION_ONLY;
- QUERY_ONLY;
- DISPUTED_PROTECTION;
- TRANSFER_REFUSED;
- REVIEW_REQUIRED.

## 13. Participant legibility

Where a participant is materially affected, the wrapper should make material transfer conditions sufficiently legible for meaningful choice where choice is applicable.

Security/confidentiality may limit disclosure, but hidden cross-boundary repurposing should not be the default.

## 14. Breach

If B breaches agreed handling:
- actual exposure state should be recorded;
- future transfers may change;
- successor protection/remedy may apply;
- dispute/escalation route activates;
- the breach does not retroactively prove every prior transfer illegitimate.

## 15. No extraterritorial sovereignty by metadata

Attaching A's classification label to D does not itself create unlimited A authority inside B.

The enforceable/legitimate interface arises from the actual wrapper agreement, applicable rights and authority.

> **Source Label != Extraterritorial Sovereignty**

Likewise, stripping A's label does not erase agreed obligations.

## 16. Central rule

> **Information May Cross A Civilisational Boundary Without Either Its Protection Or The Receiving Civilisation's Sovereignty Being Silently Erased; The Transfer Itself Must Be A Bounded, Explicitly Governed Interoperability State.**
