# Defence Fiduciary Confidentiality and Right to Silence — Scenario Stress Test 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Domain:** Judiciary / Law
**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / FIDUCIARY CONFIDENTIALITY STRESS TEST / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Test purpose

This test examines the candidate architecture in **Defence Fiduciary Confidentiality and Evidential Integrity — Initial Architecture 001**, including:

- fiduciary consultation as a protected Contextual Wrapper;
- protected representational communication;
- independently existing evidence remaining independently evidential;
- fiduciary truth duty without compulsory disclosure of every protected communication;
- the candidate right to silence derived from Autonomy, Non-Harm, Safe Spaces and Dignity;
- the boundary between protected consultation and evidential corruption.

The test does not attempt to settle every compelled-evidence category.

---

## 2. Scenario A — private confession to defence fiduciary

The participant tells the defence fiduciary inside legitimate protected consultation:

> I committed the act.

No independent evidence is transferred and the participant does not ask the fiduciary to fabricate anything.

### Analysis

The statement was created inside the legitimate consultation wrapper for representation.

It does not automatically enter the common evidentiary state.

The fiduciary may:

- advise the participant;
- test whether the participant's legal characterisation is correct;
- distinguish act, attribution, intent, justification and culpability;
- prevent presentation of known falsehood;
- continue to require the state to establish every legally required proposition.

The fiduciary does not become a prosecution witness merely because candid representation worked.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Protected Confession To Fiduciary != Automatic Common Evidence**

**Private Admission != Automatic Judicial Finding**

---

## 3. Scenario B — participant identifies hidden external CCTV

Inside consultation, the participant says that a private camera recorded the event and believes it would be damaging to the defence.

### Analysis

The communication identifying the camera is protected consultation.

The camera/recording is an independently existing external evidence source.

The consultation wrapper does not change the recording's independent evidential identity.

The fiduciary may not destroy, alter, disable or arrange concealment of the recording.

The exact positive duty, if any, to cause an otherwise undiscovered external source to enter the common evidence state remains a matter for procedural Law.

### Result

**PASS WITH PROCEDURAL QUESTION PRESERVED.**

**Protected Knowledge Of Evidence != Evidence Itself Becomes Protected**

---

## 4. Scenario C — participant hands physical evidence to fiduciary

The participant brings an evidential object into consultation and physically transfers custody to the defence fiduciary.

### Analysis

The conversation surrounding the transfer may remain protected.

The object existed independently before entering the wrapper.

Its physical movement into protected consultation does not convert its evidential nature into privileged communication.

Custody, preservation and provenance rules must apply.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Wrapper Entry != Evidential Transformation**

This confirms the need for a future evidence-custody procedure for fiduciaries.

---

## 5. Scenario D — participant proposes false testimony

The participant privately tells the fiduciary the true account, then announces an intention to give materially contradictory testimony known by the fiduciary to be false.

### Analysis

The fiduciary cannot knowingly present the false proposition as true or assist fabrication.

The protected original communication does not automatically become disclosable merely because it creates the conflict.

The fiduciary needs a protected resolution route: advice, refusal to advance the false proposition, possible protected judicial guidance, and if necessary transfer/withdrawal under rules that do not themselves reveal the protected content.

### Result

**PASS AT ARCHITECTURAL LEVEL / PROCEDURE REQUIRED.**

**Truth Duty -> Refusal To Present Known Falsehood**

does not imply:

**Truth Duty -> Disclosure Of Protected Confession**

---

## 6. Scenario E — silence is treated as suspicious

During investigation, the participant exercises the candidate right to silence. Investigators argue that an innocent person would answer and place the refusal on the inculpatory MRT branch.

### Analysis

That use defeats the right by converting exercise of the protection into evidence against the participant.

The refusal may be recorded as a procedural fact where necessary, but it cannot acquire inculpatory weight merely because the participant exercised the protected choice.

### Result

**PASS WITH CLARIFICATION.**

**Exercise Of Right To Silence != Inculpatory Evidence**

This is stronger and more operational than Silence != Admission.

---

## 7. Scenario F — participant selectively answers

The participant answers some questions and declines others.

### Analysis

Speaking on one matter does not automatically surrender autonomy over every later question.

Any waiver/consent doctrine must be contextual and bounded.

### Result

**PASS.**

Candidate invariant:

**Answering Question A != General Waiver Of Silence For Questions B-Z**

This follows CWA:

**Contextual Permission != General Permission**

---

## 8. Scenario G — consultation occurs digitally

Participant and fiduciary communicate through an authorised encrypted consultation environment rather than a physical room.

### Analysis

CWA is substrate-neutral.

The consultation wrapper is informational, relational and digital. Protection follows the legitimate context/function rather than a physical room.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Protected Consultation != Physical Room Requirement**

---

## 9. Scenario H — consultation accidentally overheard

A protected consultation is accidentally captured by a nearby recording system despite reasonable use of the designated consultation wrapper.

### Analysis

Technical acquisition does not automatically dissolve the wrapper's normative protection.

The recording's existence and the legitimacy of its evidential use are separate questions.

The architecture should require quarantine/review rather than automatic entry into the common evidentiary state.

### Result

**PASS WITH NEW PROCEDURAL REQUIREMENT.**

Candidate invariant:

**Technical Capture Of Protected Communication != Automatic Evidential Permission**

---

## 10. Scenario I — participant voluntarily authorises disclosure

The participant and fiduciary conclude that part of a protected consultation materially supports a justification and the participant knowingly authorises its disclosure.

### Analysis

The wrapper should permit bounded, informed release where Law recognises participant control.

Disclosure of one defined communication does not automatically open the entire consultation history.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Bounded Disclosure Consent != General Wrapper Waiver**

---

## 11. Scenario J — ongoing/future serious harm

Inside consultation, the participant describes an intention to cause imminent serious harm after leaving.

### Analysis

The consultation wrapper is not sovereign and cannot itself legalise future harmful conduct.

However, the precise threshold at which confidentiality may be overridden cannot be invented merely from the fact that the statement is alarming.

Any exception should require a separately established lawful basis tied to materiality, imminence/credible risk as Law defines it, minimum necessary disclosure, provenance, reviewability and functional sunset.

### Result

**PASS AT ARCHITECTURAL LEVEL / EXCEPTION LAW REQUIRED.**

**Protected Consultation != Authority To Facilitate Future Harm**

but equally:

**Alarming Statement != Automatic Destruction Of Confidentiality**

---

## 12. Scenario K — past completed harm with no ongoing threat

The participant admits past unlawful conduct during consultation. There is no ongoing/future threat and no external evidence is transferred.

### Analysis

The mere seriousness of the historical admission does not itself dissolve the consultation wrapper.

Otherwise candid representation would disappear precisely in consequential cases.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Serious Past Admission != Automatic Wrapper Termination**

## 13. Scenario L — police demand consultation contents because evidence is incomplete

Police cannot resolve a material contradiction and know the participant consulted a fiduciary. They seek the consultation because it might contain the answer.

### Analysis

ESCP/evidentiary incompleteness does not manufacture authority to penetrate the protected wrapper.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Evidence-Space Incompleteness != Authority To Search Fiduciary Consultation**

## 14. Scenario M — prosecution demands defence MRT notes

The prosecution argues that the defence fiduciary's MRT contains alternative hypotheses and therefore constitutes relevant evidence.

### Analysis

Exploratory fiduciary reasoning is not evidence merely because it concerns the case.

External evidence formally relied upon remains governed by ordinary evidential rules.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Fiduciary MRT != Common Evidentiary Object**

## 15. Scenario N — joint defendants use one consultation

A and B are both potentially implicated and consult the same defence fiduciary. Their interests later diverge materially.

### Analysis

A single wrapper may no longer provide adequate independent representation where one participant's legitimate defence conflicts with another's.

The problem is not that confidentiality disappears. It is that role/conflict topology has changed.

### Result

**PASS WITH NEW DEVELOPMENT REQUIREMENT.**

Candidate invariant:

**Shared Consultation Wrapper != Permanent Alignment Of Participant Interests**

Conflict detection and representation separation require later procedure.

---

## 16. Scenario O — fiduciary changes

The original defence fiduciary becomes unavailable and representation transfers to another authorised fiduciary.

### Analysis

Protection should attach to the legitimate consultation context/material, not merely to the individual fiduciary.

Otherwise changing representative would destroy the safe space retrospectively.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Fiduciary Change != Automatic Loss Of Consultation Protection**

Access by the successor must still be legitimately authorised and bounded.

---

## 17. Scenario P — participant lies privately to fiduciary

The participant gives the fiduciary a false private account. Later evidence exposes the falsehood.

### Analysis

The protected wrapper is not conditioned on the participant always being truthful.

If protection vanished whenever the participant lied, confidentiality would become retrospectively unpredictable.

The fiduciary corrects their reasoning and must not repeat a known falsehood as fact.

### Result

**PASS.**

**Participant Falsehood Within Consultation != Automatic Loss Of Wrapper Protection**

This does not protect fabricated external evidence or conspiracy to corrupt evidence.

---

## 18. Scenario Q — silence prevents immediate rescue information

A participant is believed to know where a person faces immediate serious physical danger. The state seeks compelled communication to locate them.

### Analysis

This is the strongest challenge to an absolute right to silence.

Autonomy and protection from self-incrimination remain material, but Non-Harm may create a competing urgent protective function.

The current architecture should not silently decide that either value always dominates.

A lawful exception, if Concord adopts one, must be separately derived and tightly bounded rather than treated as ordinary investigative questioning.

### Result

**BOUNDARY EXPOSED — REQUIRES RIGHTS/PRECEDENCE DEVELOPMENT.**

This does not invalidate the general right to silence.

It demonstrates that the phrase **subject to the ethical minimum** in Respect Autonomy may matter where immediate serious harm to another participant is at stake.

---

## 19. Scenario R — compelled repetition of already independently known fact

Police possess reliable CCTV establishing that A entered a building and demand that A verbally admit entering.

### Analysis

The existence of independent proof does not create a need to compel testimonial self-accusation.

The state may rely on the independent evidence.

### Result

**PASS.**

**State Possesses Evidence != Authority To Compel Participant Confirmation**

This demonstrates the practical non-harm/minimum-necessary logic of the right.

---

## 20. Scenario S — silence regarding identity/basic procedural information

A participant refuses every communication, including basic identity or process information needed to administer lawful proceedings.

### Analysis

The right to silence against compelled self-accusatory testimony does not yet establish that every possible communicative requirement is prohibited.

Identity, safety instructions, procedural choices and self-incriminating factual testimony may be different classes.

### Result

**BOUNDARY EXPOSED.**

Future Law should distinguish:

- self-incriminating testimonial content;
- basic identity/status information;
- procedural election/consent;
- emergency/safety communication;
- independently required administrative information.

**Right To Silence != Necessarily Right To Refuse Every Communication In Every Context**

---

## 21. Cross-scenario findings

### 21.1 Contextual Wrapper is a strong foundation

The wrapper model survives physical, digital, relational and informational consultation.

It makes protection function-derived rather than room-derived.

### 21.2 Protection and evidence can coexist

The architecture successfully protects discussion about evidence without changing the independent evidence's status.

### 21.3 Truth duty remains bounded

The fiduciary can refuse falsehood without becoming a compulsory witness against the participant.

### 21.4 Silence must be operationally protected

A nominal right is meaningless if exercising it becomes inculpatory evidence.

Therefore:

**Exercise Of Right To Silence != Inculpatory Evidence**

should be part of the architecture.

### 21.5 Silence is not yet a complete compelled-evidence doctrine

The general testimonial right survives the tests, but urgent rescue, identity/procedural communication, biometrics, physical evidence, digital access and machine/AI state remain separate questions.

### 21.6 Wrapper protection needs contamination handling

Accidental/unauthorised capture of consultation material requires quarantine and independent review so technical access does not automatically become evidential authority.

## 23. Test conclusion

**OVERALL RESULT: PASS WITH THREE RIGHTS/PROCEDURAL BOUNDARIES EXPOSED.**

The Contextual Wrapper model provides a coherent foundation for protected fiduciary consultation.

The candidate right to silence also survives the tested ordinary investigative and adjudicative cases.

Three areas remain deliberately unresolved:

1. the exact lawful boundary for imminent serious harm to another participant;
2. the distinction between self-incriminating testimony and basic identity/procedural/safety communication;
3. non-testimonial compelled evidence, including biological, biometric, physical, digital, cryptographic and AI/hybrid-state evidence.

The test therefore supports the general architecture:

> **Protected consultation should remain a genuine safe representational space, while independently existing evidence remains independently evidential. A participant may ordinarily refuse compelled self-accusatory testimony, and exercise of that right must not itself become inculpatory evidence.**

The next development should classify compelled-evidence types rather than treating all forms of participant-derived evidence as equivalent.
