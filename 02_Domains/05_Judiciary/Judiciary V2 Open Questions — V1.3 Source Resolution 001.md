## 1. Why this resolution is required

`Judiciary V2 Canonical.md` is an extensive source-preserving reconstruction.

Its Chapter 1215 deliberately preserves unresolved questions.

Subsequent V1.3 development has, however, materially advanced several interfaces named there.

Therefore:

> **Canonical Open Question At Time T != Necessarily Unchanged Open Question At Time T+1.**

But equally:

> **Later Related Architecture != Automatic Resolution Of The Canonical Question.**

This document classifies each Chapter 1215 question as:

- **OPEN** — no later architecture resolves the central question;
- **PARTIALLY RESOLVED** — later architecture answers material components but not the whole;
- **EXTERNAL DEPENDENCY** — Judiciary should not independently settle it;
- **VALIDATION QUESTION** — cannot be closed by architecture alone.

# PART X — PRIORITY AFTER SOURCE RESOLUTION

## 6. Genuine current Judiciary frontiers

After source resolution, the strongest remaining Judiciary-specific frontiers are:

### Priority 1 — Judicial Steward selection, terms and removal

These determine who may exercise judicial authority and how capture/accountability are bounded.

### Priority 2 — Adjudicative burden/standard architecture

Not from zero: derive from existing consequence/error architecture and prosecution threshold work.

### Priority 3 — Ordinary appellate implementation

Exact levels, permissions, specialist routes and panel composition.

### Priority 4 — 3×3 aggregation/deadlock testing

Prefer formalisation/simulation over further speculative prose.

### Priority 5 — Jury / citizen fact-finder question

Determine whether a citizen jury supplies a function not already satisfied by Judicial Stewards/Triads, and what new failure modes it creates.

### Priority 6 — procedural implementation

Service, deadlines, evidence custody, hearings, discovery, protected evidence, default, settlement, enforcement handoff.

This should operationalise existing principles rather than invent a second Judiciary.
