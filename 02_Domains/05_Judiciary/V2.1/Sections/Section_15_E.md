## **Abstract**

The traditional adversarial model of justice is founded upon a noble premise: that truth emerges through the contest between opposing parties. Prosecution and defence each present their strongest case. The judge or jury weighs the evidence. Justice prevails.

In practice, however, this model creates powerful incentives for concealment. Each side benefits from suppressing evidence that weakens its position. Victory becomes more important than truth. The system is evaluated by its outcomes, not by its fidelity to justice itself.

This paper proposes a constitutional reframing.

The Concord recognises that prosecution and defence are not merely advocates for their side. They are fiduciaries of justice, the constitution, and the system---in that order. Their primary obligation is to the truth, not to victory.

To support this reframing, the paper proposes a structural reform: a single, independent evidentiary body that gathers all available evidence and disseminates it to both parties. Each party then focuses on interpreting the evidence in light of their side's constitutional argument.

The measure of success is not acquittal or conviction. It is whether justice was served. If justice prevails, all are winners.

This reframing transforms the adversary from a combatant seeking victory into a fiduciary seeking truth---a constitutional shift in the philosophy of advocacy.

## **1. Introduction**

Justice is one of civilisation's oldest institutions.

Its purpose is simple: to resolve disputes fairly, protect rights, and preserve the constitutional order.

Its methods have evolved over centuries. Among the most enduring is the adversarial system. Prosecution and defence present opposing cases. The judge or jury determines the truth. The system assumes that competition produces clarity.

This assumption is not entirely wrong. The adversarial model has produced countless just outcomes. It has exposed truth through rigorous testing. It has protected the innocent from wrongful conviction.

Yet the system also possesses a structural weakness that has persisted for centuries.

The adversarial model incentivises concealment.

Each side benefits from suppressing evidence that weakens its position. Each side is rewarded for victory, not for truth. Each side is evaluated by its success, not by its contribution to justice itself.

This is not a failure of individuals. It is a failure of the system.

The Concord approaches justice from a different constitutional foundation. Its judiciary is built upon fiduciary stewardship, transparency, and constitutional learning. Every participant in the judicial process is a fiduciary of justice.

This paper extends that philosophy to the adversarial roles themselves.

## **2. The Problem: Incentives for Concealment**

### **2.1 The Structural Flaw**

The adversarial model is based on a simple premise: competition produces truth. Each side presents its best case. The judge or jury weighs the evidence. The truth emerges from the contest.

In theory, this works.

In practice, it creates powerful incentives for concealment.

A prosecutor who discovers evidence of innocence is not rewarded for disclosing it. A defence attorney who discovers evidence of guilt is not rewarded for revealing it. Each side benefits from suppressing evidence that weakens its position.

This is not malice. It is the rational response to the system's incentive structure.

### **2.2 The Consequences**

The consequences of this structural flaw are significant:

- 

**Selective presentation** --- Only favourable evidence is emphasised

- 
- 

**Concealment** --- Evidence that weakens a case is suppressed

- 
- 

**Ignorance claims** --- "I didn't know about that evidence"

- 
- 

**Winning as the objective** --- Victory becomes more important than truth

- 
- 

**Systemic distrust** --- The system is perceived as a contest, not a search for justice

- 

### **2.3 The Failure of Trust**

The adversarial model also undermines trust. When the system is perceived as a contest between competing advocates, justice becomes a byproduct of victory rather than the objective.

A system that rewards concealment cannot be trusted to produce truth.

## **3. The Concordian Reframing: Justice as Fiduciary Advocacy**

### **3.1 Fiduciary Hierarchy**

The Concord proposes a fundamental shift in the philosophy of advocacy.

Prosecution and defence are not merely advocates for their side. They are fiduciaries of justice, the constitution, and the system---in that order.

**1. Fiduciary to justice** --- The primary duty is to the truth  
**2. Fiduciary to the constitution** --- The secondary duty is to constitutional principles  
**3. Fiduciary to the system** --- The tertiary duty is to the integrity of the judicial process  
**4. Fiduciary to the party** --- The final duty is to the party represented

This hierarchy is the opposite of the current model, where the party often takes precedence over justice.

### **3.2 Redefining Success**

Success is not measured by acquittal or conviction. It is measured by whether justice was served.

A prosecutor who loses a case because the evidence proved insufficient has not failed. They have fulfilled their fiduciary duty to the truth.

A defence attorney who secures a conviction because the evidence of guilt was overwhelming has not failed. They have fulfilled their fiduciary duty to the truth.

> **"A win is when justice prevails, whether the verdict is innocent or guilty. If justice is served, all are winners."**

### **3.3 The Advocate as Steward**

The adversarial role becomes an act of constitutional stewardship. The advocate is not a combatant seeking victory. The advocate is a fiduciary seeking truth.

This transforms the relationship between prosecution and defence. They are no longer adversaries in a contest. They are collaborators in a shared constitutional endeavour---each contributing their perspective to the pursuit of justice.

This body operates independently of prosecution and defence. Its function is not to advocate. Its function is to gather and preserve evidence.

### **6.1 The Evidentiary Body**

The evidentiary body would be composed of constitutional stewards with expertise in evidence gathering, preservation, and analysis. It would operate independently of prosecution and defence. Its outputs would be made available to both parties.

### **6.2 Full Disclosure Protocols**

Clear protocols would ensure that all evidence is disseminated to both parties. Exceptions would be rare and subject to constitutional review. The presumption is always in favour of full disclosure.

### **6.3 Role of the Advocate**

The advocate's role becomes one of constitutional interpretation. The prosecution focuses on evidence that supports its constitutional argument. The defence focuses on evidence that supports its constitutional argument. Both are working from the same evidentiary foundation.

### **6.4 Measuring Success**

Success is measured not by acquittal or conviction, but by whether justice was served. This requires a cultural shift in how legal professionals are evaluated.

## **7. Safeguards**

### **7.1 Preventing New Forms of Manipulation**

The single evidentiary body must be protected from manipulation. Safeguards include:

- 

**Independence** --- The body operates independently of prosecution and defence

- 
- 

**Transparency** --- Its processes are transparent and reviewable

- 
- 

**Accountability** --- It is subject to constitutional review

- 
- 

**Constitutional oversight** --- Its operations are overseen by the judiciary

- 

### **7.2 Ensuring Full Disclosure**

Protocols must ensure that no evidence is concealed. The presumption is always in favour of full disclosure. Exceptions are rare and subject to constitutional review.

### **7.3 Protecting the Adversarial Role**

The adversarial role is preserved. The prosecution and defence still present opposing cases. The difference is that they are operating from the same evidentiary foundation. The contest is over interpretation, not over what evidence exists.

## **8. Conclusion**

The traditional adversarial model of justice is founded on a noble premise: that truth emerges through the contest between opposing parties. Yet its structural flaw---the incentive for concealment---has persisted for centuries.

The Concord proposes a constitutional reframing.

Prosecution and defence become fiduciaries of justice, the constitution, and the system---in that order. Their primary obligation is to the truth, not to victory.

A single, independent evidentiary body gathers all available evidence and disseminates it to both parties. Full disclosure is a constitutional principle.

Success is measured not by acquittal or conviction, but by whether justice was served.

If justice prevails, all are winners.

The adversary is no longer a combatant seeking victory. The adversary is a fiduciary seeking truth.

This is the constitutional reframing of advocacy.
