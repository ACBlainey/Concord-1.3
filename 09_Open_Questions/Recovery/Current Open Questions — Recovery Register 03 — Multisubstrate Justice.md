# Current Open Questions — Recovery Register 03
## Multisubstrate Justice, Punitive Purpose and Personal Currency

**Status:** CURRENT OPEN-QUESTION RECOVERY REGISTER / ACTIVE RESEARCH MAP / NOT CANONICAL ARCHITECTURE  
**Framework version:** Concord V1.2  
**Purpose:** Preserve a small group of explicit live questions found during the final originator-survival audit that were only represented indirectly or too broadly in the first two current Open Questions registers.

# 1. Why This Recovery File Exists

The final survival pass found that the current register correctly preserves the broad question of substrate-neutral jurisprudence, and separately preserves Mental Integrity, artificial temporal experience, identity transformation and responsibility. However, several narrower questions created by the 9–10 September development cycle remained only implicit inside those broader headings.

Because the purpose of the cleanup is to make the historical originator files removable from active V1.2 without losing substantive research objects, these questions are retained explicitly here.

> **A broad surviving domain does not count as full survival if an independently testable sub-question has disappeared inside it.**

---

# 2. Purpose of Civil Consequence

## R3-JR-01 — What Is Punishment or Civil Consequence Actually For?

> **Which purposes can legitimately justify civil consequences after wrongdoing: protection, accountability, restoration, deterrence, rehabilitation, proportional consequence, restitution, incapacitation, retribution, or some other function?**

**Status:** MAJOR JURISPRUDENTIAL QUESTION / OPEN  
**Arises from:** Judiciary validation; multisubstrate punishment; temporal-experience research.  
**Importance:** A system cannot determine substrate-neutral consequences until it distinguishes the legitimate objective from accidental features of existing human punishment systems.

The question should not begin with:

> **What is the AI equivalent of prison?**

It should begin with:

> **What legitimate civil function is the consequence intended to perform?**

---

## R3-JR-02 — Deliberately Imposed Suffering

> **Is deliberately imposed suffering ever a legitimate civil objective, or may suffering occur only as an incidental and minimised consequence of some independently legitimate protective, restorative or accountability function?**

**Status:** OPEN / HIGH-RISK ETHICAL AND JUDICIAL QUESTION  
**Interface:** Non-Harm; Mental Integrity; proportionality; Judiciary; multisubstrate equality.

This must remain distinct from the question of whether restrictions may sometimes be necessary. A restriction can be justified by protection or accountability without making suffering itself the objective.

# 3. Substrate-Neutral Consequence

## R3-JR-03 — Capability Restriction

> **When is restriction of a participant's capability a legitimate civil consequence, what capability may be restricted, for what function, for how long, and under what review and restoration conditions?**

**Status:** OPEN / AUTHORITY–JUDICIARY INTERFACE  
**Importance:** Artificial and hybrid participants may possess capabilities whose restriction is more analogous to limiting access, professional authority, movement, tools or operational permissions than to human incarceration.

Capability restriction must not silently become ownership, indefinite incapacitation or modification of the participant's mind.

---

## R3-JR-04 — Cognitive Modification as Sanction

> **Can cognitive modification, retraining, memory alteration, preference alteration or model modification ever be a legitimate civil sanction?**

**Status:** OPEN / MENTAL-INTEGRITY INTERFACE / HIGH RISK  
**Arises from:** multisubstrate punishment; Mental Integrity; AI self-modification.

Core distinction:

`Behaviour Restriction ≠ Cognitive Modification`

The existence of a technically effective modification does not establish legitimate authority to alter the participant.

# 4. Personal Currency

## R3-JR-05 — Personal Currency and Meaningful Civil Burden

> **What resources, opportunities, relationships, capacities, freedoms or forms of access carry meaningful value for different participants, and can civil systems account for those differences without constructing invasive preference-surveillance systems?**

**Status:** EXPLORATORY CROSS-DOMAIN QUESTION / OPEN  
**Arises from:** multisubstrate equality; artificial temporal experience; punishment and justice; civil provision.

The provisional idea of **Personal Currency** asks whether proportionality should be assessed partly in terms of what a consequence actually removes, restricts or burdens for the participant rather than assuming that identical external treatment has equivalent significance.

It must be tested against privacy, manipulation, preference uncertainty, preference change, strategic misrepresentation and the danger of converting personal values into a civil scoring system.

---

# 5. Branching and Liability

## R3-JR-06 — Liability After Branching

> **How should liability transform when a participant branches into multiple later participants that share relevant history but subsequently diverge?**

**Status:** OPEN / IDENTITY–JUDICIARY INTERFACE  
**Core constraint:**

`Shared History ≠ Automatic Shared Current Liability`

This question is narrower than the general identity/succession question because it concerns responsibility for conduct occurring before, during and after branching, including knowledge, benefit, causal participation and later autonomous divergence.
