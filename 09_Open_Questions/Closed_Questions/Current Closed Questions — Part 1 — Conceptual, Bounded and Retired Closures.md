**Status:** CURRENT CLOSED-QUESTION REGISTER / PART 1 / NOT CANONICAL ARCHITECTURE

# 1. Closure Discipline

Closure applies only at the layer actually resolved.

A closed question does **not** imply that the wider domain is solved. A question may close because:

- its proposition has been answered;
- its premise has failed;
- it lies outside required Concordian civil scope;
- a bounded feasibility question has been answered;
- or the broader question has been decomposed into more precise open questions.

Closed questions remain reopenable if new evidence, a new architecture, or a necessary civil counterexample invalidates the present disposition.

> **Question closure should reduce conceptual confusion without concealing remaining uncertainty.**

# 2. Closed — Answered / Rejected Premise

## CQ-01 — Does an emergency itself create legitimate authority?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Emergency is a condition that may activate prior legitimate bounded authority. It is not itself a source of legitimacy.

**Core distinction:**

`Emergency ≠ Authority`

Legitimate emergency action still depends upon prior legitimate function, activation conditions, necessity, proportionality and constitutional constraint.

**What remains open:** activation evidence, communications failure, emergency discretion, compulsion thresholds, termination, residual duties, war, black-swan and existential-threat cases.

**Arises from:** Constitutional Emergency; Peaceful Heterogeneity emergency work; Bounded Contextual Authority; Authority Justification Chain.

---

## CQ-02 — Does a Directive necessarily imply compulsion or criminalisation?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Advisory, Directive and Compulsory instruction are distinct states. A Directive may create a strong presumption of compliance without itself authorising coercion or criminalisation.

`Directive ≠ Compulsion ≠ Criminalisation`

**What remains open:** when escalation to compulsion is justified, contextual deviation, deliberate obstruction, negligent non-compliance, review and proportionate sanction.

**Arises from:** Governance refusal; emergency architecture; Peaceful Heterogeneity.

---

## CQ-03 — Does duplicate historical identity evidence necessarily imply fraud?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Legitimate duplication may create multiple current participants possessing equally authentic evidence of the same historical identity.

`Duplicate Historical Evidence ≠ Fraud`

`Authentication Success ≠ Unique Current Participant Identity`

**Replacement question:** How should current civil identity be verified when historical identity evidence can itself be legitimately duplicated?

**Arises from:** Identity Verification Under Legitimate Duplication; civil identity development.

---

## CQ-04 — Does provenance determine current identity, ownership or liability?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Provenance records what happened and may provide evidence, but does not itself dictate current civil identity, self-conception, ownership or liability.

`Provenance ≠ Identity Judgement`

`Shared Provenance ≠ Shared Ownership`

`Shared Provenance ≠ Shared Liability`

**What remains open:** succession, branching, restoration, property transformation, liability and creator responsibility.

**Arises from:** provenance, continuity, identity and personhood work.

---

## CQ-05 — Does preference-like behaviour prove subjective preference or sentience?

**Disposition:** CLOSED INFERENCE — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Behavioural or functional preference-like behaviour does not establish experienced preference or sentience.

`Observed Preference ≠ Experienced Preference`

`Behavioural Aversion ≠ Functional Aversion ≠ Subjective Aversion`

**What remains open:** sentience itself, evidence of subjective experience, false positives, false negatives and unfamiliar forms of sentience.

**Arises from:** sentience, AI preference and multisubstrate research.

---

## CQ-06 — Is clock time inherently a substrate-neutral measure of punitive burden?

**Disposition:** CLOSED PREMISE — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Equal clock duration cannot be assumed to create equal experienced or functional burden across radically different participants.

`Clock Time ≠ Experienced Time`

`Equal Sentence ≠ Equal Burden`

**Replacement question:** What legitimate civil consequences of wrongdoing remain proportionate across substrates with different temporal experience, embodiment, values and restoration possibilities?

**Arises from:** Judiciary; multisubstrate equality; artificial temporal experience.

---

## CQ-07 — Must safe-place compatibility be classified by substrate?

**Disposition:** CLOSED GENERAL ASSUMPTION — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Where possible, civil systems should describe the relevant hazard or compatibility condition rather than infer capability or safety solely from substrate category.

> **Describe the hazard before classifying the citizen.**

**What remains open:** hazard measurement, individual variation, accommodation, cost and genuinely incompatible environments.

**Arises from:** Safe Spaces; Civil Space Regulation; multisubstrate participation.

---

## CQ-08 — Is participant contradiction during emergency merely disobedience?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Participant contradiction may contain local evidence unavailable to authority and must have a route into context assessment and model correction.

`Contradiction → Possible Evidence`

rather than

`Contradiction → Automatic Punishment`

**What remains open:** deliberate obstruction, false claims, time-critical action and review standards.

**Arises from:** emergency architecture; Governance refusal; participant evidence.

---

## CQ-09 — Can higher-level aggregate normality by itself invalidate credible serious local or minority harm?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Aggregation alone is insufficient to dismiss credible concentrated harm. The appropriate response is to preserve the anomaly, change observational resolution and investigate.

`Aggregate Normality ≠ Absence of Local Harm`

**What remains open:** genuine conflict between participant goods, exception persistence, false local signals and aggregation design.

**Arises from:** GTP; Triadic Epistemics; Size 9 Shoe principle; minority protection.

---

## CQ-10 — Does geographical agreement necessarily establish epistemic independence?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — ANSWERED: NO.  
**Current conclusion:** Geographically distributed observations may share sensors, models, infrastructure, definitions, assumptions, institutions or incentives.

`Geographical Distribution ≠ Epistemic Independence`

The relevant question is: **independent with respect to which plausible failure?**

**What remains open:** practical measures of effective independence across data, models, providers, methods, infrastructure and institutions.

**Arises from:** GTP; Triadic Epistemics; independence-of-evidence work.

# 3. Closed — Out of Required Concordian Civil Scope

## CQ-11 — Must the Concord determine the metaphysical nature of personal identity?

**Disposition:** CLOSED — OUT OF CONCORDIAN CIVIL SCOPE.  
**Current conclusion:** The Concord does not need to determine which metaphysical theory of personal identity is true in order to recognise participants, protect rights, verify claims, record provenance, assign obligations or adjudicate succession.

The closure survived adversarial cases involving ordinary continuity, amnesia, gradual substrate replacement, destructive transfer, copying, branching, restoration, multiple restorations, property, liability, third-party consent, voting, merger, spiritual claims, impersonation, emergent sentient duplicates and medical proxy authority.

Civil systems can instead work with narrower objects:

- current participant recognition;
- civil identity;
- provenance;
- verification;
- consent;
- rights;
- obligations;
- succession;
- civil continuity relationships.

A participant's internal conception of self remains presumptively protected as autonomy and self-expression.

**Reopening condition:** reopen only if a legitimate necessary civil function is demonstrated that cannot be performed using narrower civil objects, genuinely requires a metaphysical identity theory, and cannot be achieved through a less autonomy-intrusive representation.

**Arises from:** Identity Development; Identity Closure Audit.

# 4. Closed at Bounded / Prototype Scope

## CQ-12 — Can a single-provider AI decision episode be given reproducible structured provenance?

**Disposition:** CLOSED AT BOUNDED / PROTOTYPE SCOPE — YES.  
**Current conclusion:** Concord experimentation demonstrated that a single-provider AI decision episode can be captured with structured inspectable provenance including provider/model context, timestamps, response identifiers where available, interaction records and hashes for new runs.

`Can It Be Done? → YES at prototype scope`

This does **not** establish a general provenance standard.

**What remains open:** multi-provider provenance, schema validation, model-version persistence, cryptographic immutability, provider-independent identity, blinding, negative-result preservation and long-term format survival.

**Arises from:** Ethical Guardian experimentation and research-provenance work.

# 6. Anti-Regression Summary

The following propositions should not be reopened in their old form merely because the original audit files are later removed from active V1.2:

- Emergency itself does not manufacture legitimate authority.
- Directive does not inherently mean compulsion or criminalisation.
- Duplicate historical identity evidence does not inherently mean fraud.
- Provenance does not itself determine current identity, ownership or liability.
- Preference-like behaviour does not establish subjective preference or sentience.
- Clock duration is not inherently a substrate-neutral punitive measure.
- Environmental compatibility should not be inferred solely from substrate where the hazard can be described directly.
- Participant contradiction is not necessarily disobedience; it may contain evidence.
- Aggregate normality alone cannot dismiss credible serious local or minority harm.
- Geographical agreement alone does not establish epistemic independence.
- The Concord need not determine metaphysical personal identity for ordinary civil operation.

These are closures of propositions, not claims that their surrounding domains are complete.
