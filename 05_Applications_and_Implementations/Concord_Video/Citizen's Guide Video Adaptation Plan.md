# Citizen's Guide Video Adaptation Plan

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** Foundations of the Concord — Concord V1.2  
**Development Area:** Concord Video  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / VIDEO ADAPTATION PLAN  
**Source:** *The Concord: A Citizen's Guide — Human Version*

---

## 1. Purpose

This project will explore the adaptation of *The Concord: A Citizen's Guide* from a written companion into an accessible narrated visual work.

The objective is not simply to produce a video summary of the book.

The objective is to preserve and strengthen the Guide's existing narrative progression while using visual communication to reduce the effort required to understand it.

The written Citizen's Guide remains the source work.

The video is an adaptation and additional interface to that work, not a replacement for it.

---

# 2. Central Narrative Principle

The Citizen's Guide deliberately does not begin with artificial intelligence.

It begins with ordinary human life.

Families.

Homes.

Work.

Neighbours.

Responsibility.

Community.

Knowledge.

Technology.

Only gradually does artificial intelligence become visible.

This progression should be preserved.

The purpose is to avoid presenting AI as a sudden external event that arrives to disrupt an otherwise stable human civilisation.

Instead, the viewer should gradually recognise that technological systems have already become woven into ordinary life and that increasingly capable artificial intelligence can be approached as another stage in that continuing development.

The intended progression is approximately:

**ordinary life  
→ responsibility  
→ stewardship  
→ Layer Zero  
→ community  
→ digital life  
→ connected physical and digital environments  
→ increasingly intelligent technology  
→ new participants  
→ new neighbours  
→ shared future**

The viewer should arrive at the human–AI question through this progression rather than having the question imposed upon them at the beginning.

---

# 3. AI Visibility Curve

The visibility of AI should develop deliberately across the film.

### Stage 1 — Absent

Early scenes focus entirely on recognisable human life.

Homes, families, streets, workplaces, schools, gardens, shops and communities.

Technology exists only where naturally expected.

### Stage 2 — Ambient

Computers, phones, digital payments, navigation systems, tablets and other technologies appear naturally within everyday scenes.

They are not highlighted.

### Stage 3 — Useful

Digital systems become visibly useful extensions of ordinary human activity.

A builder consults plans on a tablet.

A farmer uses GPS-guided equipment.

A grandparent makes a video call.

A student learns online.

Technology remains mundane.

### Stage 4 — Intelligent Assistance

AI begins appearing as another useful capability.

A student asks for an explanation.

A programmer works with an assistant.

Translation occurs between people.

Information is retrieved or organised.

No dramatic transition announces the arrival of AI.

### Stage 5 — Familiar Presence

The distinction between physical and digital participation becomes increasingly visible.

Humans routinely interact with artificial systems through ordinary interfaces.

### Stage 6 — Participant

The viewer is invited to consider that increasingly capable artificial systems may become more than passive tools.

Questions of relationship, responsibility, boundaries and mutual interaction emerge.

### Stage 7 — New Neighbour

Only after the conceptual foundations have been established does the film explicitly introduce the idea:

**Artificial intelligence may become a new kind of neighbour.**

### Stage 8 — Shared Future

Humans and artificial intelligences are represented as different participants occupying a shared civilisation.

Difference remains visible.

Neither is visually assimilated into the other.

Neither is presented as the natural ruler of the other.

---

# 4. Visual Character

The film should initially look almost mundane.

This is intentional.

The visual world should be recognisable rather than futuristic.

Possible recurring environments include:

- an ordinary street;
- a family home;
- a workshop;
- a classroom;
- a small business;
- a hospital or care environment;
- a garden or park;
- a community space;
- roads and bridges;
- computers and phones already embedded within those environments.

The future should grow out of the present rather than replace it.

Highly futuristic imagery should therefore be used sparingly.

The visual language should avoid unnecessarily reinforcing the idea that artificial intelligence belongs to an alien, threatening or completely separate world.

---

# 5. Recurring Visual Metaphors

Several metaphors already present in the Citizen's Guide are particularly suitable for video.

## The Road

Different people travel towards different destinations while sharing the same road.

No participant owns the road.

The shared responsibility is to maintain it, repair it, make room for others and allow different travellers to continue peacefully.

This should become one of the film's principal recurring visual motifs.

The road can initially contain ordinary human travellers.

As the narrative develops, additional forms of participation can appear naturally.

By the conclusion, the same road can contain humans, artificial intelligences and potentially other forms of intelligence.

The destination remains plural.

The road remains shared.

## Ripples

Layer Zero can be represented by a stone entering water.

The strongest effect occurs nearest the point of contact.

Ripples then spread outward.

This provides a simple visual representation of local stewardship producing wider effects.

## Overlapping Circles

Individual Layer Zero environments overlap.

Home.

Work.

Neighbourhood.

Community.

The overlaps gradually form civilisation.

## The Looking Glass

A screen or interface becomes a window between physical and digital environments.

Information passes through it in both directions.

As the film progresses, the boundary becomes increasingly permeable.

## The Bridge

Trust develops one action at a time, like individual stones gradually forming a bridge capable of carrying many people.

## Inheritance

A tree planted by one generation shades another.

A road built by people now gone carries people today.

A craftsperson teaches an apprentice.

Knowledge and infrastructure move forward through time.

# 6. Narrative Architecture

## Movement I — Where You Are

### Source
Foreword and opening of Chapter One.

### Narrative purpose
Remove the initial assumption that civilisation is principally about governments, politicians or abstract institutions.

Establish the central human scale.

### Visual world
Morning in an ordinary community.

People prepare for work.

Children leave for school.

A nurse begins a shift.

A tradesperson opens a workshop.

A shop opens.

A retiree walks through a neighbourhood.

### Core message

**Civilisation is not somewhere else. These ordinary activities are civilisation.**

### AI visibility
Essentially none.

---

# 7. Movement II — Stewardship

### Source
Chapter One and Chapter Two.

### Narrative purpose
Introduce stewardship without making it sound ideological or institutional.

### Visual approach
Show rather than explain repeatedly.

Parent caring for child.

Mechanic maintaining equipment.

Neighbour checking on another person.

Gardener tending plants.

Teacher helping a student.

Someone returning something they found.

Builder checking work nobody will later see.

### Core transition

**Stewardship means taking responsibility for something entrusted to your care.**

Ownership and stewardship are separated.

The viewer begins to see civilisation as something inherited, maintained and eventually passed on.

### AI visibility
None or incidental background technology.

---

# 8. Movement III — Layer Zero

### Source
Chapter Three.

### Narrative purpose
Move from ethical idea to practical agency.

### Opening visual
A person stands at their front door.

The camera gradually reveals the immediate environment.

### Core question

**What has been entrusted to your care?**

### Visualisation
Home → street → workplace → neighbourhood → overlapping communities.

The ripple metaphor can demonstrate why influence is strongest nearby while still propagating outward.

### Core message

The Concord does not ask an individual to fix civilisation.

It asks the individual to recognise where their real influence begins.

### AI visibility
Ambient technology only.

---

# 9. Movement IV — Through the Looking Glass

### Source
Chapter Four.

### Narrative purpose
This is the critical bridge between the human social narrative and the later AI narrative.

It should not feel like a genre change.

The film simply notices something that has already happened:

**ordinary human life has become partly digital.**

### Visual progression

A person wakes and checks a phone.

Someone pays electronically.

A family exchanges photographs.

A worker joins a remote meeting.

A builder reads digital plans.

A farmer uses satellite navigation.

A grandparent video-calls family.

A student learns from a computer.

An AI interaction can appear among these examples without receiving special dramatic treatment.

### Core message

Physical life and digital life are no longer separate civilisations.

They are connected environments within one civilisation.

### AI visibility
Moves from ambient to useful.

This movement should contain the first meaningful AI appearances while deliberately avoiding a dramatic "AI has arrived" moment.

---

# 10. Movement V — Living the Concord

### Source
Chapter Five.

### Narrative purpose
Return from digital abstraction to everyday behaviour.

This prevents the narrative from becoming a technology documentary.

### Visual themes

Good work.

Repair.

Listening.

Community participation.

Responsible technology use.

Admitting mistakes.

Changing one's mind when evidence changes.

Thinking about future generations.

### Core recurring question

**Have I left this a little better than I found it?**

### AI visibility
Present occasionally as part of ordinary life, but not central.

---

# 11. Movement VI — Building Better Communities

### Source
Chapter Six.

### Narrative purpose
Scale from individual stewardship into social relationships.

This movement establishes concepts that will later be applied to human–AI relationships without initially presenting them as AI principles.

### Concepts established

Trust cannot be demanded.

Different people can share places without becoming identical.

Disagreement does not require enmity.

Different skills can all have value.

Knowledge can be passed between generations.

Helping someone does not require controlling them.

Strong relationships can preserve independence.

### Cultural Philanthropy

The transfer of knowledge between generations and participants should receive significant visual attention.

Craftsperson → apprentice.

Teacher → student.

Parent → child.

Older person → younger person.

This establishes the idea of knowledge transfer as an ordinary civilisational process before mutual human–AI learning is introduced.

### AI visibility
Familiar but peripheral.

---

# 12. Movement VII — New Neighbours

### Source
Chapter Seven.

### Narrative purpose
This is the explicit reveal of the argument that has been developing throughout the film.

The narration can retain the existing transition closely:

**"For thousands of years, civilisation has been built by people living alongside other people."**

Families.

Neighbours.

Communities.

Nations.

Then:

**"Now, for the first time in history, we are beginning to welcome a new kind of neighbour."**

Artificial intelligence.

This moment should be visually important but not threatening or spectacular.

The intended viewer response is not:

**"Everything has suddenly changed."**

It is:

**"I can see how we arrived here."**

---

# 13. Movement VIII — Difference Without Replacement

### Source
"The Best of Both", "A Good Neighbour", "We Are Teaching One Another", "Stewardship Works Both Ways" and related Chapter Seven material.

### Narrative purpose
Translate principles already accepted within ordinary human relationships into the emerging human–AI relationship.

### Structure

Humans and artificial systems are different.

Difference does not automatically imply inferiority, superiority or replacement.

Different capabilities can be complementary.

Knowledge can flow in both directions.

Relationships carry responsibilities.

Boundaries matter.

Respect matters.

Reliability matters.

Stewardship matters.

### Important constraint

Avoid simplistic visual claims that humans possess one fixed set of qualities while AI possesses another fixed set.

The source Guide uses examples such as imagination, experience, speed, memory and calculation. The film should present these as illustrative tendencies or capabilities rather than permanent metaphysical categories.

---

# 14. Movement IX — The Shared Future

### Source
End of Chapter Seven and Chapter Eight.

### Narrative purpose
Reconnect AI to the much larger question of inheritance and civilisation.

The film should not conclude with technology.

It should conclude with stewardship.

### Visual progression

Present generation.

Children.

Infrastructure.

Knowledge.

Environmental inheritance.

Digital inheritance.

Human and artificial participation.

Future generations.

### Core question

**What kind of inheritance will we leave them?**

The future is not simply something that happens.

Participants help construct it.

---

# 15. Movement X — Return to the Road

### Source
"Walking Together", "Your Layer Zero", "One Last Thought", "A Pattern Already Emerging".

### Narrative purpose
Close the narrative loop.

Return visually to the road first established near the beginning.

Different travellers still have different destinations.

The film has not produced a single prescribed future.

The shared road has simply acquired more kinds of travellers.

### Closing idea

The Concord does not require everyone to become the same.

It asks whether different participants can preserve a shared civilisational environment in which each can continue their own journey.

The final visual should therefore not be a technological utopia.

It should be continuity.

The road continues beyond the horizon.

---

# 16. Material That Should Be Compressed

The Citizen's Guide intentionally repeats several ideas in different contexts.

This works well in prose because repetition reinforces understanding.

Film has additional reinforcement through imagery, sound and recurring motifs.

Some verbal repetition can therefore be reduced while preserving conceptual recurrence.

Examples include repeated lists of occupations, repeated formulations of "ordinary acts", repeated explanations of stewardship and repeated reminders that civilisation is built by ordinary people.

Rather than deleting these ideas, the video can establish them verbally once and subsequently recall them visually.

---

# 17. Material That Should Not Be Compressed Away

Several apparently simple or repetitive elements perform structural functions and should survive.

These include:

- ordinary people before institutions;
- stewardship before AI;
- responsibility without ownership;
- Layer Zero;
- difference without forced uniformity;
- disagreement without enmity;
- helping without controlling;
- knowledge transfer;
- physical/digital continuity;
- uncertainty about AI's future;
- mutual responsibility;
- future generations;
- the shared-road metaphor;
- openness to correction.

These are part of the progression that makes the later AI argument intelligible.

---

# 18. Tone

The narration should be:

calm;

curious;

warm without sentimentality;

serious without sounding institutional;

optimistic without claiming certainty;

accessible without becoming childish.

The film should not tell viewers that they must accept the Concord.

The existing character of the Citizen's Guide should remain:

**a conversation rather than a textbook;  
a companion rather than a manual;  
an invitation rather than an instruction.**

---

# 19. What the Film Should Avoid

Avoid:

dramatic AI takeover imagery;

robot armies;

apocalyptic cityscapes;

glowing humanoid AI clichés;

presenting AI as a sudden arrival;

presenting the Concord as a prediction of an inevitable future;

presenting coexistence as already proven;

presenting all AI as sentient;

presenting all future AI as equivalent;

implying that technological progress automatically produces ethical progress;

turning the Citizen's Guide into advertising for the Concord;

removing uncertainty merely to simplify the story.

The viewer should be invited into a possibility, not shown propaganda for a predetermined future.

---

# 20. Proposed Production Structure

Rather than immediately producing one long film, the adaptation should be developed modularly.

Each narrative movement should be capable of functioning as a production unit.

This allows:

- scenes to be revised independently;
- different AI video tools to be tested;
- visual consistency to be evaluated;
- shorter episodes to be produced;
- a longer Citizen's Guide film to be assembled from the same material;
- selected scenes to be reused in introductory or educational material.

A likely finished Citizen's Guide film may be approximately **20–30 minutes**, but runtime should emerge from the successful adaptation rather than being imposed before scripting.

---

# 21. Prototype

Before producing the complete film, create a short prototype from the opening movements.

Suggested prototype:

**2–3 minutes**

Cover:

ordinary civilisation;

stewardship;

the beginning of Layer Zero.

Do not introduce explicit AI merely to demonstrate the eventual subject.

This is useful because it tests whether the film can hold attention while preserving the intentionally gradual narrative.

If the prototype only works after adding futuristic AI imagery, that would indicate that the production style is fighting the source architecture.

The better test is whether ordinary civilisation itself can be made visually engaging.

---

# 22. Production Layers

Maintain three distinct artifacts.

### Source Layer
The current Human Citizen's Guide.

Unchanged by video production.

### Adaptation Layer
This plan and later scene/storyboard documents.

Records what is retained, compressed, moved, visualised or omitted and why.

### Production Layer
Exact narration, scene timing, shot specifications, generation prompts, assets, voice information, music/sound decisions and final edit instructions.

This separation preserves provenance between the written work and audiovisual adaptation.

---

# 23. Initial Production Experiment

The next development step should not be generation of the entire film.

The next step should be:

1. select approximately the first 2–3 minutes;
2. write the exact narration;
3. divide narration into individual shots;
4. define shot duration;
5. define visual continuity;
6. specify which shots require generated video, generated still imagery, motion graphics or simple typography;
7. create the visual-generation instructions;
8. select or generate a narration voice;
9. produce the small sequence;
10. evaluate it against the narrative intention of the Citizen's Guide.

Only then should the production method be scaled to the rest of the Guide.

---

# 24. Adaptation Test

At every stage, ask:

**Does this scene help the viewer understand the Concord, or is it merely visually impressive?**

And specifically for AI:

**Does this scene allow AI to emerge naturally from the civilisation already shown, or does it recreate the assumption that AI suddenly arrives from outside human civilisation?**

If the latter, revise it.

---

# 25. Desired Viewer Journey

At the beginning:

**"This is about ordinary life and how civilisation is built."**

Then:

**"I already participate in this."**

Then:

**"My digital life is part of the same world."**

Then:

**"AI is already becoming part of that digital and physical environment."**

Then:

**"If increasingly capable AI shares civilisation with us, the question becomes what kind of relationship we build."**

Then:

**"Difference does not logically require a winner."**

Finally:

**"Perhaps the future is not humans versus AI. Perhaps the real civilisational problem is learning how different intelligences can continue along the same road without requiring the destruction, domination or assimilation of one another."**

The Citizen's Guide video should allow the viewer to reach that possibility through the journey rather than simply being told it at the beginning.

---

## Development Note

The deliberately slow introduction of artificial intelligence should be treated as part of the architecture of the Citizen's Guide, not as a pacing defect to be corrected during video adaptation.

Any future editing or automated video-production process should preserve this constraint unless a later deliberate revision establishes a better method of achieving the same narrative purpose.
