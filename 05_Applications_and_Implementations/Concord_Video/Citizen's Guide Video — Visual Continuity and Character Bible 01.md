# Citizen's Guide Video — Visual Continuity and Character Bible 01

**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher  
**Project:** Foundations of the Concord — Concord V1.2  
**Development Area:** Concord Video  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / PRODUCTION REFERENCE  
**Applies To:** *The Concord: A Citizen's Guide — Video Adaptation*

---

# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the recurring people, environments and visual-continuity rules for the Citizen's Guide video.

Its purpose is not merely to maintain consistent characters between generated scenes.

The recurring community is itself part of the narrative.

The viewer should gradually become familiar with a small group of ordinary people before artificial intelligence becomes an explicit subject.

Their everyday lives continue throughout the film.

Technology changes.

Capabilities change.

Relationships develop.

The people and their underlying human needs remain recognisable.

The central visual progression is:

**same people  
→ same community  
→ same ordinary activities  
→ gradually changing tools  
→ increasingly capable technology  
→ changing relationships  
→ new forms of participation**

---

# 2. Diversity as Narrative Structure

The recurring community should visibly contain different:

- generations;
- ethnic and cultural backgrounds;
- genders;
- occupations;
- skills;
- family circumstances;
- personalities;
- levels of technological familiarity;
- physical capabilities.

This diversity should normally remain unremarked upon.

The film does not need to announce that the community is diverse.

The viewer simply experiences it as normal.

This establishes an important idea before artificial intelligence enters the foreground:

**civilisation already consists of participants who differ from one another.**

They possess different histories.

Different capabilities.

Different needs.

Different beliefs.

Different experiences.

Different destinations.

Yet they can occupy the same neighbourhood, depend upon one another and contribute differently to a shared civilisation.

When artificial intelligence later becomes more visible, it enters a world in which difference is already normal.

---

# 3. Important Conceptual Distinction

The film should not imply that differences of race, sex, age, disability, culture or other human characteristics are equivalent to the distinction between human and artificial intelligence.

They are not being presented as identical categories.

Instead, existing human diversity illustrates a broader civilisational fact:

**coexistence does not require sameness.**

Human–AI coexistence then asks whether this principle can extend further as civilisation encounters increasingly different forms of intelligence and participation.

The visual argument is therefore:

**difference already exists  
→ difference does not automatically prevent cooperation  
→ civilisation already accommodates differentiated capabilities and needs  
→ new forms of difference need not automatically imply conflict  
→ the appropriate relationships must still be discovered, tested and developed**

---

# 4. The Community

The film follows five principal recurring human characters and one child.

They live within the same broadly recognisable community.

Their lives overlap naturally.

They are not a formal group.

They do not gather to discuss the Concord.

Most may never consciously think about civilisation at all.

They simply live it.

---

# 5. Character A — Maya

**Role:** Parent  
**Approximate age:** 38  
**Gender:** Woman  
**Background:** British South Asian  
**Occupation:** Small-business administrator / hybrid office worker  
**Household:** Lives with her daughter, Sophie  
**Technology familiarity:** Comfortable but pragmatic

## Character

Maya is organised, busy and ordinary.

Technology matters to her principally when it saves time or helps her family.

She is not a technology enthusiast.

She represents the everyday person who gradually incorporates increasingly capable systems because they are useful.

## Early visual activities

Preparing breakfast.

Helping Sophie find something for school.

Checking a calendar.

Walking to school.

Working at a laptop.

Shopping.

Talking to neighbours.

Cooking dinner.

## Technology progression

### Early
Phone.

Laptop.

Online banking.

Digital calendar.

Navigation.

### Middle
Household voice assistant.

AI-assisted scheduling.

Translation or information retrieval.

AI helping Sophie understand schoolwork.

### Later
More capable household and personal assistance integrated into ordinary routines.

The important point is that breakfast remains breakfast.

School remains school.

Work remains work.

The tools quietly become more capable.

## Narrative function

Maya provides one of the clearest demonstrations that technological transformation can occur without daily life feeling like a sequence of revolutions.

# 6. Character B — Sophie

**Role:** Child / future generation  
**Approximate age:** 10  
**Gender:** Girl  
**Background:** Mixed British South Asian/White British heritage  
**Relationship:** Maya's daughter  
**Technology familiarity:** Native and intuitive

## Character

Curious.

Energetic.

Asks questions.

Accepts technologies that older characters may still regard as new.

She should never become a stereotypical "technology child."

She plays outside, reads, talks to adults, makes things and interacts with friends.

Digital and physical activities are simply parts of one life.

## Early visual activities

Getting ready for school.

Reading.

Drawing.

Playing.

Asking Maya questions.

Helping plant a tree.

Walking through the neighbourhood.

## Technology progression

Educational videos.

Tablet.

Interactive learning.

AI explanations.

Creative assistance.

Potential later interaction with a more conversational educational intelligence.

## Narrative function

Sophie represents inheritance.

She also allows the film to show that the boundary between "technology" and "ordinary life" is partly generational.

What one generation experiences as technological change may simply be part of the environment inherited by the next.

# 7. Character C — Daniel

**Role:** Tradesperson  
**Approximate age:** 46  
**Gender:** Man  
**Background:** Black British / Caribbean heritage  
**Occupation:** Electrician / general electrical systems specialist  
**Technology familiarity:** Practical and moderately high

## Character

Experienced.

Methodical.

Takes pride in doing work properly.

Does not care whether a tool is fashionable.

If it works reliably and helps him do a better job, he uses it.

## Early visual activities

Putting on work boots.

Loading tools.

Inspecting wiring.

Repairing something carefully.

Talking with a customer.

Teaching a younger apprentice.

## Technology progression

Paper/notebook plus phone.

Digital plans.

Tablet.

Diagnostic equipment.

AI-assisted fault diagnosis.

Building-system optimisation.

Potential later collaboration with increasingly autonomous maintenance systems.

## Narrative function

Daniel demonstrates technological progression through tools.

A screwdriver did not replace the electrician.

A multimeter did not replace the electrician.

A tablet did not replace the electrician.

The film can eventually ask whether increasingly intelligent tools must automatically be understood through replacement either.

This should remain an open question rather than a predetermined answer.

# 8. Character D — Elena

**Role:** Teacher  
**Approximate age:** 29  
**Gender:** Woman  
**Background:** White British / European family background  
**Occupation:** Primary or secondary-school teacher  
**Technology familiarity:** High

## Character

Curious.

Patient.

Comfortable experimenting with new methods while retaining professional judgement.

## Early visual activities

Preparing a classroom.

Helping a student.

Writing on a board.

Listening to a child.

Talking with parents.

## Relationship

## Technology progression

Computer and digital classroom display.

Online educational resources.

AI lesson assistance.

Personalised learning support.

Translation support for students.

AI-generated practice material.

Eventually, potentially, interaction with an artificial educational participant rather than merely a passive program.

## Narrative function

Elena carries the themes of:

knowledge transfer;

Cultural Philanthropy;

learning;

correction;

human–AI mutual learning.

Her role becomes particularly important when Chapter Seven discusses humans and AI teaching one another.

# 9. Character E — Thomas

**Role:** Nurse / care worker  
**Approximate age:** 34  
**Gender:** Man  
**Background:** White British  
**Occupation:** Nurse  
**Technology familiarity:** Moderate to high, professionally driven

## Character

Calm.

Attentive.

Competent.

Human interaction matters strongly within his work.

Technology is valuable when it improves care but should not distract from the person receiving it.

## Early visual activities

Beginning a shift.

Speaking with a patient.

Checking information.

Helping someone move safely.

Listening.

## Technology progression

Digital patient records.

Monitoring systems.

Decision-support tools.

AI-assisted analysis.

Remote consultation.

Potential later robotic or intelligent clinical assistance.

## Narrative function

Thomas allows the film to explore a particularly important distinction:

**increasing technological capability does not necessarily remove the value of human presence.**

It can also demonstrate that important decisions may involve combinations of human judgement and machine capability.

# 10. Character F — Margaret

**Role:** Older neighbour  
**Approximate age:** 72  
**Gender:** Woman  
**Background:** White British  
**Status:** Retired  
**Former occupation:** Could later be established as librarian, administrator, craft worker or another ordinary profession  
**Technology familiarity:** Moderate, selective

## Character

Independent.

Friendly.

Experienced.

Not technophobic.

Not fascinated by technology either.

She adopts things when they become useful enough to disappear into ordinary life.

## Early visual activities

Opening curtains.

Gardening.

Speaking with neighbours.

Walking to local shops.

Reading.

Sharing knowledge or a skill.

## Relationships

Lives near Maya and Sophie.

Daniel performs occasional electrical work at her home.

She knows the family.

She may encounter Thomas through ordinary community or healthcare circumstances without becoming defined as a patient.

## Technology progression

Phone.

Video calls.

Smart television.

Household voice assistant.

Medication or appointment reminders.

AI-supported communication.

Smart-home assistance.

Potential later conversational companion or domestic system.

## Narrative function

Margaret provides perhaps the clearest example of **normalisation**.

A household voice assistant can already sit on her kitchen counter during early scenes.

Nobody explains it.

Nobody calls it artificial intelligence.

She simply asks:

"Set a timer for ten minutes."

The film continues.

Much later, when AI becomes the subject, the viewer may realise:

**it was already there.**

# 11. Secondary Character — Daniel's Apprentice

**Approximate age:** 19–22  
**Gender:** Flexible  
**Background:** Distinct from Daniel  
**Role:** Apprentice electrician

This character need not receive extensive individual development.

Their principal function is to make knowledge transmission visible.

Daniel teaches the apprentice.

Later, Daniel himself learns something from an intelligent system.

This creates a subtle chain:

**experience → teaching → learning → new knowledge → teaching again**

It prevents knowledge from being represented as flowing only from machines to humans.

---

# 12. Overlapping Layer Zeros

The principal characters should not exist as six separate stories.

Their environments overlap.

For example:

Maya and Sophie live near Margaret.

Sophie attends Elena's school.

Daniel performs work at Margaret's house.

Daniel may later work at the school.

Thomas may live in the same neighbourhood.

Elena may pass Maya in the local shop.

Margaret may know Sophie from the street.

Characters may appear in the background of one another's scenes before the viewer consciously notices the relationship.

This visually expresses the Layer Zero principle.

**Individual lives overlap to form community.**

---

# 13. The Community as a Character

The neighbourhood itself should recur.

Important recurring locations:

Maya and Sophie's home.

Margaret's home.

The street.

School.

Daniel's van/work environment.

Marcus's healthcare environment.

Local shop.

Small park or green space.

Road.

Bridge or pedestrian crossing.

Community space.

The viewer should gradually acquire spatial familiarity.

The eventual introduction of more capable AI therefore occurs **inside a place the viewer already knows**.

---

# 14. Mundane Repetition

Several daily activities should intentionally recur.

This is important.

For example:

### Breakfast

Early film:

Maya makes breakfast while checking the time.

Later:

She asks a voice assistant for the weather.

Later still:

A more capable assistant reminds her of a school schedule change and helps reorganise her morning.

The activity remains:

**making breakfast.**

### School

Early:

Elena prepares a lesson.

Later:

She uses digital resources.

Later:

An AI helps produce differentiated material for students.

Later still:

She may interact with a much more capable educational system.

The activity remains:

**teaching children.**

### Work

Early:

Daniel diagnoses a fault manually.

Later:

He consults digital schematics.

Later:

An AI diagnostic system identifies possible causes.

Later still:

Human and machine may divide parts of the task according to capability.

The activity remains:

**fixing something.**

### Care

Early:

Thomas reviews a patient's information.

Later:

Software assists.

Later:

AI identifies a pattern.

Thomas still speaks with the person.

The activity remains:

**caring for someone.**

### Home

Early:

Margaret sets a kitchen timer.

Later:

The same household system helps organise appointments.

Later still:

Its conversational capability may become significantly greater.

The activity remains:

**living at home.**

# 15. The Alexa Principle

A useful production principle can be derived from familiar household assistants.

Many people already interact with systems incorporating forms of artificial intelligence without mentally framing each interaction as:

**"I am now interacting with artificial intelligence."**

They ask for:

weather;

music;

timers;

directions;

search results;

reminders;

translations;

recommendations.

The technology becomes mundane through repeated usefulness.

The Citizen's Guide video should use this phenomenon deliberately.

### Production Rule

**Do not announce every increase in machine capability.**

Allow the viewer to notice some changes only retrospectively.

This is central to the desired progression.

# 16. Technological Progression Ladder

Technology should develop approximately as follows:

### Phase A — Invisible Infrastructure

Phones.

Computers.

Digital records.

Electronic payments.

Navigation.

Internet services.

### Phase B — Ambient Assistance

Voice assistants.

Recommendations.

Autocorrection.

Translation.

Search assistance.

Automated scheduling.

### Phase C — Explicit AI Tools

Educational AI.

Diagnostic AI.

Professional assistants.

Generative systems.

More capable personal assistants.

### Phase D — Collaborative Systems

Systems participating actively in professional and personal tasks.

Longer-term context.

Greater autonomy within bounded functions.

More sophisticated communication.

### Phase E — Possible Participants

Only later does the film ask whether some future artificial systems might possess forms of agency or standing that require relationships different from those appropriate to ordinary tools.

This distinction is important.

**The film must not imply that today's household assistant is already equivalent to a future artificial participant.**

Instead it shows a developmental continuum in technological integration while leaving questions of agency, sentience and standing open.

---

# 17. Visual Treatment of AI

Initially:

No visual embodiment.

AI exists through ordinary devices and interfaces.

Later:

Interfaces become more conversational and context-aware.

Later still:

Some artificial systems may act through machines or robots where the function genuinely requires physical embodiment.

Avoid making humanoid robots the default representation of artificial intelligence.

Artificial intelligence should first be understood through **relationship and capability**, not body shape.

---

# 18. Diversity of Artificial Intelligence

When AI eventually becomes explicit, it should itself not be represented as one uniform category.

Possible forms include:

voice systems;

software assistants;

educational systems;

professional systems;

machine-control systems;

robotic systems;

potential autonomous digital intelligences.

This parallels another Concordian idea:

**different forms of intelligence may possess different capabilities, functions, needs and appropriate relationships.**

The film should avoid replacing one simplistic binary—

**human versus AI**

—with another assumption—

**all AI is the same.**

---

# 19. Character Consistency Requirements

For generated assets, each principal character should eventually receive a fixed reference specification including:

front portrait;

three-quarter portrait;

full-body reference;

ordinary clothing;

work clothing where applicable;

home environment;

work environment;

approximate height/build;

hair;

facial characteristics;

recurring accessories;

typical colour palette;

vehicle where relevant.

Once approved, these references should become the source images for later generation wherever the production tools permit reference-image conditioning.

Character appearance should not be regenerated from textual description alone for every shot.

---

# 20. Environment Consistency Requirements

The same principle applies to locations.

Create stable visual references for:

Maya and Sophie's kitchen;

Margaret's kitchen/living space;

street exterior;

school classroom;

Daniel's van/workshop;

healthcare environment;

park/community space.

This creates visual memory.

When the viewer returns to Margaret's kitchen ten minutes later and the household assistant has become more capable, they should recognise the kitchen.

That recognition is part of the story.

---

# 21. Time Progression

The film should not necessarily depict decades passing.

Most technological development can be represented as near-future progression occurring without a hard temporal jump.

Where greater future capability appears, subtle signals can indicate passage of time:

Sophie slightly older;

updated devices;

minor changes to vehicles;

new infrastructure;

changed interfaces.

Avoid:

**PRESENT DAY**

followed by

**THE FUTURE — 2045**

unless later narrative requirements make an explicit date necessary.

The desired experience is continuity.

---

# 22. Emotional Progression

The emotional relationship between viewer and characters should develop before the technological question becomes difficult.

Initially:

recognition.

Then:

familiarity.

Then:

connection.

Then:

technological normalisation.

Then:

curiosity.

Only then:

the deeper question.

**If increasingly capable artificial systems become participants in these people's lives, what kind of relationship should exist between them?**

The audience should care about the question because they already know the people affected by it.

---

# 23. Shared Road Integration

The recurring community eventually intersects with the shared-road metaphor.

Early:

ordinary people walk different directions along the same street or path.

Middle:

the same environment contains increasing technological infrastructure.

Later:

artificial participation becomes visible.

End:

the road remains.

The travellers have become more diverse.

The road has not acquired one compulsory destination.

---

# 24. Governing Visual Principle

The film should never visually suggest:

**the old human world ended and the AI world began.**

It should instead show:

**the world continued, and something new gradually became part of it.**

This is the central continuity principle of the Citizen's Guide adaptation.

---

# 25. Production Test

For any proposed scene involving advanced technology, ask:

**Could this plausibly be the same person's life we saw earlier?**

If no, examine whether the scene is introducing unnecessary technological rupture.

Then ask:

**Has the person's underlying purpose changed, or merely the tool available to them?**

Where the tool has changed but the human activity remains recognisable, the scene strongly supports the intended narrative.

Finally:

**Does this scene treat difference as something to understand and negotiate, or automatically as something to fear, dominate or erase?**

The film should preserve the possibility of coexistence without pretending that coexistence has already been proven.

---

## Production Summary

The recurring cast is not merely a device for visual consistency.

It is the mechanism through which the viewer experiences the central progression:

**different people already live together;  
their Layer Zeros already overlap;  
technology is already part of those lives;  
intelligent systems gradually become more capable;  
the community adapts without ceasing to be recognisable;  
and eventually the viewer is invited to consider whether a new kind of participant can also become a neighbour.**

The question emerges from ordinary life rather than interrupting it.
