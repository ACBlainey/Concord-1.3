# Unified Paths Methodology
## M4 — Lineage Dependence and Cross-Family Convergence

## Status

**Methodological control for E4 and subsequent anchor-reversal experiments.**

## 1. Problem

The Unified Paths programme compares ethical structures derived from multiple religious and philosophical traditions. However, the traditions are not equally independent historical observations.

Christianity, Judaism and Islam are closely related Abrahamic traditions with substantial historical, textual and conceptual inheritance. Ethical agreement among them may therefore reflect:

- recurrent ethical constraints;
- common historical inheritance;
- direct or indirect textual transmission;
- shared cultural ancestry;
- or combinations of these factors.

Accordingly, three Abrahamic agreements must not automatically be interpreted as three independent discoveries of the same ethical principle.

## 2. Tradition prevalence and independent convergence are different metrics

Future analysis should distinguish at least:

### Tradition prevalence
How many studied traditions contain a functional counterpart to a principle.

### Within-family convergence
Whether the principle recurs among historically or textually related traditions.

### Cross-family convergence
Whether comparable principles arise across substantially different cultural-textual lineages.

### Within-tradition recurrence
Whether the principle appears independently across multiple source strata, genres or periods inside a tradition.

A statement such as **8/8 traditions** therefore remains useful descriptive information, but it is not by itself a measure of eight independent ethical discoveries.

## 3. Abrahamic cluster

For purposes of dependence-aware analysis, the Christian, Jewish and Islamic anchor experiments should initially be treated as an **Abrahamic cluster**.

This does not imply that their ethical systems are identical or that one can be reduced to another. It records a known historical dependence relevant to evidential interpretation.

E4 Judaism therefore has two roles:

1. an additional anchor-reversal and source-architecture test;
2. a within-Abrahamic replication.

Its eventual convergence with the Psalm and Qur'an studies should not be presented as fully lineage-independent evidence of universality.

## 4. Out-of-family tests

Subsequent non-Abrahamic experiments provide increasingly important tests of the natural-law hypothesis.

Potentially relevant comparison families include, subject to precise corpus definitions:

- Dharmic/Hindu traditions;
- Buddhist traditions;
- Sikh tradition;
- Chinese/Confucian traditions;
- African Traditional Religions.

These labels themselves must not be assumed to represent fully independent or internally uniform populations.

Historical contact, borrowing, syncretism and shared cultural environments should be recorded where material.

Sikhism is an important example: it is a distinct developed tradition, but arose in a historical environment involving substantial interaction with Hindu and Islamic traditions. Its evidential independence therefore requires more nuance than a binary independent/dependent label.

## 5. Pre-registered natural-law prediction

Before completing the remaining anchor reversals, the emerging natural-law/fractal-ethics hypothesis makes the following provisional prediction:

> **If deep ethical structures substantially reflect recurrent constraints of multi-agent existence rather than primarily common textual ancestry, independently derived non-Abrahamic corpora should tend to converge at sufficiently high levels of ethical abstraction despite substantial theological, metaphysical, linguistic and cultural differences.**

A related resolution prediction is:

**deep roots — comparatively high convergence**

**major ethical branches — moderate convergence**

**domain-specific rules — increasing cultural variation**

**ritual/theological implementation — high divergence**

This is a prediction to test, not a target for extraction.

## 6. Alternative outcomes

### Outcome A — deep cross-family convergence
Supports further investigation of substrate-independent or recurrent civilisational ethical constraints, while not proving natural law.

### Outcome B — root convergence with branch divergence
Consistent with common deep constraints undergoing culturally distinct decompression and domain application.

### Outcome C — substantial root divergence
Evidence against a strong universal ethical-root hypothesis or evidence that the proposed compression level is inadequate.

### Outcome D — unusually strong detailed convergence
Requires careful investigation of historical transmission, shared sources, translation effects and methodological over-normalisation before being treated as evidence of universality.

### Outcome E — apparently different roots reduce to alternative decompositions of common compound structures
Evidence relevant to the emerging Ethical Topology and fractal-ethics hypotheses.

All outcomes must be retained.

## 7. Cultural-lineage dependency map

The mature Unified Paths analysis should maintain a dependency map recording known or plausible relationships among studied traditions.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- shared canonical sources;
- direct textual inheritance;
- common language families where ethically relevant;
- known historical contact;
- philosophical borrowing;
- geographical interaction;
- syncretic development;
- shared predecessor traditions;
- degree of temporal separation.

The purpose is not to calculate an arbitrary independence score prematurely. It is to prevent simple prevalence counts from being mistaken for independent replication counts.

## 8. Reporting rule

Future convergence reporting should, where possible, state both:

> **tradition prevalence**

and

> **lineage breadth / cross-family recurrence.**

For example, a future result should prefer language such as:

> “The principle was recovered in X of Y studied traditions spanning Z substantially distinct cultural-textual lineages.”

rather than implying that every tradition is an independent observation.

## 9. Relationship to E3

The E3 result remains a meaningful anchor-reversal finding. It demonstrated that a Qur'an-first extraction independently reconstructed a higher-order architecture substantially overlapping the pre-existing Concord Ethical Kernel and the earlier comparative work.

M4 does not weaken that result. It clarifies the evidential weight that can responsibly be assigned to agreement among historically related traditions.

The strongest universality tests remain ahead in the non-Abrahamic anchor experiments.

## 10. Anti-bias safeguard

Knowledge of the predicted cross-family convergence must not affect extraction, normalisation or catalogue closure.

The correct sequence remains:

**define corpus → extract independently → normalise → freeze → validate externally → only then compare across traditions and lineages.**

Non-convergence is evidence and must not be normalised away.

## 11. Wider implication

If similar ethical roots are independently recovered across substantially separated cultural-textual lineages, historical inheritance becomes progressively less sufficient as a complete explanation.

Such a result would not by itself establish ethical natural law. It would, however, strengthen the case for testing whether recurring ethical structures emerge from general constraints of cooperative multi-agent existence.

Conversely, systematic cross-family divergence would place important limits on that hypothesis.
