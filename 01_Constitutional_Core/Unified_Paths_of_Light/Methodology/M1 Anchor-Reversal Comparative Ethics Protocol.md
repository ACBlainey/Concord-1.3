# Unified Paths Methodology
## M1 — Anchor-Reversal Comparative Ethics Protocol

## 1. Objective

The protocol provides a common experimental architecture for deriving ethical catalogues independently from religious and sacred traditions whose source forms may differ radically.

It generalises lessons from the Psalm-first Unified Paths study and the Qur'an-first E3 experiment.

The aim is not to force equal text quantities or identical output counts. The aim is to impose **equivalent epistemic controls**.

## 2. Four quantities that must remain distinct

**Source coverage** — how much of the declared search space was examined.

**Ethical saturation** — whether additional material continues producing materially new normalised principles.

**Principle convergence** — whether different source propositions consolidate into the same principles.

**Cross-traditional agreement** — whether independently derived principles find functional counterparts in other traditions.

None may be substituted for another.

## 3. Stage A — Define the anchor population

Before extraction, define what tradition or textual population the experiment actually claims to study.

Avoid claims such as “the ethics of Hinduism” when the experiment examines only a bounded subset.

The correct claim is:

> **These are the ethical principles recovered by this protocol from the declared anchor corpus.**

## 4. Stage B — Freeze the Anchor Corpus Boundary

Define the **Anchor Corpus Boundary (ACB)** before ethical extraction.

The ACB records:

- included texts, collections, oral corpora or documented traditions;
- excluded material;
- authority/canonical status;
- translation/edition;
- rationale for inclusion;
- known limitations;
- whether the corpus is exhaustive, representative or stratified.

Corpus size need not equal that of other experiments.

## 5. Stage C — Define Ethical Source Units

An **Ethical Source Unit (ESU)** is:

> **The smallest contextually sufficient passage, teaching, saying, narrative element, rule or documented oral formulation from which a distinct behavioural ethical proposition can responsibly be extracted.**

An ESU may therefore be:

- a complete short text;
- one verse;
- several adjacent verses;
- a stanza;
- a dialogue segment;
- a rule;
- a narrative episode where the behavioural proposition is separable;
- a proverb plus sufficient provenance/context;
- a documented oral teaching.

Document boundaries must not be mistaken for ethical boundaries.

## 6. Stage D — Extract before comparison

Traverse the ACB using the predefined ESU rules.

For each occurrence preserve:

1. source locator;
2. sufficient context;
3. extracted behavioural proposition;
4. interpretive note where necessary;
5. translation/provenance information;
6. uncertainty where present.

Do not consult the desired final Concord correspondence during extraction.

## 7. Stage E — Normalise

Translate extracted propositions into substrate-neutral behavioural language while preserving meaningful distinctions.

Maintain three layers:

**A — Source occurrence**  
**B — Extracted proposition**  
**C — Normalised principle**

Source frequency is evidence of emphasis, not principle multiplicity.

## 8. Stage F — Consolidate

Merge propositions only where the behavioural distinction can be removed without meaningful ethical information loss.

The goal is:

> **the lowest stable number of principles that preserves meaningful behavioural distinctions.**

Do not target a predetermined count.

## 9. Stage G — Freeze the anchor-derived catalogue

Before cross-traditional comparison, freeze:

- identifiers;
- names;
- definitions;
- source mappings;
- unresolved ambiguities.

Later crosswalk results must not be allowed to rewrite the catalogue merely to improve agreement.

## 10. Stage H — Corpus expansion where required

For large or distributed traditions, use predefined expansion strata rather than attempting arbitrary equality of word count.

A possible structure is:

**Core Anchor Corpus → Expansion 1 → Expansion 2 → Expansion 3 ...**

Expansion strata should deliberately cover different genres, schools, periods or communities where relevant.

For each expansion measure:

- new source occurrences;
- new propositions;
- refinements of existing principles;
- genuinely new stable principles.

## 11. Stage I — Closure

Permitted closure modes include:

### Corpus exhaustion
All predefined source material examined.

### Representative-corpus exhaustion
All material in a preregistered representative corpus examined.

### Principle saturation
Further independent sampling ceases to generate materially new stable principles.

### Marginal-information closure
Further extraction still generates propositions, but they overwhelmingly consolidate into existing principles and no longer materially change the catalogue.

Closure must be justified from the anchor experiment itself.

**Forbidden closure criterion:** stop because the catalogue now resembles the Concord or another tradition sufficiently closely.

## 12. Stage J — Post-freeze external validation

After the catalogue is frozen, sample material deliberately outside the derivation corpus.

The external sample should be adversarial where possible: different genre, school, period, geography or social context.

Classify each comparable result:

**V1 — Recovery:** already represented.

**V2 — Nuance/refinement:** same underlying principle with additional culturally/contextually specific detail.

**V3 — Compound expression:** apparently new rule decomposes into combinations of existing principles.

**V4 — Genuine candidate addition:** cannot be represented without meaningful information loss.

**V5 — Context-bound/non-comparable:** theological, ritual, cosmological or culturally specific content that cannot responsibly be normalised into the behavioural catalogue.

V4 findings trigger targeted investigation; they do not silently rewrite the frozen catalogue.

## 13. Stage K — Residual diagnosis

Observed differences may arise from:

**real ethical difference + extraction method + translation + semantic range + cultural context + source genre + abstraction level.**

These possibilities must be investigated before declaring a new ethical primitive.

A residual is evidence, not an inconvenience.

## 14. Stage L — Cross-anchor comparison

Only after the independent catalogue and validation phase are complete should the experiment compare with:

- other frozen anchor catalogues;
- the original Psalm-derived work;
- E3;
- higher-order attractors;
- Blainey's Laws / Concord Ethical Kernel.

Comparison should preserve direct matches, partial matches, failures and asymmetries.

## 15. Expected variation

Different anchors are **not expected** to produce identical counts, labels or intermediate attractors.

Indeed, differing surface catalogues that independently compress toward similar deeper structures provide stronger evidence of convergence than suspiciously identical outputs.

## 16. Governing maxim

> **Derive internally. Freeze. Challenge externally. Compare only afterward.**

This protocol is intended to govern E4 and subsequent anchor-reversal experiments, subject to explicitly documented methodological amendments.

> **End of M1**
