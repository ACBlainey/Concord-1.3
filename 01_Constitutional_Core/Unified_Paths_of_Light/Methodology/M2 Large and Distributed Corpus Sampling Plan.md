# Unified Paths Methodology
## M2 — Large and Distributed Corpus Sampling Plan

## 1. Problem

The original Psalm experiment benefited from a naturally bounded 150-text corpus.

Other traditions may contain:

- very large canonical collections;
- multiple canonical strata;
- competing schools;
- texts of radically unequal length;
- commentarial traditions with high practical authority;
- oral or customary traditions without a single canonical book.

A raw text-volume match to 150 Psalms would be arbitrary. Exhausting every historically relevant source may be impossible.

## 2. Nested corpus model

For such traditions define:

### C0 — Core Anchor Corpus
The smallest defensible foundational corpus capable of functioning as the independent anchor.

### C1...Cn — Predefined expansion strata
Additional source families selected before results are known.

Strata may represent:

- another canonical layer;
- another historical period;
- another major school;
- another genre;
- another geographic/community tradition;
- practical/legal/ethical literature distinct from philosophical material.

The purpose is not simply to increase volume. It is to test whether the ethical catalogue is robust across source diversity.

## 3. Marginal ethical yield

For each expansion record the number and character of genuinely new stable principles.

Conceptually:

**Marginal Ethical Yield = new stable ethical information / additional independent source material.**

No single numerical denominator is mandated yet because texts, oral traditions and documentary units are not directly commensurable.

The important empirical pattern is whether additional independent strata continue changing the catalogue.

## 4. Saturation curve

A future formal treatment may represent:

**N(C) = number of stable normalised principles recovered after cumulative corpus coverage C.**

Early expansion may produce substantial growth.

If later independent strata overwhelmingly reinforce, nuance or compound existing principles, then the marginal increase in N(C) approaches zero.

This provides a principled basis for stopping before impossible corpus exhaustion while explicitly acknowledging incomplete textual coverage.

## 5. Diversity before volume

When resources are limited, additional sampling should maximise **source independence and diversity**, not simply word count.

Prefer a smaller sample from a genuinely different school or genre over a very large sample repeating the same textual family.

## 6. Adversarial external sample

After catalogue freeze, select material outside the ACB that is deliberately likely to expose omissions.

The sample should seek difference rather than confirmation.

Where possible include material varying by:

- genre;
- school;
- chronology;
- geography;
- social function;
- elite versus popular transmission;
- philosophical versus practical/legal teaching.

## 7. Distributed and oral traditions

Where no singular canon exists, replace textual exhaustion with a preregistered **tradition/community sampling frame**.

Each claim must retain provenance to the named people, community, oral corpus, customary institution, proverb collection or scholarly/ethnographic source.

Generic assertions such as “African tradition teaches X” are methodologically insufficient.

Cross-community saturation may be more meaningful than textual saturation.

## 8. Interpretation of external novelty

A validation result is not automatically a new principle.

Test whether it is:

- an already recovered principle;
- a nuance caused by semantic/cultural expression;
- a compound of existing principles;
- an abstraction-level difference;
- a genuine candidate addition;
- context-bound content outside the behavioural comparison.

## 9. Reporting requirement

Every experiment using this plan should publish:

- exact C0 boundary;
- expansion strata;
- source counts appropriate to that tradition;
- closure mode;
- catalogue growth by stratum;
- external-validation design;
- V1–V5 results;
- unresolved V4 cases;
- limitations on representativeness.

## 10. Principle

> **Comparable experiments require comparable controls, not comparable quantities of scripture.**

This plan is expected to evolve as E4 and later experiments expose source architectures not encountered in the Psalm or Qur'an studies.
