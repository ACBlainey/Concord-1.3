# Ethical Topology of Civilisation

## Status

**Exploratory research programme — hypotheses preserved for independent derivation and testing.**

## Purpose

This folder develops the Ethical Topology of Civilisation (ETC) hypothesis that emerged unintentionally from the E3 Qur'an-first ethical comparison and the subsequent Ten Commandments comparison.

The originating chain was:

**E3 convergence → ethical compression → Decalogue compound rules → fractal ethics → ethical intersections → civilisational functions → institutional topology → transgression topology → rehabilitation mapping → institutional-response mapping → system-state monitoring and drift detection.**

This provenance is methodologically important. The topology was not introduced to justify a preselected institutional design.

## Central hypothesis

> **Persistent civilisational functions may arise at recurrent intersections between fundamental ethical constraints and recurrent conditions of multi-agent existence. If such a topology can be derived independently, it may help derive, locate, delimit, measure and audit civilisational architecture.**

A provisional general chain is:

**Ethical Kernel → Ethical Topology → Derived Function → Institutional Implementation → Observed Action → Metrics → Drift Detection → Correction.**

## Research discipline

The strongest applications must remain downstream of the derivation.

Criminal justice, rehabilitation, constitutional boundaries, system-state metrics, Concord architecture and first-contact implications must not be used as inputs when constructing the topology.

The intended order is:

1. candidate primitive compression;
2. compound-rule regeneration;
3. blind topology derivation;
4. inference of persistent civilisational functions;
5. comparison with Concord and historical institutions;
6. historical and computational testing;
7. held-out application tests;
8. cross-substrate investigation.

## Critical distinction

Even if a substrate-independent ethical topology exists:

**ethical reality, if it exists ≠ our current model of ethical reality.**

The model must remain corrigible. No claim of natural law may be used to make the current representation immune to evidence or recursive oversight.
