# Ethical Topology of Civilisation
## ETC01 — Transgression, Justice and Rehabilitation

## Status

**Held-out application hypothesis — not an input into topology derivation.**

## 1. Origin

Once civilisational functions were considered as recurring intersections in ethical space, criminal transgression became an obvious possible application.

A crime may violate several ethical dimensions simultaneously. The same topology may then be inverted to identify the ethical capabilities relevant to restoration and rehabilitation.

This creates a possible two-directional mapping:

**transgression → ethical structure of the wrong**

and

**ethical structure → candidate restorative/rehabilitative requirements.**

## 2. Compound transgression

A legal offence should not be assumed to correspond to one ethical principle.

Illustrative mappings include:

**Fraud ≈ Truth⁻ × Autonomy⁻ × Stewardship⁻**

Deception corrupts information, defeats informed agency and enables illegitimate resource transfer.

**Robbery ≈ Non-Harm⁻ × Autonomy⁻ × Stewardship⁻**

**Perjury ≈ Truth⁻ × Non-Harm⁻ × Justice⁻**

because corruption of evidence can produce harm through corruption of adjudication.

These examples are provisional and must not be used to construct the underlying topology.

## 3. Ethical multiplicity is not severity

The number of ethical dimensions violated cannot by itself determine seriousness.

A low-impact act may touch several principles while catastrophic intentional harm may primarily violate one.

A future severity model would require additional variables such as:

**magnitude, intent, knowledge, agency, victim impact, duration, reversibility, recurrence, vulnerability, power asymmetry and systemic effects.**

Therefore:

> **Topology describes the structure of the wrong; it does not by itself determine punishment.**

## 4. Offence topology is not person diagnosis

A particularly important safeguard is:

**Offence Topology ≠ Person Diagnosis ≠ Rehabilitation Prescription.**

An offence can be mapped without assuming why a particular person committed it.

Ethical knowledge may not be deficient at all. Relevant causes may include impulse control, coercion, addiction, social environment, distorted incentives, lack of legitimate alternatives, deliberate choice or other factors.

Individual assessment and evidence remain necessary.

## 5. Rehabilitation inversion

Where an individual assessment supports it, the topology may identify ethical domains relevant to rehabilitation.

Conceptually:

**transgression topology → assessed capability/behavioural deficit → targeted rehabilitation → restored cooperative agency.**

Fraud-related rehabilitation might engage Truth, Autonomy and Stewardship.

Coercive violence might engage Non-Harm, Autonomy, Self-Regulation and Dignity/Anti-Domination.

The purpose is not merely moral instruction. A mature model would distinguish understanding, practical capability, behavioural regulation, environmental conditions and demonstrated restoration.

## 6. Restoration rather than reciprocal harm

A Concordian justice model may eventually be able to ask:

> **What intervention protects others while restoring, where possible, the individual's capacity to operate successfully within the ethical topology of cooperative civilisation?**

This does not eliminate restraint, incapacitation or accountability where required. It changes the optimisation target from punishment alone to protection, proportional accountability, repair and restoration of agency.

## 7. Held-out test

This application should remain sealed while the topology is independently derived.

A strong result would be that independently generated ethical intersections later classify recognised offence families, distinguish superficially similar offences and identify meaningful rehabilitation dimensions without criminal law having shaped the topology.

Failure to do so would be evidence against the model or evidence that additional variables are required.
