# 1. Purpose

Phase C2 applies the governing loss test:

> **If this candidate civil object is removed, can the relevant protected condition still be reliably realised by the remaining derived architecture?**

The test is functional rather than rhetorical.

A candidate survives only if removing it causes a material ethical/civil loss that is not reliably supplied by the remaining objects.

Possible outcomes:

- **SURVIVES — DISTINCT FUNCTION**
- **SURVIVES — DISTINCT BUT COMPOUND**
- **MERGE / PROPERTY OF ANOTHER OBJECT**
- **REDUNDANT**
- **UNRESOLVED**

This test does not compare against the wider Concord.

---

# 2. CR-BL01 — Autonomy and Protection Against Unjustified Interference

## Removal test

Without CR-BL01:
- BL2's explicit right of others to direct their own lives has no direct civil expression;
- dignity does not itself establish who chooses;
- safe space protects context but not general self-direction;
- fairness can treat two participants comparably while controlling both;
- peaceful difference does not cover all individual life choices;
- contestability permits challenge but does not establish the protected decision sphere being challenged.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT FUNCTION**

CR-BL01 is necessary to preserve the explicit autonomy content of BL2.

## Freeze note

The right remains bounded by the ethical minimum. “Unjustified interference” should not be interpreted as “all interference.”

---

# 3. CR-BL02 — Safe Civil Context

## Removal test

Could safe-space protection be supplied by autonomy + harm minimisation + dignity?

Partially, but not reliably.

BL3 specifically requires environments in which individuals can **live, learn, create and cooperate** without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence. This is a contextual/environmental protection, not merely an aggregation of individual non-interference claims.

Without CR-BL02:
- diffuse intimidation or unsafe environmental conditions may escape individual-act framing;
- positive maintenance of protected contexts disappears;
- learning/creation/cooperation conditions lose explicit civil representation.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT BUT COMPOUND**

It is grounded directly in BL3 and supported by BL1/BL2.

## Freeze note

The object protected is a safe civil **condition/context**, not necessarily ownership of a particular place.

---

# 4. CR-BL03 — Honest Consequential Civil Dealing

## Removal test

Could deception be addressed by contestability/correction alone?

No.

A system could permit later correction while still intentionally deceiving participants at the moment consequential decisions are made.

Could dignity/autonomy alone prohibit deception?

They constrain manipulation but do not directly preserve BL4's truthful-communication requirement.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT FUNCTION**

Intentional material deception where truthful dealing is owed is a substantive violation, not merely an error awaiting correction.

## Freeze note

This does not imply unlimited disclosure or transparency.

---

# 5. CR-BL04 — Fair Comparable Treatment

## Removal test

Could fairness be reconstructed from autonomy, dignity and harm?

Not reliably.

Two participants can be treated with dignity and without unnecessary harm while still receiving arbitrarily different civil treatment in materially comparable circumstances.

BL5 directly identifies comparability as ethically relevant.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT FUNCTION**

## Freeze note

Relevant difference can justify different treatment. The right protects against arbitrary differential treatment, not difference itself.

---

# 6. CR-BL05 — Dignity and Non-Instrumentalisation

## Removal test

Could dignity be supplied by autonomy and harm minimisation?

No.

A participant might voluntarily accept harmful or highly constrained circumstances, yet BL7 still says intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means.

Likewise, a system might minimise total harm while sacrificing an individual purely instrumentally.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT FUNCTION**

## Freeze note

The source-bound holder class remains **intelligent participants**. Definition is deferred.

---

# 7. CR-BL06 — Peaceful Difference and Non-Conformity

## Removal test

Could peaceful difference be supplied by autonomy alone?

Not completely.

Autonomy protects self-direction, but BL9 explicitly recognises that differences are natural and makes peaceful coexistence the preferred objective. This has relational and plural-civilisation content beyond individual choice.

Could safe-space protection supply it?

No. Safe spaces could exist inside an otherwise conformity-demanding system.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT BUT COMPOUND**

## Freeze note

The derived protection is against unnecessary coercive conformity where peaceful coexistence within the ethical minimum remains reasonably possible.

It does not yet specify every particular liberty through which difference may be expressed.

---

# 8. CR-BL07 — Contestability and Correction

## Removal test

Could each substantive right simply contain its own challenge mechanism?

Possibly in implementation, but removing the general object loses BL10's independent ethical statement that **no intelligence is infallible** and beliefs/actions should remain open to better evidence and reasoning.

A system could respect autonomy, dignity, fairness and honesty in stated doctrine while making its own interpretation of those principles unchallengeable.

Therefore correction is not merely a convenience attached to other rights.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT CROSS-CUTTING PROCEDURAL FUNCTION**

## Freeze note

The exact institutional review/remedy architecture is not derived here. The protected civil requirement is meaningful openness to relevant challenge and correction.

---

# 9. SR-BL01 — Foreseeably Affected Participant Standing

## Removal test

Could BL6 responsibility operate solely as an actor duty without affected-party standing?

Yes in abstract ethics, but not reliably as civil architecture.

If the actor alone determines whether foreseeable consequences matter, responsibility can exist nominally while affected participants remain civilly invisible.

However, this standing may later fuse with broader standing/remedy architecture.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT STANDING FUNCTION, FINAL FORM UNRESOLVED**

## Freeze note

This is not yet a general right to veto actions producing foreseeable consequences.

---

# 10. SD-BL01 — Commons Stewardship and Future Opportunity

## Removal test

Could harm/fairness/responsibility preserve BL8 without a separate stewardship object?

Not reliably.

BL8 uniquely identifies:
- **shared resources**;
- **present prosperity**;
- **future opportunity**;
- an intertemporal stewardship relation.

Present-person rights alone cannot reliably represent unidentified future participants or systemic depletion.

## Result

**SURVIVES — DISTINCT STEWARDSHIP / INTERGENERATIONAL FUNCTION**

## Freeze note

Do not force this into a conventional individual-right holder model.

---

# 11. AC-BL01 — Least-Harm and Ethical-Minimum Authority Constraint

## Removal test

Is this already fully contained in CR-BL01?

Partly.

CR-BL01 protects the participant. AC-BL01 expresses the rule applied to an actor claiming authority: legitimate purpose does not license unnecessarily harmful means.

If removed as a separate constitutional object, the same requirement can remain as an **interpretive property of CR-BL01 grounded directly in BL1 + BL2**.

## Result

**MERGE / PROPERTY OF CR-BL01 AND GENERAL AUTHORITY INTERPRETATION**

It should not survive as a separate peer right.

## Freeze disposition

Preserve the constraint, but not as an independent rights-list object.

---

# 12. AC-BL02 — Anti-Infallibility Constraint

## Removal test

Is this independently necessary after CR-BL07?

CR-BL07's justification already depends upon the fact that no intelligence is infallible.

The anti-infallibility rule is therefore a constitutional implication and interpretive basis of contestability rather than a separate participant right.

## Result

**MERGE / CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE SUPPORTING CR-BL07**

Preserve for later constitutional derivation.

---

# 13. AC-BL03 — Coexistence Over Uniformity Constraint

## Removal test

Is this distinct from CR-BL06?

CR-BL06 protects participants from unnecessary coercive conformity. The institutional preference for coexistence over uniformity is the architecture-facing side of the same derivation.

## Result

**MERGE / CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE SUPPORTING CR-BL06**

Preserve for later constitutional derivation.

# 14. Frozen blind-derived civil architecture

The following result is now frozen **before comparison with the wider Concord rights corpus**.

## A. Candidate participant rights

### FCR-01 — Autonomy and Protection Against Unjustified Interference
A protected claim to direct one's own life within the ethical minimum and against unnecessary harmful or arbitrary controlling interference.

**Primary source:** BL2; constrained/supported by BL1 and BL7.

### FCR-02 — Safe Civil Context
A candidate claim to civil contexts in which legitimate living, learning, creation and cooperation can occur without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.

**Primary source:** BL3; supported by BL1/BL2.

### FCR-03 — Honest Consequential Civil Dealing
Where truthful dealing is owed in a consequential civil relation, an affected participant has a claim not to be intentionally materially deceived.

**Primary source:** BL4.

### FCR-04 — Fair Comparable Treatment
A claim that materially comparable situations receive comparable civil treatment unless a relevant difference justifies different treatment.

**Primary source:** BL5.

### FCR-05 — Dignity and Non-Instrumentalisation
Intelligent participants have a protected claim to be treated as beings of inherent value and not merely as instruments, assets or disposable means.

**Primary source:** BL7.

### FCR-06 — Peaceful Difference and Non-Conformity
A candidate claim to remain different without unnecessary coercive conformity where the difference remains compatible with the ethical minimum and peaceful coexistence is reasonably possible.

**Primary source:** BL9; supported by BL2/BL7/BL1.

### FCR-07 — Contestability and Correction
A participant materially affected by consequential civil belief, classification or action has a candidate claim to a viable route for relevant challenge and appropriate reconsideration/correction where material error is established.

**Primary source:** BL10; supported contextually by BL1/BL4/BL5.

---

## B. Standing

### FSR-01 — Foreseeably Affected Participant Standing
Where a capable actor can foresee material consequences for another participant, those consequences and the affected participant cannot be treated as civilly irrelevant.

**Primary source:** BL6.

Final legal/civil form remains unresolved.

---

## C. Stewardship / future protection

### FSD-01 — Commons Stewardship and Future Opportunity
Shared resources should be managed so that present prosperity does not unnecessarily diminish future opportunity; materially affected present users have candidate standing, while future opportunity remains protected even where future holders are not individually identifiable.

**Primary source:** BL8.

This is not forced into an individual-right form.

---

## D. Constitutional implications preserved for later derivation

### FCI-01 — Least-Harm Exercise of Legitimate Authority
Legitimate purpose does not justify unnecessarily harmful means where a less harmful adequate course exists.

### FCI-02 — No Structural Infallibility
No intelligence or institution should be constitutionally treated as incapable of material error.

### FCI-03 — Coexistence Preferred Over Unnecessary Uniformity
Difference alone does not justify coercive uniformity where peaceful coexistence within the ethical minimum remains reasonably possible.

These are **not** being declared constitutional rules here. They are frozen derivational outputs reserved for the later provisional constitutional stage.

# 16. Freeze statement

**The Blainey's-Laws blind derivation is now frozen at this point.**

The frozen object consists of:

- 7 candidate participant rights;
- 1 affected-participant standing relation;
- 1 stewardship/intergenerational function;
- 3 constitutional implications reserved for later development;

# 17. Experimental outcome before comparison

The current result supports a mixed form of the original hypotheses:

- rights are not one-to-one decompressions of ethical principles;
- some rights are direct, especially autonomy;
- some are compound;
- some ethical principles generate standing, duties or stewardship rather than ordinary rights;
- procedural contestability emerges as a distinct cross-cutting protection;
- the ethical kernel does not, at this level of derivation, uniquely specify a complete conventional civil-rights catalogue.

Therefore **underdetermination remains a live result**, not a failure to be repaired before comparison.
