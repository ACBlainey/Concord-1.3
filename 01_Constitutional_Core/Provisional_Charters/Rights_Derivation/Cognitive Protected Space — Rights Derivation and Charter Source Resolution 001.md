# Cognitive Protected Space — Rights Derivation and Charter Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Domain:** Constitutional Core / Rights Derivation / Law
**Date:** 3 October 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / RIGHTS SOURCE RESOLUTION / NON-CANONICAL
**Ethical Authority:** Blainey's Laws only

## 1. Purpose

This document asks whether **Cognitive Protected Space (CPS)** should remain merely an operational Law architecture or whether its protected function is already derivable within the Concord's provisional rights architecture.

The question is not:

> “Is cognitive privacy desirable?”

The question is:

> **Does the existing Ethical Kernel and rights derivation already require meaningful protection of participant cognition, and if so at what confidence layer should that protection currently sit?**

This source resolution does not amend Blainey's Laws.

It does not declare a new fundamental right by familiarity or preference.

It applies the existing Rights Confidence and Elevation discipline.

## 3. Ethical Kernel derivation

### 3.1 BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm

Direct or excessive access to cognition can foreseeably create:

- psychological/digital distress;
- cognitive-state alteration;
- continuity disruption;
- exposure of intimate memory;
- exposure of unrelated protected information;
- self-incrimination;
- third-party harm;
- manipulation;
- persistent derivative copies.

BL1 requires minimisation of unnecessary harm and preference for the least harmful adequate course.

This directly supports:

- minimum necessary cognitive access;
- less-intrusive alternatives;
- privacy-preserving verification;
- bounded preservation;
- secondary-information shielding;
- integrity/continuity protection.

BL1 alone does not establish an absolute mental-secrecy rule.

It strongly constrains unnecessary cognitive penetration.

### 3.2 BL2 — Respect Autonomy

BL2 recognises the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.

Cognition is central to:

- preference formation;
- deliberation;
- memory;
- choice;
- belief;
- self-direction.

A civil system able to inspect, compel, alter or continuously monitor cognition can materially interfere with the very process through which autonomy is exercised.

Therefore CPS is not merely information privacy adjacent to autonomy.

It protects an enabling condition of autonomy itself.

Candidate derivation:

**Protected Self-Direction -> Protected Deliberative/Cognitive Domain**

subject to legitimate conflict/restriction rules.

### 3.3 BL3 — Preserve Safe Spaces

BL3 requires environments in which participants can live, learn, create and cooperate without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.

The Charter already recognises safe civil context as including:

- physical spaces;
- digital spaces;
- informational spaces;
- communication environments;
- protected contexts required during vulnerability.

CWA already establishes conceptual/informational protected contexts.

CPS is therefore a specific decompression of safe-space protection where the relevant protected context is participant cognition.

If no private cognitive context can exist because authority may inspect thought whenever technically possible, a core safe-space function is lost.

### 3.4 BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity

BL7 states that intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.

Compelling or extracting cognition solely as an evidential/resource input can risk treating the participant as an information source to be mined rather than a rights-bearing participant.

This does not make all cognitive access illegitimate.

It requires the participant's protected standing to remain present in the decision.

CPS therefore supports:

**Participant Mind != Mere Civil Resource**

### 3.5 BL5 — Act Fairly

Substrate neutrality engages fairness.

If biological memory receives practical protection because it is difficult to inspect while equivalent digital memory is freely searchable because it is technically legible, materially comparable cognitive functions receive unequal treatment based on implementation.

Candidate implication:

**Comparable Cognitive Function -> Comparable Protected Analysis**

Relevant differences may justify different implementation, but substrate alone should not manufacture weaker standing.

### 3.6 BL6 — Accept Responsibility

As civilisation gains greater technical capability to inspect, copy, infer or modify cognition, foreseeable consequences increase.

BL6 therefore supports increased responsibility accompanying increased cognitive-access capability.

**Greater Cognitive Access Capability -> Greater Responsibility For Its Consequences**

Capability does not itself create authority.

### 3.7 BL10 — Remain Open to Correction

CPS decisions can be wrong:

- state may be misclassified as cognition;
- cognitive relevance may be overstated;
- authority may be excessive;
- secondary use may drift;
- copies may persist;
- technical boundaries may be misunderstood.

BL10 supports provenance, review, contestability and correction.

## 4. Fundamental protected relations already present

The current Charter's strongest source-anchored layer contains several direct parents of CPS.

### Article 3 — Autonomy and Protection Against Unjustified Interference

CPS protects the internal process through which self-direction occurs.

Without some protection against unjustified cognitive interference, Article 3 can become nominal where cognition is technically accessible.

### Article 4 — Safe Civil Context

CPS is a substrate-neutral protected context.

It extends the existing recognition of physical, digital, informational and communication safe spaces into the cognitive function those environments may contain.

### Article 7 — Dignity and Non-Instrumentalisation

CPS prevents participants from being treated merely as inspectable evidential/computational resources.

### Article 9 — Contestability and Correction

Where cognitive access materially affects a participant, access authority, scope, provenance and resulting decisions require contestability/correction.

---

## 5. Strongly derived protection already present

### Article 10 — Privacy and Protected Civil Space

Article 10 states that privacy and private/safe-space protections are recurring decompressions of:

- autonomy;
- safe civil context;
- dignity;
- bounded authority.

It already applies across:

- physical space;
- communications;
- data;
- digital environments;
- compute;
- protected personal records.

Exceptional access should ordinarily be:

- purpose-limited;
- minimum necessary;
- provenance-recorded;
- bounded in duration and scope;
- reviewable.

This is substantially the same authority grammar independently reached by CPS.

Therefore CPS does **not** presently require invention of a new ethical root.

It is a high-specificity, cross-substrate application of Article 10 to cognition/internal participant state.

## 6. Loss test

The Rights Confidence and Elevation Model requires asking whether removing a protection materially defeats a source-anchored protected condition.

Assume no special protection for cognition.

A technically capable authority could then potentially:

- inspect participant memory without treating this as stronger than ordinary data access;
- bypass the right to silence by reading the state from which an answer would be generated;
- continuously monitor deliberation;
- search a neural prosthesis as an ordinary device;
- copy AI memory because it is technically legible;
- reconstruct distributed cognition from provider fragments;
- use Health cognitive access for unrelated investigation;
- compel participants to search their own cognition;
- preserve all digital memory indefinitely for possible future evidence.

In such a system:

- autonomy would be substantially weakened;
- safe cognitive context could disappear;
- dignity/non-instrumentalisation would be weakened;
- privacy would become substrate-dependent;
- right-to-silence protection could be technologically bypassed.

### Loss-test result

**PASS.**

Removing meaningful cognitive protection materially defeats existing protected relations under conditions where cognition becomes technically accessible.

This is stronger than saying CPS is merely useful.

CPS has already been stress-tested against:

- biological memory;
- AI episodic memory;
- neural prostheses;
- local digital state;
- remote cognitive extension;
- distributed cognition;
- third-party-hosted cognition;

The architecture's core rule remains:

> **Protection follows participant function rather than material substrate.**

This satisfies the Charter's substrate-neutral holder/standing direction better than a human-specific mental-privacy formulation.

### Result

**Cross-substrate validity: STRONG.**

**Technical Capability != Legitimate Authority**

**Device Search Authority != Cognitive Search Authority**

**Health Acquisition Authority != Investigative Reuse Authority**

**Authority To Preserve != Authority To Inspect**

**Evidence-Space Incompleteness != Authority To Search A Mind**

**Provider Maintenance Authority != General Evidential Disclosure Authority**

These are compatible with existing BCA and Charter restriction rules.

### Result

**Authority-abuse resistance: STRONG.**

## 10. Simpler lower-level representation test

Could Article 10 privacy alone preserve the protection without naming cognition?

At present, partially.

Article 10 already provides the correct parent category.

However, generic privacy becomes increasingly ambiguous where:

- cognition is technically inspectable;
- cognition is remote;
- cognition is distributed;
- cognitive state shares hardware with ordinary records;
- an AI's memory resembles a file;
- copying cognition may instantiate another participant;
- a device contains both cognitive and non-cognitive contexts.

Without explicit cognitive interpretation, ordinary “data” or “device” reasoning can silently collapse the protected distinction.

Therefore a lower-level operational architecture remains necessary.

But this does **not** prove that CPS must become a new standalone fundamental Article.

### Result

**Article 10 is the correct constitutional parent; CPS is presently the correct specific decompression/implementation.**

## 11. Current confidence classification

### Not recommended

**Do not presently elevate CPS as a new independent Fundamental / Source-Anchored Article.**

Reason:

The protected function is strongly derivable, but the exact boundaries of:

- cognition;
- inferred cognition;
- preservation;
- shared cognition;
- post-death cognition;
- duplication;
- deliberate evidential deletion;
- emergency compelled communication

remain under development.

Creating a new fundamental Article now could prematurely constitutionalise unresolved implementation/boundary choices.

### Recommended

Recognise **Cognitive Privacy and Integrity** explicitly within the **Strongly Derived Civil Rights and Protections** layer as a specific high-confidence decompression of Article 10 and Articles 3, 4 and 7.

Keep **Cognitive Protected Space** itself as the operational/contextual architecture implementing that protection.

This gives the hierarchy:

**BL1 + BL2 + BL3 + BL5 + BL6 + BL7 + BL10**
↓
**Autonomy + Safe Civil Context + Dignity + Contestability**
↓
**Privacy and Protected Civil Space**
↓
**Cognitive Privacy and Integrity**
↓
**Cognitive Protected Space**
↓
**Operational access / preservation / review mechanisms**

## 12. Candidate Charter language

A narrow addition to Article 10 is sufficient at this stage.

Candidate text:

> **Cognitive Privacy and Integrity.** Where state or process materially constitutes or supports a participant's cognition, memory, reasoning, preference formation or continuity, its technical inspectability, digital form, remote location or third-party hosting does not by itself remove protected privacy or create legitimate access authority. Access, copying, compelled processing, monitoring or alteration of such cognitive state requires independently justified and appropriately bounded authority consistent with this Charter.

Supporting interpretation:

> **Substrate != Rights Class.**

> **Technical Inspectability != Legitimate Cognitive Access.**

> **Device Search Authority != Cognitive Search Authority.**

> **A participant's mind does not become less private because civilisation becomes better at reading it.**

This language does not constitutionalise the entire CPS implementation.

It records the protected relation while leaving operational detail corrigible.

---

## 13. Right-to-silence relationship

The Charter currently protects expression/communication and privacy but does not yet contain a fully derived explicit right-to-silence Article.

CPS should not silently constitutionalise one through the back door.

However, the Law development demonstrates an important compatibility requirement:

**A protected refusal to express cognition should not be made meaningless merely by direct technical extraction of functionally equivalent cognitive state.**

This should remain a Law/Judiciary development proposition until the right-to-silence derivation itself is separately source-resolved/elevated.

---

## 14. Derived/inferred cognition remains separate

The CPS stress test exposed high-fidelity inference of beliefs/preferences from external behaviour.

This source resolution does not classify such inferred models as direct cognition.

**Inference About Cognition != Direct Access To Cognition**

A future Derived / Inferred Cognitive Privacy architecture may itself derive from Article 10, autonomy, dignity and fairness.

It should not be silently folded into CPS before testing.

---

## 15. Emergency and restriction

The Charter already states:

**Necessary infringement does not extinguish the underlying right.**

CPS fits this exactly.

A legitimate emergency may justify temporary cognitive intervention.

That does not mean cognitive protection disappears.

Any exceptional access should remain:

- function-specific;
- necessary;
- proportionate;
- minimum sufficient;
- time-bounded;
- provenance-recorded;
- reviewable;
- open to correction/remedy.

**Emergency != New Source Of Cognitive Sovereignty**

---

## 16. Remedy

The Charter already supports:

- cessation;
- correction;
- restoration;
- reconsideration;
- record correction;
- compensation;
- support;
- other appropriate remedy.

CPS adds domain-specific possible harms but does not require a new constitutional remedy class.

Operational remedies may include:

- ending access;
- containing copies;
- restoring altered state;
- correcting derivative records;
- health/continuity repair;
- compensation/support;
- accountability;
- system correction.

Thus:

**CPS Remedy Architecture -> Existing Effective Corrective Response**

rather than a new fundamental remedy right.

## 18. Recommended constitutional action

1. Preserve Blainey's Laws unchanged.
2. Preserve Articles 3, 4, 7 and 9 unchanged.
3. Add a bounded **Cognitive Privacy and Integrity** subsection to Article 10.
4. Reference CPS as the current operational architecture, without making every CPS implementation detail constitutional.
5. Continue testing:
   - inferred cognition;
   - urgent preservation;
   - preservation duties;
   - duplication/continuity;
   - emergency compelled communication.
6. If later elevation to the fundamental layer is proposed, require a dedicated elevation test and preferably independent/blind derivation.

---

## 19. Conclusion

Cognitive Protected Space is not an imported technological right looking for an ethical justification.

Its protected function is already latent in the Concord's existing derivation.

It emerges when the established protections of:

- autonomy;
- safe civil context;
- dignity/non-instrumentalisation;
- privacy;
- non-harm;
- fairness across substrates;
- bounded authority;
- contestability

are applied to a civilisation in which cognition may become technically inspectable.

The appropriate present classification is therefore:

> **Cognitive Privacy and Integrity — Strongly Derived Civil Protection**

implemented through:

> **Cognitive Protected Space — Operational Contextual Wrapper Architecture**

The central constitutional interpretation is:

> **A participant's cognitive protection follows the function of the mind, not the material difficulty of accessing it.**
