# 1. Experimental boundary

This is the first Concord-rights derivation using the actual Concord Ethical Kernel.

Only the ten principles of Blainey's Laws are used as ethical input:

1. Avoid Unnecessary Harm
2. Respect Autonomy
3. Preserve Safe Spaces
4. Act Honestly
5. Act Fairly
6. Accept Responsibility
7. Respect Life and Dignity
8. Steward the Commons
9. Seek Peaceful Coexistence
10. Remain Open to Correction

Later Unified Paths kernels and the wider Concord rights corpus are excluded from this blind phase.

The purpose is not to produce attractive rights language. It is to test what civil claims, standing, duties, protections, immunities or constraints actually decompress from Blainey's Laws.

# 2. BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm

**Source principle:** “Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.”

## Protected condition

Participants should not be subjected to harm that can reasonably be avoided where a less harmful adequate course exists.

The principle does not prohibit every harm. It directly introduces necessity and comparison among available courses.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL1-A — Candidate right against unnecessary harmful interference

A participant subjected to harmful action appears to have a claim that avoidable harm not be imposed without sufficient reason.

**Holder:** affected participant.  
**Duty bearer:** actor/institution imposing or materially causing harm.  
**Claim:** where adequate alternatives exist, prefer the less harmful course.  
**Limits:** necessity, competing harms, feasibility, uncertainty and consequences to others.

### BL1-B — Standing to challenge claimed necessity

Because the ethical distinction is between necessary and unnecessary harm, an affected participant appears to require standing to challenge whether the harmful course was actually necessary or whether a materially less harmful adequate alternative existed.

**Classification:** candidate procedural standing/right.

### BL1-C — Least-harm constraint on legitimate intervention

Where intervention is otherwise legitimate, its form and intensity should remain bounded by the least harmful adequate course.

**Classification:** likely authority constraint rather than freestanding right.

# 3. BL2 — Respect Autonomy

**Source principle:** “Recognise the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.”

This principle is unusual within the kernel because it explicitly uses the word **right**.

## Protected condition

Participants possess a protected sphere of self-direction.

The principle itself also supplies a limit: autonomy is **subject to the ethical minimum**.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL2-A — Right to self-direction

This is directly stated rather than merely inferred.

**Holder:** “others” capable of directing their own lives; exact participant/capability boundary remains to be specified.  
**Duty bearer:** other participants and institutions capable of overriding that direction.  
**Claim:** legitimate self-direction within the ethical minimum.  
**Limits:** the other kernel principles and conflicts among protected participants.

### BL2-B — Candidate immunity from arbitrary control

Respect for self-direction implies that another actor's superior power, capability or institutional position does not alone create legitimate authority to direct the participant's life.

**Classification:** candidate immunity/authority constraint.

### BL2-C — Candidate requirement for meaningful consent where another actor relies on consent to override or enter the participant's autonomous sphere

Consent is not named in the principle, so this remains a derived candidate rather than an explicit kernel right.

**Unresolved:** conditions for meaningful consent, capacity, coercion, dependency, withdrawal and emergency exceptions.

---

# 4. BL3 — Preserve Safe Spaces

**Source principle:** “Maintain environments in which individuals can live, learn, create and cooperate without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.”

## Protected condition

Individuals require environments in which ordinary life, learning, creation and cooperation can occur without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.

This is stronger than a general aspiration to safety because it identifies specific conditions whose preservation has civil value.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL3-A — Candidate right to protected civil space

An individual appears to have a claim to access or preservation of some context in which they can conduct legitimate life activity without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.

**Status:** candidate positive/negative mixed right.  
**Unresolved:** whether this means a universal right to a specific physical place, a protected contextual condition, or both.

### BL3-B — Claim against coercive or violent invasion of legitimately protected space

Where a safe/protected context has been legitimately established, participants appear to have a claim against unjustified coercive or violent intrusion.

**Classification:** candidate contextual right.

### BL3-C — Civil duty to maintain conditions of safe participation

The wording “maintain environments” appears to generate positive stewardship duties as well as restraints.

**Classification:** duty/institutional function; not automatically an individually unlimited entitlement.

---

# 5. BL4 — Act Honestly

**Source principle:** “Truthful communication is fundamental to trust.”

## Protected condition

Civil cooperation should not depend upon intentional material falsehood or deception where truthful communication is ethically required.

The principle does not state that every participant must disclose every fact to everyone.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL4-A — Candidate right against material deception in consequential civil relations

Where another participant or institution bears a responsibility to communicate truthfully, an affected participant may have a claim not to be intentionally materially deceived.

**Duty type:** negative/relational.  
**Limits:** privacy, confidentiality, uncertainty, legitimate non-disclosure and contexts where no duty to disclose exists.

### BL4-B — Candidate claim to honest representation by authority

Where an institution justifies consequential action to a participant, deliberate fabrication or materially deceptive representation appears incompatible with the kernel.

**Classification:** procedural/institutional right or authority constraint.

## Non-derived claims

BL4 alone does not generate:
- unlimited transparency;
- universal access to information;
- abolition of confidentiality;
- a guarantee of factual infallibility.

---

# 6. BL5 — Act Fairly

**Source principle:** “Comparable situations should be treated comparably.”

## Protected condition

Civil treatment should not vary arbitrarily between materially comparable situations.

The principle does not require identical treatment where relevant differences exist.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL5-A — Candidate right to comparable treatment in materially comparable circumstances

**Holder:** participant subject to civil treatment/decision.  
**Duty bearer:** actor/institution responsible for that treatment.  
**Claim:** relevantly comparable cases should not receive materially different treatment without relevant justification.

### BL5-B — Standing to challenge arbitrary differential treatment

If a participant can identify a materially comparable case treated differently, they appear to require standing to ask what relevant distinction justified the difference.

**Classification:** procedural standing/right.

### BL5-C — Relevant-difference rule

Different treatment may be fair where a relevant material difference exists.

**Classification:** interpretive constraint, not a right.

## Unresolved

The principle does not itself specify every relevant comparison class or every legitimate distinction. Those cannot be invented during blind derivation.

---

# 7. BL6 — Accept Responsibility

**Source principle:** “Capability creates responsibility for foreseeable consequences.”

## Protected condition

Actors with capability should not externalise foreseeable consequences of their actions merely because they possess the power to act.

This principle is primarily phrased as a duty.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL6-A — Standing of foreseeably affected participants

Where a capable actor's conduct creates foreseeable consequences for another participant, the affected participant appears to acquire standing to have those consequences considered.

**Classification:** standing/protection, not yet necessarily a freestanding right.

### BL6-B — Candidate claim to accountable consequence-bearing

Where a capable actor foreseeably causes wrongful harm or burden, affected participants may have a candidate claim that responsibility not simply disappear at the point of consequence.

**Unresolved:** exact remedy is not specified by BL6 alone.

### BL6-C — Capability-responsibility constraint

Greater relevant capability can create greater responsibility; capability is not by itself a source of greater entitlement or authority.

**Classification:** ethical/civil constraint.

## Important asymmetry

BL6 naturally generates **duties more strongly than rights**. It should not be forced into one-right-per-law symmetry.

# 8. BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity

**Source principle:** “Intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.”

## Protected condition

Intelligent participants have inherent value independent of their instrumental usefulness to another actor or institution.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL7-A — Candidate right to recognition as an end-bearing participant

An intelligent participant appears to have a claim not to be treated solely as an instrument, asset, resource or disposable means for others.

**Classification:** foundational dignity right/protection.

### BL7-B — Candidate protection against instrumental sacrifice

Collective benefit or another actor's objective cannot automatically erase the participant's inherent value.

**Limits:** BL7 does not by itself resolve every conflict where harms or lives compete.

### BL7-C — Candidate standing independent of utility

A participant's civil standing should not depend solely upon economic productivity, usefulness, popularity or service to the system.

**Classification:** standing principle / anti-instrumentalisation constraint.

## Critical unresolved boundary

The source wording says **“Intelligent participants.”** The blind derivation must preserve that wording. It cannot silently replace it with every living organism, every personhood theory, or a later definition of sentience.

The definition and recognition of “intelligent participant” is therefore a major later constitutional-development question.

# 9. BL8 — Steward the Commons

**Source principle:** “Shared resources should be managed so that present prosperity does not unnecessarily diminish future opportunity.”

## Protected condition

Present use of shared resources should not unnecessarily destroy future opportunity.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL8-A — Standing of materially affected users of shared resources

Participants materially dependent upon a shared resource appear to have standing where stewardship decisions threaten their legitimate opportunity or access.

**Classification:** standing.

### BL8-B — Candidate protection against unnecessary destructive depletion of shared resources

Current users should not manage shared resources in ways that unnecessarily diminish future opportunity.

**Classification:** stewardship protection/duty; right-holder structure may be distributed or intergenerational.

### BL8-C — Candidate future-opportunity protection

Future participants' opportunity has ethical weight even where the future holder is not presently identifiable.

**Classification:** protected condition / intergenerational duty rather than conventional present individual right.

## Non-derived claims

BL8 does not establish:
- that all resources are common property;
- equal shares of every resource;
- prohibition of present prosperity;
- an absolute prohibition on depletion.

# 10. BL9 — Seek Peaceful Coexistence

**Source principle:** “Differences are natural, but peaceful coexistence should remain the preferred objective.”

## Protected condition

Difference itself should not automatically become grounds for domination, forced conformity or conflict.

The principle prefers peaceful coexistence; it does not state that every disagreement can or must be reconciled into uniformity.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL9-A — Candidate protection of peaceful difference

Participants whose differences remain compatible with the ethical minimum appear to have a claim not to be subjected to coercive conformity merely because they differ.

**Classification:** candidate right/immunity.

### BL9-B — Candidate claim to peaceful handling of civil disagreement where feasible

Where legitimate alternatives to violence or coercive conflict exist, participants may have a claim that civil systems prefer peaceful resolution.

**Classification:** procedural/institutional candidate.

### BL9-C — Coexistence over uniformity constraint

Civil architecture should not treat uniformity as an end in itself where peaceful coexistence remains possible.

**Classification:** constitutional/design constraint.

## Unresolved

BL9 does not independently specify rights to expression, association, religion, political organisation, secession or cultural autonomy. Those may later derive through decompression or compound interaction, but must not be inserted at this stage.

# 11. BL10 — Remain Open to Correction

**Source principle:** “No intelligence is infallible; beliefs and actions should remain open to better evidence and reasoning.”

## Protected condition

Consequential belief and action should not become correction-proof merely because an actor or institution currently holds authority or confidence.

## Candidate civil relations

### BL10-A — Candidate right to challenge consequential error

Where a belief or action materially affects a participant, that participant appears to have a candidate claim to a viable route for presenting better evidence or reasoning.

**Duty type:** procedural.

### BL10-B — Candidate right to correction/reconsideration after material error is established

If materially consequential belief/action is shown to be wrong, openness to correction implies more than merely hearing the challenge; some appropriate reconsideration must be possible.

**Classification:** procedural/institutional candidate.

### BL10-C — Anti-infallibility constraint on authority

No intelligence or institution should be treated as structurally incapable of error.

**Classification:** constitutional/epistemic constraint.

### BL10-D — Candidate preservation of dissent/evidence sufficient for correction

A correction system cannot function if contrary evidence or reasoned challenge is automatically erased merely because it loses initially.

**Classification:** possible procedural protection; exact scope unresolved.

# 12. Initial cross-law observations

These are observations from the derivation itself, not comparison with later Concord architecture.

## 12.1 The kernel already contains one explicit rights statement

BL2 directly says to “recognise the right of others to direct their own lives.” Autonomy therefore has unusually direct rights provenance.

## 12.2 The laws produce heterogeneous civil objects

The derivation does not yield ten equivalent rights.

It yields:
- rights;
- standing;
- immunities;
- positive duties;
- stewardship duties;
- authority constraints;
- procedural requirements;
- protected conditions.

This supports preserving the topology rather than forcing a conventional rights list.

## 12.3 Several procedural rights appear independently

BL1, BL5 and BL10 each generate reasons for challenge/review:
- was harm necessary?
- was differential treatment justified?
- can error be corrected?

This may later fuse into a general contestability architecture, but fusion must occur in the next phase.

## 12.4 Dignity changes the aggregation problem

BL7 means participant value cannot be represented solely as contribution to aggregate outcomes. This potentially constrains interpretations of harm, fairness, responsibility and stewardship.

## 12.5 Peaceful coexistence protects difference without yet defining all liberties

BL9 strongly resists forced uniformity, but the blind phase does not justify silently translating that into a complete catalogue of expression, religion, association or political rights.

## 12.6 The Commons principle is explicitly temporal

BL8 directly introduces future opportunity. The rights architecture therefore cannot be purely contemporaneous.

## 12.7 Correction is itself part of the ethical kernel

Contestability is not merely an implementation convenience. BL10 makes openness to better evidence/reasoning ethically foundational, while the exact civil mechanisms remain downstream and corrigible.

- safe-space maintenance duty;
- coexistence-over-uniformity constraint.

# 14. Important absences after root-by-root derivation

The blind derivation has **not yet** established complete freestanding rights to:
- property;
- privacy;
- expression;
- association;
- religion/belief;
- voting;
- citizenship;
- nationality;
- movement;
- work;
- family;
- secession;
- specific criminal procedure;
- specific welfare/services.

These absences must remain visible.

Some may emerge through cross-law fusion/decompression. Some may be implementation forms of more basic rights. Some may require civil information not uniquely contained in the kernel.

Absence at this stage is not rejection.
