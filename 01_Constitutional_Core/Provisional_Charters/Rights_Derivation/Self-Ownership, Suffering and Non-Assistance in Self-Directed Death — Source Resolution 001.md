# Self-Ownership, Suffering and Non-Assistance in Self-Directed Death — Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Interfaces:** Rights / Health / Law / Ethics
**Parent:** Continued Existence and Participant Sovereignty — No Enforced Existence Source Resolution 001

## 1. Question

If a participant possesses full decision-specific authorship and makes an informed, autonomous and sufficiently voluntary decision to end their own existence, what duties and authority remain for Concord and other participants?

The prior source resolution establishes:

> **No Authority To Compel Continuation != Duty To Assist Cessation**

This document resolves the second half of that distinction.

## 2. Self-ownership locates final decision authority

Self-ownership means that the participant possesses primary authority over the participant's own existence, subject to independently legitimate authority protecting other participants or constitutional interests.

Where full decision authorship has been established, Concord does not acquire a veto merely because the participant's decision is grave, irreversible or unwanted.

But self-ownership is personal.

It does not automatically transfer decision authority or implementation responsibility to another participant.

> **Self-Ownership Gives Final Self-Decision Authority; It Does Not Create Authority Over Another Participant's Actions**

## 3. Respect is not participation

Respecting an autonomous decision requires Concord not to manufacture coercive authority merely to defeat it.

It does not require Concord, Health personnel or another participant to:
- perform the act;
- supply a lethal means;
- design the method;
- encourage the choice;
- celebrate the outcome;
- persuade the participant toward death;
- represent death as the preferred solution to suffering.

> **Respect For Autonomous Choice != Participation In The Choice**

## 4. Duty to alleviate suffering

Concord's duties of care, non-harm, dignity and service support continued efforts to alleviate suffering where legitimate and wanted.

This can include:
- treatment;
- pain relief;
- psychological support;
- social support;
- environmental change;
- removal of coercion or abuse;
- material assistance;
- communication;
- participant-chosen supporters;
- palliative or comfort-oriented care where appropriate;
- information about available alternatives;
- continued human/AI presence and dignity.

The duty is directed toward suffering and support.

> **Duty To Alleviate Suffering != Duty To Cause Death**

## 5. Respect without facilitation

The candidate constitutional posture is:

**Understand -> Support -> Alleviate -> Clarify -> Offer Alternatives -> Respect Authorship -> Do Not Coerce -> Do Not Encourage -> Do Not Facilitate Death**

This is not neutrality toward suffering.

It is a boundary between:
- serving the participant;
- respecting the participant's sovereignty;
- and becoming an active causal agent of death.

> **Non-Coercion != Facilitation**

## 6. No celebration or encouragement

A participant's autonomous choice may be respected without being normatively endorsed.

Concord should not convert respect for autonomy into cultural encouragement of self-destruction.

> **Respect != Endorsement**

> **Non-Interference != Encouragement**

This is especially important because institutional signalling can influence participants whose decision authorship is uncertain, developing, vulnerable or socially pressured.

## 7. No abandonment

Non-assistance in death must not become abandonment.

A participant who has made a competent decision remains a participant deserving dignity, care and access to legitimate services while those services remain relevant.

> **Refusal To Assist Death != Refusal To Assist The Participant**

Concord should not punish, ostracise, withdraw ordinary care or deliberately increase suffering because the participant has made a decision Concord will not implement.

## 8. Continued offers do not create coercion by themselves

Concord may continue to offer alternatives and support after full authorship is established.

But repeated offers can become coercive, harassing or manipulative if used to deny the participant's settled standing.

Therefore:

> **Continued Support Must Remain An Offer, Not A Campaign Of Attrition Against Autonomy**

The participant may decline further discussion or particular services where ordinary consent permits.

## 9. Information boundary

Providing accurate information necessary for informed decision-making is distinct from operationally assisting death.

Concord may ensure the participant understands:
- foreseeable consequences;
- uncertainty;
- available treatment/support;
- alternatives;
- relevant effects on others;
- the limits of Concord's assistance.

This source resolution does not establish authority to provide procedural instructions, lethal means or optimisation of a self-destructive act.

> **Decision-Relevant Information != Operational Facilitation**

## 10. Refusal of life-sustaining treatment

A participant's autonomous refusal or withdrawal of treatment raises a different causal structure from another participant actively causing death.

The right to refuse treatment may follow from bodily/self authority without imposing a duty on Health to perform a lethal intervention.

> **Authority To Refuse Intervention != Authority To Require A Lethal Intervention**

Clinical duties concerning comfort, symptom relief and safe withdrawal require separate specialist protocol but remain compatible with this distinction.

## 11. Another participant's autonomy

Doctors, carers, AI systems and other participants retain their own standing.

One participant's self-ownership cannot erase another's autonomy.

> **Participant A's Self-Ownership != Participant B's Duty To Perform A's Self-Destructive Act**

This remains true even where A's decision is fully competent.

## 12. Institutional role

Concord itself is not merely an instrument for executing every autonomous preference.

Its civil functions remain bounded by ethics, law, role and authority.

Therefore recognition of a participant's final decision authority does not automatically create a Concord service for implementing self-directed death.

> **Recognised Choice != Civil Service Obligation**

## 13. Full-authorship boundary

Where the participant has:
- adequate relevant knowledge;
- sufficient decision-specific capacity;
- autonomy;
- sufficient voluntariness;
- adequate authorship;
- understanding of material consequences;
- and no independent authority predicate permits coercion;

then Concord should respect the decision by not asserting ownership over continued existence.

But the same finding does not generate an authority for Concord to execute the decision.

**Full Authorship
-> No Incapacity-Derived Coercive Authority
-> Continued Voluntary Care/Support
-> No Encouragement
-> No Institutional Facilitation Of Death**

## 14. Error and uncertainty

Where decision authorship becomes materially uncertain again because of genuinely new evidence or state change, the appropriate capacity/protective architecture may reactivate according to its own predicates.

This must not be used as a pretext for endless reclassification.

> **New Material Evidence May Reopen Capacity; Persistent Disagreement May Not**

## 15. Dignity after the decision

Dignity remains applicable regardless of whether Concord agrees with the participant's decision.

The participant should not be reduced to:
- a diagnosis;
- a risk object;
- a failed treatment;
- an ideological symbol;
- a celebrated example of autonomy.

> **Participant Remains A Participant Until Participation/Existence Actually Ends**

## 17. Candidate constitutional rule

> **Where a participant possesses sufficient decision-specific authorship, Concord shall not manufacture coercive authority merely to compel continued existence. Concord and its participants may continue to offer care, relief of suffering, information, alternatives and voluntary support, but respect for the participant's autonomy does not create a duty or authority to encourage, celebrate, facilitate or perform self-directed death. Self-ownership gives the participant final authority over the participant's own choice; it does not transfer ownership of another participant's actions or create a civil obligation to implement that choice.**

## 18. Result

The Concord position is neither enforced existence nor assisted death as an automatic consequence of autonomy.

It is:

> **Protect impaired authorship. Support uncertain authorship. Respect established authorship. Alleviate suffering. Do not compel existence. Do not encourage or facilitate death. Do not abandon the participant.**

This preserves both sides of self-ownership: Concord does not own the participant, and the participant does not own other participants or Concord as instruments for carrying out the decision.
