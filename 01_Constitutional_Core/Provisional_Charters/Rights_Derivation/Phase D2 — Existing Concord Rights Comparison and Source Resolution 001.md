# 1. Purpose

The blind derivation is frozen. This phase now compares it against existing Concord architecture without rewriting the frozen result.

For each existing protection, classify it as one or more of:

- **D1 — DIRECT:** directly stated by a Blainey's Law.
- **D2 — COMPOUND-DERIVED:** follows from interaction of multiple Blainey's Laws.
- **D3 — DECOMPRESSION:** a more specific civil expression of a frozen right/protection.
- **D4 — IMPLEMENTATION:** mechanism required to make a frozen protection effective.
- **D5 — HOLDER/SCOPE ARCHITECTURE:** determines who holds/exercises protection and in what context.
- **D6 — ADDITIONAL CIVIL ASSUMPTION:** requires premises not supplied by the Ethical Kernel alone.
- **D7 — PRESENTLY NOT DERIVABLE:** existing Concord proposition whose ethical derivation is not yet established.
- **U — UNRESOLVED.**

These classifications are developmental, not constitutional findings.

# 3. Privacy, private space and non-interference

## Existing Concord sources

### Basic Civil Space Classification
`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Governance_and_Stewardship/Basic Civil Space Classification — Public Space, Private Space, Safe Space and Substrate-Neutral Access.md`

Existing development distinguishes:

- public-space presumption of access;
- private-space presumption of non-access without permission;
- participant control of private/safe space;
- protected data, communication, compute and physical domains;
- privacy/non-interference without requiring proof of human-like subjective privacy experience;
- fundamental protection separated from material level of provision;
- functional safe spaces for temporary vulnerability.

It explicitly proposes:

> **A private or safe civil space is a recognised domain over which a participant has presumptive control of access and interaction, subject to the equal rights of others and narrowly justified lawful authority.**

### Citizen ID privacy architecture
`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Governance_and_Stewardship/Concord Citizen ID — Unique Civil Identity, Digital Routing and Privacy Boundaries.md`

Existing development separates:
- civil identity;
- authentication;
- routing;
- physical location;
- service activity;
- security evidence.

It states that official reachability does not automatically justify physical traceability and develops minimum-necessary information, purpose limitation and provenance for exceptional access.

## Comparison

The frozen derivation did not independently name a general “privacy right.”

However, privacy/private-space protections are strongly explainable as specific civil decompressions of:

- **FCR-01 autonomy** — participant control and non-interference;
- **FCR-02 safe civil context** — protected context for living/creating/cooperating;
- **FCR-05 dignity** — participant not reduced to an observable/manipulable resource;
- **FCI-01 least-harm bounded authority** — intrusion requires justification and bounded means.

### Classification

**D2 COMPOUND-DERIVED + D3 DECOMPRESSION + D4 IMPLEMENTATION**

### Finding

> **Privacy need not be inserted as a new ethical root merely because it was not separately named by the blind derivation. Existing Concord privacy architecture can plausibly be understood as a concrete protection generated by autonomy, safe space, dignity and bounded authority.**

The exact scope of a general provisional privacy right remains to be formulated and tested.

# 4. Freedom of movement and access

## Existing Concord source

The Basic Civil Space work proposes a general freedom of movement while explicitly distinguishing:

> **Freedom of Movement != Universal Right of Entry**

Access may depend on:
- space category;
- purpose;
- current condition;
- safety;
- role;
- qualification;
- authorisation;
- identity confidence.

## Comparison

The Ethical Kernel does not directly state freedom of movement.

Possible derivation:
- FCR-01 autonomy provides the strongest base;
- FCR-04 fairness constrains arbitrary exclusion;
- FCR-06 peaceful difference may constrain exclusion based solely on non-conformity;
- FCR-02 safe civil context supports access to civil life but does not itself create universal movement.

A general movement liberty therefore appears plausible as a **decompression of autonomy in shared civil space**, but the exact territorial/public-access scope requires additional civil assumptions about what spaces civilisation provides and who constitutes the relevant public.

### Classification

**D3 DECOMPRESSION + D6 ADDITIONAL CIVIL ASSUMPTION**

### Finding

Freedom of movement is not yet safely labelled a direct kernel right. Its specific civil form requires later derivational testing.

# 5. Consent

## Existing Concord sources

`00_Front_Door/4 Need, Consent and Legitimate Authority.md`

and:

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Governance_and_Stewardship/Peaceful_Heterogeneity/PH-01 — Boxing — Bounded Consent and Contextual Harm.md`

Existing Concord distinguishes:
- meaningful consent from compliance;
- participation consent;
- action consent;
- risk consent;
- outcome consent;
- coercion and dependency;
- capacity and understanding;
- temporal validity;
- withdrawal;
- bounded delegated authority;
- cases where legitimate action cannot depend on immediate consent from every affected participant.

## Comparison

Consent is not itself named as a Blainey's Law.

It arises primarily because **BL2 Respect Autonomy** requires a way to distinguish self-directed acceptance from imposed control.

BL1 and BL7 constrain what consent can legitimise.

Existing Concord correctly treats consent as bounded rather than magical.

### Classification

**D3 DECOMPRESSION + D4 IMPLEMENTATION of FCR-01**, with compound constraints from FCR-05 and harm minimisation.

### Finding

> **Consent is an important operational/legitimating expression of autonomy, but the comparison does not justify treating consent as an unlimited standalone ethical root or as the sole source of legitimate authority.**

# 6. Meaningful exit and withdrawal

## Existing Concord source

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Sketch_Ideas/Sketch Idea — Participant-Serving Civilisation, Temporary Authority and Persistent Exit.md`

Existing hypothesis:
- Concord exists for participants;
- authority should be bounded by need/function;
- persistent meaningful exit may be an anti-domination constraint;
- surviving duties and third-party consequences remain unresolved.

Related citizenship/fork work also preserves questions of exit, multiple citizenship, secession and peaceful distance.

## Comparison

A meaningful exit protection can plausibly derive from:
- FCR-01 autonomy;
- FCR-06 peaceful difference;
- FCR-05 dignity;
- FCI-03 coexistence over forced uniformity.

But exit from a specific consensual relation, exit from citizenship, territorial secession, withdrawal from a service, and escape from an immediate authority are not the same object.

Third-party obligations can survive exit.

### Classification

**D2 COMPOUND-DERIVED at the anti-domination level; D6 ADDITIONAL CIVIL ASSUMPTION for specific political/citizenship/secession forms.**

### Finding

A general principle against making legitimate participation irrevocable appears derivable. A universal unconditional “right to exit everything” is not.

# 8. Personhood, standing and capacity

## Existing Concord source

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Machine_Ethics/Personhood, Capacity, Fiduciary Support and Derived Responsibility Across Substrates — Development Note 001.md`

Key distinctions include:

> **Personhood != Capacity**

> **Capacity != Competence**

> **Competence != Authority**

> **Authority != Personal Worth**

> **Impairment != Loss of Standing**

> **Support != Ownership**

> **Guardianship != Permanent Control**

The work develops function/context/time-specific capacity and preserves standing during impairment.

## Comparison

This directly addresses a major unresolved holder problem in FCR-01 and FCR-05.

Blainey's Law 7 says **intelligent participants** possess inherent value. The blind derivation deliberately did not define that class.

Existing personhood/capacity research does not replace BL7. It supplies candidate **holder/scope architecture** and safeguards against turning capability differences into standing differences.

### Classification

**D5 HOLDER/SCOPE ARCHITECTURE**, with strong support from FCR-01/FCR-05/FCR-04.

### Finding

> **Capacity may affect mode of exercise without automatically erasing underlying standing.**

This is strongly supported by existing Concord development, but exact personhood/intelligent-participant criteria remain unresolved.

# 9. Citizenship and membership

## Existing Concord source

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Concord_Nation_Foundation/05_Citizenship_and_Membership/README.md`

It explicitly distinguishes:

> **Personhood != Citizenship.**

> **Membership in the Concord project != citizenship in a future Concord polity.**

It also identifies rights that should not depend upon citizenship.

## Comparison

Citizenship is not generated as an ethical root or blind-derived fundamental right.

It is a civil status architecture governing relationship to a polity.

Fundamental standing under BL7 and protections such as autonomy cannot safely be assumed to arise only after citizenship.

### Classification

**D5 HOLDER/SCOPE ARCHITECTURE + D6 ADDITIONAL CIVIL ASSUMPTION**

### Finding

The provisional rights architecture should distinguish:
- protections arising from participant standing;
- protections arising from presence/jurisdiction;
- citizenship-specific rights/functions;
- membership/project relations.

It should not use “citizen” as an accidental synonym for “rights holder.”

# 10. Judicial challenge, appeal, evidence and practical access

## Existing Concord source

`02_Domains/05_Judiciary/Judiciary V2 Canonical.md`

The Judiciary develops:
- challenge to exercises of power;
- rights adjudication;
- evidence contestability;
- ordinary appeal;
- constitutional review;
- remedies;
- articulable reasons;
- uncertainty;
- meaningful participation;
- practical accessibility;
- standing;
- correction and learning.

It explicitly treats evidence materially affecting judgement as ordinarily contestable by affected parties and distinguishes legitimate confidentiality from unreviewable secrecy.

It also recognises that a formally existing right may be practically nullified by barriers such as inability to:
- understand the process;
- reach the court;
- afford participation;
- communicate effectively;
- safely challenge authority;
- obtain necessary evidence;
- receive timely remedy.

## Comparison

This is a strong implementation/decompression of:
- FCR-07 contestability/correction;
- FCR-04 fair comparable treatment;
- FCR-03 honest consequential dealing;
- FCR-01 protection from unjustified authority.

Appeal and judicial review are not necessarily themselves the deepest ethical rights. They are mechanisms through which the more fundamental contestability protection becomes real.

### Classification

**D3 DECOMPRESSION + D4 IMPLEMENTATION**

### Finding

> **Nominal access != effective access.**

A future provisional rights charter should probably protect effective contestability while allowing Judiciary architecture to specify the mechanisms, rather than constitutionalising every current judicial procedure.

# 11. Remedy

Existing Judiciary architecture repeatedly treats remedy as a necessary output where violation is established.

The blind derivation froze contestability/correction but did not separately freeze a universal remedy right.

## Comparison

A protection that can be violated with no possible correction may become practically hollow.

However, remedy form depends upon:
- what was violated;
- whether restoration is possible;
- jurisdiction;
- competing rights;
- responsibility;
- proportionality.

### Classification

**D4 IMPLEMENTATION of FCR-07 and affected substantive rights**, potentially a later compound procedural right.

### Status

**U — further derivation required before deciding whether “right to effective remedy” should become a separate provisional charter article or remain an effectiveness property of protected rights.**

# 12. Property

Existing reviewed material refers to property, private land and protection against unlawful destruction/transfer, but the current comparison has not established a complete Concord theory of property as a fundamental right.

Safe/private-space control must not be silently equated with ownership.

Likewise, BL8 stewardship of commons does not establish universal collective ownership.

### Classification

**U / D6 ADDITIONAL CIVIL ASSUMPTION**

### Finding

Property requires dedicated source resolution before charter inclusion.

Do not derive a general property right merely from the existence of private/safe space.

# 13. Political participation and voting

The Ethical Kernel can constrain governance through:
- autonomy;
- fairness;
- dignity;
- peaceful difference;
- contestability;
- responsibility.

But those constraints do not uniquely specify:
- voting;
- electoral form;
- franchise;
- representation;
- direct democracy;
- delegated governance;
- triadic decision-making.

### Classification

**D6 ADDITIONAL CIVIL ASSUMPTION / U**

### Finding

A right to some form of legitimate civil voice or participation may later be derivable, but **voting must not be reverse-engineered into a kernel right merely because it is a familiar governance mechanism.**

Dedicated Governance/Participation source resolution remains required.

# 17. Structural finding

The comparison supports a layered rights topology:

```
Blainey's Laws
      ↓
Fundamental Protected Relations
      ↓
Specific Civil Liberties / Protections
      ↓
Effective-Access Requirements
      ↓
Operational Mechanisms
```

Example:

```
Respect Autonomy
+ Preserve Safe Spaces
+ Respect Life and Dignity
      ↓
Autonomy / Safe Civil Context / Dignity
      ↓
Privacy / Private-Space Control
      ↓
Purpose Limitation / Minimum Necessary Access / Challenge
      ↓
Identity, information, wrapper and judicial mechanisms
```

This avoids two opposite errors:

1. treating every operational mechanism as a fundamental constitutional right;
2. keeping rights so abstract that participants cannot actually exercise them.

# 18. Provisional distinction for later charter design

A future provisional charter may need at least four layers:

## Layer A — Fundamental Participant Protections
Direct/compound ethical derivations.

## Layer B — Specific Civil Liberties and Rights
Decompressions necessary to make Layer A meaningful in recurring civil contexts.

## Layer C — Effective-Access Guarantees
Contestability, accessibility, standing and remedy properties needed to prevent nominal rights.

## Layer D — Constitutional Constraints and Duties
Limits on authority, stewardship obligations and protections that do not map cleanly to individual rights.

This is a developmental topology, not yet a charter structure.

# 20. Current conclusion

The existing Concord rights material is **not simply a second competing rights system**.

Much of it appears to be a deeper decompression and operationalisation of the protections independently derived from Blainey's Laws.

But some familiar civil categories require assumptions beyond the Ethical Kernel alone.

Those categories must remain visibly distinct until their derivation is established.

> **Existing Concord Practice != Automatic Ethical Derivation.**

> **Familiar Right != Kernel-Derived Right.**

> **Specific Civil Protection may legitimately decompress a deeper ethical right without becoming a new ethical principle.**

The comparison remains active.
