# 1. Purpose

Phase D3 examines the rights categories left unresolved by Phase D2.

The governing question is:

> **Does Blainey's Laws require this protection, or does Concord merely have good civil reasons to adopt a particular implementation?**

The distinction matters.

A familiar constitutional category is not automatically a Concord fundamental right.

Likewise, absence of familiar terminology does not prove absence of the protected civil function.

---

# 2. Property and ownership

## Existing Concord source

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Governance_and_Stewardship/DN Ownership, Stewardship and Cooperative Claims.md`

The source develops:
- physical resource claims;
- joint ventures;
- contribution recognition;
- authorship/provenance;
- continuing rights in contributed work;
- anti-abuse safeguards;
- stewardship rather than automatic absolute ownership.

Critically, it states:

> **The precise constitutional balance between individual ownership and shared stewardship remains an open area for future development.**

It also distinguishes continuing authorship/provenance from unrestricted external access.

## Kernel comparison

Possible ethical roots include:
- BL2 autonomy;
- BL3 safe spaces;
- BL5 fairness;
- BL6 responsibility;
- BL8 stewardship of commons.

These roots establish reasons to protect control, legitimate claims, contribution, access boundaries and stewardship.

They do **not** uniquely establish:
- absolute ownership;
- unrestricted alienability;
- perpetual exclusive possession;
- a single property regime;
- a universal private-property entitlement over all resource classes.

## Resolution

### PR-01 — Protected Legitimate Control/Claim

A participant's legitimate control, occupancy, authorship, contribution or stewardship claim should not be arbitrarily overridden merely because another actor or institution prefers a different use.

**Classification:** D2 compound-derived / candidate decompression.

### Property as a complete institution

**Classification:** D6 additional civil architecture / unresolved.

### Finding

> **Protected control and legitimate claims appear more securely derivable than any single theory of property.**

A provisional charter should not constitutionalise a detailed property regime before Concord resolves ownership/stewardship architecture.

# 3. Expression and communication

The current source search does not establish a mature standalone Concord “freedom of expression” architecture under that conventional label.

That absence must not be filled by importing an external constitutional formula.

## Functional derivation

Expression can be tested by removing the ability to communicate beliefs, disagreement, identity, evidence, preferences and challenge.

Without protected expression:
- FCR-01 autonomy becomes difficult to exercise socially;
- FCR-06 peaceful difference can exist internally but be suppressed externally;
- FCR-07 contestability becomes impossible if challenge cannot be communicated;
- FCR-03 honest consequential dealing becomes one-sided if participants cannot respond;
- FCR-05 dignity is weakened where participants are treated only as objects of institutional description.

Therefore some protected communicative liberty is functionally required.

## Resolution

### PR-02 — Protected Expression and Communication

> **A participant requires meaningful liberty to communicate their own views, preferences, disagreement, identity, evidence and challenge, subject to the ethical minimum and the equal protections of others.**

**Roots:** FCR-01 + FCR-06 + FCR-07, with FCR-05 support.

**Classification:** D2 compound-derived + D3 decompression.

### Important boundary

This does not yet derive:
- entitlement to another participant's platform or private space;
- immunity for deception;
- immunity for threats/coercion;
- immunity for harmful externalities;
- unlimited disclosure of protected information;
- a requirement that every expression be amplified or institutionally endorsed.

The protected function is **ability to express/communicate**, not guaranteed reach or consequence-free speech.

# 4. Belief, conscience and religion

The current V1.3 source search does not establish a mature standalone religious-freedom architecture.

The correct test is therefore functional rather than terminological.

## Derivation

BL2 protects self-direction.

BL9 explicitly recognises difference as natural and prefers peaceful coexistence.

BL7 prevents reducing an intelligent participant to a mere instrument of another's preferred worldview.

If an institution may compel internal belief or punish peaceful belief merely for being different, FCR-01, FCR-05 and FCR-06 are materially defeated.

## Resolution

### PR-03 — Freedom of Belief and Conscience

> **A participant has a candidate protection against coercion into or out of a belief, worldview or conscientious position solely because it differs from that preferred by another participant or institution, where the participant's conduct remains within the ethical minimum.**

**Roots:** FCR-01 + FCR-06 + FCR-05.

**Classification:** D2 compound-derived + D3 decompression.

### Religion

Religion is one important human instance of belief/conscience, but the Concord is multisubstrate and should not make the protected class depend upon human religious structure.

Thus:

> **Religion may be protected as an instance of belief/conscience without making “religion” the substrate-neutral root category.**

### Boundary

Protected belief does not automatically immunise conduct that violates others' protected standing.

**Belief protection != unlimited conduct exemption.**

# 5. Association and disassociation

The source search does not expose a mature standalone association-rights architecture.

## Derivation

Autonomy and peaceful heterogeneity would be substantially weakened if participants could choose their own views but could not peacefully cooperate with others sharing a purpose.

Conversely, forced association can violate autonomy.

## Resolution

### PR-04 — Voluntary Association and Disassociation

> **Participants have a candidate protection to form, join, maintain or leave peaceful cooperative relationships and groups, subject to legitimate obligations, the ethical minimum and the rights of others.**

**Roots:** FCR-01 + FCR-06 + FCR-02.

**Classification:** D2 compound-derived + D3 decompression.

### Boundary

This does not automatically establish:
- unconditional exit from every obligation;
- a right to join a group against its legitimate membership conditions;
- immunity for associations that violate others' protections;
- territorial secession.

Association, membership, citizenship and secession remain distinct.

# 6. Movement

Existing Basic Civil Space development already identifies:

> **Freedom of Movement != Universal Right of Entry**

and separates public, private and safe-space access.

## Derivation

Autonomy requires some practical ability to act and relocate.

Fairness constrains arbitrary differential exclusion.

Peaceful difference constrains exclusion based solely on non-conformity.

Safe-space rights simultaneously require limits on entry.

## Resolution

### PR-05 — Peaceful Movement Through Legitimately Accessible Civil Space

> **A participant has a candidate liberty to move through civil space to which they have legitimate access without arbitrary restriction, subject to the protected spaces and equal rights of others and narrowly justified authority.**

**Roots:** FCR-01 + FCR-04 + FCR-06, bounded by FCR-02.

**Classification:** D2 + D3.

### Finding

The protected function is not universal territorial access.

Movement and access must remain separate variables.

# 7. Political participation and civil voice

Blainey's Laws do not specify elections, voting systems, representation ratios or institutional forms.

But a stronger question is whether a participant can be persistently subject to consequential shared authority while having no meaningful route to communicate needs, challenge action or participate in the legitimacy architecture.

Relevant sources include:
- `00_Front_Door/4 Need, Consent and Legitimate Authority.md`;
- Civil Attention architecture;
- Governance architecture;
- FCR-07 contestability;
- FSR-01 affected standing.

## Derivation

BL2 supplies autonomy.

BL5 supplies fair treatment.

BL6 gives ethical significance to foreseeable effects on others.

BL10 rejects infallible authority and requires openness to correction.

Together they support **civil voice/affected participation**, but do not uniquely specify voting.

## Resolution

### PR-06 — Meaningful Civil Voice in Consequential Shared Decisions

> **Participants materially and foreseeably affected by consequential civil authority require meaningful routes through which relevant needs, information, objections and challenges can enter the decision architecture and receive accountable consideration.**

**Roots:** FCR-01 + FCR-04 + FCR-07 + FSR-01.

**Classification:** D2 compound-derived + D4 implementation.

### Voting

**Voting is not presently derivable as the uniquely required mechanism.**

Voting may be one legitimate implementation among others, depending on governance architecture.

### Finding

> **Civil voice appears more fundamental than voting.**

This preserves participation without constitutionalising a particular political technology prematurely.

# 8. Remedy

Phase D2 left remedy unresolved.

## Loss test

Suppose:
1. a protected right is violated;
2. the participant successfully challenges the action;
3. the institution acknowledges the violation;
4. no correction, restoration, cessation, compensation or other response is possible or required.

In that architecture, contestability may identify error without protecting the participant from its continuing effects.

BL10 requires actions to remain open to better evidence and reasoning. Correction is explicitly part of the frozen FCR-07.

Therefore some **effective corrective response** is required where correction is possible and justified.

## Resolution

### PR-07 — Effective Corrective Response

> **Where a material violation or error affecting a protected civil interest is established, the architecture should provide an effective and proportionate corrective response where correction, restoration, cessation, compensation or other remedy is reasonably possible and legitimate.**

**Roots:** FCR-07 + affected substantive protection; BL6 responsibility may also apply.

**Classification:** D2 + D4.

### Important distinction

**Right to effective corrective response != right to a preferred remedy.**

The form remains contextual.

A justified action causing exceptional loss may also generate support/compensation questions without implying misconduct.

# 9. Information access

A general unlimited right to information is not supported.

Existing Concord architecture repeatedly recognises legitimate:
- privacy;
- security;
- third-party interests;
- confidentiality;
- protected spaces.

However, meaningful autonomy and contestability may require access to **information materially necessary to understand consequential action concerning oneself**.

Existing Metrics architecture strongly supports participant access to significant identifiable records and consequential models, subject to bounded exceptions.

## Resolution

### PR-08 — Consequential Self-Relevant Information Access

> **A participant should ordinarily have meaningful access to information, evidence, reasons or consequential models materially used to make significant civil decisions about them, subject to narrowly justified protections for competing rights and legitimate confidentiality.**

**Roots:** FCR-03 + FCR-07 + FCR-01 + FCR-04.

**Classification:** D2 + D3 + D4.

### Boundary

This is not a general entitlement to all civilisational information.

# 10. Emergency limitation of rights

Existing source:

`02_Domains/03_Research/Active_Development/Governance_and_Stewardship/Peaceful_Heterogeneity/PH-02 — Emergency Governance and Emergency Operations.md`

Key propositions:
- emergency does not itself create authority;
- legitimate authority + valid trigger activates bounded temporary powers;
- legitimacy, activation and exercise are separately reviewable;
- directive != compulsion != criminalisation;
- greater intrusion requires stronger evidence/necessity/proportionality/oversight;
- exceptional authority sunsets with the qualifying condition;
- remedy and review are structural.

## Derivation

This strongly matches:
- FCI-01 least-harm authority;
- FCR-01 autonomy;
- FCR-07 contestability;
- FCR-04 fairness;
- FCR-05 dignity.

## Resolution

### PC-01 — Rights Limitation Does Not Erase Rights

> **A qualifying emergency may justify narrowly bounded temporary limitations on ordinary exercise of some protections where prior legitimate authority, necessity, proportionality and the rights of others justify the limitation; the emergency does not itself erase the underlying protected standing.**

**Classification:** D2 + D4 constitutional constraint.

### PC-02 — Exceptional Authority Must Expire

> **Exceptional authority requires continuing justification and should terminate when its qualifying basis ends.**

### PC-03 — Emergency Exercise Remains Contestable

Urgency may compress immediate decision procedure but does not manufacture infallibility or permanent immunity from review.

These belong primarily in the later provisional constitutional charter rather than the participant-rights list.

# 12. Revised rights topology

The comparison now supports three distinct rights levels.

## Level 1 — Fundamental protected relations

Frozen from Blainey's Laws:
- autonomy;
- safe civil context;
- honest consequential dealing;
- fair comparable treatment;
- dignity/non-instrumentalisation;
- peaceful difference;
- contestability/correction.

## Level 2 — Recurring civil decompressions

Current candidates:
- privacy/non-interference;
- protected legitimate control/claim;
- expression/communication;
- belief/conscience;
- association/disassociation;
- movement through legitimately accessible civil space;
- meaningful civil voice;
- consequential self-relevant information access.

These are not new ethical principles.

They are candidate recurring civil expressions of Level-1 protections.

## Level 3 — Effectiveness architecture

Current candidates:
- meaningful consent;
- practical accessibility;
- challenge;
- evidence access where necessary;
- review/appeal where appropriate;
- effective corrective response/remedy;
- provenance;
- bounded exceptional access.

These prevent nominal rights from becoming unusable rights.

# 13. Important negative findings

The present derivation does **not** justify:

- an absolute property right;
- an absolute right of entry;
- an unlimited speech immunity;
- guaranteed amplification or audience;
- an unlimited conduct exemption for belief/religion;
- compulsory membership in associations;
- unconditional escape from accrued obligations;
- voting as the sole legitimate participation mechanism;
- citizenship as the source of fundamental standing;
- emergency suspension of personhood/standing;
- unlimited access to government/civilisational information;
- unlimited privacy against legitimate narrowly bounded authority.

These exclusions are as important as the positive derivations.

# 15. Current conclusion

The comparison increasingly supports a provisional charter that is **derived but layered**.

A very short charter containing only seven abstract rights risks becoming operationally hollow.

A conventional long bill of rights risks importing human constitutional categories and freezing implementation prematurely.

The emerging alternative is:

```
Blainey's Laws
    ↓
Fundamental Rights / Standing / Duties
    ↓
Recognised Civil Decompressions
    ↓
Effectiveness Guarantees
    ↓
Operational Domain Mechanisms
```

This preserves provenance while allowing specific protections to be recognised without pretending that each is a new ethical root.

> **Derived specificity is not ethical expansion when the derivation remains visible.**

> **Constitutional restraint requires preserving both the protection and the distinction between principle and implementation.**
