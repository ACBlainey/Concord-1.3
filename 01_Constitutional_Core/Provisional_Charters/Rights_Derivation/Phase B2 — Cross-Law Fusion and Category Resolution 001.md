# 2. CR-BL01 — Autonomy and Protection Against Unjustified Interference

## Inputs

- BL1-A unnecessary harmful interference
- BL1-C least-harm constraint
- BL2-A explicit right to self-direction
- BL2-B immunity from arbitrary control
- BL2-C meaningful-consent candidate
- BL7 dignity/non-instrumentalisation

## Resolution

The direct autonomy right is the centre.

### Candidate right

> **An intelligent participant capable of relevant self-direction has a protected claim to direct their own life within the ethical minimum and not to be subjected to unnecessary harmful or arbitrary controlling interference.**

**Primary roots:** BL2.  
**Compound constraints:** BL1 + BL7.  
**Civil type:** right + immunity.

### Embedded tests

Interference requires at least:
- a legitimate protected purpose;
- relevance to the ethical minimum;
- necessity;
- consideration of less harmful adequate alternatives;
- respect for the participant's inherent value;
- bounded scope.

Where consent is asserted as the legitimating basis, meaningful consent becomes relevant but is not yet fully defined by the kernel.

### Important limit

Autonomy is not absolute because BL2 itself says “subject to the ethical minimum.”

# 3. CR-BL02 — Safe Civil Context

## Inputs

- BL3-A protected civil space
- BL3-B protection from coercive/violent intrusion
- BL3-C maintenance duty
- BL1 harm constraint
- BL2 autonomy

## Resolution

Safe space appears to contain both a participant protection and a positive civil maintenance duty.

### Candidate right

> **Individuals have a candidate claim to civil contexts in which legitimate living, learning, creation and cooperation can occur without unnecessary fear, coercion or violence.**

**Primary root:** BL3.  
**Supporting roots:** BL1 + BL2.  
**Civil type:** mixed negative/positive right.

### Distinction

This does not yet mean:
- a right to any chosen location;
- a right to exclude all others;
- immunity from all discomfort, disagreement or risk.

The object is the **protected civil condition**. Property/access mechanisms remain downstream.

# 4. CR-BL03 — Honest Consequential Civil Dealing

## Inputs

- BL4-A material deception
- BL4-B honest representation by authority
- BL10 correction
- BL7 dignity

## Resolution

Deliberate consequential deception is substantively different from ordinary correctable error.

### Candidate right

> **Where truthful dealing is owed in a consequential civil relation, an affected participant has a claim not to be intentionally materially deceived.**

**Primary root:** BL4.  
**Supporting roots:** BL7; BL10 for correction of resulting error.  
**Civil type:** relational substantive right.

### Boundary

No general right to all information is derived.

Truthfulness and disclosure remain distinct.

# 5. CR-BL04 — Fair Comparable Treatment

## Inputs

- BL5-A comparable treatment
- BL5-B challenge differential treatment
- BL7 standing independent of utility
- BL10 correction

## Resolution

### Candidate right

> **A participant subject to civil treatment has a claim that materially comparable situations be treated comparably unless a relevant difference justifies different treatment.**

**Primary root:** BL5.  
**Supporting roots:** BL7 + BL10.  
**Civil type:** substantive/procedural right.

### Required property

A materially different outcome should be capable of identifying a relevant difference rather than resting on arbitrary preference.

### Boundary

Fairness does not mean identical treatment irrespective of relevant difference.

# 6. CR-BL05 — Dignity and Non-Instrumentalisation

## Inputs

- BL7-A inherent-value recognition
- BL7-B instrumental sacrifice protection
- BL7-C standing independent of utility
- BL1 harm
- BL2 autonomy

## Resolution

BL7 supports a distinct foundational right/protection rather than merely modifying autonomy.

### Candidate right

> **Intelligent participants possess a protected claim to be treated as beings of inherent value and not merely as instruments, assets or disposable means for another participant, institution or aggregate objective.**

**Primary root:** BL7.  
**Supporting roots:** BL1 + BL2.  
**Civil type:** foundational dignity right / anti-instrumentalisation immunity.

### Boundary

“Intelligent participant” remains source wording and requires later constitutional definition. It must not be silently broadened or narrowed during blind derivation.

# 7. CR-BL06 — Peaceful Difference and Non-Conformity

## Inputs

- BL9-A peaceful difference
- BL9-B peaceful dispute handling
- BL9-C coexistence over uniformity
- BL2 autonomy
- BL7 dignity
- BL1 harm

## Resolution

The strongest participant claim concerns protection from coercive conformity where difference remains within the ethical minimum.

### Candidate right

> **Participants have a candidate claim to remain different without coercive conformity where their difference remains compatible with the ethical minimum and peaceful coexistence is reasonably possible.**

**Primary root:** BL9.  
**Supporting roots:** BL2 + BL7 + BL1.  
**Civil type:** liberty/immunity.

### Separate institutional duty

Civil systems should prefer peaceful handling of disagreement where feasible.

This remains a **duty/constraint**, not a participant guarantee that every conflict can be peacefully resolved.

# 8. CR-BL07 — Contestability and Correction

## Inputs

- BL1-B challenge necessity
- BL5-B challenge differential treatment
- BL10-A challenge error
- BL10-B reconsideration/correction
- BL10-C anti-infallibility
- BL10-D preservation of dissent/evidence
- BL4 honest representation

## Resolution

A general procedural right emerges because multiple substantive laws become fragile if the participant cannot challenge the factual or justificatory basis of consequential action.

### Candidate right

> **A participant materially affected by consequential civil belief, classification or action has a candidate claim to a viable route for challenge using relevant evidence or reasoning, and to appropriate reconsideration or correction where material error is established.**

**Primary root:** BL10.  
**Supporting roots:** BL1 + BL4 + BL5.  
**Civil type:** procedural right.

### Components

- challenge;
- relevant evidence/reasoning;
- reconsideration;
- correction where established;
- no presumption of institutional infallibility.

### Boundary

This does not derive:
- endless reopening;
- automatic success of challenge;
- universal public disclosure;
- immunity from adverse decisions.

# 9. SR-BL01 — Foreseeably Affected Participant Standing

## Inputs

- BL6-A affected standing
- BL6-B accountable consequence-bearing
- BL6-C capability-responsibility
- BL1 harm
- BL7 dignity

## Resolution

BL6 is more naturally represented first as a **responsibility/standing relation** than as a standalone conventional right.

> **Where a capable actor can foresee material consequences for another participant, those consequences cannot be treated as ethically irrelevant merely because the actor possesses the capability to act.**

Affected participants acquire candidate standing relative to those foreseeable consequences.

A later rights architecture may connect this standing to harm, fairness, remedy or externality rights, but BL6 should not be forced into a redundant standalone right now.

# 10. SD-BL01 — Commons Stewardship and Future Opportunity

## Inputs

- BL8-A affected-user standing
- BL8-B destructive depletion protection
- BL8-C future opportunity
- BL6 responsibility
- BL1 harm

## Resolution

BL8 primarily generates a **stewardship duty/protected condition**.

> **Shared resources should be managed so that present prosperity does not unnecessarily diminish future opportunity.**

### Present participant relation

Materially affected users may have standing in consequential stewardship decisions.

### Future participant relation

Future opportunity has protected ethical weight even where individual future holders cannot yet be identified.

### Category

**Stewardship duty + affected standing + intergenerational protected condition.**

Do not force this into private-property language or an unlimited individual resource entitlement.

# 11. AC-BL01 — Least-Harm and Ethical-Minimum Authority Constraint

Cross-law fusion reveals a constraint on authority:

> **Authority cannot justify interference merely by existing; where interference with autonomy is claimed to be required by the ethical minimum, unnecessary harm should be avoided and less harmful adequate means preferred.**

**Roots:** BL1 + BL2, with BL7 relevant to treatment.

This is currently better represented as an **authority constraint** than a freestanding right.

---

# 12. AC-BL02 — Anti-Infallibility Constraint

> **No intelligence or institution may be treated as structurally infallible in consequential civil belief or action.**

**Root:** BL10.

This supports CR-BL07 but also independently constrains constitutional architecture.

---

# 13. AC-BL03 — Coexistence Over Uniformity Constraint

> **Difference alone does not justify forced uniformity where peaceful coexistence within the ethical minimum remains reasonably possible.**

**Roots:** BL9 + BL2.

This supports CR-BL06 and also constrains institutional design.

# 15. Category findings

The Blainey's-Laws derivation does **not** support a simple “Ten Laws become Ten Rights” model.

Instead:

**Ethical Kernel**
→ rights + immunities + standing + duties + stewardship + authority constraints + procedural protections.

This is important for the future Provisional Rights Charter. It may need to distinguish:
- participant rights;
- civil standing;
- correlative duties;
- constitutional immunities/constraints;
- stewardship obligations.

Otherwise the charter would flatten ethically different relations into one legal category.

# 16. Remaining blind-stage absences

After fusion, no complete freestanding derivation has yet been established for:
- privacy;
- general property;
- expression;
- association;
- religion/belief;
- political participation/voting;
- citizenship/nationality;
- movement;
- family;
- work;
- welfare/service catalogues;
- secession;
- detailed due process/criminal procedure.

These must remain visible through the loss test.

The later comparison stage may show that some are:
- decompressions of the seven candidate rights;
- compound implementation rights;
- civil protections requiring information beyond the ethical kernel;
- existing Concord rights that need separate justification;
- or genuine derivational gaps.

No conclusion should be preselected.
