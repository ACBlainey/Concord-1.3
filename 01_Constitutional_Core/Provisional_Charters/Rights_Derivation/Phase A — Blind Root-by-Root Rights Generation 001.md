# 2. U1 — Protection From Unjustified Harm

## Protected condition

Participants should not be subjected to harm lacking sufficient ethical justification.

The protected condition is not absolute absence of harm. Some actions may impose harm, risk or burden where competing protected conditions make that unavoidable. The root therefore requires a distinction between **harm** and **unjustified harm**.

## Candidate standing

A participant exposed to actual, threatened or materially foreseeable harm appears to acquire standing to:
- resist unjustified harmful action;
- request protection;
- challenge the justification for imposed harm;
- seek review after harm;
- seek appropriate repair where violation occurred.

## Candidate civil claims

### U1-A — Claim against unjustified harmful interference
A participant may claim that another participant or institution should not impose avoidable or unjustified harm upon them.

**Duty bearer:** actors capable of imposing the harm.  
**Duty type:** primarily negative, with possible protective/institutional duties.  
**Context:** broad, but justification may vary with necessity, competing claims and consequence.  
**Limits:** does not imply immunity from every burden, risk, restraint or consequence.

### U1-B — Claim to protection where serious preventable harm cannot reasonably be self-avoided
Where a civil system possesses responsibility and legitimate capability to address serious preventable harm, affected participants may have a candidate claim to reasonable protection.

**Duty bearer:** not automatically everyone; requires identification of a legitimate responsible actor or institution.  
**Duty type:** positive/contextual.  
**Unresolved:** threshold of severity, foreseeability, feasibility and allocation.

### U1-C — Claim to challenge harmful action and its justification
If harm is imposed under claimed necessity or authority, the affected participant appears to require a viable route to challenge whether the harm was justified.

**Duty type:** procedural/institutional.  
**Potential remedy:** cessation, modification, review, restoration, compensation or other repair depending on context.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- General duty to reduce unnecessary harm.
- Protective safeguards where no participant-controlled claim exists.
- Permission to undertake consensual risk.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U1 alone does not define when harm is justified. Other roots will almost certainly constrain necessity, agency, evidence, accountability and repair.

# 3. U2 — Reality-Responsive Epistemic Integrity

## Protected condition

Consequential civil treatment should not depend upon knowingly false, fabricated, materially distorted or correction-resistant representations of reality.

The protected interest is not a guarantee that others will always be correct. Error is unavoidable. The stronger candidate protection concerns the integrity of processes by which consequential claims are formed, represented, challenged and corrected.

## Candidate standing

A participant materially affected by a factual claim, classification, representation or decision appears to acquire standing to:
- know enough about the consequential basis to challenge it where legitimate;
- contest material error;
- introduce relevant evidence;
- seek correction of demonstrably false or materially misleading representations.

## Candidate civil claims

### U2-A — Claim against deliberate material deception in consequential civil relations
Participants may claim not to be subjected to materially consequential deception by actors bearing relevant civil responsibility.

**Duty type:** negative.  
**Limits:** does not establish unlimited transparency; legitimate confidentiality and uncertainty may exist.

### U2-B — Claim to epistemic contestability where a factual representation materially affects the participant
Where a consequential decision relies on a representation of a participant or relevant reality, the affected participant may have a claim to a viable challenge/correction path.

**Duty type:** procedural/institutional.  
**Access requirement:** enough information to identify and contest the consequential proposition without automatically requiring unrestricted disclosure.

### U2-C — Claim that material uncertainty not be falsely represented as certainty
A participant materially affected by a decision may have a claim against institutional presentation of known uncertainty as settled fact where that distortion changes consequential treatment.

**Duty type:** epistemic/procedural.  
**Unresolved:** thresholds for materiality and legitimate simplification.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- General duty to seek truth.
- Scientific or institutional standards not individually enforceable in every case.
- Universal access to all information.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U2 appears especially likely to combine with U6 for correction/remedy and U3 where false representation becomes a mechanism of domination.

# 4. U3 — Protected Agency and Non-Domination

## Protected condition

Participants capable of meaningful agency should retain legitimate control over their own choices and should not be placed under arbitrary, unlimited or unjustified control by others.

Protection may remain even where mode of exercise varies with capability.

## Candidate standing

A participant whose choices, body, substrate, actions, access or life-course are being controlled by another appears to acquire standing to:
- resist unjustified control;
- require justification for imposed authority;
- choose where the decision legitimately belongs to them;
- withdraw from consensual arrangements where withdrawal is legitimately available;
- challenge domination or authority exceeding its function.

## Candidate civil claims

### U3-A — Claim to self-direction within the participant's legitimate sphere
A participant may claim a protected sphere of self-directed choice where exercising that choice does not itself justify overriding competing protected conditions.

**Duty type:** negative/immunity-like.  
**Limits:** scope depends on effects on others, capability, context and legitimate obligations.

### U3-B — Claim against arbitrary or functionally unjustified domination
No actor should acquire general control over another merely through power, dependency, capability or institutional position.

**Duty type:** negative/constitutional constraint.  
**Potential form:** may ultimately be partly an immunity rather than only a right.

### U3-C — Claim to bounded justification of authority
Where authority is exercised over a participant, the participant may claim that its legitimate function, scope and limits be capable of justification.

**Duty type:** procedural/institutional.  
**Unresolved:** what information and review are necessary in emergency or high-risk contexts.

### U3-D — Claim to meaningful consent where consent is the asserted basis of authority or interference
If an action is justified by participant consent, the participant may claim that consent not be reduced to mere compliance or nominal assent.

**Duty type:** relational/procedural.  
**Unresolved:** capacity, information, coercion, dependency, withdrawal and temporal validity.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- Unlimited freedom of action.
- Authority to impose one's preferences on others.
- Assumption that every participant exercises agency identically.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U3 strongly generates constraints on power but does not alone determine the complete legitimacy of collective authority or intervention.

# 5. U4 — Care, Reciprocity and Support Under Need

## Protected condition

Serious need, dependency or vulnerability should not become ethically invisible merely because the affected participant cannot independently solve it.

The root appears to create positive obligations, but it does not specify an unlimited entitlement to any desired resource or service.

## Candidate standing

A participant experiencing material need may acquire standing to:
- request support;
- have relevant need considered rather than ignored arbitrarily;
- receive support where a legitimate duty bearer, feasible capability and justified allocation exist;
- challenge support arrangements that unnecessarily erase agency.

## Candidate civil claims

### U4-A — Candidate claim to reasonable support under serious need
Where a participant faces substantial need and a legitimate civil support function exists, the participant may have a claim to reasonable support within feasible and fairly allocated capability.

**Duty type:** positive/contextual.  
**Limits:** feasibility, scarcity, competing need, responsibility and proportionality.

### U4-B — Claim that receiving support not automatically extinguish agency
Dependency or need should not by itself convert the recipient into an object of unrestricted control.

**Duty type:** negative/procedural.  
**Likely compound relation:** U4 + U3.

### U4-C — Candidate claim to non-arbitrary consideration of relevant need
Where a civil system allocates support, materially comparable needs should not be ignored without relevant reason.

**Duty type:** procedural/institutional.  
**Unresolved:** relationship to fairness principles not explicitly named as a U-root.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- Unlimited entitlement to all beneficial support.
- A duty on every participant to satisfy every need.
- Authority to force unwanted support merely because another actor judges it beneficial.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U4 generates strong positive-duty pressure but appears underdetermined without allocation rules, duty-bearer identification and agency constraints.

# 6. U5 — Responsible Resource Stewardship

## Protected condition

Shared or dependency-critical resources should not be used in ways that unjustifiably destroy the conditions needed by other legitimate users or future participants.

The root concerns stewardship, not automatic collective ownership of everything.

## Candidate standing

Participants materially dependent upon a shared or stewardship-governed resource may acquire standing to:
- challenge destructive or exclusionary use that threatens protected access or continuity;
- seek fair consideration in stewardship decisions materially affecting them;
- seek protection against depletion or capture that externalises severe consequences onto them.

## Candidate civil claims

### U5-A — Candidate claim against unjustified deprivation of dependency-critical shared resources
A participant should not be arbitrarily deprived of access to a resource necessary for protected civil function where the resource is legitimately shared or subject to stewardship obligations.

**Duty type:** negative/positive mixed.  
**Unresolved:** ownership, scarcity, allocation, necessity and legitimate priority.

### U5-B — Claim to consideration of materially affected resource interests
Where stewardship decisions impose significant consequences on affected participants, those interests should enter the decision process rather than being treated as nonexistent.

**Duty type:** procedural.  
**Unresolved:** whether this is a right, standing relation or governance duty.

### U5-C — Candidate protection of future opportunity against reckless depletion
Present actors may bear duties not to destroy shared resource conditions required by future participants.

**Status:** likely duty/protection first; individual right-holder structure is uncertain because future holders may not yet be identifiable.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- Absolute equal shares of every resource.
- Unlimited right to consume.
- Automatic abolition of private or local control.
- Treating every future interest as overriding present need.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U5 exposes a likely category where ethical duties and protections may exist without mapping cleanly onto conventional individual rights.

# 7. U6 — Accountability, Correctability and Repair

## Protected condition

Consequential civil action should remain attributable, challengeable and capable of correction; violation should not become final merely because authority has already acted.

## Candidate standing

A participant materially affected by a consequential action, decision or error appears to acquire standing to:
- know where responsibility lies sufficiently to seek review;
- challenge relevant decisions;
- seek correction;
- seek repair where a protected condition was wrongfully violated.

## Candidate civil claims

### U6-A — Claim to meaningful challenge/review of consequential civil action
Where a decision materially affects a participant's protected interests, there should be a viable route to challenge relevant error, misuse or unjustified action.

**Duty type:** procedural/institutional.  
**Limits:** review form and timing may vary with consequence, urgency and context.

### U6-B — Claim to correction of material civil error
Where a consequential record, classification or decision is shown to be materially wrong, the affected participant may claim appropriate correction rather than indefinite propagation of known error.

**Duty type:** procedural/positive.

### U6-C — Claim to appropriate remedy or repair following wrongful violation
Where a protected civil claim has been violated, a participant may have a claim to a remedy proportionate and relevant to the violation where repair is possible.

**Duty type:** positive/institutional.  
**Possible classes:** cessation, restoration, correction, compensation, rehabilitation, acknowledgment or other context-appropriate repair.

### U6-D — Claim against consequence without accountable provenance
Where coercive or otherwise serious civil consequences are imposed, affected participants may have a claim that responsibility and justificatory provenance not be intentionally made untraceable.

**Duty type:** procedural/institutional.  
**Limits:** does not imply public disclosure of all sensitive information.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- Punishment as an automatic response to every failure.
- Guarantee that every loss can be fully restored.
- Unlimited reopening of every settled matter.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U6 appears to generate the clearest institutional rights layer and may be necessary for making rights from other roots practically real.

# 8. U7 — Capability-Sensitive Development

## Protected condition

Participants should not be denied legitimate development, support or participation merely because capability is incomplete, uneven or developing; equally, capability-sensitive risk cannot simply be ignored.

The protected condition concerns development and appropriate modes of participation, not fictional equality of capability.

## Candidate standing

A developing or capability-variable participant may acquire standing to:
- have actual relevant capability considered rather than status labels alone;
- access reasonable developmental opportunity where a legitimate duty exists;
- use supported or adapted modes of exercising protected claims where feasible;
- challenge unnecessary permanent exclusion based solely on earlier incapacity.

## Candidate civil claims

### U7-A — Candidate claim to capability-sensitive mode of exercise
Where an underlying protection applies but independent exercise is limited, the participant may have a claim to an adapted or supported mode rather than automatic elimination of the protection.

**Duty type:** positive/procedural.  
**Limits:** feasibility, risk, consequence and actual capability.

### U7-B — Candidate claim to reasonable developmental opportunity
Where civilisation assumes responsibility for developing participants or provides developmental systems, participants may have a claim to reasonable opportunity to develop relevant capability.

**Duty type:** positive/contextual.  
**Unresolved:** scope, duty bearer and resource threshold.

### U7-C — Claim to reassessment where restrictions depend on capability
A restriction justified by insufficient capability should be capable of review when capability materially changes.

**Duty type:** procedural.  
**Likely compound relation:** U7 + U6.

### U7-D — Claim against status-based substitution for relevant capability assessment
Where a consequential decision turns on capability, inherited category or status alone should not automatically substitute for relevant functional evidence.

**Duty type:** procedural/epistemic.  
**Likely compound relation:** U7 + U2.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- Equal capability.
- Immediate access to every high-consequence function.
- Removal of legitimate competence requirements.
- A guarantee of successful development.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U7 strongly suggests that **capacity affects mode of exercise rather than automatically eliminating underlying protection**, but exact applicability will differ across rights and functions.

# 9. U8 — Adaptive Continuity of Cooperative Function

## Protected condition

Civil cooperation and essential shared functions should be capable of surviving change, disruption and correction without requiring participants to surrender all local agency or accept institutional permanence for its own sake.

The ethical object appears to be continuity of legitimate function rather than continuity of any particular institution.

## Candidate standing

Participants materially dependent on legitimate shared functions may acquire standing to:
- expect reasonable continuity/recovery of critical functions;
- resist arbitrary abandonment where a recognised duty exists;
- challenge institutional self-preservation that no longer serves the legitimate function;
- preserve relevant claims through transitions where feasible.

## Candidate civil claims

### U8-A — Candidate claim to reasonable continuity of dependency-critical civil function
Where a civil system has accepted responsibility for a critical function on which participants materially depend, abrupt unjustified abandonment may violate a candidate continuity claim.

**Duty type:** positive/institutional.  
**Limits:** feasibility, disruption severity, alternatives and legitimate transition.

### U8-B — Candidate claim to preservation of protected civil position through system transition
Changing mechanisms or institutions should not automatically erase valid participant claims, standing or unresolved remedies.

**Duty type:** procedural/continuity.  
**Unresolved:** which claims survive and under what successor architecture.

### U8-C — Candidate claim against institutional self-preservation as an end in itself
Participants should not remain subject to authority merely because an institution seeks its own continuation after its legitimate function has disappeared or can be better supplied without that authority.

**Status:** may be better represented as constitutional immunity/constraint than ordinary individual right.

### U8-D — Candidate claim to adaptive rather than brittle fulfilment
Where a protected function can no longer be delivered in its existing form, responsible systems should consider viable alternative means rather than treating mechanism failure as automatic termination of the protected function.

**Duty type:** positive/institutional.  
**Unresolved:** feasibility and minimum-function threshold.

## Adjacent concepts not yet rights

- Right to preservation of every institution.
- Guarantee of uninterrupted service under impossible conditions.
- Immunity from all social or technological change.

## Phase-A uncertainty

U8 may generate more institutional duties and continuity protections than conventional individual rights.

# 10. Phase-A observations — not yet fusion findings

Without comparing to the wider Concord rights corpus, several structural observations emerge.

## 10.1 Rights do not appear one-to-one with roots

Even at root-by-root generation, several candidate claims visibly require support from more than one root to become complete. This is consistent with, but does not prove, the frozen prediction of many-to-many derivation.

## 10.2 Some roots generate duties or constitutional constraints more naturally than individual rights

U5 and U8 especially produce stewardship and continuity obligations whose holder structure is not always obvious.

Forcing these into conventional individual-right language would lose information.

## 10.3 Procedural effectiveness emerges repeatedly

Challenge, reassessment, correction and remedy recur independently across U1, U2, U3, U6 and U7.

This suggests that a nominal protected claim without an exercise or correction route may be structurally incomplete. This remains to be tested in later phases.

## 10.4 Positive claims are conditional rather than unlimited

U4, U5, U7 and U8 generate candidate positive claims, but each requires duty-bearer, feasibility, scarcity, allocation, responsibility and/or context to be specified.

## 10.5 Capability does not naturally appear as a universal rights-off switch

The derivation repeatedly distinguishes the underlying protected condition from the mode by which it can safely or meaningfully be exercised.

## 10.6 Authority appears downstream

Nothing in Phase A naturally generates authority merely from capability, institutional existence or claimed benefit. Where authority may be required, justification appears dependent upon protected function, context, necessity and accountability.

# 11. Unresolved Phase-A questions

The following must remain open for Phase B/C rather than being solved by importing existing Concord answers:

1. Which candidate claims fuse into compound rights?
2. Which are actually immunities, standing relations, protections, entitlements or institutional duties?
3. Which positive claims survive duty-bearer and feasibility analysis?
4. How should future/non-identifiable participants be represented under U5?
5. What threshold makes a civil decision sufficiently consequential to generate U2/U6 procedural claims?
6. When can U1 harm prevention legitimately constrain U3 agency?
7. When can U8 continuity justify temporary burdens without becoming institutional self-preservation?
8. What common access/remedy structure, if any, is necessary across otherwise different rights?
9. Which candidate claims are redundant under the loss test?
10. Is the final rights architecture uniquely derivable, or does the kernel constrain several legitimate rights architectures?
