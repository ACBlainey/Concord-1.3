# Inalienable Participant Standing — Temporal Consent and Self-Ownership Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Constitutional Core / Cross-Domain Architecture  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CONSTITUTIONAL DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Question:** Can participant consent alienate the standing from which future autonomy, consent, contestability and remedy arise?

## 1. Purpose

Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal exposed a difficult edge case:

> A participant at T1 voluntarily agrees to become permanently owned, permanently unchallengeable, or otherwise incapable in civil terms of withdrawing from the authority created by that same consent.

The issue is not whether participants may make irreversible decisions.

The issue is whether consent can be used to create a **continuing authority relationship** whose legitimacy depends upon permanently eliminating the participant's future standing to contest that authority.

This document source-resolves that question against existing Concord constitutional sources.

It does not amend Blainey's Laws.

## 3. Ethical Kernel source

Three Blainey's Laws are directly relevant.

### Law 2 — Respect Autonomy

> Recognise the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.

This is not merely a preference for consensual transactions.

It identifies a continuing protected relationship between a participant and direction of their own life.

### Law 7 — Respect Life and Dignity

> Intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.

The value is described as **inherent**.

It is not described as:

- granted by institution;
- granted by citizenship;
- dependent on productivity;
- dependent on current capacity;
- transferable by contract;
- extinguished by prior consent.

### Law 1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm

> Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.

A standing-destroying relationship creates a structural risk that future harm cannot be meaningfully refused, challenged or corrected.

### Source-level implication

The Kernel supports:

**Participant Autonomy + Inherent Dignity + Non-Harm -> Protected Participant Standing**

The Kernel does not expressly use the word **inalienable**.

Therefore inalienability must remain a derived constitutional proposition rather than being silently inserted into the Ethical Kernel.

---

## 4. Rights Charter source

The Provisional Rights Charter provides stronger explicit support.

Its foundational relationship states:

> **The Concord serves its participants; its participants do not serve as property of the Concord.**

It also establishes:

> **Necessary infringement does not extinguish the underlying right.**

Article 2 provides:

- fundamental standing is not citizenship;
- capacity is not standing;
- reduced, developing, fluctuating or impaired capacity does not by itself erase underlying protected standing.

Article 3 provides:

> **Consent != Unlimited Authority.**

and requires temporal validity and viable withdrawal where a relationship is legitimately voluntary.

Article 7 provides that participants should not be treated merely as:

- instruments;
- assets;
- resources;
- disposable means;
- institutional inputs.

Article 9 establishes viable contestability for consequential civil belief, classification, decision or action.

Article 24 requires restriction to be:

- necessary;
- proportionate;
- no broader than reasonably required;
- no longer than reasonably required;
- open to appropriate challenge.

Article 26 provides:

> **Meaningful Exit != Erasure of Legitimate Surviving Obligations.**

and also:

> voluntary civil relationships should not ordinarily be made artificially irrevocable merely to preserve institutional control.

Surviving obligations must themselves remain legitimate, proportionate and contestable and should not be manufactured primarily to obstruct exit.

Article 27 states that even emergency does not:

- erase underlying participant standing;
- create a new source of sovereignty;
- create unlimited authority;
- permanently transfer power;
- abolish contestability.

### Charter implication

The Charter repeatedly distinguishes:

**restriction of exercise**

from:

**extinction of underlying standing**.

That distinction is central.

## 5. Self-Ownership source

The dedicated Self-Ownership derivation states:

> **A participant possesses primary legitimate authority over their own self and is not property or a general-purpose resource of another participant, institution or the Concord.**

It derives this from:

**Autonomy + Non-Harm + Life/Dignity -> Self-Ownership / Primary Self-Authority**

and explicitly states:

**Participant != Property Of Concord**

**Participant != Property Of Another Participant**

**Capability != General Claim Over That Capability**

**Citizenship != Ownership By Civilisation**

Its scope includes the participant's:

- embodiment;
- mind/cognitive process;
- labour;
- capabilities;
- movement and withdrawal;
- consent to ordinary use of person/capabilities.

It also explicitly preserves voluntarily accepted obligations.

Therefore self-ownership already rejects the false binary:

> either the participant is completely unconstrained, or another actor owns them.

Obligation is compatible with self-ownership.

Ownership of the participant is not required to enforce legitimate obligation.

---

## 6. Consent source

Need, Consent and Legitimate Authority distinguishes consent from authority.

The wider Concord architecture repeatedly establishes:

**Capability != Permission != Authority**

Temporal Consent adds:

**Consent != Obligation != Authority**

Therefore consent can be:

- one source of permission;
- one source of commitment;
- one contributor to obligation;

without becoming a universal source of unlimited authority.

This is important because permanent ownership attempts to make a finite consent event generate indefinite general authority.

---

## 7. Bounded Contextual Authority source

BCA establishes that authority should be:

- functional;
- contextual;
- minimum necessary;
- reviewable;
- subject to contraction;
- subject to sunset when justification ends.

Therefore:

**Past Authority != Permanent Authority**

A purported ownership relationship instead claims something structurally different:

> authority continues because the participant once consented that authority would never again require their consent, review or challenge.

That reverses BCA.

Instead of:

**Continuing Function -> Continuing Bounded Authority**

it becomes:

**Historical Consent -> Unlimited Persistent Authority**

The latter is not supported by BCA.

---

## 8. The key distinction — exercise versus standing

A participant can legitimately make decisions affecting future exercise of rights.

Examples:

- enter a contract;
- delegate authority;
- accept risk;
- transfer property;
- disclose information;
- undergo irreversible surgery;
- create a bounded commitment;
- accept a time-limited restriction.

These can be highly consequential.

But they do not necessarily transfer the participant's **underlying civil standing**.

Therefore:

**Exercise Of Right != Ownership Of Right-Holder**

and:

**Restriction Of Exercise != Extinction Of Standing**

This distinction is already implicit in the Rights Charter's emergency and restriction rules.

---

## 9. Alienation of a particular interest

Some interests may be alienable.

A participant may transfer:

- an asset;
- a licence;
- a contractual claim;
- a defined permission;
- a role;
- a delegated function.

The exact property regime remains unresolved.

This source resolution does not establish a general theory of alienability.

It asks only whether **participant standing itself** is transferable.

---

## 10. Participant standing

For this analysis, participant standing means the protected civil position from which a qualifying participant can, subject to legitimate limits:

- possess dignity;
- exercise autonomy;
- give or refuse consent;
- hold protected interests;
- challenge consequential authority;
- seek correction;
- seek remedy;
- be treated as a participant rather than property or a mere resource.

Standing is not identical to capacity.

A participant may temporarily lack capacity to exercise some decisions while retaining standing.

This follows Article 2.

Therefore:

**Capacity != Standing**

and:

**Exercise Capacity != Existence Of Protected Standing**

---

## 11. Inalienability candidate

The sources support the following candidate constitutional proposition:

> **A participant's fundamental protected standing is not an alienable civil asset. A participant may create legitimate obligations, permissions, commitments and bounded restrictions affecting future exercise, including some irreversible consequences, but consent alone cannot transfer or extinguish the underlying standing from which the participant remains a rights-bearing subject rather than property, a mere resource or an unchallengeable object of another's authority.**

This is **derived**, not a new Blainey's Law.

---

## 12. Why this is not paternalism

A rule against alienating standing could be misread as:

> civilisation knows better than the participant and therefore forbids serious choices.

That would contradict autonomy.

The distinction is narrower.

The participant remains free to make consequential and even irreversible decisions where legitimate.

Examples include:

- irreversible medical treatment;
- dangerous activity;
- permanent artistic modification;
- disclosure that cannot be recalled;
- long-term contract;
- bounded hard commitment;
- transfer of alienable property.

The protected boundary concerns a continuing authority relationship that makes the participant's future status equivalent to property or permanently eliminates civil contestability.

Therefore:

**Protection Of Standing != Prohibition Of Irreversible Choice**

and:

**Inalienability Of Standing != Inalienability Of Every Right Exercise**

---

## 13. Irreversible action versus continuing domination

Temporal Consent supplied the decisive distinction:

**Irreversible Consequence != Irrevocable Continuing Authority**

A completed irreversible act may have no continuing authority holder.

Example:

P validly chooses surgery.

The operation is completed.

The surgeon does not thereby acquire continuing sovereignty over P.

By contrast:

P signs a document declaring Q owns P forever.

The claimed authority exists only if Q continues to possess power over P after P withdraws.

The object is not merely an irreversible consequence.

It is a continuing domination relationship.

---

## 14. Self-cancelling consent

Permanent ownership creates a logical and constitutional circularity.

At T1:

P's consent matters because P has autonomy and standing.

At T2:

P withdraws.

Q replies that P's autonomy and standing no longer matter because P used them at T1 to eliminate them permanently.

Thus:

**Standing -> Consent -> Destruction Of Standing -> Permanent Authority**

The authority consumes the source that legitimised it and then claims to survive independently.

Candidate finding:

**Consent Cannot Derive Continuing Legitimacy From Permanently Invalidating The Standing That Made Consent Normatively Relevant**

---

## 15. Consent cannot bootstrap sovereignty

A participant may grant bounded authority.

But a grant does not automatically transform into sovereignty.

Therefore:

**Consent To Bounded Authority != Transfer Of General Sovereignty Over Participant**

and:

**Delegation != Alienation Of Personhood**

This follows Self-Ownership and BCA.

---

## 16. Anti-ownership conclusion

The existing Self-Ownership source is unusually direct:

**Participant != Property Of Another Participant**

A contract purporting to convert the participant into property conflicts with that protected relation.

The fact that the participant signed the contract does not change the object being created.

Therefore candidate rule:

> **A civil agreement may allocate obligations and bounded authority, but it cannot make a participant themselves the transferable property object.**

This remains provisional constitutional development because Self-Ownership itself is provisional/non-canonical.

---

## 17. Inherent dignity and alienability

Law 7 describes participant value as **inherent**.

If dignity could be fully alienated by contract, it would become contingent on whether the participant had previously sold or waived it.

That is difficult to reconcile with inherent value.

Candidate inference:

**Inherent Dignity != Dignity Existing Only Until Waived**

This does not mean every dignity-related interest is non-waivable.

It means the participant cannot cease to be a dignity-bearing subject merely through private agreement.

---

## 18. Contestability

Article 9 protects viable challenge.

Article 27 states emergency does not abolish contestability.

Article 26 requires surviving obligations to remain contestable.

This suggests contestability is not merely a convenience attached to voluntary relationships.

It is part of Concord's anti-error architecture.

A clause stating:

> “I permanently waive every future ability to challenge whether this authority remains legitimate”

would remove the correction mechanism required by the Charter.

Candidate principle:

**Agreement To A Decision Process != Permanent Elimination Of All Future Contestability**

This does not prohibit:

- deadlines;
- final judgments;
- bounded arbitration;
- procedural waiver;
- reasonable limitation periods.

Those are narrower procedural questions.

---

## 19. Correction and fallibility

Blainey's Law 10 states:

> No intelligence is infallible; beliefs and actions should remain open to better evidence and reasoning.

A permanently unchallengeable domination relationship would create an authority insulated from correction by the very participant most directly affected.

This does not independently prove a general right to revoke every agreement.

But it strengthens the case that **total permanent unreviewability** is inconsistent with the Kernel.

Therefore:

**Commitment Finality != Institutional Infallibility**

---

## 20. Necessary restriction does not extinguish standing

The Charter already handles the strongest counterexample: legitimate coercive restriction.

A participant may be:

- detained;
- restricted;
- subject to legal obligation;
- temporarily prevented from exercising autonomy.

Yet the Charter says the underlying right remains.

This means Concord already possesses the conceptual machinery required for inalienable standing:

**Exercise Can Be Restricted While Standing Persists**

There is therefore no need to treat standing as alienable merely to permit Law to operate.

---

## 21. Obligation without ownership

A participant can owe:

- money;
- performance;
- restoration;
- safe handoff;
- care;
- compliance with lawful adjudication.

None requires ownership of the participant.

Therefore:

**Obligation != Ownership**

**Enforcement Authority != Ownership**

**Liability != Transfer Of Personhood**

This preserves temporal responsibility while blocking domination.

---

## 22. Consent to slavery / permanent servitude

The existing sources support a provisional answer.

A participant may voluntarily undertake labour obligations.

A participant may enter long-duration service.

A participant may accept bounded exit conditions or handoff duties.

But a relationship whose defining legal object is ownership of the participant or permanent destruction of withdrawal/contestability is inconsistent with the existing Self-Ownership derivation.

Therefore:

**Voluntary Labour Obligation != Voluntary Slavery**

and:

**Long Duration != Ownership**

and:

**Consent Does Not Convert Participant Into Property**

Exact Law definitions of slavery, servitude and prohibited coercive labour remain downstream work.

---

## 23. Could a participant waive self-ownership?

Self-ownership is not best modelled as a detachable property title that the participant happens to own.

If it were, “self-ownership” could paradoxically justify sale of the owner.

The source derivation instead describes a **protected relation**:

> primary legitimate authority over one's own self and non-property status relative to others.

Therefore the better formulation is:

**Self-Ownership Is A Protected Participant Relation, Not A Transferable Title In The Participant**

This avoids importing ordinary property mechanics into personhood.

---

## 24. Better terminology

The phrase **self-ownership** remains useful in the existing source document.

But this source resolution exposes a possible interpretive hazard:

> ordinary ownership often includes alienation.

Participant self-ownership does not appear to mean the participant holds a transferable property title in themselves.

It means:

- primary self-authority;
- non-ownership by others;
- non-instrumental standing;
- protected autonomy;
- legitimate ability to create obligations without becoming property.

Therefore future constitutional drafting may prefer to pair:

**Self-Ownership / Primary Self-Authority / Non-Property Status**

rather than rely on “ownership” alone.

No rename is made here.

---

## 25. Standing floor

A useful model is a **standing floor**.

Above the floor, rights exercises may vary with:

- capacity;
- role;
- obligation;
- emergency;
- adjudication;
- consent;
- participation status.

But the participant remains above a minimum protected civil status:

- not property;
- not merely a resource;
- dignity-bearing;
- capable of protected interests;
- entitled to appropriate contestability;
- entitled to restoration of restricted exercise when legitimate restriction ends.

Candidate principle:

> **Legitimate restriction may alter what a participant may presently do without converting the participant into an object that another actor owns.**

---

## 26. Standing and capacity

The Charter already establishes:

**Capacity Is Not Standing**

This is crucial for temporal consent.

If reduced capacity erased standing, a guardian could acquire ownership-like authority.

Instead:

**Reduced Capacity -> Different Exercise/Support Architecture**

not:

**Reduced Capacity -> Transfer Of Participant Standing**

The same applies to developing participants.

---

## 27. Standing and citizenship

Article 2 establishes:

**Fundamental Standing != Citizenship**

Therefore leaving citizenship, losing membership or changing participation level cannot by itself transform a participant into property.

Likewise, joining Concord cannot constitute alienation of standing.

**Joining Concord != Transfer Of Self To Concord**

---

## 28. Standing and substrate

The Ethical Kernel uses intelligent participant rather than human.

The Rights Charter explicitly rejects substrate as the source of fundamental standing.

Therefore the candidate inalienability principle is substrate-neutral.

If an AI or hybrid qualifies as a protected participant:

**Technical Ownership Of Hardware != Automatic Ownership Of Participant**

**Creator Status != Automatic Ownership Of Participant**

**Hosting Dependency != Ownership Of Participant**

Exact resource/property relationships still require separate analysis.

---

## 29. Standing and copies/branches

Inalienability does not resolve who the participant is after:

- copying;
- branching;
- merger;
- restoration.

Identity/Continuity must first resolve the relevant holder(s).

But once protected participant standing attaches:

**Continuity Complexity != Permission To Treat Candidate Participant As Property By Default**

This is a cautious interface, not a final identity rule.

---

## 30. Can standing ever end?

This architecture does not answer when participant standing begins or ends.

Possible issues include:

- death;
- irreversible cessation;
- merger;
- dissolution;
- loss of qualifying participant status;
- multiple successors.

Those are Identity/Continuity questions.

The present conclusion is narrower:

> **While qualifying participant standing exists, private consent does not itself appear capable of converting that standing into transferable property status.**

---

## 31. Standing versus every right

The conclusion must not be over-expanded.

It does **not** establish that:

- every right is absolute;
- every right is non-waivable;
- every contract is revocable;
- every restriction is illegitimate;
- every obligation requires current consent;
- every action must remain reversible.

Instead:

**Inalienable Standing != Absolute Exercise Of Every Right**

This distinction should remain explicit wherever the principle is used.

---

## 32. Candidate constitutional relation

The source chain supports a provisional derived relation:

### Inalienable Participant Standing

> **A qualifying participant retains a fundamental protected civil standing as a rights-bearing subject rather than property or a mere instrument. Particular permissions, roles, claims, obligations and exercises of rights may be transferred, restricted, waived or bounded where legitimately justified, but the participant's underlying standing cannot be alienated by consent alone. No agreement may derive continuing general authority solely from treating the participant's future autonomy, dignity, contestability or non-property status as permanently extinguished.**

This is a candidate **Fundamental Protected Relation**, not a proposed eleventh Blainey's Law.

## 34. Loss test

Remove the candidate relation and ask what becomes possible.

Without inalienable standing:

1. a participant could contractually become property;
2. an institution could obtain permanent unreviewable authority through one consent event;
3. future refusal could be rendered legally meaningless;
4. dignity could become contingent on prior waiver;
5. contestability could be permanently abolished by agreement;
6. developmental or impaired participants could be exposed to ownership-like authority if capacity changes;
7. a voluntary civil relationship could become permanently involuntary solely because it once began voluntarily;
8. BCA sunset could be bypassed by historical consent.

These outcomes conflict with multiple existing source relations.

The candidate therefore passes the preliminary loss test strongly.

---

## 35. Counter-test — what becomes impossible if standing is inalienable?

A rule may be overbroad if it destroys legitimate autonomy.

Under the formulation above, participants can still:

- make irreversible choices;
- accept risk;
- enter contracts;
- incur debts;
- delegate authority;
- accept long-duration roles;
- create hard self-commitments;
- make advance directives;
- waive specific procedural opportunities where legitimate;
- transfer alienable property;
- accept lawful restriction.

Therefore the candidate does not require a general paternalistic veto over consequential choice.

The protected object is the **participant's status as a rights-bearing subject**, not freedom from consequence.

## 36. Interaction with Temporal Consent

Temporal Consent should therefore distinguish:

### Alienable temporal objects

Potentially:

- permissions;
- some claims;
- some property interests;
- some procedural opportunities;
- roles;
- delegated functions.

### Binding but non-ownership objects

- contracts;
- debts;
- care duties;
- safe-handoff duties;
- adjudicated obligations;
- bounded hard commitments.

### Non-alienable standing floor

- participant non-property status;
- inherent dignity-bearing status;
- underlying protected standing;
- minimum ability for legitimate contestability/review;
- restoration of ordinary exercise when temporary authority sunsets.

Exact content of the floor remains provisional.

## 37. Interaction with CMI

Cognitive modification creates a direct standing risk if an actor modifies P so that P can no longer meaningfully contest the actor's authority.

Therefore:

**Authority Must Not Manufacture The Consent Used To Justify Its Own Continuation**

is strengthened by:

**Authority Must Not Manufacture Loss Of Standing To Prevent Its Own Contestation**

A participant may voluntarily seek major cognitive transformation.

Again:

**Transformative Choice != Transfer Of Ownership**

---

## 38. Interaction with Behavioural Influence

Influence may alter preferences without formally removing rights.

But if an institution deliberately creates dependency or preference in order to make withdrawal nominal rather than meaningful, standing can be hollowed out operationally.

Therefore inalienable standing requires **effective**, not merely textual, exit and contestability where those protections apply.

---

## 39. Interaction with BCA

Inalienable standing does not compete with BCA.

It strengthens BCA's boundary.

Authority may temporarily override a particular exercise of autonomy.

But:

**Bounded Authority Acts Upon A Rights-Bearing Participant**

not:

**Bounded Authority Converts Participant Into An Owned Object**

When the legitimate function ends:

**Authority Sunsets -> Ordinary Protected Exercise Restores**

Standing did not need to be recreated because it was never extinguished.

---

## 40. Interaction with Law

Law will need to distinguish:

- valid commitment;
- invalid standing-alienation clause;
- severability;
- surviving legitimate obligations;
- remedy;
- coercive labour;
- slavery/ownership;
- specific performance;
- capacity;
- advance directive;
- guardianship;
- fiduciary authority.

An invalid ownership clause should not necessarily erase every otherwise legitimate part of an agreement.

**Invalid Standing-Alienation Clause != Automatic Erasure Of Every Independent Legitimate Obligation**

This requires later contract doctrine.

---

## 41. Interaction with Judiciary

Judiciary should be able to ask:

1. What did the participant validly agree to?
2. What obligation was created?
3. Does enforcement require treating the participant as property?
4. Does the claimed authority survive independently of present consent?
5. What legitimate source supports that survival?
6. Does the arrangement preserve participant standing and contestability?
7. Can a less rights-destructive remedy satisfy the legitimate obligation?

This preserves contract enforcement without converting it into ownership enforcement.

---

## 42. Interaction with Historical

Historical should preserve:

- original agreement;
- original consent state;
- amendments;
- withdrawal;
- challenge;
- adjudication;
- correction.

But:

**Historical Record Of Consent != Permanent Present Authority**

This follows the wider distinction between provenance and current status.

---

## 43. Interaction with participation

Participation status may affect role-specific entitlements.

It does not create or remove fundamental standing merely by membership classification.

Therefore:

**Participation Level != Ownership Status**

**Exit From Participation != Exit From Protected Participant Standing**

subject to the unresolved scientific/legal boundary of who qualifies as a protected participant.

## 46. Classification finding

The source resolution does **not** support creating a new Ethical Kernel principle.

It does support treating **Inalienable Participant Standing** as a candidate **Fundamental Protected Relation** downstream of existing Blainey's Laws.

It is best understood as a clarification/decompression of:

- Respect Autonomy;
- Respect Life and Dignity;
- Avoid Unnecessary Harm;
- Remain Open to Correction;

combined with the already-developed Self-Ownership / Primary Self-Authority relation.

This places it above ordinary contract doctrine and below the fixed Ethical Kernel.

---

## 47. Recommended constitutional treatment

The Provisional Rights Charter should eventually make explicit that:

1. fundamental participant standing persists through legitimate restriction;
2. consent can create obligations without creating ownership;
3. participant standing itself is not an ordinary alienable asset;
4. no private or institutional arrangement may derive unlimited continuing authority solely from a prior waiver of future standing;
5. irreversible choices remain possible;
6. the rule does not erase legitimate surviving obligations.

Because the Charter is provisional, this can be developed without altering the fixed Ethical Kernel.

A Charter amendment should follow adversarial testing of this source resolution rather than be inserted immediately.

---

## 48. Development conclusion

The Temporal Consent stress test exposed something deeper than a contract rule.

The Concord's existing sources already distinguish the **participant** from the participant's individual permissions, obligations, roles and rights exercises.

The participant remains the protected subject through:

- consent;
- contract;
- incapacity;
- restriction;
- emergency;
- participation change.

The emerging constitutional relation is therefore:

> **A participant may bind themselves in legitimate ways, but cannot by consent alone cease to be the rights-bearing participant to whom those bindings apply.**

Or more formally:

> **Participant standing is not an ordinary alienable civil asset.**

This preserves both sides of the temporal problem:

> **Autonomy includes the power to make consequential commitments.**

and:

> **Autonomy does not become a one-use power by which a participant can permanently convert themselves into another actor's property.**

The next development step should adversarially test this candidate protected relation before any amendment to the Provisional Rights Charter.
