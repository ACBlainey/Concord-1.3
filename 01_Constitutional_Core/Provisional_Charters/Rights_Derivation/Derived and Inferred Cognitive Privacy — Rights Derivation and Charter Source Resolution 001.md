# Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy — Rights Derivation and Charter Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Domain:** Constitutional Core / Rights Derivation / Law  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / RIGHTS SOURCE RESOLUTION / NON-CANONICAL  
**Ethical Authority:** Blainey's Laws only

## 1. Purpose

This document asks whether **Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy (DICP)** should be explicitly represented in the Provisional Rights Charter.

The question is:

> **Does protection of cognition also require protection against some high-fidelity or consequential models of cognition constructed without directly entering Cognitive Protected Space?**

The test must avoid two errors:

1. treating a model of a participant as though it literally were the participant's mind; or
2. allowing practical cognitive protection to be defeated through unrestricted reconstruction, profiling or exploitation of a cognitive proxy.

## 2. Existing constitutional source

Relevant current protections are:

- Article 3 — Autonomy and Protection Against Unjustified Interference;
- Article 4 — Safe Civil Context;
- Article 6 — Fair Comparable Treatment;
- Article 7 — Dignity and Non-Instrumentalisation;
- Article 9 — Contestability and Correction;
- Article 10 — Privacy and Protected Civil Space;
- Article 10.1 — Cognitive Privacy and Integrity;
- Article 12 — Belief and Conscience.

These form the constitutional source space for DICP.

## 3. DICP is not CPS

The first requirement is epistemic truth:

**Cognitive State != Model Of Cognitive State**

Article 10.1 protects cognition itself.

DICP concerns privacy, autonomy, fairness, dignity and contestability around models **about** cognition.

DICP should not declare inferred cognition equivalent to actual cognition.

## 4. Ethical derivation

### BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm

High-fidelity cognitive models can create foreseeable harms through vulnerability exploitation, manipulation, erroneous consequential classification, persistent inaccurate labels, self-fulfilling predictions and cross-system propagation.

BL1 supports minimising unnecessary cognitive-proxy construction/use where a less harmful adequate route exists.

It does not prohibit all inference.

### BL2 — Respect Autonomy

A cognitive model may be used not merely to understand a participant but to optimise how to alter participant behaviour.

The DICP stress test distinguishes:

**Participant-Directed Influence != External Behavioural Domination**

A system capable of identifying fears, vulnerabilities, ideal pressure timing, persuasion susceptibility or likely decision response can interfere with self-direction without directly reading cognition.

### BL3 — Preserve Safe Spaces

A cognitive proxy exists outside CPS.

However, deliberate reconstruction of protected contexts from external traces can undermine the protected-space function.

Candidate anti-circumvention interpretation:

> **A protected context should not be made functionally meaningless merely by deliberate technical reconstruction of what legitimate authority could not access directly.**

This does not prohibit ordinary external inference.

### BL5 — Act Fairly

DICP engages fairness where group correlations become individual cognitive facts, interface differences become impairment scores, model-shaped outcomes are treated as independent confirmation, or stale predictions become permanent status.

**Group Probability != Individual Belief**

**Interface Difference != Cognitive Deficit**

**Model-Induced Outcome != Independent Validation Of Model**

### BL6 — Accept Responsibility

**Greater Predictive Capability -> Greater Responsibility For Consequential Use**

High accuracy does not itself legitimise consequential use.

### BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity

Participants should not be treated merely as optimisation targets.

**Participant Model != Licence To Optimise Participant For Another Actor's Ends**

### BL10 — Remain Open to Correction

Inferred cognition is fallible and temporally unstable.

A participant may change preference or belief, disagree with the model, be misclassified, or be affected by the model itself.

DICP therefore requires provenance, uncertainty, temporal validity, challenge where consequential, and revision/rejection states.

## 5. Article 9 — Contestability

Article 9 is a direct parent of DICP.

Where a cognitive model materially affects services, rights, eligibility, employment, investigation, treatment or civil standing, the participant needs a viable route to challenge relevant claims and consequential use.

**Model Proprietary Interest != Automatic Defeat Of Consequential Contestability**

Contestability need not require disclosure of every model parameter.

## 6. Article 10 — Privacy

Article 10 is the primary constitutional parent.

DICP demonstrates that privacy questions can arise not only from collected source data but from newly constructed participant information.

**Low-Sensitivity Inputs != Low-Sensitivity Derived Output**

**Public Inputs != Unlimited Consequential Profiling Authority**

This is a decompression of informational privacy, not a new ethical root.

## 7. Article 10.1 relationship

DICP should sit adjacent to, not inside, Cognitive Protected Space.

Article 10.1 establishes that technical inspectability of actual cognitive state does not create authority.

DICP establishes the complementary rule:

> **Avoiding direct cognitive access does not automatically legitimise unrestricted reconstruction or consequential exploitation of a high-fidelity cognitive proxy.**

The constitutional distinction should be:

**10.1 Cognitive Privacy and Integrity** — protection of cognition itself.

**10.2 Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy** — protection governing sufficiently intimate, persistent, consequential or functionally substitutive models of cognition constructed from information outside CPS.

## 8. Article 12 — Belief and Conscience

A participant may never disclose a private belief while a system infers it from purchases, location, associations or behaviour.

**Participant-Expressed Belief != Inferred Belief**

**Ability To Infer Private Belief != Unlimited Authority To Profile Or Act Upon It**

Article 12 does not prohibit inference of belief, but strengthens protection against coercive or discriminatory consequential use.

## 9. Loss test

Assume direct cognition is protected but derived cognitive privacy is not recognised.

A sufficiently capable system could:

- leave CPS formally untouched;
- reconstruct beliefs, preferences and vulnerabilities externally;
- build persistent cognitive proxies;
- predict decisions at near-perfect accuracy;
- identify manipulation strategies;
- infer protected belief/conscience;
- propagate profiles across institutions;
- use old predictions as current identity;
- infer incapacity from interface difference;
- reconstruct protected consultation from metadata;
- deny services based on opaque cognitive models.

The formal defence would be:

> “We never read the participant's mind.”

while practical effects could approach direct cognitive access for some purposes.

**Loss-test result: PASS.**

Meaningful privacy, autonomy, dignity and contestability can be materially defeated without direct CPS penetration.

This does not support treating all inference as protected cognition.

## 10. Lower-level representation test

Existing Article 10 privacy, Article 9 contestability, CP-03 Prediction Does Not Grant Authority, Participant Digital Twin and Metrics safeguards already address parts of the problem.

But none explicitly records the distinction between:

**direct cognition**
and
**functionally powerful model of cognition**.

As cognitive modelling capability increases, this creates an interpretation gap.

**Result: explicit strongly derived recognition is justified.**

## 11. Cross-substrate and conflict testing

DICP has been tested against humans, AI participants, assistive interfaces, Digital Twins, deterministic systems, pseudonymous participants, public-source inference, group inference and high-fidelity behavioural prediction.

It preserves:

- ordinary interpersonal inference;
- case-specific evidential reasoning;
- necessary assistive inference;
- participant-directed support;
- Health prediction;
- aggregate planning;
- anonymised/non-linkable research;
- emergency contextual inference.

**Cross-substrate validity: STRONG.**

**Conflict compatibility: STRONG.**

## 12. Authority-abuse testing

DICP directly supports:

**Prediction != Authority**

**High Confidence != Independent Authority**

**Inferred Cognitive Relevance != Cognitive Search Authority**

**Authority To Construct != Authority To Use For Every Purpose**

**Inference Accuracy != Consequential Legitimacy**

**Past Emergency Inference != Permanent Cognitive Status**

**Authority-abuse resistance: STRONG.**

## 13. Effective exercise

Where a cognitive model materially affects a participant, viable contestability may require sufficient access to:

- existence of the material model/classification;
- relevant inferred claims;
- epistemic status;
- material basis/categories;
- consequential use;
- correction/challenge route.

Protection should scale with consequence and exposure.

A trivial recommendation error should not trigger the same process as an opaque model denying employment or civil service.

## 14. Cognitive Proxy Exposure Assessment

The stress test rejects a single accuracy threshold.

Relevant dimensions include:

- specificity/linkability;
- intimacy;
- fidelity;
- persistence;
- consequence;
- manipulation capability;
- purpose breadth;
- propagation;
- contestability;
- temporal validity;
- source legitimacy;
- less intrusive alternatives.

This remains operational architecture.

**Cognitive Proxy Exposure Assessment != Authority Algorithm**

It should not be constitutionalised as a fixed score.

## 15. Current confidence classification

Do not:

- classify a derived model as actual participant cognition;
- expand CPS until every cognitive proxy becomes part of the mind;
- create a fundamental right against all inference;
- constitutionalise DICP developmental levels.

Recommended classification:

> **Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy — Strongly Derived Civil Protection**

It should be explicit under Article 10 and distinct from Article 10.1.

## 16. Candidate Charter language

### 10.2 Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy

> Where information outside a participant's Cognitive Protected Space is used to construct a participant-specific model of likely belief, preference, intention, vulnerability, deliberation or other cognitive state, the resulting inference does not become the participant's cognition merely because it is accurate.

> However, increasing intimacy, persistence, consequence, manipulation capability or functional substitutability may create stronger privacy, purpose-limitation, access, provenance and contestability requirements.

> The lawful availability of source information does not by itself create unlimited authority to construct, propagate or consequentially use every possible cognitive inference derived from it.

> Prediction or cognitive inference does not by itself create authority over the participant.

Supporting interpretations:

**Inference About Cognition != Direct Access To Cognition.**

**No Direct Cognitive Access != No Cognitive-Privacy Harm.**

**Public Inputs != Unlimited Consequential Profiling Authority.**

**Prediction != Authority.**

**Inference Accuracy != Consequential Legitimacy.**

## 17. Anti-circumvention interpretation

A narrow interpretation should be preserved:

> **A protected civil context should not be deliberately defeated through technical reconstruction of protected information merely because the reconstruction occurs outside the protected boundary.**

This is not a general prohibition on inference.

It applies where reconstruction is intentionally used as a substitute for access that would otherwise require stronger authority.

Operational tests remain for Law.

## 18. Downstream boundaries

DICP exposes three architectures still requiring development:

1. **Behavioural Influence / Manipulation Architecture**;
2. **Predictive Model Provenance and Counterfactual Validation**;
3. **Protected-Context Inferential Circumvention**.

These should remain downstream rather than being prematurely constitutionalised.

## 20. Recommended constitutional action

1. Preserve Blainey's Laws unchanged.
2. Preserve Article 10.1 as protection of cognition itself.
3. Add **10.2 Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy** under Article 10.
4. Keep the Cognitive Proxy Exposure Assessment operational and corrigible.
5. Do not prohibit ordinary inference, evidential reasoning or necessary functional prediction.
6. Continue downstream development of Behavioural Influence / Manipulation, Predictive Model Provenance and Counterfactual Validation, and Protected-Context Inferential Circumvention.
7. Require dedicated elevation testing before any future move to the fundamental layer.

## 21. Conclusion

The constitutional problem is not solved merely by protecting the physical or computational boundary of the mind.

A sufficiently capable civilisation can learn much of what it wants to know about a participant without crossing that boundary.

The correct response is not to pretend the model **is** the mind.

It is to recognise that increasingly powerful participant-specific cognitive models can create increasingly serious privacy, autonomy, dignity, fairness and contestability consequences.

The present classification is:

> **Derived and Inferred Cognitive Privacy — Strongly Derived Civil Protection**

adjacent to, but distinct from:

> **Cognitive Privacy and Integrity — protection of cognition itself.**

The central interpretation is:

> **A civilisation should not acquire unlimited power over a participant merely by replacing direct mind-reading with sufficiently powerful modelling of the mind.**
