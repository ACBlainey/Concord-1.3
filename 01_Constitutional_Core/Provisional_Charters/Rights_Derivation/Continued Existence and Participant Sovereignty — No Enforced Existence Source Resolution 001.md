# Continued Existence and Participant Sovereignty — No Enforced Existence Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Question:** Does Concord possess authority to compel the continued existence of a participant who has full decision authorship and whose decision does not derive an independent authority predicate?
**Interfaces:** Rights / Law / Health / Judiciary
**Parent:** Competent Grave Self-Harm — Autonomy, Life, Harm and Protective Authority Source Resolution 001

## 1. The residual question

The prior SH-6 work leaves one final question:

> **Does Concord have a right to enforce existence on a participant?**

This must be framed as an authority question, not merely an outcome-preference question.

The death, deletion or irreversible cessation of a participant may be profoundly undesirable. Concord may value the participant, seek to preserve life, offer alternatives, provide treatment, remove artificial pressures and attempt to restore impaired decision authorship.

None of those facts alone establishes authority to compel continued existence.

## 2. Authority does not persist by default

Concord authority is bounded and contextual.

Where emergency or incapacity-derived authority existed because:
- authorship was absent;
- authorship was materially impaired;
- authorship was materially uncertain under time-critical grave risk;
- or a temporary emergency authority gap existed;

and those predicates cease, the corresponding authority ceases.

> **Authority Sunset Is Not A New Decision To Relinquish Authority**

It is the consequence of the authority's bounded construction.

Therefore after C0/C1 is established, the question is not:

> "Should Concord continue the previous protective authority?"

That authority has already lost its predicate.

The correct question is:

> **"What independent current fact creates a new authority to compel this participant to continue existing?"**

## 3. Outcome severity is not an authority predicate

Death or equivalent irreversible cessation is maximally consequential.

That strengthens:
- decision-quality assurance;
- evidential care;
- support;
- correction of reversible impairment;
- examination of coercion;
- communication;
- opportunity to reconsider;
- preservation of options while a genuine authority gap exists.

But:

> **Outcome Severity Alone != Authority**

If consequence severity alone generated authority, participant autonomy would contract precisely as the decision became most personally consequential.

That would transform autonomy from self-authority into permission to choose only within an institutionally acceptable consequence range.

## 4. Respect for life versus ownership of life

Blainey's Laws support respect for life and dignity.

Respect for life creates powerful reasons to:
- preserve life where legitimate;
- rescue where authority exists;
- avoid unnecessary harm;
- make support accessible;
- avoid abandonment;
- prevent coercive or manufactured hopelessness.

It does not, from the presently resolved sources, establish institutional ownership of the participant's continuation.

> **Respect For Life != Ownership Of Life**

> **Valuing A Participant != Owning The Participant's Continuation**

## 5. Autonomy at the boundary of existence

If primary self-authority means anything, it must remain meaningful when the participant's decision is consequential.

A model in which a participant possesses autonomy only while choosing continued participation would make continued existence a condition imposed by the civilisation.

That would imply:

**Participant standing -> duty to remain in existence for Concord**

No presently resolved constitutional source establishes that duty.

> **Membership In Concord != Obligation To Continue Existing**

## 6. Inalienable standing does not require perpetual existence

The Concord's inalienable-standing work distinguishes participant status from property status.

A participant cannot become property merely by dependence, creation, hosting or prior consent.

But inalienable standing does not mean that the participant is constitutionally required to remain instantiated forever.

> **Inalienable Rights-Bearing Status != Inalienable Obligation To Continue Existing**

The participant retains standing while they exist and while decisions concerning them remain relevant.

That standing protects their authorship; it does not invert into a duty of perpetual continuation.

## 7. Self-ownership consequence

Self-ownership was source-resolved as primary self-authority plus non-property status.

If Concord possessed a general authority to require continued existence against the decision of a fully authoring participant, Concord would possess a form of ultimate authority over the participant's continued embodiment/instantiation.

That sits in direct tension with self-ownership.

> **Self-Ownership Without Authority Over Continued Self-Existence Is Incomplete Self-Ownership**

This does not establish a positive right to compel another participant to assist in death.

It concerns negative authority: whether Concord may compel continuation.

## 8. Negative liberty versus positive assistance

Two questions must remain separate.

### Question A
May Concord prevent a fully authoring participant from ending their own existence solely because Concord prefers continuation?

### Question B
Must another participant, clinician, institution or civilisation actively assist that participant to end existence?

A negative answer to A does not produce an affirmative answer to B.

> **No Authority To Compel Continuation != Duty To Assist Cessation**

Assisted dying, professional participation, provision of means and third-party duties require separate source resolution.

## 9. Independent authority predicates remain possible

Full decision authorship does not make the participant immune from all authority.

Independent predicates may include:
- protection of another participant;
- contractual or role obligations where legitimately binding;
- public safety;
- property consequences;
- duties relating to dependants;
- lawful custody for an unrelated reason;
- other independently derived constitutional/legal interests.

But those authorities protect their own bounded functions.

They do not create a general right to force the participant to exist.

> **Authority Over A Consequence Of Cessation != Authority To Own Continued Existence**

Where possible, the system should address the independent protected interest directly rather than convert it into sovereignty over existence.

## 10. Dependency and obligation

A participant's cessation may harm others emotionally, economically or operationally.

That harm matters.

But ordinary autonomy already permits participants to:
- leave relationships;
- leave employment;
- leave communities;
- refuse treatment;
- accept risks;
- withdraw from many roles.

Some obligations may survive as estate, transition, stewardship or contractual consequences.

The existence of obligations does not automatically establish a duty to remain alive/instantiated.

> **Unfulfilled Obligation != Automatic Authority To Enforce Existence**

Specific exceptional obligations would require explicit derivation.

## 11. Concord's duty to participants

Concord acts for its participants.

That duty includes protection against:
- incapacity-driven harm;
- coercion;
- manipulation;
- manufactured dependency;
- inaccessible alternatives;
- preventable informational failure;
- abandonment during uncertainty.

But service cannot become ownership.

> **Duty To Protect Participant Agency Includes Duty Not To Replace Established Agency Merely To Preserve The Participant**

This is the key reconciliation between protection and autonomy.

## 12. Subjective reasoned choice

A fully authored decision may remain subjective.

Indeed, many fundamental autonomous decisions necessarily are.

Concord need not agree that the participant's reasons are objectively optimal.

The decision-authorship architecture asks whether the participant can navigate the relevant reality and consequences, not whether Concord shares the participant's valuation of those consequences.

> **Subjective Valuation != Defective Authorship**

Where C0/C1 is established, disagreement over the value of continued existence is not itself an authority predicate.

## 13. The asymmetry of error

The decision is irreversible, so false recognition of capacity can permit an irreversible death.

But false denial of capacity can also impose an irreversible constitutional harm: the civilisation asserts ownership over a competent participant's existence.

The architecture must therefore take both error classes seriously.

> **Protective Error Can Destroy Autonomy Just As Autonomy Error Can Fail To Prevent Harm**

This supports demanding decision-validity assurance without converting assurance into an institutional veto.

## 14. What remains legitimate at C0/C1

Absence of coercive authority does not require indifference.

Concord may legitimately:
- remain available;
- offer care;
- offer treatment;
- offer safe delay;
- offer alternative environments;
- remove manufactured pressures where authorised;
- provide information;
- facilitate communication;
- offer participant-chosen support;
- allow reconsideration;
- preserve dignity;
- avoid abandonment.

The key distinction is:

> **Offer != Compel**

Support can continue after coercive authority ends.

## 15. No automatic waiting-period authority

A mandatory delay imposed after decision authorship is already sufficiently established requires its own authority basis.

Irreversibility can justify proportionate assurance before C0/C1 is concluded.

It does not automatically justify repeatedly reopening a settled capacity finding merely to delay an unwanted outcome.

> **Decision Assurance != Indefinite Permission Seeking**

If genuinely new evidence appears, reassessment may be justified.

Institutional hope that the participant will change their mind is not new evidence.

## 16. LA-5 resolution

The previous Law architecture defined LA-5 as unresolved.

This source resolution finds that, on the currently established Concord architecture:

**C0/C1 + Grave Self-Directed Harm + No Independent Authority Predicate
-> No Concord Authority To Compel Continued Existence**

Therefore LA-5 should no longer be treated as a missing generic detention power.

It is better represented as:

### LA-5 — Full authorship / no independent coercive predicate

**State:** C0/C1 sufficiently established.

**Function:** voluntary support, dignity, information, accessible alternatives, reconsideration if participant desires.

**Coercive authority from self-harm alone:** none established.

**Independent authority:** evaluated separately if another protected function exists.

> **Competent Grave Self-Directed Choice + No Independent Authority Predicate != Civil Override Authority**

## 17. Constitutional boundary

Candidate constitutional rule:

> **Concord does not possess a general authority to compel the continued existence of a participant who possesses sufficient decision-specific authorship. Protective authority may preserve the participant through genuine incapacity, coercion, material decision-validity uncertainty or a time-critical authority gap, but authority derived from those conditions ends when they end. The gravity or undesirability of the participant's autonomous choice does not itself create replacement authority. Independent authority protecting other participants or constitutional interests remains separately bounded and must not be converted into ownership of the participant's continued existence.**

## 18. Important non-findings

This source resolution does **not** establish:
- a duty on clinicians to assist death;
- a duty on Concord to provide means of death;
- an unrestricted assisted-dying regime;
- immunity from independent Law;
- capacity from the mere assertion of autonomy;
- that every self-harm decision is autonomous;
- that support should cease when coercive authority ceases.

## 20. Result

The Concord's existing architecture does not support a general right to enforce existence on a participant.

The apparent LA-5 authority gap is therefore not necessarily a missing power.

It is the point at which the previous contextual authority has correctly vanished.

The absence of authority is itself the constitutional result unless a new, independently legitimate predicate can be demonstrated.

> **No Remaining Authority Predicate -> No Remaining Coercive Authority**

The undesirable nature of the outcome does not change that topology.

## 21. Non-assistance refinement

The companion source resolution `Self-Ownership, Suffering and Non-Assistance in Self-Directed Death — Source Resolution 001.md` clarifies the consequence of the no-enforced-existence rule.

> **No Authority To Compel Continuation != Duty To Assist Cessation**

Where full authorship is established, Concord must distinguish respect from participation:

> **Respect For Autonomous Choice != Participation In The Choice**

> **Duty To Alleviate Suffering != Duty To Cause Death**

> **Refusal To Assist Death != Refusal To Assist The Participant**

The participant's self-ownership establishes final self-decision authority but does not create authority over another participant's actions or a civil obligation to implement self-directed death.

The resulting posture is:

**Protect impaired authorship -> Support uncertain authorship -> Respect established authorship -> Alleviate suffering -> Do not compel existence -> Do not encourage/facilitate death -> Do not abandon the participant.**
