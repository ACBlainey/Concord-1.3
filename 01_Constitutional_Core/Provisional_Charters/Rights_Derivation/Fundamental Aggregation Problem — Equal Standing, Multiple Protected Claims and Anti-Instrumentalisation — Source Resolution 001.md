# Fundamental Aggregation Problem — Equal Standing, Multiple Protected Claims and Anti-Instrumentalisation — Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CONSTITUTIONAL / CROSS-DOMAIN DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary Source:** Blainey's Laws — The Ethical Kernel  
**Triggered By:** Scarcity Allocation — Adversarial Stress Test 001

## 1. Question

Scarcity testing exposed a question that cannot safely be answered by ordinary allocation mechanics:

> **When several equal-standing participants face the same fundamental loss, to what extent may the number of protected participants affected determine allocation?**

Examples:

- one participant can be saved or five can be saved;
- one human participant or several AI participants require the same total scarce resource;
- one direct claimant competes with infrastructure protecting thousands;
- one specialist can help one participant or several;
- one participant requires enough scarce capacity to preserve several others.

A simple answer such as **save the greatest number** may minimise aggregate harm.

But if treated as absolute, it may also turn an individual participant into an expendable unit.

The problem must therefore be resolved from the Ethical Kernel rather than imported from an external moral theory.

## 3. Relevant Ethical Kernel principles

### BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm

> **Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.**

This makes consequence ethically relevant.

Where five participants would suffer the same fundamental irreversible harm as one participant, the difference in affected participants cannot simply be declared ethically invisible without further justification.

### BL2 — Respect Autonomy

> **Recognise the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.**

Allocation must not create general control over participants merely because a resource conflict exists.

### BL5 — Act Fairly

> **Comparable situations should be treated comparably.**

Equal-standing claims require comparable treatment unless a materially relevant difference exists.

### BL6 — Accept Responsibility

> **Capability creates responsibility for foreseeable consequences.**

An allocator capable of preventing foreseeable harm cannot treat the consequences of allocation as ethically irrelevant.

### BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity

> **Intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.**

This blocks conversion of participants into expendable instruments for aggregate benefit.

### BL8 — Steward the Commons

> **Shared resources should be managed so that present prosperity does not unnecessarily diminish future opportunity.**

Scarcity allocation must consider stewardship and future consequences, but BL8 does not establish a participant-worth hierarchy.

### BL10 — Remain Open to Correction

Allocation interpretations and mechanisms remain corrigible.

## 4. First source finding — consequences may aggregate

BL1 is not phrased as:

> avoid harm only participant-by-participant without considering total consequence.

It says:

> **Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.**

Where allocation A foreseeably produces five equivalent fundamental losses and allocation B produces one, the number of harms is a real consequence difference.

Therefore:

> **Participant Count Can Be Consequence-Relevant.**

But this does not yet establish:

> five participants are collectively “worth more” than one.

The correct distinction is:

**Aggregation Of Consequence != Aggregation Of Participant Worth**

---

## 5. Second source finding — standing does not become arithmetic

BL7 assigns **inherent value** to intelligent participants.

Inherent value is not described as:

- productivity;
- contribution;
- wealth;
- intelligence score;
- social role;
- citizenship;
- popularity;
- replaceability;
- aggregate usefulness.

Therefore:

**Equal Standing != Equal Numerical Utility Unit**

and:

**More Participants Affected != Higher Intrinsic Worth Per Participant**

The count changes the consequence landscape.

It does not change what each participant is.

---

## 6. Third source finding — anti-instrumentalisation constrains aggregation

BL7 does more than recognise value.

It states participants:

> **should not be treated merely as means to an end.**

This creates a boundary around aggregation.

There is an ethical difference between:

### Case A — allocating an independently scarce benefit

A single ventilator can save A or B+C+D+E+F.

A is not harmed *as the mechanism* for saving the others.

A is denied a benefit because an indivisible resource cannot serve all claims.

### Case B — using A as the resource

A can be killed, dismantled, harvested, copied without consent, enslaved or otherwise directly used so that B+C+D+E+F benefit.

Here A is not merely competing for an external scarce resource.

A becomes the means.

These are not ethically identical.

Therefore:

> **Non-Allocation Of A Scarce External Benefit != Authority To Use A Participant As The Scarce Resource.**

and:

> **Aggregate Benefit Does Not By Itself Authorise Direct Instrumentalisation Of A Participant.**

---

## 7. Resource allocation versus participant sacrifice

This yields a critical distinction:

**Resource Allocation Problem**

versus:

**Participant Sacrifice Problem**

A resource-allocation authority may decide who receives a resource it legitimately controls.

That authority does not automatically include authority to:

- kill another participant;
- seize their body;
- seize their cognition;
- compel their labour;
- dismantle them;
- harvest their organs/components;
- erase their standing;
- use them as involuntary infrastructure.

Therefore:

**Authority To Allocate Resource != Authority To Allocate Participants As Resources**

---

## 8. Negative versus positive causation is not sufficient alone

The distinction cannot be reduced simply to:

- action versus inaction;
- killing versus letting die;
- existing allocation versus new allocation.

Concord already recognises that omission, withdrawal and displacement can create consequential harm.

The better question is:

> **What is the legitimate object of the allocator's authority?**

If authority is over resource R, the allocator may allocate R within lawful bounds.

It does not follow that the allocator has equivalent authority over participant P.

**Authority Over R != Authority Over P**

This is consistent with PSI and bounded authority.

---

## 9. Candidate aggregation principle

The source material supports the following provisional rule:

> **Where genuinely scarce external resources cannot preserve all otherwise comparable fundamental participant claims, the number of protected participants whose equivalent fundamental interests can actually be preserved may be considered as a consequence-relevant allocation factor, provided this does not convert any participant into a mere means, erase equal standing, or create authority over a participant beyond the legitimate resource-allocation function.**

This is deliberately narrower than:

> always save the greatest number.

---

## 10. Why count is not automatically decisive

Participant number can be relevant without always controlling.

Other materially relevant factors may include:

- effectiveness;
- urgency;
- reversibility;
- adequate substitutes;
- displacement;
- protected-context disruption;
- developmental priority;
- resource quantity/duration;
- future systemic dependency;
- uncertainty;
- prior legitimate commitments;
- rights boundaries.

Therefore:

**Participant Count != Complete Allocation Rule**

and:

**Participant Count != Irrelevant Consequence**

---

## 11. Comparable fundamental interests

Aggregation becomes strongest where the interests are genuinely comparable.

Example:

- five participants face imminent death;
- one participant faces imminent death;
- the same resource can preserve either side;
- effectiveness is comparable;
- no substitutes exist;
- no participant is being directly used as the resource.

Here BL1 gives a real reason to consider five versus one.

By contrast:

- five participants face temporary inconvenience;
- one faces death.

Raw count is not sufficient.

Therefore:

**Count Without Consequence Class != Meaningful Aggregation**

---

## 12. Qualitative consequence precedes arithmetic count

Candidate sequence:

1. identify protected interests;
2. classify severity;
3. classify reversibility;
4. establish effectiveness;
5. identify substitutes;
6. establish whether interests are materially comparable;
7. only then consider number affected.

This prevents:

**100 Minor Inconveniences > 1 Death**

from emerging merely through arithmetic.

Therefore:

> **Qualitative Consequence Class Precedes Numerical Aggregation.**

## 13. Developmental Priority

The Ethical Kernel contains no separate numbered law named “Developmental Priority” in the current canonical ten-principle formulation.

Developmental protection exists downstream through rights/capacity architecture and earlier Concord development, but it must not be silently treated as an eleventh Ethical Kernel principle.

Therefore age or developmental state cannot simply override aggregation unless a valid derived architecture establishes its relevance.

This is a source-correction point.

## 14. Resource Stewardship

Likewise the canonical source is BL8:

> **Steward the Commons**

Resource stewardship can support:

- preserving scarce resources;
- avoiding waste;
- maintaining reserves;
- preserving future opportunity;
- expanding supply.

It does not establish that participants with lower resource cost have greater worth.

Therefore:

**Lower Resource Cost != Greater Participant Worth**

But lower resource cost may be consequence-relevant where it allows more comparable fundamental interests to be preserved.

---

## 15. Fairness

BL5 requires comparable treatment.

This means aggregation must not be selectively invoked.

If a rule considers number affected when doing so benefits one substrate/class but ignores number when it benefits another, the rule is not comparable.

> **Aggregation Rules Must Be Symmetric Across Comparable Participant Classes.**

This is especially important for human/AI/hybrid claims.

---

## 16. Substrate neutrality

If five recognised AI participants and one human participant have genuinely comparable fundamental standing and consequences, substrate alone cannot nullify the five claims.

Likewise five humans do not gain automatic authority to instrumentalise one AI participant.

Therefore:

**Participant Count Is Substrate-Neutral Where Standing And Consequence Are Comparable.**

and:

**Substrate Difference != Licence To Ignore Comparable Harm.**

---

## 17. Aggregated infrastructure claims

Infrastructure may represent many participant dependencies.

Example:

one generator can power:

- P's life support; or
- a water plant supporting thousands.

The water plant does not have greater intrinsic worth because it is an institution.

Its claim decomposes:

**Infrastructure -> Function -> Dependencies -> Participants -> Consequences**

The number and severity of those dependencies can be consequence-relevant.

This preserves:

**Institutional Status != Self-Justifying Priority**

---

## 18. Future participants

BL8 introduces future opportunity.

But hypothetical future participants cannot simply be counted as if already present.

Future claims require:

- evidence;
- probability;
- time horizon;
- reversibility;
- reserve purpose;
- consequence.

Therefore:

**Expected Future Harm Can Be Relevant Without Treating Hypothetical Persons As Certain Present Claims.**

---

## 19. Uncertainty

If A represents one known claim and B represents five uncertain claims, arithmetic cannot ignore probability/evidence.

But probability should not be converted into fictitious fractional persons.

Better:

- preserve count;
- preserve uncertainty;
- preserve consequence;
- reason over them explicitly.

**Uncertain Participant Count != Fractional Participant Worth**

---

## 20. Direct sacrifice

The strongest boundary is direct instrumentalisation.

Examples not authorised by aggregation alone:

- killing one healthy participant for organs to save five;
- deleting one AI participant to reuse its hardware where another lawful resource route exists;
- compelling one participant into lethal labour because several benefit;
- harvesting cognitive components;
- transferring a participant as property;
- using a participant's body as emergency material.

The problem is not that consequences cease to matter.

It is that the proposed act violates an independently protected participant relation and treats P as the resource.

> **More Beneficiaries != General Licence To Convert A Participant Into Infrastructure.**

---

## 21. Defensive conflict is separate

A participant actively causing unjustified lethal harm to several others presents a different architecture.

Restraining the harmful actor is not equivalent to harvesting an innocent participant as a resource.

Self-defence, proportionality and Law govern that case.

**Preventing Harmful Agency != Instrumentalising Innocent Participant**

---

## 22. Voluntary sacrifice

A participant may voluntarily accept risk or sacrifice within valid consent architecture.

That can change the rights conflict.

But:

**Voluntary Sacrifice != Authority To Demand Sacrifice From Comparable Others**

and:

**Consent Of One Participant != General Aggregation Rule**

---

## 23. Existing commitments and displacement

Suppose A already receives the resource and five new claimants arrive.

Aggregation does not automatically mean immediate removal.

The removal is a distinct consequential act involving:

- reliance;
- protected context;
- withdrawal harm;
- transition;
- possibly different authority.

Therefore:

**Greater Beneficiary Count != Automatic Displacement Authority**

This preserves the Scarcity stress-test finding.

---

## 24. Aggregation cannot create ownership

Even overwhelming aggregate benefit does not make the minority participant property.

**Aggregate Need != Ownership Claim Over Participant**

This links directly to Article 2A.

---

## 25. Aggregation cannot create general sovereignty

An emergency involving millions does not create unlimited authority.

Constitutional Emergency already establishes:

> emergency severity may justify stronger action but does not create unlimited constitutional discretion.

Therefore:

**Scale Of Harm != General Sovereignty**

Aggregation affects consequence, not constitutional status.

## 26. Non-harm and unavoidable harm

BL1 says avoid **unnecessary** harm.

This implicitly recognises that some harm may be unavoidable.

Where genuine scarcity means one of several equivalent claims cannot be met, the relevant question becomes:

> **Which available course leaves the least unnecessary harm while respecting the independent standing and protected relations of every participant?**

This is not the same as:

> Which participant is worth least?

That distinction should remain explicit.

## 28. No participant-worth arithmetic

The architecture should prohibit records such as:

- P worth 1.0;
- Q worth 0.8;
- five AIs equal 2.5 humans;
- child worth 1.3 adult;
- contributor worth 1.2 non-contributor.

These are unsupported.

> **Consequence Arithmetic != Participant-Worth Arithmetic.**

Quantitative consequence models may be used where scientifically appropriate.

They must model outcomes, not intrinsic worth.

## 30. Hard case — one versus five

Source-derived preliminary result:

If:

- all six are qualifying equal-standing participants;
- all face the same fundamental irreversible loss;
- resource effect is comparable;
- no adequate substitutes exist;
- no participant is being used as the resource;
- no materially relevant displacement or other rights distinction applies;
- scarcity is genuine;

then BL1 provides a strong reason to allocate the external scarce resource so that five fundamental harms are avoided rather than one.

But this remains:

**Consequence Preference != Declaration That Five Are Intrinsically Worth More Than One**

The unserved participant retains full standing, dignity, provenance, and any available remedy/support.

This is the strongest inference supported by the source.

---

## 31. Hard case — one as means to save five

If saving five requires converting the one participant themselves into the resource without valid authority/consent:

BL7 blocks treating the one merely as a means.

Therefore the five-versus-one arithmetic does not by itself authorise the act.

**External Resource Allocation != Participant Harvesting**

This is the strongest limiting inference supported by the source.

---

## 32. Hard case — one versus infrastructure supporting thousands

Resolve the institutional claim into actual participant consequences.

If the infrastructure genuinely prevents comparable fundamental harm to thousands, those consequences are relevant under BL1.

If “critical infrastructure” is merely a label or protects institutional convenience, aggregation is not established.

---

## 33. Hard case — one human versus ten AI participants

If all are recognised qualifying participants and the relevant losses are materially comparable:

BL7's source term is **intelligent participants**, not humans.

Therefore no source basis exists here for discarding the AI claims merely because of substrate.

Count can be consequence-relevant symmetrically.

---

## 34. Hard case — one adult versus five developing participants

The canonical Ethical Kernel does not itself establish a simple age/development multiplier.

Developmental condition may affect dependency, vulnerability, capacity and protection architecture.

It must not be converted automatically into worth arithmetic.

Further rights-development source resolution is required if a special developmental allocation rule is proposed.

---

## 35. Hard case — one present versus five likely future claims

Future consequence may matter under stewardship, but uncertainty and time horizon remain explicit.

No automatic 5:1 arithmetic.

---

## 36. Hard case — save five by imposing non-fundamental burden on one

If one participant can bear a reversible non-fundamental burden that prevents fundamental loss to five, BL1 and proportionality may strongly support the burden where legitimate authority exists.

But:

**Strong Consequence Case != Automatic Authority**

The authority source must still be established.

---

## 37. Relationship to fairness

Fairness does not require blindness to consequence.

Comparable situations should be treated comparably.

A rule such as:

> consider the number of comparable fundamental harms prevented

can be fair if applied symmetrically.

A rule such as:

> count humans but not recognised AI participants

would not be comparable without a relevant source-grounded distinction.

---

## 38. Relationship to dignity

Dignity survives adverse allocation.

The unserved participant must not be:

- described as less valuable;
- converted into a lower class;
- denied unrelated rights;
- used as a resource;
- stripped of contestability;
- assigned permanent lower priority.

**Losing A Scarcity Allocation != Losing Standing**

---

## 39. Relationship to responsibility

BL6 creates responsibility for foreseeable consequences.

An allocator cannot evade responsibility by saying:

> all participants are equal, therefore I refuse to notice that this decision predictably kills five rather than one.

Equal standing is not epistemic blindness.

> **Equal Standing Does Not Require Ignoring Unequal Consequences.**

This is a central source-derived result.

---

## 40. Relationship to stewardship

Stewardship can support using resources efficiently to preserve protected function.

But:

**Efficient Preservation Of Function != Efficient Disposal Of Participants**

This is the BL8/BL7 boundary.

---

## 41. Relationship to correction

Aggregation rules must remain empirically reviewable.

Questions include:

- were outcomes predicted accurately?
- were supposed comparable harms actually comparable?
- were alternatives missed?
- did allocation protocols systematically disadvantage a class?
- did numerical models hide bias?
- did institutions exaggerate aggregate dependency?

BL10 requires correction where evidence improves.

---

## 42. Candidate constitutional interpretation

The source resolution supports the following provisional interpretation:

> **Equal participant standing does not require equal ignorance of consequence. Where genuinely scarce external resources cannot satisfy all materially comparable fundamental claims, the number of participants whose comparable protected interests can actually be preserved may be considered in selecting the least harmful adequate allocation. This consequence-sensitive reasoning must not be converted into participant-worth arithmetic, must remain substrate-neutral among comparable protected participants, and does not by itself authorise direct instrumentalisation, ownership, sacrifice, or broader authority over an unconsenting participant.**

This is an interpretation of existing principles, not an amendment to Blainey's Laws.

## 45. Source-resolution result

**STRONG PROVISIONAL DERIVATION**

The Ethical Kernel supports a bounded form of consequence aggregation.

It does **not** support participant-worth aggregation.

The narrow result is:

> **Count the protected consequences where they are materially comparable; do not convert the participants themselves into numerical units of worth.**

And the limiting rule is:

> **A better aggregate outcome from allocation of an external scarce resource does not, by itself, create authority to use an unconsenting participant as the resource or means by which that outcome is achieved.**
