# Competent Grave Self-Harm — Autonomy, Life, Harm and Protective Authority Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Question:** What, if any, continuing protective authority can be derived when a participant with established decision-specific capacity intentionally chooses an irreversible act causing grave harm or death to themselves?
**Trigger:** SH-6 in the Health self-harm contextual-envelope architecture

## 1. Purpose

This analysis does not ask whether suicide or grave self-harm is medically desirable.

It asks whether Concord's existing ethical and constitutional sources themselves establish authority for another actor or institution to override a participant after the temporary emergency authority gap has closed.

The distinction matters:

> **Ethical Concern About An Outcome != Authority To Control The Participant**

## 2. Ethical sources in tension

Blainey's Laws supply several simultaneously relevant principles.

### BL1 — Avoid Unnecessary Harm

Minimise unnecessary harm and prefer the least harmful available course.

A preventable death or grave injury is an extreme harm. BL1 therefore creates strong reason to offer protection, treatment, support and reversible preservation where legitimate.

But BL1 is not stated as:

**Prevent Every Harm At Any Cost.**

The word **unnecessary** requires comparison against other legitimate interests and available courses.

### BL2 — Respect Autonomy

Recognise the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.

A competent participant's direction of their own body/life is therefore ethically material.

BL2 is not absolute, because the ethical minimum and other participants' protections remain relevant.

### BL7 — Respect Life and Dignity

Intelligent participants possess inherent value and should not be treated merely as means to an end.

This strongly rejects casual disregard for participant death.

But inherent value does not by itself establish that an institution owns the continuation of a participant's life.

> **Respect For Life != Institutional Ownership Of Life**

### BL10 — Remain Open to Correction

Where an irreversible act is contemplated under uncertainty, preserving time for correction can be ethically important.

This supports option-preserving intervention where the participant's decision state, information, coercion, diagnosis or temporal stability is genuinely unresolved.

It does not automatically create indefinite authority once those uncertainties have been adequately resolved.

## 3. Self-ownership / primary self-authority

Existing constitutional development states:

> **A participant possesses primary legitimate authority over their own self and is not property or a general-purpose resource of another participant, institution or the Concord.**

and:

> **Protection Of Standing != Prohibition Of Irreversible Choice**

The source explicitly recognises that participants may make consequential and even irreversible decisions.

This is highly relevant.

If self-ownership permitted every institution to override a competent participant whenever the institution regarded the participant's choice as severely harmful, primary self-authority would become conditional on institutional approval.

> **Primary Self-Authority != Authority Only For Choices Others Approve**

## 4. Standing versus exercise

Inalienable Participant Standing protects the participant as a rights-bearing subject.

It does not establish an obligation to preserve every possible future exercise of that standing at all costs.

A participant can make irreversible choices without thereby becoming property of another.

Therefore:

> **Inalienable Standing != Mandatory Biological Or Substrate Continuation**

This does not establish a positive right to assisted death. It establishes only that inalienable standing cannot itself be used as a simple derivation of compulsory continuation.

## 5. Emergency Health boundary

Emergency Health establishes authority where:
1. material harm is serious;
2. delay matters;
3. ordinary consent/authority resolution cannot safely be completed;
4. an available action may preserve the participant/future options;
5. required authority is otherwise unavailable.

The architecture explicitly says:

> **Emergency Authority = Temporary Bridge, Not Transfer Of Participant Sovereignty**

and:

> **Stabilised != Continuing Emergency Authority**

Therefore emergency authority is strongest where uncertainty and time pressure prevent ordinary authority resolution.

It weakens when:
- communication is restored;
- decision-specific capacity is established;
- relevant information is available;
- coercion/manipulation is excluded as far as reasonably possible;
- the participant's preference is sufficiently stable to evaluate;
- the immediate action has been interrupted;
- review becomes possible.

> **Closing The Authority Gap Removes The Basis For Authority Derived Solely From The Gap**

## 6. Self-defence analogy and limit

Self-Defence derives bounded authority from:

**Legitimate Protective Context + Protective Function + Necessity -> Bounded Defensive Authority**

But classic self-defence concerns protection against wrongful harm by another actor.

A competent participant acting upon themselves is not straightforwardly equivalent to an aggressor wrongfully attacking a separate rights-holder.

Therefore:

> **Self-Protection Architecture Can Borrow The Contextual Method Without Assuming Self-Defence's Wrongful-Aggressor Relation**

The useful transfer is necessity, proportionality, context, reasonable perception, cessation and review — not automatic authority.

## 7. Irreversibility and correction

Death and many forms of grave self-harm are irreversible.

BL10 and non-harm therefore support strong epistemic caution.

But:

> **Irreversibility Increases The Burden Of Decision Quality; It Does Not Automatically Transfer Decision Ownership**

Before accepting SH-6 as genuinely reached, the system should proportionately test whether the decision is:
- informed;
- decision-capable;
- voluntary;
- not the product of coercion;
- not based on a remediable information error;
- not merely an acute transient state being misclassified as stable;
- not the product of an untreated condition that actually defeats decision capacity;
- not a request for relief where an acceptable alternative is unknown to the participant.

This is a **decision-validity inquiry**, not a requirement that the participant choose continued life.

## 8. Temporal stability

A preference can be real while also being temporally unstable.

Because the contemplated consequence is irreversible, temporal stability is relevant where delay itself does not impose disproportionate harm or become disguised indefinite detention.

> **Temporary Preservation For Genuine Decision Clarification != Permanent Veto Over A Competent Decision**

However:

> **Waiting Period != Automatic Legitimacy**

A fixed delay cannot substitute for contextual evidence. The appropriate period, if any, depends on the decision, condition, evidence, reversibility and Law.

## 9. Treatable/remediable cause

The existence of a potentially treatable mental or physical condition does not itself establish incapacity.

> **Treatability != Incapacity**

> **Available Treatment != Authority To Force Treatment**

But an untreated condition may be relevant evidence if it materially prevents the participant from understanding, evaluating or voluntarily making the specific decision.

The correct question is capacity/decision validity, not whether medicine has something it could attempt.

## 10. Suffering and alternatives

Where a participant seeks death primarily to escape severe suffering, Concord should ensure that known legitimate alternatives are discoverable.

This can include treatment, palliation, environmental change, social support, rehabilitation or other relevant options.

> **Offer Of Alternatives != Requirement To Accept Alternatives**

A system that withholds tolerable alternatives and then treats the resulting decision as fully unconstrained would also be ethically defective.

> **Manufactured Hopelessness != Neutral Choice Environment**

## 11. Coercion and external pressure

A decision influenced by threat, abuse, financial pressure, institutional neglect, discriminatory burden, manipulation or another participant's coercion requires independent investigation.

> **External Pressure Can Corrupt Apparent Self-Directed Choice**

This is especially important where another actor benefits from the participant's death, disappearance or loss of claim.

## 12. Capacity threshold

SH-6 must require more than a generic declaration that the participant "has capacity."

The relevant object is:

**CAPACITY(participant, grave irreversible self-harm decision, context, time)**

The assessment should be proportionate to consequence without becoming an impossible standard applied only to disfavoured choices.

> **Higher Consequence Can Justify Greater Decision-Validity Assurance Without Requiring Institutional Agreement**

## 13. Asymmetry problem

A dangerous architecture would scrutinise a decision to die under extraordinary standards while accepting equally consequential decisions to undergo risky treatment, refuse life-saving treatment or undertake hazardous activity under ordinary standards solely because institutions prefer those choices.

The standards need not be identical because contexts differ, but differences require justification.

> **Disfavoured Outcome != Permission For Arbitrarily Higher Capacity Standard**

## 14. Refusal of treatment

Existing Emergency Health already recognises competent present refusal as highly significant.

A participant can refuse continuing treatment once ordinary authority is restored, subject to independently valid surviving authority.

This means Concord already accepts that respecting refusal may foreseeably permit grave harm or death in some contexts.

Therefore:

> **Foreseeable Death Following Refusal != Automatic Proof That Refusal May Be Overridden**

This does not settle active self-harm, but it prevents BL1/BL7 from being interpreted as a universal compulsory-life rule.

## 15. Active act versus refusal

There may be legally relevant distinctions between:
- refusing treatment;
- withdrawing consent to continuing intervention;
- declining food/support;
- actively causing self-injury;
- requesting another actor to assist;
- requesting a professional to perform a death-causing act.

This source resolution does not collapse them.

> **Right To Refuse Treatment != Automatically Right To Compel Assistance In Death**

> **Primary Self-Authority != Automatic Authority Over Another Participant's Actions**

Assisted dying therefore requires a separate Law/Health analysis.

## 16. Third-party consequences

A nominally self-directed act can materially endanger others.

Where third parties are endangered, separate public-protection/defensive authority may arise.

> **Self-Directed Purpose != Exclusively Self-Directed Consequence**

That authority protects the third party; it does not automatically settle the participant's self-directed choice once third-party danger is removed.

## 17. Candidate SH-6 admission test

Before classifying a case as SH-6, the system should establish, to the standard eventually defined by Law, that:

1. immediate emergency authority-gap conditions have ended;
2. the participant is identifiable as the relevant rights-holder;
3. the participant has decision-specific capacity for the contemplated irreversible act;
4. communication/access barriers have been adequately addressed;
5. material consequences are understood;
6. material alternatives are known or reasonably available for consideration;
7. coercion/manipulation/conflicted third-party pressure has been proportionately examined;
8. acute reversible causes capable of invalidating the decision have been proportionately examined;
9. the preference is sufficiently stable for the context, rather than inferred from one transient signal;
10. the participant continues to express the decision without the decision being manufactured by the authority process itself;
11. third-party protective functions have been separated;
12. current exceptional authority is not merely inherited from SH-4/SH-5.

> **SH-6 Should Be Difficult To Enter Because Its Predicates Are Demanding, Not Because The Participant Must Agree To Live**

## 18. What existing sources support strongly

Existing sources strongly support:
- immediate rescue during a genuine time-critical authority gap;
- reversible/option-preserving action where legitimate;
- supported decision-making;
- robust capacity/decision-validity assessment;
- investigation of coercion and remediable error;
- offering meaningful alternatives;
- reassessment as facts change;
- termination of emergency authority when its predicates end;
- participant standing throughout;
- independent review of continuing coercion.

## 19. What existing sources do not presently establish

Existing sources do **not** presently establish:
- a general indefinite power to detain every competent participant who intends suicide;
- a general power to force psychiatric treatment solely to make the participant prefer life;
- that BL7 creates institutional ownership of participant continuation;
- that BL1 overrides BL2 whenever death is possible;
- that a competent participant possesses an unrestricted positive right to assistance in dying;
- the exact Law rule for active competent self-harm after SH-6 is genuinely established.

## 20. Candidate constitutional boundary

The strongest derivable boundary is:

> **Concord may use bounded, proportionate and preferably reversible protective authority to preserve a participant through a genuine time-critical authority or decision-validity gap. As that gap closes, authority derived from it must contract. A competent, informed and sufficiently voluntary participant's sustained grave self-directed choice cannot be converted into continuing institutional authority merely by relabelling disagreement as incapacity, emergency or respect for life. Any further override requires an independently derived and explicitly stated constitutional or legal authority.**

This does not settle whether such an additional authority should exist.

It prevents it from being smuggled in through Health.

## 21. Predetermined envelope implication

SH-6 should remain a **constitutional handoff state**, not an ordinary clinical detention state.

Health can establish clinical facts and decision-specific capacity.

If Law later establishes a bounded civil override, it should define in advance:
- trigger;
- protected function;
- evidence standard;
- permitted acts;
- maximum initial duration;
- mandatory independent review;
- appeal/challenge;
- renewal standard;
- prohibited purposes;
- sunset;
- remedies for wrongful intervention.

> **If Exceptional Authority Exists, Its Envelope Should Precede The Case Rather Than Be Invented Around The Participant**

## 23. Result

**SOURCE RESOLUTION RESULT: PARTIALLY DETERMINATE.**

The corpus strongly supports temporary option-preserving intervention while authority/decision validity is genuinely unresolved and strongly rejects indefinite continuation of that authority merely because the participant's competent decision remains unacceptable to others.

The corpus does not yet determine whether a separate, narrowly defined civil authority may override a genuinely competent sustained active self-harm decision.

That residual question belongs primarily to Constitutional Core / Law, with Health supplying clinical facts rather than owning the answer.
