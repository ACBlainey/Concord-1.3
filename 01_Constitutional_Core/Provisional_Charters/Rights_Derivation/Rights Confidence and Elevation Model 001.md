# Rights Confidence and Elevation Model 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / NON-CANONICAL  
**Purpose:** Prevent uncertain or convention-derived civil protections from being presented at the same authority level as rights strongly anchored in Blainey's Laws.

---

# 1. Governing principle

The Provisional Rights Charter should distinguish **derivational confidence and authority level**.

A useful civil protection does not become fundamental merely because it is familiar, desirable or already implemented.

Likewise, a protection need not be rejected merely because its derivation is incomplete.

The correct response to uncertainty is **classification**, not forced elevation or deletion.

> **Unclear Right != No Protection.**

> **Useful Protection != Fundamental Right.**

> **Later Elevation Requires Stronger Derivation, Not Familiarity.**

---

# 2. Class A — Source-Anchored / Fundamental Rights and Protected Relations

These are the strongest current outputs.

They are either:
- directly stated by Blainey's Laws; or
- survive blind derivation and loss testing as necessary civil expressions of a specific Blainey's Law.

Current Class A candidates:

1. **Autonomy / self-direction and protection against unjustified interference**
2. **Safe civil context**
3. **Honest consequential civil dealing**
4. **Fair comparable treatment**
5. **Dignity / non-instrumentalisation**
6. **Peaceful difference / non-conformity**
7. **Contestability / correction**

Associated non-right Class A objects:
- foreseeably affected participant standing — BL6;
- commons stewardship/future opportunity — BL8.

## Status

These may form the **Fundamental Rights / Protected Relations** part of the provisional charter.

“Fundamental” here describes derivational position, not final constitutional entrenchment.

The entire Charter remains provisional.

---

# 3. Class B — Strongly Derived Civil Rights / Liberties

These are not directly stated as separate rights in the Ethical Kernel.

They appear to be recurring decompressions required to make one or more Class-A protections meaningful in ordinary civil conditions.

Current Class B candidates:

- privacy / protected private space;
- expression / communication;
- belief / conscience;
- association / disassociation;
- peaceful movement through legitimately accessible civil space;
- meaningful civil voice;
- consequential self-relevant information access;
- effective corrective response;
- capacity-sensitive exercise/support.

## Status

Recognise provisionally as **Derived Civil Rights / Protections**.

Do not describe them as absolute or source-equivalent to Class A.

They may later be:
- elevated;
- narrowed;
- merged;
- reclassified as implementation guarantees;
- returned to domain architecture

after further testing.

---

# 4. Class C — Assumed / Incompletely Derived / Civil-Architecture Protections

These are plausible or useful protections for which the current derivation is incomplete, depends materially on additional civil assumptions, or concerns an institution rather than a fundamental participant relation.

Current examples:

- complete property regime;
- citizenship-specific political rights;
- voting/franchise;
- representation form;
- unconditional territorial secession;
- exact positive-service floor;
- detailed emergency powers;
- detailed guardianship/substitution mechanisms;
- exhaustive rights precedence;
- specific remedy forms;
- exact personhood/intelligent-participant boundary.

## Status

These should **not** be presented as fundamental rights.

They may be:
- recorded as candidate protections;
- implemented provisionally where justified;
- researched;
- tested;
- elevated later if derivation becomes sufficiently strong.

---

# 5. Class D — Operational Mechanisms

Examples:
- a particular voting method;
- appeal tiers;
- court procedure;
- identity technology;
- access-control implementation;
- specific compensation formula;
- emergency command structure;
- authentication method;
- service-delivery workflow.

These may be necessary to implement rights but are not rights merely because rights depend upon them.

> **Mechanism Necessity != Fundamental Status.**

---

# 6. “Absolute” terminology

The term **absolute right** should be used cautiously.

The current derivation does not establish that every Class-A right is unlimited in exercise.

BL2 itself says autonomy is subject to the ethical minimum.

Blainey's Laws also require harm minimisation, fairness, responsibility, dignity, stewardship, peaceful coexistence and correction.

Therefore the more precise current distinction is:

- **Fundamental / Source-Anchored**
- **Strongly Derived**
- **Assumed / Incompletely Derived**
- **Operational**

If “absolute” is later used, it should mean an aspect of protected standing that cannot legitimately be extinguished, not that every possible exercise of the right is unlimited.

Example:

**underlying dignity may remain protected even when a particular action is legitimately restricted.**

---

# 7. Elevation test

A Class-B or Class-C protection may be considered for elevation when it passes a documented test such as:

1. clear derivation from Blainey's Laws;
2. loss test — removing it materially defeats a source-anchored protected condition;
3. distinction from mere implementation preference;
4. cross-substrate validity;
5. conflict testing;
6. authority-abuse testing;
7. holder/scope clarity;
8. effective-exercise analysis;
9. cross-system compatibility;
10. clean-instance or independent derivation where useful;
11. no simpler lower-level representation adequately preserves the protection.

Elevation must be explicit.

It must not occur silently through repeated use.

---

# 8. Demotion test

A provision should be demoted where:
- it depends primarily on one implementation;
- alternative mechanisms preserve the same protected function;
- derivation is circular;
- it imports external constitutional convention without Concord-specific support;
- it creates authority not required by the underlying protection;
- its claimed universality fails across substrates or contexts;
- later evidence shows it is a contingent civil choice.

Demotion is not necessarily rejection.

A useful mechanism can remain useful without constitutional status.

---

# 9. Charter implication

Revision 002 should visibly divide the Charter into at least:

## Part I — Fundamental / Source-Anchored Rights and Protected Relations

## Part II — Strongly Derived Civil Rights and Protections

## Part III — Effective Exercise, Standing and Support

## Part IV — Provisional Constraints and Interpretation

## Part V — Unresolved / Assumed Rights Questions

Architecture-facing constitutional constraints that do not belong in a Rights Charter should migrate into the later Provisional Constitution development.

---

# 10. Preservation rule

The original blind derivation and frozen Phase C2 result must remain unchanged.

Reclassification changes the **presentation and confidence level**, not the historical derivational record.

> **Confidence classification must preserve provenance.**
