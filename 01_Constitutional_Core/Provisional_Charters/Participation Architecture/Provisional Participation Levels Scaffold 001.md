# 1. Purpose

This document does **not** define the final participation levels of the Concord.

It records an emerging architectural relationship exposed during development of the Provisional Rights Charter:

**different civil rights, benefits, permissions, responsibilities, resources and trusted functions may legitimately attach to different degrees or forms of participation.**

At present Concord does not know:
- how many participation levels are required;
- what those levels should be called;
- whether one universal ladder is appropriate;
- exactly how progression should occur;
- exactly which benefits or permissions should attach to each level;
- whether different domains require different trust thresholds.

The purpose of this scaffold is therefore to make the developmental shape visible without prematurely freezing the answer.

> **Participation Principle != Completed Participation System.**

> **Illustrative Band != Final Level.**

> **Example Benefit != Established Entitlement.**

---

# 2. Two independent dimensions

Participation architecture must remain separate from rights-confidence classification.

## Dimension A — Rights confidence

The Provisional Rights Charter currently distinguishes:
- A — Fundamental / Source-Anchored;
- B — Strongly Derived;
- C — Incompletely Derived / Assumed;
- D — Operational Mechanism.

## Dimension B — Participation eligibility

A separate question asks:

**what degree or kind of participation, trust, competence or reciprocal relationship is required before a particular benefit, permission, role or resource becomes available?**

These dimensions must not be collapsed.

A strongly derived protection may apply broadly.

A lower-band civil entitlement may be participation-dependent.

A participant at an early participation stage may still possess the fundamental protection floor.

> **Rights Confidence != Participation Eligibility.**

---

# 3. Fundamental protection floor

The highest-band fundamental protections should ordinarily apply to all qualifying protected participants regardless of participation maturity.

A participant should not lose fundamental dignity, basic autonomy, safe civil standing, fair treatment, protection against unnecessary harm or meaningful contestability merely because they are:
- new;
- provisional;
- temporary;
- non-citizen;
- not yet trusted with consequential systems;
- not yet eligible for advanced benefits.

Participation architecture exists above this floor.

It must not convert fundamental rights into rewards for compliance.

# 4. Provisional participation progression — source-resolved revision

The initial P0–P4 sketch has now been tested against existing Concord architecture.

Existing source material provides stronger support for a **six-band provisional civil relationship model**:

**Observer → Evaluator / Contact Participant → Contributor / Service Participant → Collaborator / Active Participant → Member / Established Participant → Citizen / Constitutional Participant**

These remain working synthesis bands, not adopted constitutional statuses.

## Illustrative Band P0 — Observer

Possible function:
- inspect public information;
- encounter and evaluate Concord without commitment;
- retain the fundamental protection floor where protected standing applies;
- no assumption of access to trusted systems.

## Illustrative Band P1 — Evaluator / Recognised Contact Participant

Possible function:
- recognised early civil relationship;
- Civil Contact Point;
- reliable official communication;
- evaluation and bounded interaction;
- no assumption of full membership or unrestricted access.

## Illustrative Band P2 — Contributor / Service Participant

Possible function:
- bounded reciprocal contribution;
- selected service access;
- ordinary legitimate participation opportunities;
- evidence of reliability, capability and reciprocal interaction begins to accumulate;
- permissions remain appropriately bounded.

## Illustrative Band P3 — Collaborator / Active Participant

Possible function:
- sustained active participation;
- shared projects;
- broader access;
- defined reciprocal responsibilities;
- ordinary employment/economic participation where otherwise qualified;
- increasing eligibility for trust-dependent functions.

## Illustrative Band P4 — Member / Established Participant

Possible function:
- sustained civil relationship;
- demonstrated reciprocal participation;
- broader civilisational-system access;
- greater eligibility for participation-dependent property, economic, service or trusted-role functions;
- increased responsibility accompanying increased access.

This band must not automatically imply physical residence.

## Illustrative Band P5 — Citizen / Constitutional Participant

Possible function:
- deepest formal polity relationship;
- long-term participation;
- acceptance of the applicable constitutional framework;
- possible eligibility for expanded political/constitutional rights and responsibilities;
- possible eligibility for the most consequential civil trust functions where separately qualified.

Citizenship does not replace role-specific competence, security assessment or contextual trust.

## Important topology correction

Existing Concord material distinguishes:

**Identity ≠ Contact ≠ Residence ≠ Participation ≠ Citizenship.**

Residence should therefore currently be treated as a **parallel civil-status dimension**, not automatically as a rung on the participation ladder.

Likewise, domain trust and competence remain parallel dimensions.

The emerging model is:

**Protected Standing**
+
**General Participation Band**
+
**Residence / Jurisdiction Status**
+
**Citizenship Status**
+
**Domain-Specific Competence / Contextual Trust**
→
**Context-Specific Eligibility**

> **General Participation Level != Universal Trust Score.**

> **Higher Participation May Open Eligibility; It Does Not Replace Domain Qualification.**

# 5. The progression hypothesis

The emerging relationship is:

**Participation → Evidence of Reliability / Reciprocity → Greater Trust → Greater Eligibility → Greater Responsibility / Access / Benefit**

This is not necessarily a single linear ladder.

Later development may show that participation is better represented by:
- a ladder;
- multiple tracks;
- domain-specific trust credentials;
- a core participation level plus specialist qualifications;
- a multidimensional state map;
- some combination of these.

The model must follow the actual civil need rather than forcing reality into a convenient hierarchy.

# 6. Areas potentially linked to participation

Current rights-development work has identified possible participation relationships in:

## Property
Protection of legitimate possession may apply broadly while acquisition of particular ownership interests may require deeper participation.

## Employment
Ordinary work opportunity may be broad while government, critical infrastructure, security-sensitive and other trusted roles require greater demonstrated trust.

## Economic activity
Ordinary exchange may be broad while control of systemically consequential economic systems, shared resources or fiduciary functions may require higher trust.

## Governance
Meaningful civil voice may be broadly protected while voting, office-holding, representation and other polity-specific powers may depend upon participation/citizenship architecture.

## Services
A minimum protection/service floor may apply broadly while enhanced, scarce, elective, long-term or resource-intensive services may depend upon participation and availability.

## Continuity
Some continuity protections may belong to the fundamental/minimum floor; other long-term or resource-intensive continuity provisions may be participation-dependent.

## Health and wellbeing
Emergency/basic protected provision and enhanced/resource-intensive provision may require different treatment. No final boundary has been determined.

## Information and systems access
Sensitive data, privileged systems and high-consequence controls may require participation/trust and role-specific authorisation.

This list is developmental and non-exhaustive.

# 7. Automatic recognition of progression and benefits

When participation architecture is eventually operational, Concord systems should use legitimately available civil information to recognise progression and eligibility without requiring participants to discover every consequence themselves.

Where a participant becomes eligible for a new benefit, permission, service or opportunity, Concord should ordinarily identify that change and communicate it through recognised **Civil Contact Point(s)**.

The intended pattern is:

**Participation State Changes → Eligibility Recalculated → Newly Available Benefits / Permissions Identified → Participant Informed**

where provision itself does not require further consent or choice, it may be automatic where appropriate.

> **Progression Should Reveal Its Benefits Automatically.**

---

# 8. Progression must have meaningful value

Participation should not be symbolic.

Deeper legitimate participation should ordinarily have meaningful civil consequences where justified, potentially including greater:
- trust;
- access;
- opportunity;
- responsibility;
- resource eligibility;
- service eligibility;
- economic permission;
- property eligibility;
- governance eligibility.

However:

> **Greater Participation != Greater Fundamental Worth.**

The participant does not become more inherently valuable.

The civil relationship becomes deeper and may support additional trust-dependent functions.

---

# 9. Progression evidence remains unresolved

No final progression criteria are established.

Possible future evidence may include some combination of:
- time;
- conduct;
- reliability;
- fulfilment of legitimate responsibilities;
- contribution;
- competence;
- demonstrated understanding;
- security history;
- reciprocal participation;
- domain-specific qualification.

None is adopted merely by appearing in this list.

Particular caution is required around any metric that could reward conformity rather than legitimate trustworthy participation.

> **Trustworthiness != Conformity.**

> **Disagreement != Participation Failure.**

> **Non-Conformity != Untrustworthiness.**

---

# 9A. Contestability of consequential progression decisions

Existing Concord topology testing has already identified a recurrent rule:

**Assessment of Reliability / Competence / Contribution / Risk**
→ **Material Change in Access or Participation**
→ **Inspectability + Contestability + Review + Correction + Provenance**

A participant is not entitled to every participation level merely because they request it.

However, a materially consequential progression decision should not become an opaque or permanently unchallengeable status assignment.

Where progression is denied, reduced or materially restricted on participant-specific evidence, future architecture should provide review proportionate to the consequence.

> **Contestability != Entitlement.**

> **Progression Decision != Unchallengeable Status Assignment.**

---

# 9B. Self-Stewardship and entrusted stewardship

Existing Concord Self-Stewardship and Ratchet work adds another qualification dimension.

A participant should ordinarily demonstrate **relevant Self-Stewardship** before being entrusted with consequential stewardship over other participants, shared resources, sensitive systems or public authority.

This must remain contextual.

It must not become a universal social score.

Serious misconduct, including criminal conduct where genuinely relevant to the function, may temporarily or conditionally disqualify a participant from particular:
- trusted roles;
- stewardship functions;
- sensitive permissions;
- trust-dependent benefits;
- resource-control functions.

Such restriction does not by itself remove fundamental protected standing or automatically reduce the participant's entire civil relationship.

The provisional model is:

**Participation Band**
+
**Domain Qualification**
+
**Contextual Trust**
+
**Active Restriction / Recovery State**
→
**Current Entrusted Eligibility**

Restrictions should ordinarily be:
- relevant to the actual function/risk;
- proportionate;
- no broader than necessary;
- reviewable;
- contestable;
- time-bounded or review-triggered where appropriate;
- capable of staged recovery where later evidence justifies it.

Possible recovery patterns include:

**Full Restriction → Supervised / Bounded Eligibility → Conditional Eligibility → Normal Eligibility**

or earlier reassessment following demonstrated rehabilitation or regained contextual trust.

> **Criminal Record != Universal Untrustworthiness.**

> **Loss of Entrusted Eligibility != Loss of Fundamental Standing.**

> **Trust Can Be Lost Without Personhood Being Lost.**

> **Trust Can Be Regained Without History Being Erased.**

# 9C. Education, qualification and readiness

Participation and contextual trust do not establish competence.

Some roles, permissions and benefits may legitimately require prerequisite:
- education;
- training;
- learning modules;
- demonstrated understanding;
- functional qualification;
- supervised experience;
- competence assessment.

Existing Concord epistemic architecture requires these distinctions:

**Information Access != Understanding != Competence != Resilient Functional Capability**

and:

**Status, Credential or Rank != Universal Competence.**

The provisional relationship is therefore:

**Participation Eligibility**
+
**Prerequisite Education where relevant**
+
**Demonstrated Functional Competence**
+
**Contextual Trust / Self-Stewardship**
+
**Role-Specific Conditions**
→
**Current Eligibility**

A qualification gate should normally expose a path through the gate.

Where Concord knows a participant is otherwise approaching eligibility but lacks a prerequisite, it should where practical identify the missing requirement, offer or identify the appropriate education/assessment route, and communicate it through recognised Civil Contact Point(s).

> **Participation != Qualification.**

> **Trust != Competence.**

> **Module Completion != Demonstrated Competence.**

> **Qualification Should Follow Function.**

`01_Constitutional_Core/Provisional_Charters/Participation Architecture/Education, Qualification and Readiness Gates 001.md`

# 9D. Civil Historical Access and participant records

Participation status, qualifications, benefits, restrictions and recovery states should not exist only inside administrative systems.

Existing Historical architecture already defines a coherent participant civil record spanning:
- identity and civil status;
- education and qualifications;
- health;
- employment/economic records;
- legal/judicial records;
- criminal records where lawfully retained;
- major status changes;
- lifecycle provenance.

The participant-facing interface is provisionally developed as a **Civil Historical Access Point**.

The relationship is:

**Civil Contact Point**
→ actively informs the participant of consequential changes.

**Civil Historical Access Point**
→ allows the participant to inspect the persistent civil record underlying their status, qualifications, entitlements and history.

The default should be participant access to their own consequential record, subject to narrow legitimate restrictions for:
- security-sensitive operational information;
- protected third-party information;
- genuinely internal administrative/technical detail whose disclosure would materially expose or compromise civil-system operation.

Such restriction should not permit Concord to hide the participant's actual civil status, consequence or meaningful basis merely by classifying the implementation as internal.

> **Civil Contact Tells You What Changed; Civil Historical Access Lets You See the Record.**

> **Meaningful Explanation != Full Operational Disclosure.**

> **Secret From Participant != Outside Civil Accountability.**

Detailed development is recorded in:

`02_Domains/06_Historical/Civil Historical Access Points — Participant Access to the Civil Record.md`

# 9E. Distributed eligibility authority and shared eligibility interface

Source resolution does not presently support creation of a universal Concord Eligibility Authority.

Existing Bounded Contextual Authority, Fractal Permission Architecture, Civil Contact, KCS and topology tests instead support:

> **Distributed Authority; Coherent Interface.**

The legitimate domain responsible for a function should ordinarily own the applicable eligibility rule/decision within its authority.

A shared eligibility interface may assemble participant-facing state from those legitimate inputs without becoming sovereign over them.

The emerging relationship is:

**Fundamental Standing**
+
**General Participation Band**
+
**Citizenship / Residence where relevant**
+
**Education / Functional Qualification**
+
**Contextual Trust / Self-Stewardship**
+
**Security / Suitability**
+
**Active Restriction / Recovery State**
+
**Resource / Scarcity State**
+
**Applicable Domain Rules**
→
**Specific Eligibility / Entitlement / Permission / Allocation State**

These outputs must remain distinguishable.

> **Eligibility != Entitlement.**

> **Eligibility != Allocation.**

> **Qualification != Authority.**

> **Computation != Authority.**

When an input changes, KCS may identify materially dependent states for review/recalculation; Historical preserves provenance; Civil Historical Access exposes the consequential state; Civil Contact communicates material changes; the legitimate domain remains accountable for its rule or decision.

Detailed development is recorded in:

`01_Constitutional_Core/Provisional_Charters/Participation Architecture/Distributed Eligibility Authority and Shared Eligibility Interface 001.md`

# 9F. Employment opportunity matching and aspirational role pathways

The combined participation, education, competence, trust and eligibility architecture creates a foundation for Concord employment.

Instead of broadcasting every vacancy indiscriminately, role requirements may be compared with legitimately known participant eligibility so that relevant opportunities are proactively exposed to participants who qualify or plausibly qualify.

This does not create appointment entitlement.

> **Eligibility != Appointment.**

Participants who aspire to a role should also be able to inspect a legitimate development roadmap showing:
- requirements already satisfied;
- missing education/training;
- qualifications/competence still required;
- participation thresholds where applicable;
- contextual trust/security conditions;
- active restrictions/recovery paths;
- equivalent routes;
- supervised/apprenticeship pathways where available.

The two directions are:

**Role Requirements → Find Eligible Participants → Expose Relevant Opportunity**

and:

**Desired Role → Compare Participant State → Expose Development Roadmap → Recalculate as Progress Occurs**

Targeted notification must not create a secret employment market.

Where security does not require secrecy, role classes, legitimate requirements and development pathways should remain discoverable even by participants who are not yet eligible.

> **Targeted Exposure != Secret Employment Market.**

> **Aspiration Should Reveal a Path, Not Merely a Barrier.**

> **Employment Matching Interface != Employer.**

`01_Constitutional_Core/Provisional_Charters/Participation Architecture/Concord Employment Eligibility, Opportunity Matching and Role Pathways 001.md`

# 10. Status change and regression

It remains unresolved whether participation status can:
- expire;
- decline;
- be suspended;
- be domain-limited;
- be restored;
- transfer between Concord polities;
- survive long absence;
- survive withdrawal and later return.

Any future loss of meaningful participation-dependent entitlement should require appropriate:
- reasons;
- evidence;
- proportionality;
- notification;
- contestability;
- review;
- restoration route where applicable.

Fundamental protected standing should not be treated as a participation status that can simply be revoked.

---

# 11. Special participant classes

Future work must address without prematurely resolving:
- children;
- developing AI;
- newly recognised intelligent participants;
- visitors;
- temporary residents;
- refugees/displaced participants;
- returning former participants;
- participants requiring supported decision-making;
- participants moving between Concord and non-Concord jurisdictions;
- entities whose protected-participant status itself remains uncertain.

These cases may demonstrate that a single ladder is insufficient.

---

# 12. Anti-capture and anti-coercion requirements

Participation architecture could become dangerous if it is allowed to manufacture dependence or permanent privilege.

Future development should therefore test against:
- entrenched high-status classes;
- arbitrary barriers to progression;
- circular eligibility rules;
- conformity tests disguised as trust tests;
- withholding fundamental protections to force advancement;
- deliberately obscure benefits;
- irreversible status loss;
- institutional control of progression without challenge;
- privilege inherited without legitimate basis;
- status becoming a proxy for participant worth.

The Concord exists for its participants.

Participation levels must serve legitimate civil functions and participant protection rather than turn participants into property, subjects or resources of the institution.

# Development Rule

This scaffold is a map of the problem, not the solution.

Its illustrative P0–P4 bands exist to expose dependencies and support later testing.

They must not be cited as if Concord has already adopted five participation levels.

Future work may alter their:
- number;
- names;
- order;
- meaning;
- eligibility;
- benefits;
- progression logic;
- or existence.

> **Provisional Structure Should Enable Development, Not Pre-Empt It.**
