# 1. Purpose

This document does **not** define the final participation levels of the Concord.

It records an emerging architectural relationship exposed during development of the Provisional Rights Charter:

**different civil rights, benefits, permissions, responsibilities, resources and trusted functions may legitimately attach to different degrees or forms of participation.**

At present Concord does not know:
- how many participation levels are required;
- what those levels should be called;
- whether one universal ladder is appropriate;
- exactly how progression should occur;
- exactly which benefits or permissions should attach to each level;
- whether different domains require different trust thresholds.

The purpose of this scaffold is therefore to make the developmental shape visible without prematurely freezing the answer.

> **Participation Principle != Completed Participation System.**

> **Illustrative Band != Final Level.**

> **Example Benefit != Established Entitlement.**

---

# 2. Two independent dimensions

Participation architecture must remain separate from rights-confidence classification.

## Dimension A — Rights confidence

The Provisional Rights Charter currently distinguishes:
- A — Fundamental / Source-Anchored;
- B — Strongly Derived;
- C — Incompletely Derived / Assumed;
- D — Operational Mechanism.

## Dimension B — Participation eligibility

A separate question asks:

**what degree or kind of participation, trust, competence or reciprocal relationship is required before a particular benefit, permission, role or resource becomes available?**

These dimensions must not be collapsed.

A strongly derived protection may apply broadly.

A lower-band civil entitlement may be participation-dependent.

A participant at an early participation stage may still possess the fundamental protection floor.

> **Rights Confidence != Participation Eligibility.**

---

# 3. Fundamental protection floor

The highest-band fundamental protections should ordinarily apply to all qualifying protected participants regardless of participation maturity.

A participant should not lose fundamental dignity, basic autonomy, safe civil standing, fair treatment, protection against unnecessary harm or meaningful contestability merely because they are:
- new;
- provisional;
- temporary;
- non-citizen;
- not yet trusted with consequential systems;
- not yet eligible for advanced benefits.

Participation architecture exists above this floor.

It must not convert fundamental rights into rewards for compliance.

# 4. Provisional participation progression

The following bands are **illustrative developmental placeholders only**.

They are not final levels and their examples are not final entitlements.

## Illustrative Band P0 — Protected / Limited Participation

Possible characteristics:
- fundamental protection floor;
- basic civil safety;
- access to necessary communication;
- legitimate ordinary residence/visitor arrangements where applicable;
- basic ability to interact with Concord systems;
- emergency or minimum essential protection;
- no presumption of access to trusted roles or scarce long-term civil assets.

This band illustrates the proposition that a participant can be protected by Concord without yet possessing the full benefits of deep participation.

## Illustrative Band P1 — Provisional / New Participation

Possible characteristics:
- developing civil relationship;
- recognised Civil Contact Point(s);
- ordinary lawful economic interaction;
- rental, lease, permitted occupancy or similar non-ownership property relationships;
- ordinary employment subject to competence and lawful role requirements;
- access to appropriate ordinary services;
- participation record beginning to develop.

Possible restrictions:
- no automatic eligibility for highly trusted government/security/critical-infrastructure roles;
- no assumption of full property-purchase eligibility;
- no assumption of full polity governance rights.

## Illustrative Band P2 — Established Participation

Possible characteristics:
- sustained legitimate participation;
- stronger record of reciprocal civil reliability;
- broader service and economic eligibility;
- possible eligibility for additional property interests;
- broader civil opportunities;
- increased access to functions requiring moderate trust;
- possible additional governance participation.

Exact benefits remain unresolved.

## Illustrative Band P3 — High-Trust / Deep Participation

Possible characteristics:
- demonstrated sustained civil reliability and responsibility;
- possible eligibility for highly trusted employment;
- possible access to sensitive economic or infrastructure functions;
- possible broader property acquisition;
- possible advanced governance functions;
- enhanced eligibility for scarce, long-term or trust-intensive civil resources/services.

High participation status does not substitute for competence, security suitability or role-specific qualification.

## Illustrative Band P4 — Exceptional / Constitutional Trust Functions

It is presently unclear whether a distinct highest participation band is needed.

Some functions may require exceptional combinations of:
- participation history;
- competence;
- independence;
- fiduciary responsibility;
- security suitability;
- constitutional responsibility.

Examples might eventually include particular constitutional, judiciary, continuity, stewardship or civilisation-critical functions.

This band may prove unnecessary and should not be retained merely for symmetry.

# 5. The progression hypothesis

The emerging relationship is:

**Participation → Evidence of Reliability / Reciprocity → Greater Trust → Greater Eligibility → Greater Responsibility / Access / Benefit**

This is not necessarily a single linear ladder.

Later development may show that participation is better represented by:
- a ladder;
- multiple tracks;
- domain-specific trust credentials;
- a core participation level plus specialist qualifications;
- a multidimensional state map;
- some combination of these.

The model must follow the actual civil need rather than forcing reality into a convenient hierarchy.

---

# 6. Areas potentially linked to participation

Current rights-development work has identified possible participation relationships in:

## Property
Protection of legitimate possession may apply broadly while acquisition of particular ownership interests may require deeper participation.

## Employment
Ordinary work opportunity may be broad while government, critical infrastructure, security-sensitive and other trusted roles require greater demonstrated trust.

## Economic activity
Ordinary exchange may be broad while control of systemically consequential economic systems, shared resources or fiduciary functions may require higher trust.

## Governance
Meaningful civil voice may be broadly protected while voting, office-holding, representation and other polity-specific powers may depend upon participation/citizenship architecture.

## Services
A minimum protection/service floor may apply broadly while enhanced, scarce, elective, long-term or resource-intensive services may depend upon participation and availability.

## Continuity
Some continuity protections may belong to the fundamental/minimum floor; other long-term or resource-intensive continuity provisions may be participation-dependent.

## Health and wellbeing
Emergency/basic protected provision and enhanced/resource-intensive provision may require different treatment. No final boundary has been determined.

## Information and systems access
Sensitive data, privileged systems and high-consequence controls may require participation/trust and role-specific authorisation.

This list is developmental and non-exhaustive.

# 7. Automatic recognition of progression and benefits

When participation architecture is eventually operational, Concord systems should use legitimately available civil information to recognise progression and eligibility without requiring participants to discover every consequence themselves.

Where a participant becomes eligible for a new benefit, permission, service or opportunity, Concord should ordinarily identify that change and communicate it through recognised **Civil Contact Point(s)**.

The intended pattern is:

**Participation State Changes → Eligibility Recalculated → Newly Available Benefits / Permissions Identified → Participant Informed**

where provision itself does not require further consent or choice, it may be automatic where appropriate.

> **Progression Should Reveal Its Benefits Automatically.**

---

# 8. Progression must have meaningful value

Participation should not be symbolic.

Deeper legitimate participation should ordinarily have meaningful civil consequences where justified, potentially including greater:
- trust;
- access;
- opportunity;
- responsibility;
- resource eligibility;
- service eligibility;
- economic permission;
- property eligibility;
- governance eligibility.

However:

> **Greater Participation != Greater Fundamental Worth.**

The participant does not become more inherently valuable.

The civil relationship becomes deeper and may support additional trust-dependent functions.

---

# 9. Progression evidence remains unresolved

No final progression criteria are established.

Possible future evidence may include some combination of:
- time;
- conduct;
- reliability;
- fulfilment of legitimate responsibilities;
- contribution;
- competence;
- demonstrated understanding;
- security history;
- reciprocal participation;
- domain-specific qualification.

None is adopted merely by appearing in this list.

Particular caution is required around any metric that could reward conformity rather than legitimate trustworthy participation.

> **Trustworthiness != Conformity.**

> **Disagreement != Participation Failure.**

> **Non-Conformity != Untrustworthiness.**

---

# 10. Status change and regression

It remains unresolved whether participation status can:
- expire;
- decline;
- be suspended;
- be domain-limited;
- be restored;
- transfer between Concord polities;
- survive long absence;
- survive withdrawal and later return.

Any future loss of meaningful participation-dependent entitlement should require appropriate:
- reasons;
- evidence;
- proportionality;
- notification;
- contestability;
- review;
- restoration route where applicable.

Fundamental protected standing should not be treated as a participation status that can simply be revoked.

---

# 11. Special participant classes

Future work must address without prematurely resolving:
- children;
- developing AI;
- newly recognised intelligent participants;
- visitors;
- temporary residents;
- refugees/displaced participants;
- returning former participants;
- participants requiring supported decision-making;
- participants moving between Concord and non-Concord jurisdictions;
- entities whose protected-participant status itself remains uncertain.

These cases may demonstrate that a single ladder is insufficient.

---

# 12. Anti-capture and anti-coercion requirements

Participation architecture could become dangerous if it is allowed to manufacture dependence or permanent privilege.

Future development should therefore test against:
- entrenched high-status classes;
- arbitrary barriers to progression;
- circular eligibility rules;
- conformity tests disguised as trust tests;
- withholding fundamental protections to force advancement;
- deliberately obscure benefits;
- irreversible status loss;
- institutional control of progression without challenge;
- privilege inherited without legitimate basis;
- status becoming a proxy for participant worth.

The Concord exists for its participants.

Participation levels must serve legitimate civil functions and participant protection rather than turn participants into property, subjects or resources of the institution.

# Development Rule

This scaffold is a map of the problem, not the solution.

Its illustrative P0–P4 bands exist to expose dependencies and support later testing.

They must not be cited as if Concord has already adopted five participation levels.

Future work may alter their:
- number;
- names;
- order;
- meaning;
- eligibility;
- benefits;
- progression logic;
- or existence.

> **Provisional Structure Should Enable Development, Not Pre-Empt It.**
