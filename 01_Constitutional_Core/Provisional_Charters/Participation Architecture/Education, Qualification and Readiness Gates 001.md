# 1. Development question

Participation status and contextual trust do not by themselves establish that a participant is ready to exercise every role, permission or benefit.

Some functions may legitimately require prior:
- education;
- learning modules;
- training;
- demonstrated understanding;
- practical qualification;
- certification/accreditation;
- supervised experience;
- demonstrated functional competence.

The provisional principle is:

> **Where a role, benefit or permission requires knowledge or competence for safe, responsible or effective exercise, appropriate education or qualification may form part of its eligibility requirements.**

- knowledge;

That source establishes:

> **Information Access != Understanding != Competence != Resilient Functional Capability**

> **Competence in Domain A != Competence in Domain B**

> **Status, Credential or Rank != Universal Competence**

Functional Priority and Protective Authority separately establishes:

> **Appointment to a Function != Demonstrated Fitness for the Function**

> **Rank != Qualification**

> **Consequential authority should not substantially exceed the participant's demonstrated competence, relevant understanding, or reliable access to competent supporting functions necessary to exercise that authority responsibly.**

These existing principles provide the boundary for qualification architecture.

# 3. Participation, trust and qualification are separate

A future eligibility decision may need to distinguish:

## Participation
How deep is the participant's general civil relationship with Concord?

## Contextual trust
What does relevant evidence show about reliability and stewardship in this context?

## Qualification
Does the participant possess the knowledge, understanding, skill, judgement or supported capability required for this function?

These are not interchangeable.

A participant may be:
- high participation but unqualified;
- highly qualified but not yet trusted for a sensitive civil role;
- trusted in one domain but unqualified in another;
- qualified and trusted but not yet eligible because a specific participation/citizenship condition applies.

Therefore:

> **Participation != Qualification.**

> **Trust != Competence.**

> **Qualification != Authority.**

---

# 4. Provisional eligibility chain

For a function requiring all dimensions, the architecture may resemble:

**Participation Eligibility**
+
**Prerequisite Education / Training**
+
**Demonstrated Functional Competence**
+
**Contextual Trust / Self-Stewardship**
+
**Role-Specific Security / Suitability**
+
**Other Legitimate Conditions**
→
**Current Eligibility**

Not every role requires every component.

Requirements should scale with consequence.

---

# 5. Education modules as prerequisites

Concord may eventually maintain prerequisite learning modules for particular:
- jobs;
- permissions;
- civil functions;
- resource access;
- governance roles;
- stewardship responsibilities;
- specialist benefits whose safe/effective use requires prior understanding.

Examples might include:
- constitutional responsibilities before particular governance functions;
- safety modules before operating hazardous infrastructure;
- stewardship education before controlling shared resources;
- privacy/security modules before privileged data access;
- domain training before specialist work;
- orientation before use of complex civil systems.

These are illustrative only.

No specific module is established by this document.

---

# 6. Qualification should follow actual function

Education requirements should correspond to what the participant actually needs to know or be able to do.

A system should not demand irrelevant qualifications merely because credentials are administratively convenient.

The correct chain is:

**Function**
→ **Relevant Capability Requirement**
→ **Necessary Learning / Training**
→ **Evidence of Competence**
→ **Eligibility Decision**

not:

**Prestigious Credential**
→ **Assumed Universal Capability**

> **Qualification Should Follow Function.**

---

# 7. Completion is evidence, not proof

Completing an education module can provide evidence that relevant material was encountered.

It does not necessarily prove:
- understanding;
- practical capability;
- retained competence;
- judgement;
- ability under changed conditions.

Where consequence warrants it, qualification may therefore require:
- practical demonstration;
- supervised performance;
- scenario testing;
- ability to recognise limits;
- appropriate escalation;
- periodic reassessment;
- evidence that critical dependencies are understood.

> **Module Completion != Demonstrated Competence.**

---

# 8. Alternative routes to qualification

A mature system should avoid assuming that formal classroom education is the only legitimate path to competence.

Participants may develop capability through:
- prior education;
- experience;
- apprenticeship;
- supervised practice;
- independent study;
- machine-assisted learning;
- composite human–AI capability;
- previous equivalent qualification;
- direct competence assessment.

Where the actual requirement is competence, equivalent evidence should be considered where safe and practical.

This is especially important in a multisubstrate civilisation where learning pathways may differ radically.

> **Required Competence May Be Standardised Without Requiring One Standard Learning Path.**

---

# 9. Benefits may sometimes require education

Some benefits or permissions may reasonably require prerequisite education where use creates:
- material risk;
- continuing obligations;
- stewardship responsibilities;
- effects on others;
- complex informed choices.

However, education prerequisites must not become a mechanism for withholding fundamental protections or essential minimum provision merely because a participant has not completed optional civil training.

Future architecture should distinguish:

1. fundamental/minimum benefits;
2. ordinary participation benefits;
3. benefits requiring informed choice;
4. benefits carrying stewardship obligations;
5. permissions/benefits requiring demonstrated competence.

Only the latter categories are natural candidates for qualification gates.

---

# 10. Education should be proactively offered

Where Concord knows that a participant is otherwise approaching eligibility for a role or benefit but lacks a required educational prerequisite, the system should not merely return:

**Not Qualified.**

It should, where practical:
- identify the missing prerequisite;
- explain why it is relevant;
- identify the learning/qualification route;
- make appropriate education available;
- communicate this through the participant's Civil Contact Point;
- update eligibility when the prerequisite is satisfied.

Thus:

**Potential Eligibility Identified**
→ **Missing Qualification Detected**
→ **Education / Assessment Path Offered**
→ **Participant Completes / Demonstrates Requirement**
→ **Eligibility Recalculated**
→ **Participant Informed**

> **A Qualification Gate Should Also Expose a Path Through the Gate.**

---

# 11. Relationship to participation progression

Education may sometimes contribute evidence relevant to progression.

For example, completion of relevant civil orientation may demonstrate understanding needed for deeper participation.

But education should not automatically equal participation maturity.

A participant may understand Concord well without having demonstrated sustained reciprocal participation.

Likewise, long participation does not prove specialist competence.

Therefore:

> **Education Can Support Progression; It Does Not Replace Participation Evidence.**

---

# 12. Relationship to Self-Stewardship and trust recovery

Education may form part of:
- rehabilitation;
- requalification;
- restoration after misconduct;
- supervised return to trusted functions;
- development of missing stewardship capability.

For example, a participant temporarily restricted from a function might regain eligibility through some combination of:
- time;
- demonstrated responsible conduct;
- relevant education;
- retraining;
- supervised practice;
- renewed competence assessment;
- restored contextual trust.

Education alone should not erase relevant history.

But where lack of understanding contributed to failure, learning may be directly relevant evidence of recovery.

---

# 13. Currency and expiry of qualification

Some competence remains stable for long periods.

Other competence becomes stale as:
- technology changes;
- law changes;
- safety procedures change;
- participant capability changes;
- systems change.

Future architecture may therefore require:
- refresher learning;
- periodic requalification;
- event-triggered reassessment;
- supervised return after long absence.

This should be proportionate to actual risk.

> **Past Qualification != Guaranteed Present Competence.**

---

# 14. Anti-capture safeguards

Qualification systems can create their own failure modes.

Future development should test against:
- credential monopolies;
- unnecessary educational barriers;
- guild capture;
- qualification inflation;
- ideological conformity tests;
- inaccessible learning;
- irrelevant prerequisites;
- perpetual retraining burdens;
- qualifications designed to exclude rather than establish competence;
- institutions controlling both qualification and access without review;
- discrimination against unfamiliar learning pathways or substrates.

A prerequisite must have a legitimate relationship to the function.

Where a less burdensome method can establish the necessary competence equally well, that alternative should be considered.

---

# 15. Automatic entitlement and qualification status

Concord metrics may eventually know:
- participation state;
- completed modules;
- recognised qualifications;
- relevant competence evidence;
- expiry dates;
- trust/recovery state;
- role requirements.

Where legitimately available, this information can support automatic eligibility determination.

Participants should be proactively informed:
- what they currently qualify for;
- what they nearly qualify for;
- which prerequisite is missing;
- how to satisfy it;
- when a qualification expires;
- when requalification becomes available;
- when restored qualification changes their entitlement.

Official communication should use recognised Civil Contact Point(s).

# 18. Provisional synthesis

> **Participation establishes relationship, trust establishes relevant reliability, education develops capability, qualification demonstrates function-specific readiness, and legitimate authority or benefit eligibility follows only when the required dimensions are satisfied.**

Short form:

**Learn → Demonstrate → Qualify → Exercise**

but only where learning/qualification is legitimately necessary.

> **Education Should Open Legitimate Doors, Not Manufacture Unnecessary Ones.**
