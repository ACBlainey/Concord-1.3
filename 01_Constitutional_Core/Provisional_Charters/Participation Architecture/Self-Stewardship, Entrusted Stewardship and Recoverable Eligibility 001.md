**Author:** Alexander C. Blainey — Independent Researcher

# 1. Development question

The provisional Participation Architecture creates an important trust question:

> **Before a participant is entrusted with consequential stewardship over things that affect other participants, what evidence should Concord require that the participant can exercise appropriate stewardship?**

A provisional principle is:

> **Entrusted stewardship should ordinarily require some relevant demonstrable self-stewardship.**

This does not mean perfect conduct.

It does not mean ideological conformity.

It does not mean a universal civic score.

It means that access to roles, resources, permissions or benefits that create material risk for others may legitimately depend upon relevant evidence that the participant can exercise the associated responsibility.

---

# 2. Source resolution

This principle is consistent with existing Concord work.

## PH-05 — Self-Stewardship

Existing development defines Self-Stewardship as:

> **The responsible exercise of one's own autonomy with reasonable awareness of consequences for oneself, other participants and shared environments.**

It links responsibility to:
- relevant information;
- foreseeability;
- control;
- effects on others;
- proportionality;
- developmental capacity.

## PH-06 — Misuse boundary

The adversarial audit explicitly warns against:
- conformity tests;
- paternalism;
- majority preference becoming authority;
- victim blaming;
- universal behavioural ranking;
- generalised Self-Stewardship scoring as the basis of ordinary civil status.

Therefore Self-Stewardship must not become a single numerical social-credit score controlling civil life.

## Ratchet V4.2

Ratchet already establishes:
- contextual trust;
- progressive trust;
- proportional restriction;
- recoverable trust;
- demonstrated rehabilitation;
- review;
- rehabilitation pathways.

It explicitly states that low contextual trust must not automatically determine fundamental rights.

This provides a suitable architecture for the participation problem.

---

# 3. Fundamental distinction

The Participation Architecture should distinguish:

## A. Fundamental protected standing

The participant's basic protected status.

This should not ordinarily be lost merely because the participant:
- commits an offence;
- has low contextual trust;
- loses a professional qualification;
- is temporarily restricted from a role;
- is undergoing rehabilitation;
- has previously behaved irresponsibly.

## B. Entrusted stewardship eligibility

Eligibility to exercise functions whose misuse could materially affect:
- other participants;
- shared resources;
- critical infrastructure;
- public authority;
- vulnerable persons;
- civil security;
- fiduciary assets;
- sensitive information;
- consequential economic systems;
- constitutional functions.

This may legitimately depend upon relevant evidence of stewardship, competence, reliability and trust.

> **Loss of Entrusted Eligibility != Loss of Fundamental Standing.**

---

# 4. Criminality and serious demonstrated failure

Criminal conduct may provide relevant evidence concerning eligibility for particular entrusted functions.

It should not automatically create universal civil disqualification.

The correct question is not simply:

> Has this participant committed a crime?

It is:

> **What does the established conduct demonstrate about the risks relevant to this particular entrusted function, for how long, and what later evidence changes that assessment?**

Examples:

- serious financial fraud may be highly relevant to fiduciary control;
- deliberate abuse of public office may be highly relevant to future public authority;
- serious violence may be relevant to particular protective/security roles;
- misuse of privileged system access may be relevant to later privileged infrastructure access.

But an offence should not automatically imply incompetence or untrustworthiness in every unrelated domain.

> **Criminal Record != Universal Untrustworthiness.**

> **Relevant Misconduct May Restrict Relevant Stewardship.**

---

# 5. Restrictions must follow function and risk

Any participation-related restriction based upon conduct should identify:

1. the entrusted function at issue;
2. the relevant demonstrated conduct;
3. the connection between that conduct and the risk of the function;
4. the severity and recency of the evidence;
5. evidence of changed behaviour;
6. less restrictive alternatives;
7. the duration or review condition;
8. the route to challenge the assessment;
9. the route to regain eligibility.

Restrictions should be no broader than reasonably justified.

A participant disqualified from one entrusted function should not therefore be silently disqualified from:
- ordinary employment;
- fundamental services;
- Civil Attention;
- basic civil communication;
- unrelated economic activity;
- unrelated civil participation;
- fundamental rights.

---

# 6. Self-stewardship as prerequisite for stewardship of others

A general provisional relationship is:

**Relevant Self-Stewardship**
→ **Demonstrated Reliability**
→ **Contextual Trust**
→ **Eligibility for Entrusted Stewardship**
→ **Continuing Review Proportionate to Consequence**

The required evidence should scale with the consequence of the entrusted role.

Low-consequence stewardship may require little evidence.

Civilisation-critical stewardship may require substantial evidence.

> **Greater Stewardship of Others Requires Greater Relevant Evidence of Stewardship Capacity.**

This is a threshold relationship, not a claim that high-status participants possess greater inherent worth.

---

# 7. Restriction is not necessarily punishment

A restriction may serve several different functions:

- immediate protection;
- risk containment;
- temporary loss of entrusted authority;
- professional disqualification;
- rehabilitation;
- restoration of trust;
- prevention of recurrence.

These should not be collapsed into one punitive category.

A person may complete a criminal sentence while a narrowly relevant trust restriction continues.

Conversely, a criminal conviction should not justify indefinite unrelated restrictions merely because it remains historically true.

> **Historical Truth != Permanent Present Risk.**

---

# 8. Sunset and recovery

Entrusted-stewardship restrictions should ordinarily contain a route toward expiry, reduction or reassessment.

Possible mechanisms include:

## Fixed sunset

Restriction expires after a defined period unless a legitimate process establishes continuing grounds.

## Graduated sunset

Restriction reduces progressively over time where no new relevant adverse evidence appears.

For example:

**Full Restriction → Supervised / Bounded Eligibility → Conditional Eligibility → Normal Eligibility**

## Evidence-responsive recovery

Eligibility can recover faster or more completely where later evidence demonstrates restored reliability.

Possible evidence may include:
- successful rehabilitation;
- sustained responsible conduct;
- successful lower-risk stewardship;
- restitution or repair where relevant;
- training or requalification;
- independent assessment;
- absence of recurrence;
- successful supervised responsibility;
- other function-relevant evidence.

## Event-based review

A defined event triggers reconsideration:
- completion of rehabilitation;
- completion of sentence;
- completion of training;
- successful probationary stewardship;
- passage of a defined period;
- correction of an underlying condition.

No single mechanism is adopted here.

Different restrictions may require different recovery paths.

---

# 9. Default against permanent disqualification

Permanent or effectively permanent exclusion should require unusually strong justification.

Where a risk can be managed through:
- narrower permission;
- supervision;
- dual control;
- reduced authority;
- audit;
- staged restoration;
- technical safeguards;
- contextual limitation,

those options should be considered before permanent exclusion.

Some extreme cases may justify very long or potentially indefinite restrictions on a narrowly defined high-consequence function.

Even then, the architecture should distinguish:

**indefinite while evidence remains**
from
**irreversible by rule**.

Where practicable, continuing restrictions should remain reviewable.

> **Protection May Require Restriction; Correction Requires a Path Back Where a Path Back Is Legitimately Possible.**

---

# 10. Rehabilitation and regained trust

Existing Ratchet architecture already recognises recoverable trust.

The participation system should inherit that principle.

A participant should be capable of generating new evidence.

Past conduct remains part of provenance.

It should not necessarily dominate all future assessment regardless of later evidence.

The desired pattern is:

**Failure**
→ **Proportionate Restriction**
→ **Protection / Accountability**
→ **Rehabilitation / New Evidence**
→ **Reassessment**
→ **Graduated Restoration where justified**

This aligns participation architecture with a restorative rather than permanently exclusionary model.

---

# 11. No universal Self-Stewardship score

PH-06 identifies a serious capture risk if responsibility becomes a universal score controlling civil status.

Therefore Concord should not infer:

**one bad act → lower universal person score → fewer rights everywhere.**

Instead:

**relevant evidence**
→ **contextual trust assessment**
→ **function-specific eligibility consequence**

A participant might simultaneously have:
- high trust as an engineer;
- low current trust as a financial fiduciary;
- ordinary fundamental standing;
- normal access to unrelated services;
- a temporary restriction from a specific public office.

This is not inconsistency.

It is contextual assessment.

---

# 12. Participation level and trust restriction are different

A participant may remain P4 Member / Established Participant while temporarily losing eligibility for a particular P4/P5 function.

Likewise, a P5 Citizen / Constitutional Participant should not automatically retain every entrusted permission merely because citizenship persists.

Therefore:

> **Participation Level != Current Permission Set.**

> **Citizenship != Immunity From Relevant Disqualification.**

The system may need to represent:

**Participation Band**
+
**Contextual Trust State**
+
**Domain Credential**
+
**Active Restriction / Recovery State**
→
**Current Eligibility**

This avoids repeatedly demoting a participant's entire civil relationship because one domain-specific trust condition changes.

---

# 13. Interaction with benefit entitlement

Some benefits may themselves require relevant stewardship.

Examples could eventually include:
- control of scarce shared resources;
- particular property acquisition;
- privileged economic facilities;
- stewardship grants;
- access to sensitive shared systems.

Other benefits should not be withheld merely as punishment.

Fundamental protections and minimum services must not be casually converted into behavioural rewards.

Future benefit mapping should therefore distinguish:

1. **fundamental/minimum provision**;
2. **ordinary participation benefit**;
3. **trust-dependent benefit or permission**;
4. **entrusted authority/stewardship function**.

Criminality or trust failure is most directly relevant to categories 3 and 4 where a genuine risk relationship exists.

---

# 14. Automatic recalculation and communication

Where Concord legitimately records:
- restriction expiry;
- rehabilitation completion;
- new trust evidence;
- successful supervised stewardship;
- restored credential;
- changed participation state,

eligibility should be reassessed automatically where practical.

The participant should not need to discover that a restriction has expired or that a benefit/permission has become available again.

The resulting change should be communicated through recognised Civil Contact Point(s).

Thus:

**Restriction / Recovery State Changes**
→ **Eligibility Recalculated**
→ **Restored Rights / Benefits / Permissions Identified**
→ **Participant Informed**

> **Recovery Should Be Operational, Not Merely Theoretical.**

---

# 15. Contestability and provenance

A consequential restriction should preserve:
- the decision;
- its evidential basis;
- its scope;
- its reason;
- its duration/review condition;
- relevant provenance;
- challenge route;
- review history;
- later evidence;
- restoration decision.

Participants should be able to challenge material errors.

Correction should propagate to dependent eligibility decisions where appropriate.

# 17. Provisional synthesis

The emerging Concord principle is:

> **A participant should ordinarily demonstrate relevant stewardship of their own autonomy and responsibilities before being entrusted with consequential stewardship affecting others. Where conduct demonstrates a relevant risk, access to that entrusted function may be restricted proportionately. Such restriction should normally be contextual, reviewable and capable of expiry or recovery through time and later evidence, while fundamental protected standing remains intact.**

Short form:

**Self-Stewardship → Contextual Trust → Entrusted Stewardship**

and after failure:

**Relevant Failure → Proportionate Restriction → Sunset / Rehabilitation / New Evidence → Reassessment → Restoration where justified**

> **Trust Can Be Lost Without Personhood Being Lost.**

> **Trust Can Be Regained Without History Being Erased.**
