## 1. Development finding

Development of the Defence domain exposed a rights and authority relationship that should be represented above Defence itself.

The Concord already establishes:
- **Avoid Unnecessary Harm**;
- **Respect Autonomy**;
- **Respect Life and Dignity**;
- the foundational relationship that the Concord exists for its participants and participants do not belong to the Concord;
- Bounded Contextual Authority as a candidate general authority architecture.

Together these support a stronger explicit protected relation:

> **A participant possesses primary legitimate authority over their own self and is not property or a general-purpose resource of another participant, institution or the Concord.**

This document uses **self-ownership** as a compact term for that protected relation.

Self-ownership does not imply that every action involving the self is consequence-free, that obligations cannot be voluntarily accepted, or that autonomy overrides the equal rights and protected standing of others.

---

## 2. Derivation of self-ownership

### Autonomy

Blainey's Law 2 requires recognition of the right of others to direct their own lives, subject to the ethical minimum.

Meaningful self-direction requires that another actor does not possess a superior general claim over the participant's body, mind, labour, capabilities or continued existence merely by status.

### Non-harm

Blainey's Law 1 requires unnecessary harm to be minimised.

A participant who could be compelled to submit to wrongful harm without a protected capacity to resist would possess autonomy only nominally.

### Life and dignity

Blainey's Law 7 recognises inherent value and rejects treating intelligent participants merely as means to an end.

Treating a participant as property, expendable material or an involuntary civil resource conflicts directly with that relation.

Candidate derivation:

**Autonomy + Non-Harm + Life/Dignity -> Self-Ownership / Primary Self-Authority**

Therefore:

**Participant != Property Of Concord**

**Participant != Property Of Another Participant**

**Capability != General Claim Over That Capability**

**Citizenship != Ownership By Civilisation**

---

## 3. Scope of self-ownership

Self-ownership protects the participant's primary relationship to their own:
- body or embodiment;
- mind/cognitive process where applicable;
- labour;
- capabilities;
- movement and withdrawal, subject to legitimate law and voluntarily accepted obligations;
- participation in dangerous activity;
- consent to ordinary use of their person or capabilities by others.

The exact expression will differ across human, AI and hybrid substrates and requires later testing.

Self-ownership is not equivalent to unrestricted sovereignty over every consequence of one's actions.

**Self-Ownership != Freedom To Harm Others Without Constraint**

**Autonomy != Immunity From Legitimate Obligation**

A participant may voluntarily create obligations through legitimate agreement. Some lawful duties may also arise from the rights of others or other strongly justified civil relations. Such obligations require independent justification; they are not evidence that the participant is owned.

---

## 4. Self-defence as a necessary protection of self-ownership

A protected self that has no legitimate authority to resist wrongful imminent or ongoing harm would possess an incomplete form of autonomy.

Self-defence therefore follows naturally from the same source relationship.

Candidate fundamental protection:

> **Every participant has a fundamental right to take necessary and proportionate action to defend themselves against an imminent or ongoing wrongful threat of harm, within legitimate legal bounds.**

This right is not created by the Defence institution.

Defence is downstream of it.

**Defence Institution != Source Of Right To Defend**

---

## 5. Defence of others

The same protected standing applies to other participants.

A participant may therefore possess legitimate authority to act in defence of another where the relevant defensive conditions exist.

Candidate protection:

> **A participant may take necessary and proportionate action to protect another participant from an imminent or ongoing wrongful threat of harm, subject to legitimate legal bounds.**

This creates a right/capacity to assist, not a general obligation to sacrifice oneself.

**Right To Defend Another != General Duty To Sacrifice Self**

A separate reasonable duty to assist may be defined by Law where assistance can be provided without disproportionate danger. Its scope requires later legal development.

---

## 6. Defensive authority as directly exercisable Bounded Contextual Authority

Self-defence exposes an important rights-authority relationship.

Ordinarily, authority may require delegation, appointment or another institutional source.

Immediate defence cannot depend upon obtaining case-specific institutional permission where delay would defeat the protected right.

The constitutional recognition of the right supplies standing authority that becomes exercisable when the relevant context exists.

Candidate architecture:

**Protected Standing + Legitimate Defensive Context + Protective Function + Necessity -> Activated Bounded Defensive Authority**

This authority is:
- contextual;
- functional;
- proportionate;
- temporary;
- non-transferable except where independently justified;
- reviewable.

**Right To Defend -> Standing Eligibility For Defensive Authority**

**Valid Threat Context -> Activation**

**Defensive Necessity Ends -> Context-Derived Authority Ends**

This is a direct application of Bounded Contextual Authority.

---

## 7. Proportionality as an authority boundary

Defensive authority is not a general permission to cause harm.

Law should define usable advance principles for necessity and proportionality.

The relevant question is not necessarily whether defensive force exactly matches aggressive force. It is whether the response falls within what is reasonably necessary and proportionate to the legitimate protective function under the circumstances.

Therefore:

**Defensive Necessity != Unlimited Force**

**Proportionality != Mechanical Equality Of Force**

**Authority To Stop Threat != Authority To Punish**

When the threat or defensive necessity ends, punishment, adjudication and continuing coercive authority require a different legal basis.

---

## 8. Reasonable perception and imperfect information

Defensive decisions may occur under uncertainty and time pressure.

Legal review should therefore distinguish:
- what later became known;
- what the participant actually knew;
- what the participant could reasonably have perceived at the time.

This does not make every claimed fear lawful.

**Claimed Threat != Established Defensive Context**

Nor should hindsight automatically convert a reasonable defensive mistake into misconduct.

Detailed evidential standards belong to Law and Judiciary.

---

## 9. Right of retreat

Self-ownership also protects the participant's ability to avoid or withdraw from danger.

A participant possessing a right to defend does not thereby acquire a duty to fight.

> **A participant may ordinarily choose withdrawal, escape or avoidance rather than defensive engagement where that option is available.**

**Right To Defend != Duty To Fight**

**Right To Assist != General Duty To Enter Disproportionate Danger**

The right of retreat exists from individual self-defence through collective and civilisational defence.

---

## 10. Voluntarily accepted obligations

Self-ownership is compatible with voluntarily accepted duty.

A participant may consensually accept a role whose function requires them to face risks that an ordinary participant may lawfully avoid.

Examples may eventually include:
- defence roles;
- emergency-response roles;
- specialist protective functions;
- other legitimate risk-bearing responsibilities.

Candidate chain:

**Self-Ownership -> Voluntary Consent -> Defined Obligation -> Bounded Duty**

The obligation derives from legitimate agreement and role, not ownership of the participant.

**Consent To Defined Duty != Transfer Of Ownership**

**Accepted Risk != Unlimited Duty Of Sacrifice**

Withdrawal conditions, duration, activation, authority and consequences should be defined as part of the legitimate obligation.

---

## 11. Collective and civilisational defence

The same constitutional mechanism can scale.

**Self-Defence -> Defence Of Others -> Voluntary Collective Defence -> Coordinated Concord Defence**

The ethical and legal foundation does not change merely because the number of protected participants increases.

What changes is:
- scale;
- coordination;
- capability;
- delegation;
- consequence;
- review requirements.

> **Scale Changes Coordination; It Does Not Create a New Moral Licence to Harm.**

Concord Defence therefore coordinates defensive capability; it does not manufacture an independent unlimited right to violence.

---

## 12. Review and accountability

Serious exercise of defensive authority may be reviewed even where it was lawful.

**Review != Presumption Of Misconduct**

Review may examine:
- whether a defensive context reasonably existed;
- what information was available;
- the function being protected;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- available alternatives;
- whether defensive authority had already terminated;
- resulting harm;
- continuing duties after the event.

The review function supports both protection against abuse and protection of participants who acted legitimately under urgent conditions.

## 13. Candidate rights classification

Subject to the existing Rights Confidence and Elevation methodology, this development proposes:

### Self-ownership / primary self-authority
**Candidate class:** A — Fundamental / source-anchored protected relation.

Primary derivation:
- BL2 Respect Autonomy;
- reinforced by BL1 Avoid Unnecessary Harm;
- reinforced by BL7 Respect Life and Dignity.

### Self-defence
**Candidate class:** A — Fundamental / strongly source-derived protection.

It is necessary for meaningful protection of autonomy, self-ownership, life and non-harm where ordinary civil protection cannot intervene in time.

### Defence of others
**Candidate class:** A/B boundary pending dedicated rights-derivation testing.

The protected standing of the threatened participant is strong, but the exact scope of third-party intervention and any associated duties require legal development.

### Right of retreat / refusal of ordinary defensive engagement
**Candidate class:** strongly derived from autonomy/self-ownership, subject to voluntarily accepted obligations and other independently justified legal duties.

No classification in this development note silently amends the current provisional Rights Charter. Elevation remains explicit and corrigible.

## 15. Cross-domain ownership

This architecture crosses several domains without collapsing them.

**Constitutional Core / Rights**
- recognises self-ownership;
- recognises the fundamental defensive protection;
- establishes the protected floor.

**Law**
- defines lawful defensive context;
- necessity;
- proportionality;
- reasonable perception;
- defence of others;
- retreat;
- duties to assist;
- cessation;
- legal consequences.

**Judiciary**
- interprets disputed cases;
- determines facts and lawful application;
- provides review/remedy according to legitimate process.

**Defence**
- coordinates defensive capability at larger scale;
- operates under the same bounded foundation.

**Governance**
- establishes legitimate institutional arrangements without becoming the source of the underlying right.

## 16. Development conclusion

The emerging chain is:

**Autonomy + Non-Harm + Life/Dignity**  
-> **Self-Ownership / Primary Self-Authority**  
-> **Right Of Self-Defence**  
-> **Directly Exercisable Bounded Contextual Defensive Authority**  
-> **Necessary And Proportionate Protective Action**  
-> **Functional Sunset**  
-> **Review / Accountability**

At larger scale:

**Individual Defensive Right**  
-> **Defence Of Others**  
-> **Voluntary Collective Defence**  
-> **Coordinated Concord Defence**

The same protected relation therefore connects personal autonomy to civilisational defence without granting either participants or institutions an unbounded authority to harm.
