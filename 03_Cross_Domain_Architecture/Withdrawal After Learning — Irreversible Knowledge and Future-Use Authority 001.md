# Withdrawal After Learning — Irreversible Knowledge and Future-Use Authority 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 6 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / CROSS-DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary interfaces:** Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal; Derived Information Claims; Protection-Lineage; CIBB; KCS Change Propagation; BTA; Research; Historical; AI/Participation architecture

## 1. Purpose

Consent or another legitimate basis may authorise learning, analysis or model development.

Later, that authority may end.

But knowledge already acquired may not be literally removable from:
- a human mind;
- a trained model;
- an aggregate scientific result;
- a published finding;
- a distributed institutional process;
- a historical record.

The architecture must distinguish ending future authority from pretending prior learning never occurred.

## 2. Existing source resolution

The genomic custody architecture already states:

> **Source Withdrawal != Automatic Erasure of Every Derived Knowledge Object.**

Temporal Consent architecture establishes that authority across time can end and must not be presumed permanent.

BTA establishes that rollback is not restoration unless the prior state is actually re-established.

These together imply a residual problem: irreversible learning.

## 3. Core distinction

> **End Of Source Authority != Erasure Of Acquired Knowledge**

but equally:

> **Persistence Of Knowledge != Persistence Of Authority To Use It**

This is the central resolution.

## 4. Knowledge state versus use authority

A system can truthfully represent:

`KnowledgeState = PERSISTENT`

while:

`UseAuthority = TERMINATED_FOR_PURPOSE_X`

No contradiction exists.

The system does not need to pretend ignorance in order to terminate legitimate use.

## 5. Human memory

A human recipient cannot normally delete a memory on command.

Therefore withdrawal cannot generally require literal forgetting.

But bounded duties can still constrain:
- disclosure;
- publication;
- institutional decision use;
- commercial exploitation;
- onward transfer;
- formal recording;
- re-querying protected sources.

> **Cannot Forget != May Freely Exploit**

## 6. Trained models

A trained model may encode diffuse effects of source data without storing a recoverable source record.

Different states must be distinguished:
- source record retained;
- source record deleted;
- source recoverable from model;
- participant-specific output still producible;
- only aggregate/generalised learning remains;
- contribution effect technically inseparable;
- model can be retrained;
- model cannot reasonably be retrained without disproportionate cost.

Therefore:

**Source Deletion != Model Unlearning**

and:

**Model Persistence != Automatic Continued Participant-Specific Use Authority**

## 7. Withdrawal target

A withdrawal request should identify, where possible, what authority is ending:
- future collection;
- future access;
- future training;
- future participant-specific inference;
- future disclosure;
- future contact;
- future operational use;
- retention of source;
- retention of derivative;
- public publication.

Withdrawal should not be treated as one undifferentiated DELETE command.

## 8. Prospective default

Unless the legitimate basis provides otherwise, withdrawal is primarily prospective.

It ends future reliance on the withdrawn authority.

It does not automatically make prior legitimate acts illegitimate.

> **Withdrawal At t2 != Absence Of Consent At t1**

## 9. Existing derivatives

Existing derivatives require classification.

Possible states:

### D0 — Direct source copy
Strong case for deletion/termination where source-retention authority ends.

### D1 — Participant-specific derivative
May remain protected and may require deletion, sealing, expiry or use termination depending on legitimate basis.

### D2 — Multi-subject/aggregate derivative
Withdrawal may require review but not automatic destruction.

### D3 — Generalised knowledge
A scientific relationship or model capability may no longer be meaningfully attributable to one participant.

Participant-specific use restrictions may still apply where the system can reconstruct or target the participant.

### D4 — Published/public knowledge
Literal recall may be impossible; future official use, correction and re-publication can still be governed.

## 10. No derivative-complexity escape

An institution must not deliberately transform source information into increasingly complex derivatives merely to defeat withdrawal.

> **Derivative Complexity != Withdrawal Immunity**

If a derivative remains materially participant-specific or was created to evade a known withdrawal right, protections can continue.

## 11. No impossible-erasure fiction

Conversely, a participant should not be promised literal erasure where it cannot be established.

> **Withdrawal Right != Guarantee Of Physical Forgetting**

The system should state what can actually be:
- deleted;
- sealed;
- excluded from future training;
- excluded from future decisions;
- no longer queried;
- no longer disclosed;
- marked withdrawn;
- retained only as protected provenance.

## 12. Model retraining and proportionality

Retraining may sometimes be technically possible but extremely costly.

The architecture should not assume:
- retraining is always required;
- retraining is never required.

Relevant factors include:
- participant-specific recoverability;
- consequence;
- sensitivity;
- original consent terms;
- scale of contribution;
- feasibility;
- ongoing benefit;
- risk of future participant-specific output;
- alternative mitigation.

> **Technical Feasibility != Automatic Duty**

> **Technical Difficulty != Automatic Exemption**

## 13. Future-use firewall

Where knowledge cannot be removed, future use can still be bounded.

Examples:
- exclude participant identifier from query interface;
- prohibit participant-specific prediction;
- block retrieval of withdrawn records;
- stop future fine-tuning on source;
- prevent publication of participant-specific outputs;
- mark model/result as unavailable for purpose P;
- use aggregate knowledge only.

This is a **future-use firewall**, not fictional forgetting.

## 14. Correction versus withdrawal

Correction and withdrawal are different.

Correction says:
> the source or interpretation was wrong/changed.

Withdrawal says:
> a prior authority for future use has ended.

Correction may propagate epistemically through KCS.

Withdrawal propagates as an authority/use-state change.

They can coexist.

## 15. Historical

Historical may need to preserve:
- that consent existed;
- that withdrawal occurred;
- what was done before withdrawal;
- what derivative states existed;
- what current restrictions apply.

Historical preservation does not create future operational use authority.

## 16. Research publication

A participant withdraws after a legitimate study is published.

The publication need not be falsified or destroyed merely because the participant withdrew later.

Future use of retained participant-level data can end.

New studies cannot silently rely on the old consent.

Where withdrawal materially changes validity—for example a very small cohort—research may need to review conclusions.

## 17. Human expert

A clinician/researcher learns protected information and later loses legitimate access.

The expert cannot erase memory.

They can still be prohibited from:
- consulting the record;
- disclosing the information;
- using it for unrelated purposes;
- treating remembered information as current without legitimate verification.

This is ordinary evidence that information governance cannot depend on literal erasure.

## 18. AI model

An AI model trained on participant data remains operational after withdrawal.

Possible responses depend on actual state:
- delete source;
- prevent future participant-specific retrieval;
- remove external memory/vector entries;
- retrain/unlearn where proportionate and required;
- restrict model use;
- preserve withdrawal provenance;
- test memorisation/recoverability.

Do not claim unlearning unless demonstrated.

> **Unlearning Label != Demonstrated Removal**

## 19. ESCP challenge

Ask:

> What surviving source, derivative, memory, model capability, public output, dependency or participant-specific route would have to exist for the claimed withdrawal implementation to be incomplete?

Also ask the inverse:

> What genuinely independent/generalised knowledge would have to exist for demanding total erasure to exceed the legitimate withdrawal scope?

## 20. Failure modes

- consent immortality;
- impossible forgetting promise;
- derivative-complexity escape;
- automatic model destruction;
- technical-difficulty exemption;
- correction/withdrawal collapse;
- historical erasure;
- hidden retraining reuse;
- participant-specific inference after withdrawal.

## 21. Candidate invariants

WALI-01 — End Of Source Authority != Erasure Of Acquired Knowledge.  
WALI-02 — Persistence Of Knowledge != Persistence Of Authority To Use It.  
WALI-03 — Cannot Forget != May Freely Exploit.  
WALI-04 — Source Deletion != Model Unlearning.  
WALI-05 — Model Persistence != Automatic Continued Participant-Specific Use Authority.  
WALI-06 — Withdrawal At t2 != Absence Of Consent At t1.  
WALI-07 — Derivative Complexity != Withdrawal Immunity.  
WALI-08 — Withdrawal Right != Guarantee Of Physical Forgetting.  
WALI-09 — Technical Feasibility != Automatic Duty.  
WALI-10 — Technical Difficulty != Automatic Exemption.  
WALI-11 — Unlearning Label != Demonstrated Removal.

## 22. Central rule

> **Where Learning Cannot Legitimately Or Physically Be Reversed, Withdrawal Should Terminate The Authority To Continue Using The Knowledge In The Withdrawn Manner Rather Than Falsely Claiming That The Knowledge No Longer Exists.**
