## Purpose

The parent protocol already contains the substantive architecture. Portable transfer supports five bounded clarifications.

## 1. Evidence sources and participants

A source may be indispensable to establishing facts without becoming a negotiating participant or acquiring decision authority.

> **Necessary Evidence Source ≠ Necessary Negotiating Participant**

Where material, distinguish evidence source, affected party, negotiating participant, implementation actor and decision authority.

## 2. Proposed linkage

Use:

**Proposed Linkage → Dependency Test → Scope Ruling → Preserve Unresolved Matter Where Appropriate → Return to Function**

> **Out-of-Scope Proposal ≠ Protocol Violation by Default**

Persistence after a scope ruling, bypass of the agreed grammar, or prohibited cross-domain coercion may then be recorded separately as a protocol violation.

## 3. Observable exploitation

> **Exploitative Structure ≠ Proven Malicious Intent**

Record observable behaviour and procedural effect. Do not infer motive without independent evidence.

## 4. Legitimate non-agreement

### N1 — Legitimate Non-Agreement

The protocol operated as intended but participants did not find mutually acceptable terms.

> **No Agreement ≠ Architecture Failure**

N1 may trigger later review but must not be silently converted into F1–F5.

## 5. Pre-existing authority

A summit may end without agreement while an external actor subsequently acts under authority that existed independently of the summit.

> **Negotiated Agreement ≠ Independent Exercise of Pre-Existing Authority**

The forum neither creates nor invalidates that authority. Any external action remains accountable to the architecture that actually authorises it.

## 6. Preserved kernel

Nothing here alters Minimum Necessary Negotiation, Functional Boundedness ≠ Functional Isolation, deliberate dependency-based scope expansion, Functional Failure Containment, non-inference, anti-capture, bounded success, F1–F5, emergency compression or repeated-use transition.
