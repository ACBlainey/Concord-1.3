# Authored Risk and Contextual Wrappers — Functional, Relational and Abstract Context Integration 001

## 1. Purpose

The new Authored Risk Acceptance architecture is not a separate solution to the contextual problem.

Concord V1.2 had already generalised the boxing case into Contextual Wrapper Architecture (CWA).

The relevant V1.2 finding is that the universal primitive is the **bounded context**, not physical space.

A bounded context may be:
- physical;
- digital;
- informational;
- computational;
- social/relational;
- functional/activity-defined;
- temporal;
- conceptual;
- hybrid.

Therefore authored risk should be integrated as a contextual-wrapper problem.

## 2. Boxing provenance

Boxing is a particularly clear functional wrapper.

Outside the boxing context, deliberately striking another participant may be prohibited assault.

Inside a legitimately constituted boxing match, specified striking becomes contextually permitted under:
- participant consent;
- sporting rules;
- defined targets;
- timing;
- roles;
- safety mechanisms;
- referee authority;
- termination conditions.

But the wrapper does not legalise or legitimise arbitrary violence.

> **Contextual Permission != General Permission**

A legal punch may cause catastrophic injury or death without that result being the participant's objective.

A prohibited low blow or other illegal technique may cause less actual harm while still violating the wrapper.

Therefore:

> **Permissibility Cannot Be Derived From Outcome Severity Alone**

## 3. The wrapper is not the ring

The physical ring may help identify the context, but it is not the complete wrapper.

A boxing match may begin or end while participants remain in the same physical space.

Training, sparring, competition, medical examination and post-bout interaction can occur in the same building under different rules.

Therefore:

> **Physical Boundary != Complete Contextual Boundary**

The boxing wrapper is substantially **functional + relational + temporal + physical + rule-defined**.

## 4. Authored risk as wrapper state

Where a participant legitimately enters a risky context, the wrapper can carry the participant's authored relation to risk.

A risk wrapper should make legible:

**<Context, Activation, Participant, Role, Objective, Material Known Risks, Accepted Residual Risks, Prohibited Risks/Acts, Safeguards, Authority, Responsibilities, Third-Party Exposure, Withdrawal/Exit, State-Change Triggers, Emergency Rules, Termination, Reversion>**

This does not mean the wrapper creates consent.

> **Wrapper != Consent Generator**

Rather, the wrapper records and operationalises the conditions under which valid consent/authorship applies.

## 5. Abstract wrappers

The important extension is that risk contexts need not be dangerous physical environments.

### Functional wrapper
A participant undertakes a hazardous activity such as boxing, diving, repair work or emergency response.

### Role wrapper
A participant assumes a bounded role carrying special permissions, responsibilities or risks.

### Employment wrapper
A participant accepts defined work under known conditions, duties and residual risks.

### Relational wrapper
A professional or fiduciary relationship changes confidentiality, duty and permissible action.

### Temporal wrapper
A temporary emergency, maintenance period or operational phase changes applicable permissions and risk.

### Informational wrapper
Access to sensitive information changes duties even if the participant never changes location.

### Conceptual wrapper
A simulation, deliberation, hypothetical exercise or research process can permit actions/reasoning whose outputs have no direct operational authority.

### Computational wrapper
An AI or human operator may act within a sandbox under permissions that do not exist in production.

Thus:

> **Context Can Travel With Function, Role, Relationship Or State**

It need not be tied to coordinates.

## 6. Wrapper activation

A wrapper requires a meaningful activation event.

Examples:
- bell/start signal begins boxing round;
- employment duty begins;
- participant assumes an emergency-response role;
- maintenance mode activates;
- clinical procedure begins;
- research protocol begins;
- confidential relationship is established;
- sandbox session begins.

> **Context Activation != Mere Presence**

A spectator beside a boxing ring does not inherit boxer permissions.

A worker inside a hazardous factory does not automatically consent to every hazardous function performed there.

## 7. Wrapper participation

Presence and participation are distinct.

> **Presence In Context != Acceptance Of Contextual Risk**

Participation should identify:
- participant;
- role;
- scope;
- relevant knowledge;
- applicable permissions;
- accepted risk;
- withdrawal conditions.

This becomes especially important in employment, where physical presence may be economically compelled or role-specific.

## 8. Wrapper risk grammar

Authored risk can be expressed as:

**Legitimate Context
+ Sufficient Decision Authorship
+ Material Risk Legibility
+ Voluntary Participation
+ Bounded Rules/Safeguards
-> Authored Contextual Risk Acceptance**

This produces no general waiver.

> **Authored Contextual Risk != General Consent To Harm**

## 9. Inherent, residual and introduced risk

Wrappers should distinguish:

### Inherent risk
Risk inseparable from the legitimate activity without materially changing its nature.

### Controlled risk
Risk reduced by rules, training, equipment, process or supervision.

### Residual risk
Risk remaining after legitimate controls.

### Introduced avoidable risk
Risk created by negligence, rule violation, deception, defective systems or unnecessary exposure.

A participant may author inherent/residual risk.

The wrapper does not automatically authorise introduced avoidable risk.

> **Acceptance Of Wrapper Risk != Acceptance Of Wrapper Violation**

## 10. Nested wrappers

Contexts can nest.

Example:

**Civil context
-> sports venue
-> boxing event
-> boxing match
-> active round
-> emergency medical state**

Each layer may change:
- access;
- rules;
- authority;
- risk;
- responsibility.

> **Nested Context != Inherited Rule Set**

A referee's authority during an active round does not become general authority over the boxer after the match.

Emergency medical authority after loss of capacity is a new/current wrapper, not an unlimited extension of sporting consent.

## 11. Wrapper transition

The risk architecture becomes especially powerful when combined with BTA.

A participant may move:

**Ordinary context
-> informed entry
-> active risky context
-> changed participant state
-> emergency/protective context
-> recovery
-> return/exit**

Permissions and authority must be re-evaluated at transitions.

> **Context Change -> Authority Revalidation**

## 12. Boxing state example

### B0 — ordinary interaction
No permission to strike.

### B1 — pre-bout preparation
Medical/sporting rules apply; no match striking permission.

### B2 — active round
Rule-bounded striking permission active; referee safety authority active; authored residual risk active.

### B3 — round stopped
Striking permission terminates even though participants remain in ring.

### B4 — participant incapacitated/injured
Sporting permission contracts; emergency Health context may activate under its own predicates.

### B5 — bout terminated
Match wrapper ends; ordinary rules/relevant medical wrapper remain.

This demonstrates:

> **Same Space + Different State = Different Wrapper**

## 13. Employment as abstract wrapper

Employment is not merely a workplace.

A role may continue:
- remotely;
- while travelling;
- at a customer site;
- during an emergency;
- across digital systems.

Therefore the employment wrapper can be primarily functional/relational rather than spatial.

It can define:
- role;
- agreed functions;
- competence requirements;
- ordinary risks;
- exceptional risks;
- employer duties;
- participant duties;
- authority;
- working-time limits;
- equipment;
- safety controls;
- refusal/withdrawal routes;
- transition when conditions materially change.

> **Employment Wrapper != Employer Ownership**

## 14. Material change

If risk materially changes outside the wrapper originally authored:

**Material Context Change
-> Wrapper State Change
-> New Legibility
-> Authority/Competence Review
-> Renewed Authorship Where Required**

Examples:
- new hazardous substance;
- equipment failure;
- changed weather;
- unexpected structural instability;
- new medical impairment;
- changed threat environment;
- substantially increased workload/fatigue.

> **Consent To Wrapper State A != Consent To Materially Different Wrapper State B**

## 15. Wrapper authority

CWA's invariant remains:

> **Boundary != Access != Rules != Authority**

A context may identify a risk without authorising anyone to impose it.

A participant may accept a risk without granting an employer unlimited authority.

A referee may possess bounded authority without possessing sovereignty.

A safety officer may stop work under defined rules without owning the worker's general risk choices.

## 16. Wrapper externalities

Risk accepted by participants may create effects outside the wrapper.

Examples:
- motorsport risk to spectators;
- laboratory contamination;
- industrial pollution;
- financial/systemic risk;
- digital sandbox escape;
- rescue action affecting third parties.

Therefore:

> **Contextual Boundary != Externality Boundary**

Participants can author their own exposure but cannot automatically author exposure for nonparticipants.

## 17. Abstract harmful permissions

The boxing example generalises beyond physical harm.

A wrapper may permit otherwise restricted actions such as:
- accessing confidential data for treatment;
- executing destructive commands in a sandbox;
- challenging arguments aggressively in a formal adversarial review;
- handling dangerous materials in research;
- temporarily controlling another participant's movement under valid emergency authority;
- taking financial risk within a bounded investment mandate.

The same logic applies:

> **Contextual Permission Must Remain Attached To Context, Function And Role**

## 18. Wrapper exit and reversion

CWA requires exit/termination/reversion.

For authored risk this is essential.

Where withdrawal is possible:

**Valid Withdrawal
-> Participation Permission Contracts/Ends
-> Contextual Authority Revalidates
-> Ordinary Rules Reassert Unless Another Wrapper Applies**

Where immediate exit is physically impossible, the wrapper should define safe transition rather than pretending withdrawal has no effect.

> **Delayed Physical Exit != Continuing Unlimited Consent**

## 19. Decision authorship inside wrappers

A participant may enter a wrapper at C0/C1 and later lose relevant authorship.

The original authored risk remains historical provenance but cannot supply unlimited present consent.

> **Prior Authorship != Permanent Present Authorship**

The current state determines which protective or emergency rules may activate.

## 20. Wrapper legibility

Higher-risk wrappers require stronger legibility.

CWA already establishes that information burden should scale with material contextual difference and risk.

Therefore risk wrappers should proportionately expose:
- material hazards;
- rules;
- unusual permissions;
- safety controls;
- withdrawal;
- emergency intervention;
- responsibility;
- externalities.

This does not require exhaustive enumeration of every imaginable hazard.

## 21. Relationship to safe spaces

Safe spaces and risky wrappers are not opposites.

A safe space can contain deliberately risky subcontexts.

A sports facility may be generally safe while containing a boxing match.
A laboratory may be institutionally protected while containing high-risk experiments.
A digital system may be protected while containing a destructive sandbox.

The wrapper allows local variation without exporting it.

> **Protected Outer Context Can Contain Legitimately Risky Inner Contexts**

## 22. General abstraction

The deeper CWA pattern is:

**Default Normative Environment
-> Legitimate Context Activation
-> Legible Material Variation
-> Participant/Role Resolution
-> Bounded Permission/Restriction/Authority/Risk
-> Responsibility
-> State Monitoring
-> Exit/Termination
-> Reversion**

Authored risk fits directly inside this grammar.

## 24. Result

The V1.2 boxing work already contained the architecture required by Authored Risk Acceptance.

The important development is not to create a new risk-boundary mechanism, but to explicitly recognise that **authored risk is one of the material state variables carried by a contextual wrapper**.

This also confirms that wrappers are substantially more abstract than dangerous physical environments.

They can bind:
- an activity;
- a role;
- a relationship;
- an information state;
- a decision process;
- a time period;
- a computational mode;
- a participant state;
- or any hybrid context where materially different rules, permissions, risks, responsibilities or authority apply.

The boxing ring was therefore an intuitive physical illustration of a much more general architecture.
