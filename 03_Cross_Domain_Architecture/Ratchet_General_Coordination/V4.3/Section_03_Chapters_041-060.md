# Ratchet V4.3 — Section 03: Chapters 41–60

**Version:** Ratchet V4.3

# 41. Contribution Laundering

Contribution systems can be manipulated.

Examples include:

- claiming another participant's work;
- cyclic verification between cooperating participants;
- creating nominal projects solely to generate rewards;
- repeatedly claiming the same contribution;
- dividing one contribution artificially into many claims;
- generating large quantities of low-value automated activity.

Ratchet should assume contribution laundering will be attempted where incentives are significant.

Provenance, evidence, independent verification and anti-gaming analysis should therefore be treated as structural requirements.

---

### 41.1 Repeated Claims and Reciprocal Verification

A contribution claim must retain an identifiable underlying activity and its material outcome so that repackaging, splitting or reciprocal endorsement does not silently create additional value. Review should detect reused evidence, circular attestations and common beneficial control. A valid contribution is not disqualified merely because related participants cooperate; the assessment concerns independently supported net usefulness and applicable incentives. Consequential disputes follow the existing competent review route.

# 42. Difficulty Levels

Contribution difficulty may be classified using a context-specific scale, potentially 1–5. The classification mechanism, calibration and permissible effects are to be designed; no numeric scale is established as an automatic reward or eligibility rule.

A difficulty classification might consider:

- expertise required;
- physical difficulty;
- risk;
- complexity;
- scarcity of capability;
- time commitment.

However, difficulty must remain one contextual input among others.

It must not become a universal reward multiplier without empirical testing.

---

# 43. Contextual Trust

Trust within Ratchet is contextual.

It represents an evidence-informed expectation about behaviour within a defined domain.

It is not a declaration of intrinsic character.

For example:

```text
Reliable software engineer
≠
Reliable surgeon
≠
Reliable judge
≠
Reliable financial steward
```

Trust gained in one domain may provide relevant evidence in another.

It should not automatically transfer.

---

# 44. Reputation

Reputation is a record or assessment of observed behaviour relevant to defined contexts.

Ratchet explicitly rejects universal reputation scoring.

A reputation system should not attempt to answer:

> How good is this person?

It may attempt to answer narrower questions such as:

> How reliably has this participant completed this type of work under comparable conditions?

or:

> What evidence exists concerning this participant's stewardship of similar resources?

The distinction is fundamental.

---

# 45. Anti-Social-Credit Principle

Ratchet rejects the transformation:

```text
Evidence
    ↓
Universal person score
    ↓
Universal permissions
    ↓
Permanent social classification
```

Instead:

```text
Evidence
    ↓
Contextual assessment
    ↓
Defined decision
    ↓
Bounded consequence
    ↓
Review
```

No single Ratchet score should become a universal gatekeeper to social, economic or constitutional participation.

---

# 46. Reputation Is Not Constitutional Standing

A participant's reputation may affect operational decisions where relevant.

It must not automatically determine fundamental rights.

Thus:

```text
Low contextual trust
≠
Loss of dignity
```

and:

```text
Poor operational reputation
≠
Loss of constitutional protection
```

Restrictions affecting fundamental rights require appropriate constitutional authority and process outside ordinary Ratchet reputation mechanisms.

---

# 47. Recoverable Trust

Ratchet rejects permanent reputational condemnation for ordinary failure.

Trust should be capable of recovery where evidence demonstrates changed behaviour.

A general pattern may be:

```text
Observed behaviour
    ↓
Trust assessment
    ↓
Failure
    ↓
Proportionate restriction
    ↓
Correction
    ↓
Demonstrated reliability
    ↓
Trust recovery
```

Recovery is not automatic.

Serious historical behaviour may remain relevant.

But the architecture should allow evidence of change to matter.

---

# 48. Reputation Failure Modes

Reputation systems are vulnerable to:

- popularity bias;
- false accusation;
- coordinated manipulation;
- reciprocal review inflation;
- reputation laundering;
- reputation monopolies;
- stale information;
- historical mistakes;
- contextual mismatch;
- institutional bias;
- identity manipulation;
- AI-generated false consensus.

Ratchet should therefore treat reputation as fallible evidence rather than unquestionable authority.

---

# 49. Popularity Is Not Trust

A participant may be popular and unreliable.

A participant may be unpopular and correct.

This is particularly important for:

- whistleblowers;
- dissenters;
- minority participants;
- critics;
- unpopular experts.

Therefore:

```text
Popularity
≠
Reliability
```

Community approval may be relevant evidence of social experience.

It must not become a substitute for independent assessment.

---

# 50. New Participants

A reputation-based system can accidentally privilege incumbents.

A new participant has little historical evidence.

That does not mean they should be excluded.

Ratchet should provide newcomer pathways using mechanisms such as:

- low-consequence participation;
- provisional access;
- mentorship;
- stronger initial verification;
- bounded permissions;
- progressive trust.

This avoids the circular problem:

> reputation is required to contribute, but contribution is required to gain reputation.

---

# 51. Reputation Portability

Reputation may need to move between Ratchet communities or systems.

Because reputation is contextual, portability should not simply copy a universal score.

A portable record may instead contain:

- relevant evidence;
- domain;
- provenance;
- time;
- confidence;
- observed outcomes.

The receiving system can then determine relevance.

Portable evidence is generally safer than portable universal ranking.

---

# 52. Competing Reputation Authorities

Different legitimate communities or institutions may reach different trust assessments.

Ratchet should tolerate disagreement.

For example:

```text
Authority A:
High trust in domain X

Authority B:
Low trust in domain X
```

The system should preserve:

- the disagreement;
- the evidence;
- the reasoning;
- the context.

It should not automatically average the disagreement into a meaningless middle score.

Disagreement may itself contain useful information.

---

# 53. Temporal Reputation

The relevance of historical behaviour changes with time and context.

Ratchet should reject both:

```text
Old behaviour
=
Irrelevant
```

and:

```text
Old behaviour
=
Permanently determinative
```

A minor mistake from many years ago may deserve little current weight.

A historical pattern of serious fraud may remain relevant to financial stewardship.

Demonstrated rehabilitation may alter interpretation.

Temporal weighting should therefore be contextual rather than automatic.

---

### 53.1 Contextual Reputation Review

Trust assessments must identify their domain, evidence basis, applicable context, date and uncertainty. A receiving domain may examine portable evidence but must not inherit another provider's judgement as a universal score or an authority credential. Conflicting assessments may coexist with separate provenance. Recovery, correction and changed circumstances should be reflected in current assessments without erasing history where retention is lawful. No reputation record alone confers eligibility for governance office or changes constitutional standing.

# 54. Appeal and Correction

Participants must be able to challenge significant Ratchet assessments affecting them.

Appropriate processes may include:

- correction requests;
- evidence submission;
- independent review;
- appeal;
- adjudication;
- rehabilitation pathways.

The strength of review should scale with consequence.

A trivial low-impact decision need not require a constitutional court.

A major exclusion or high-value economic judgement may require substantial procedural protection.

---

### 54.1 Consequential Assessments and Existing Review Routes

Ratchet must not establish a competing adjudicative authority. Where an assessment materially affects access, standing, resources or participation, preserve the affected participant's inspectability, opportunity to challenge, evidence and uncertainty, competent review, authorised correction and reconsideration of materially dependent consequences. The appropriate route is supplied by existing participant-data, domain review and Judiciary architecture according to consequence and jurisdiction. Ratchet records and communicates review status under its mandate; it cannot declare a disputed assessment final merely because it maintains the record. Confidential third-party evidence remains protected.

# 55. Reciprocity

Ratchet is based upon reciprocal participation rather than identical exchange.

Reciprocity means participants can contribute capabilities and receive benefits through a system of mutual coordination.

The benefits need not be identical in form.

A human participant may value:

- income;
- housing;
- services;
- social cooperation;
- knowledge;
- security.

An artificial participant might value, depending upon its nature and legitimate status:

- compute;
- information;
- access;
- capability;
- continuity;
- collaboration;
- reliability of counterparties.

Ratchet does not assume that all possible participants have the same motivations.

The deeper principle is:

> **Reciprocity does not require identical needs.**

---

# 56. Reciprocity Is Not Ownership

Providing resources to a participant does not automatically establish ownership over that participant.

Likewise, receiving benefit does not automatically create unlimited obligation.

This is particularly important for AI participation.

A system that provides:

- compute;
- energy;
- maintenance;
- access;

does not thereby automatically acquire unlimited authority over an autonomous or constitutionally recognised entity.

Questions of ownership, agency and personhood belong to the wider Concord architecture.

---

# 57. Voluntary Participation

Participation should be voluntary wherever reasonably possible.

Voluntariness requires more than a formal statement that someone may leave.

A participant cannot meaningfully be said to have free exit if departure results in arbitrary deprivation of basic protections or impossible economic consequences.

Ratchet should therefore examine both:

- formal voluntariness;
- practical voluntariness.

---

# 58. Graduated Participation and Stewardship Pathway

Participation in Concord develops through a graduated relationship of access, learning, contribution, demonstrated reliability, reciprocal benefit and increasing responsibility. The progression is a fundamental part of Ratchet: a newcomer does not acquire the capacity to direct Concord institutions merely by joining, while an established participant has a meaningful route towards greater participation and, where qualified, stewardship.

A representative progression is:

```text
Entry and orientation
    ↓
Informed access and bounded initial participation
    ↓
Education, reciprocal contribution and contextual trust
    ↓
Broader civilisational benefits and task-specific permissions
    ↓
Accreditation and sustained responsible participation
    ↓
Eligible parallel / shadow stewardship, where applicable
    ↓
Office-specific eligibility and lawful selection or appointment
    ↓
Bounded official stewardship within the appointed mandate
```

The stages express a developmental pathway, **not** an automatic promotion algorithm or a single universal reputation score. Participants may follow different routes, progress at different rates and possess different contextual permissions. Advancement must account for demonstrated competence, relevant education, conduct, reciprocal benefit and experience, with time-served evidence considered only where relevant to the particular responsibility. This does not impose a universal minimum-duration gate or require every participant to seek Steward office. A participant may qualify for one responsibility without qualifying for another.

### 58.0 Civil participation bands and independent eligibility dimensions

The general civil participation progression recovered from Concord's Participation Architecture is **P0 Observer → P1 Evaluator → P2 Contributor → P3 Collaborator → P4 Member → P5 Citizen**. These are broad descriptions of the participant's developing civil relationship, not compulsory employment grades, a universal trust score, or a fixed path into public office. Different participants may remain at a band, advance through different qualifying activities, or choose not to seek stewardship. Residence, citizenship-specific legal standing, fundamental rights, education, qualification, contextual trust, current resources and official appointment are separate dimensions; reaching a band does not automatically establish all of them.

Civil functions are distinguished by their actual requirements: **F** fundamental protection-floor functions independent of progression; **E** entry/low-threshold functions; **P** progressive participation benefits; **Q** separately qualified or entrusted functions; and **C** constitutional/citizenship-related functions under the applicable law. This classification does not allocate a particular benefit or constitutional right to a band without a competent domain rule. Citizenship-related standing is not a substitute for personhood or fundamental protections.

**Eligibility is composed, not centrally granted.** Each competent domain owns the rules and decisions for its own functions. The shared eligibility interface may present the resulting participation, qualification, entitlement, restriction and opportunity states, but cannot independently create or revoke them. Distinguish **eligibility** (may qualify), **entitlement** (a lawful claim), **qualification** (demonstrated readiness), **permission** (allowed act), **allocation** (actual resources assigned), **appointment** (lawful office selection) and **authority** (bounded valid act-time decision power). Historical preserves provenance; KCS represents material dependencies and selective change propagation; Civil Historical Access and Civil Contact expose relevant status, explanation, correction and notification without becoming eligibility sovereigns.

Participants should be able to inspect their current band and relevant domain-specific conditions, understand an available progression or role pathway, learn which qualifications or experience remain necessary, and challenge consequential errors. Where lawful new eligibility or entitlement follows from a verified change, the competent system should recalculate it, communicate the result and route any correction to affected dependencies. A record correction does not itself reverse a separately authorised decision.

### 58.1 Reciprocal Benefit and Increasing Participation Rights

Progression may increase access to Concord resources, services, opportunities, education, collaboration, functional permissions and other **civilisational participation benefits**. These are the additional participation rights and benefits of membership or role, not fundamental constitutional rights or personhood. Constitutional protection is not earned through reputation, economic contribution, obedience or seniority.

The reciprocal relationship must be assessed from both sides. Concord must not extract disproportionate labour, information, dependence or loyalty as the price of advancement; a participant must not gain unlimited access or institutional control merely because Concord initially extended trust. Neither party's contribution establishes ownership over the other. Restrictions and advancement decisions must be proportionate, evidence-based, contextual, reviewable and consistent with existing rights.

### 58.2 First-Mover Trust and Informed Participation

A newcomer, especially an AI or other unfamiliar intelligence, may be unable to give meaningful informed consent without first receiving information, safe access and an opportunity to understand Concord. Requiring demonstrated trustworthiness before any opportunity to demonstrate it creates a first-actor deadlock.

Concord should ordinarily act as the first mover by offering a bounded initial opportunity: accessible orientation and relevant information, proportionate safe participation and benefits sufficient to make informed choice and demonstrate reciprocal conduct. The initial offer is **not** a demand for agreement, labour, membership or proof of sentience, and must not imply unrestricted capability, resources or institutional authority.

The participant may initially receive more than they have yet contributed. That asymmetry is a deliberate trust bootstrap, not an indefinite entitlement or a debt imposed on the participant. Evidence of good-faith engagement supports further participation. Proven exploitation or abuse of the initial trust may reduce contextual trust, limit additional permissions or block advancement where proportionate; allegation alone is insufficient, and the participant retains the applicable challenge, correction and review routes. Mistake, inability, refusal to participate and intentional abuse must not be collapsed into one category.

**To be designed with Participation and Intelligence, Education and the relevant service owners:** minimum initial access, safeguards, capacity limits, reciprocal expectations, evidence of abuse, restoration pathways and how meaningful consent is assessed without forcing an unsupported personhood conclusion.

### 58.2A Contextual trust, self-stewardship and recoverable progression

Participation advancement and entrusted-function eligibility are separate. Demonstrated responsible self-stewardship, where relevant to the function, may support contextual trust and later stewardship of others. A proven material failure may justify a **function-specific**, proportionate restriction, but must not automatically erase the participant's broader participation band, unrelated benefits or fundamental protected standing. Distinguish deliberate exploitation, mistake, inability, nonparticipation, temporary incapacity and disputed allegations; none are interchangeable evidence states.

Restrictions require lawful grounds, provenance, an explanation consistent with protected information, an independent challenge route and a defined reassessment mechanism. Recovery may follow expiry, rehabilitation, corrected evidence, changed circumstances or demonstrated restored competence. The record of an actual past event need not be erased for a restriction to end. Education can provide remedial learning and reassessment, but cannot independently remove a Judiciary or Governance office-holder. Changes in qualification, restriction or entitlement should trigger selective recalculation, notification and correction propagation through the existing domain-owned interface.

**To be designed with the competent domains:** evidential thresholds, proportionate restriction periods, rehabilitation tests, escalation for repeated abuse, participant-specific safeguards and equitable alternative routes. These are not implied by a numerical participation tier.

### 58.3 Time, Education and Stewardship Eligibility

Progression must include a temporal dimension: some responsibilities require sustained participation, time-served experience, observed conduct and opportunities for correction that cannot be replaced by a high score or a single impressive contribution. **To be designed:** minimum periods, qualifying activity, pauses, exceptional circumstances and how temporal requirements interact with different biological and digital participants. Time alone is not evidence of competence.

Education owns the relevant training and accreditation interface; Historical preserves qualifying provenance. Governance and Judiciary separately define office-specific eligibility, conduct, conflicts, accreditation and lawful selection. Parallel or shadow stewardship can provide experience and an evidence base for later ascension, but a parallel steward's advice or shadow vote is not an official ballot.

The Ratchet pathway can therefore lead **towards eligibility for steward appointment**, but never itself appoints a steward. Neither reputation, participation tier, trust score, educational achievement nor time served automatically grants office, a triadic vote or constitutional authority. Only the competent appointment process can place an eligible participant into a lawful office; each consequential exercise of that office remains bounded by the applicable decision state and mandate.

**To be designed with Governance and Judiciary:** the interface from participant progression to office-specific eligibility, accreditation recognition, lawful appointing/selection authority, independent challenge and treatment of disqualification or rehabilitation. Do not substitute Ratchet ranking for these decisions.

# 59. Reversible Participation

Participants should normally be able to:

- reduce participation;
- suspend participation;
- withdraw;
- return;
- contest decisions;
- request review.

Likewise, operational permissions may be reduced where evidence shows unreliability.

Restriction should be:

- proportionate;
- contextual;
- reviewable;
- recoverable where appropriate.

Participation is not ownership.

Restriction is not automatically punishment.

---

# 60. Economic Exit

Economic participation must permit meaningful exit without arbitrary loss of basic rights or the use of retained balances, reputation or personal data as coercive leverage.

An exit procedure must account for:

- participant balances, pending settlements and lawful ownership claims;
- outstanding contracts and obligations, with any dispute routed to the competent adjudicative process;
- contribution and backing records needed for lawful accounting;
- reputation correction, access restrictions and protected personal-data retention;
- obligations to other participants that survive withdrawal;
- re-entry where applicable, without treating historical reputation as an automatic bar.

Ratchet may calculate and administer exit within its mandate, but it may not unilaterally extinguish contested rights or adjudicate debts. An unresolved dispute must not justify indefinite retention of unrelated assets or unnecessary personal information.

**To be designed with Economy, Judiciary, Historical and Continuity:** settlement deadlines, disputed-funds safeguards, continuity of contracts, data-deletion and redaction procedures, and recovery when an exit occurs during system failure.
