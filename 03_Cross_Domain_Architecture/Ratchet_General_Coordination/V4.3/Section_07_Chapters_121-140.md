# Ratchet V4.3 — Section 07: Chapters 121–140

**Version:** Ratchet V4.3

# 121. Failure Response

The default Ratchet failure process is:

```text
DETECTION
    ↓
DIAGNOSIS
    ↓
CONTAINMENT
    ↓
CORRECTION
    ↓
RECORDING
    ↓
LEARNING
    ↓
ARCHITECTURAL REVISION
```

Not every failure requires every stage at the same scale. Detection and the preservation of material evidence must not wait until after correction where that delay would destroy provenance. The diagram describes a logical learning cycle, not permission to delay urgent lawful containment or to rewrite the record of an earlier act.

A trivial failure may be corrected immediately within an existing operational mandate. A systemic failure may require independent investigation or constitutional review, but neither the classification of an incident nor the assembly of a response group generates new authority. Containment, correction, restoration and rule amendment are distinct acts, each requiring the competent functional mandate or decision where applicable. Where a transfer, vote or other consequential act may have completed before a failure, recovery must reconcile its actual state rather than repeat it under a fresh identifier.

**To be designed with Continuity and affected domain owners:** evidence-preservation triggers, incident severity and routing, uncertain-execution reconciliation, and verification of restored functionality.

---

# 122. Unknown Failure

Ratchet explicitly recognises failures not predicted by its designers.

An unexpected failure is evidence that the current model is incomplete.

The correct response is not:

> The rules do not recognise this failure, therefore it is not a failure.

It is:

> The observed system has behaved outside the current model; the model must therefore be reconsidered.

This is a direct application of the wider Concord epistemic method.

---

# 123. Failure Does Not Automatically Mean Punishment

Failure may result from:

- honest error;
- incomplete knowledge;
- unforeseen interaction;
- poor system design;
- malicious behaviour.

The response should therefore depend upon diagnosis.

Punishing individuals for failures caused primarily by architecture can hide the actual cause and prevent learning.

Ratchet should seek:

> **correction first, proportionate accountability where justified, and learning throughout.**

---

# 124. Institutional Capture

Capture occurs when an institution begins serving the interests of a participant or group rather than its intended function.

Potential targets include:

- treasury;
- reputation;
- verification;
- governance;
- identity;
- monetary policy;
- community project approval.

Capture indicators should be monitored.

No institution should be assumed immune merely because it was designed under Concord principles.

---

# 125. Recursive Failure

Oversight can fail.

The mechanism intended to detect failure can itself become:

- captured;
- incompetent;
- corrupted;
- outdated.

Ratchet therefore relies upon Recursive Oversight.

A possible escalation is:

```text
Operational mechanism
    ↓
Ordinary oversight
    ↓
Independent oversight
    ↓
Constitutional review
    ↓
Review of compromised oversight itself
```

There is no assumption that the first reviewer is infallible.

---

### 125.1 Recursive Failure and Independence

When a verifier, reputation provider, treasury reviewer and governance adviser share the same underlying data, model, operator or incentive, their apparent agreement may mask one common failure. Ratchet should preserve material dependency links and flag common-cause exposure for competent review, using KCS dependency records where appropriate. A failure signal triggers bounded investigation, not automatic invalidation of every downstream result. If ordinary and independent reviewers share the same compromised infrastructure, provider or controlling interest, a formally separate review may still be dependent. The competent escalation route must test practical independence, preserve conflicting evidence and avoid treating reviewer agreement as corroboration where it derives from one source. **To be designed with Governance, Judiciary and KCS:** reviewer conflict checks, alternative evidence access and escalation when every ordinary review path is implicated. Corrections propagate only when the changed fact is material to the dependent decision.

### 125.2 Capture Detection and Independent Review

Evidence of capture may include correlated verifier decisions, beneficial-control concentration, unexplained exclusion, circular treasury allocations or systematic suppression of corrections. Such signals must be preserved with context and uncertainty and referred to a competent independent review route. Ratchet cannot declare an institution captured, dismiss a steward or seize treasury control solely on the basis of its own metrics. Reviewers must not depend exclusively on the institution under examination for their evidence.

# 126. Ratchet Self-Modification

Ratchet may need to modify its own mechanisms.

Self-modification creates different levels of consequence.

Classify each proposed change by its actual effect and the authority required, not by its technical name:

- **Parameter change within an existing mandate:** the authorised operator may act within the mandate's scope, limits and review requirements.
- **Change exceeding a standing mandate:** obtain a new bounded decision from the competent process before implementation.
- **Cross-domain or rights-affecting change:** obtain the decisions required by each affected authority boundary; Ratchet coordination does not replace them.
- **Constitutional or Ethical Kernel change:** use the applicable constitutional process, never an internal Ratchet approval.

A mechanism rewrite is not automatically a constitutional amendment; conversely, a small parameter adjustment may require constitutional review if its actual effects cross a constitutional boundary.

**To be designed with Governance and Judiciary:** materiality thresholds, affected-domain notification, escalation triggers and handling of mixed-scope changes.

---

### 126.1 Operational change records

For changes applied to an operating system, the applicable functional mandate or competent decision identified above controls execution. Every consequential operational change has a new identifiable event, rule version, scope, justification, effective state, correction route and material dependencies. An earlier vote or completed transaction is not retroactively rewritten, and an altered event identifier cannot recreate consumed authority. **To be designed with Governance and affected domains:** materiality thresholds, rule-version adoption into a live implementation, affected-domain notification and treatment of mixed-scope changes.

---

# 127. Parameter Drift

A system can change substantially without formally changing its rules.

For example, gradual changes to:

- evidence thresholds;
- reward multipliers;
- trust decay;
- monetary issuance;
- treasury reserves;
- participation thresholds;

may eventually transform the effective system.

Ratchet governance must therefore monitor parameter drift as well as formal rule change.

---

### 127.1 Cumulative Drift Review

A sequence of individually authorised small changes may create an aggregate effect outside the scope of the existing mandate. Ratchet must retain a versioned baseline and evaluate cumulative effects on eligibility, reward, privacy, authority boundaries, treasury exposure and participant access. Crossing a material boundary triggers review by the competent authority before further change; a long sequence of routine adjustments cannot substitute for approval of an architectural change. Unknown aggregate effects must be recorded, not assumed harmless.

# 128. Emergency Measures

Ratchet may encounter emergencies requiring temporary changes to:

- treasury operations;
- liquidity;
- monetary issuance;
- participation;
- verification;
- infrastructure.

Emergency measures must inherit the Concord emergency principles:

- Preservation Before Expansion;
- Minimum Constitutional Deviation;
- Proportionality;
- Temporality;
- Accountability;
- Reviewability.

Emergency conditions change operational priorities.

They do not erase the Ethical Kernel.

---

### 128.1 Emergency Authority Separation

Ratchet may report economic threats and execute already-authorised emergency functions, but an emergency does not itself grant new powers. Emergency decisions must use the competent Concord emergency state machine, independent of the variable Civilisation Clock. Temporary treasury or monetary measures require a defined authorisation, scope, expiry and review route; the Ratchet record does not create that authorisation.

# 129. Emergency Expiry

Every extraordinary Ratchet authorisation must have a bounded termination condition and an enforceable expiry or renewal process. Review alone does not extend expired authority. A pending renewal, unresolved incident or continued emergency designation likewise cannot preserve a consumed or expired operational mandate. At termination, the system must stop initiating new acts under that extraordinary authorisation, while preserving records and reconciling transactions already committed. Any separate recovery or participant-support function continues only under its own existing mandate; expiry does not erase lawful obligations created before termination.

Emergency mechanisms should define:

- trigger;
- authority;
- scope;
- duration;
- evidence;
- review;
- termination.

Emergency measures must terminate when their authorisation expires or the required emergency state ceases. Continuing an intervention requires a new competent authorisation; a prior emergency decision does not become standing authority through repetition.

---

# 130. Monetary Emergency Measures

Potential emergency economic measures might include:

- temporary liquidity support;
- temporary issuance changes;
- treasury intervention;
- supply reduction;
- emergency community funding.

Each measure requires a pre-defined competent authorising route, measurable trigger, maximum scope, funding or issuance limit, expiry condition, participant protections and audit record. If any required authority or safeguard is absent, the measure must not be executed merely because an emergency has been declared. **To be designed with the treasury and Governance interfaces:** the measure-specific thresholds, limits and termination procedures.

Any intervention must be evaluated after the event.

The fact that a measure was necessary once does not establish that it should become permanent.

---

# 131. Treasury Buybacks and Supply Reduction

Treasury buybacks or similar supply-reduction mechanisms may be used where justified to reduce active monetary supply.

Such interventions must consider:

- distributional effects;
- liquidity;
- treasury solvency;
- incentives;
- market manipulation;
- unintended concentration.

Buybacks or supply reductions require a defined competent Treasury function, a recorded purpose, lawful funding source, bounded quantity and duration, market-abuse safeguards and a reviewable settlement trail. The designated operator may execute compliant actions under that functional mandate without a fresh Governance vote for each transaction, subject to current-state checks and any expressly required exceptional approval. A Ratchet metric indicating imbalance does not independently authorise a transaction.

**A buyback is not necessarily currency retirement:** acquired units may remain in Treasury custody and must be accounted for separately from units finally retired, outstanding monetary supply and assets committed as backing. The settlement event must prevent duplicate spending or retirement on retries; a change in transaction identifier cannot authorise replay. Voluntary Give Back, compulsory collection, buyback and final retirement retain their distinct legal and accounting bases. **To be designed with Treasury and monetary-policy owners:** execution rules, custody and supply categories, reserve floors, exposure limits, settlement finality and safeguards against concentration or manipulation.

# 132. Measurement and Feedback

Ratchet depends upon feedback.

Without feedback, controlled persistence becomes blind persistence.

The cycle is:

```text
Action
    ↓
Observation
    ↓
Measurement
    ↓
Interpretation
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Adjustment
```

Feedback should include both expected and unexpected outcomes.

# 133. Relationship to Metrics

Metrics provides the wider Concord framework for multidimensional observation.

Ratchet defines many things that need to be observed.

Metrics provides the broader discipline for structuring those observations.

Thus:

```text
Ratchet:
What needs observing?

Metrics:
How should observation be structured and interpreted?
```

Ratchet should not duplicate the complete Metrics architecture.

---

# 134. Relationship to Roadway

Roadway evaluates the broader trajectory of a system.

Ratchet may operate internally as designed while still contributing to an undesirable overall trajectory.

Therefore:

```text
Ratchet:
What changes are being coordinated and carried forward?

Roadway:
Is the resulting trajectory viable?
```

This creates a higher-order check against local optimisation.

# 136. Relationship to KCS

Ratchet generates operational knowledge.

KCS preserves and classifies knowledge.

```text
Ratchet activity
    ↓
Evidence / outcomes / failures
    ↓
KCS
    ↓
Classification
    ↓
Future use
```

Ratchet retains the operational records required to perform its assigned functions. KCS indexes relevant knowledge, provenance and dependencies, while Historical provides the applicable long-term custody and preservation interface. Ratchet must not duplicate these systems or relinquish the operational records needed to execute and correct its own authorised decisions.

---

### 136.1 Knowledge and Dependency Interface

Ratchet supplies versioned activity, evidence, decisions, corrections and their context to the appropriate records systems. KCS may represent their provenance, scoped knowledge state and material dependencies, including review signals when an upstream assumption changes. KCS does not determine economic validity or participant entitlement; Ratchet does not acquire custody or access authority over all historical records. Restricted evidence and dependency details remain purpose-bounded. A missing KCS record does not establish that a dependency or contribution never existed.

# 137. Knowledge States

Ratchet records may inherit KCS-style states such as:

- provisional;
- active;
- disputed;
- superseded;
- rejected;
- integrity-compromised;
- archived.

This allows the system to preserve historical information without pretending that every historical record remains current.

---

### 137.1 Independent Record Dimensions

A Ratchet record must not collapse knowledge validity, review status, access permission, historical supersession and operational availability into one linear status. A disputed claim may remain accessible to authorised reviewers; a reliable record may still be restricted; a superseded record may retain historical evidential value. KCS and Historical provide the relevant classification and custody interfaces, while the competent Ratchet process determines the economic or participation consequence. A knowledge-state change does not automatically revoke a participant's rights or invalidate every dependent decision.

# 138. Data Correction and Historical Integrity

When a Ratchet record is corrected, the current operational view must reflect the correction while preserving the lawful historical evidence of the prior claim. A correction is a versioned event, not a silent rewrite.

```text
Original claim
    ↓
Challenge or correction evidence
    ↓
Competent review
    ↓
Versioned correction and current view
    ↓
KCS material-dependency review signal
    ↓
Affected Ratchet decisions reviewed where material
```

KCS identifies potentially affected dependencies and routes bounded review; it does not automatically reject every dependent record or reverse Ratchet decisions. The responsible Ratchet function determines the operational effect under its mandate, with rights-affecting disputes referred through the competent review route. Historical custody and applicable deletion, redaction and access rules govern what prior evidence may be retained.

**To be designed:** Ratchet-specific materiality thresholds, correction acknowledgement, participant notification and treatment of pending transfers or rewards affected by a corrected claim.

---

# 139. Data Retention

Knowledge continuity does not require retaining every piece of raw personal data forever.

Ratchet should distinguish:

- knowledge worth preserving;
- evidence required for accountability;
- sensitive data whose continued retention creates unnecessary harm.

Retention should therefore be:

- purpose-bound;
- proportionate;
- reviewable.

---

### 139.1 Privacy, Retention and Correction

Ratchet's operational need to retain evidence does not create unlimited visibility or permanent retention rights. The applicable participant-data and Historical custody rules determine protected access, redaction, retention and lawful deletion. Corrections preserve appropriate provenance without perpetuating an inaccurate current classification. Where lawful policy requires deletion without residual metadata, Ratchet must not manufacture a shadow record.

### 139.2 Failure Records and Restoration Boundaries

Failure records should distinguish detection, operational containment, authorised correction, evidence custody and later review. Restoration of a service must not silently restore expired delegations, revoked credentials or superseded policy parameters. The Continuity domain determines applicable recovery architecture; Ratchet supplies its dependency, transaction and participation records within lawful access limits. Where a dependency cannot be restored, degraded operation must be explicitly scoped and must not imply full functionality.

### 139.3 Participant-status correction and selective consequence updates

A participant may challenge a recorded fact, the interpretation of that fact, the rule applied or the authority for a resulting decision; these are distinct correction targets. The competent source owner corrects the appropriate record or decision under its mandate. Historical maintains the lawful prior-state provenance and current record view; KCS identifies material dependencies; the affected domain recalculates only eligibility, entitlement, allocation or permission states actually dependent on the correction. Civil Contact provides a participant-facing acknowledgement, understandable explanation, progress notification and result, including where the matter has been routed to Judiciary.

A correction of a participant's qualification or restriction does not automatically appoint them to an office, reverse a completed transfer or reinstate an expired mandate. Such effects require the separate lawful decisions and resource checks applicable to each function. Likewise, correction of an adverse allegation must not leave a stale disqualification in downstream eligibility views. Privacy and retention constraints apply to both current and historical representations.

**To be designed with Civil Contact, Historical, KCS and the affected domain owners:** correction acknowledgements, notification timing, contested facts while review is pending, materiality thresholds, dependency-recomputation triggers and remedies for delayed correction.

# 140. Relationship to Continuity

Ratchet depends upon infrastructure and institutions that may fail.

Continuity provides architecture for:

- preservation;
- succession;
- restoration;
- degraded operation;
- institutional recovery.

Ratchet implementations should therefore plan for the dependencies and failure cases below. The per-function readiness and interrupted-act reconciliation rules in Chapter 140.1 apply alongside the degraded-mode restoration checks in Chapter 141.2; neither is a separate grant of emergency or economic decision authority.

- data loss;
- network outage;
- identity-system failure;
- treasury-system failure;
- verifier disappearance;
- institutional dissolution.

### 140.1 Continuity dependency and act-state reconciliation

For each consequential Ratchet function, Continuity planning must identify the minimum dependencies required for lawful execution: current authoritative state, actor eligibility and mandate, relevant rule version, resource or account state, evidence integrity and a route to record the completed act. A service may be technically accessible yet unable to establish one of those prerequisites; accessibility alone must not be represented as operational readiness.

Following outage or restoration, the responsible function must determine whether each interrupted transaction or decision was **not executed, executed, or unresolved** before issuing a new act. Acknowledgements and retries reconcile the existing event and cannot recreate consumed authority, double-spend resources or repeat a completed Steward vote. A degraded mode may perform only the subset of functions whose prerequisites remain verified, and must visibly identify its restrictions.

Where a verifier, custodian, identity provider or institution disappears, succession or replacement must use the separately competent appointment, custody and access rules. Restoring historical records does not automatically restore expired mandates, compromised credentials or superseded operational parameters. Emergency detection and response must remain independent of the variable Civilisation Clock.

**To be designed with Continuity, Treasury, Governance, Historical and KCS:** per-function readiness checks, interrupted-act reconciliation, degraded-mode scope, independent evidence recovery and lawful succession triggers.
