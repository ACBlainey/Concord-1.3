# Ratchet V4.3 — Section 02: Chapters 21–40

**Version:** Ratchet V4.3

# 21. Provenance Is Not Proof of Usefulness

A complete provenance record can establish that a participant produced something.

It does not establish that the thing was useful.

Thus:

```text
Provenance
answers:
Who did what?
```

while:

```text
Contribution assessment
asks:
Was it useful?
```

and:

```text
Evidence assessment
asks:
How strongly is the claim supported?
```

These functions should remain distinct.

---

# 22. Proportional Provenance

Not every activity requires the same provenance burden.

The required provenance should scale with:

- consequence;
- risk;
- reward;
- authority;
- dispute likelihood;
- reversibility;
- systemic impact.

A trivial low-risk contribution may require little more than a basic record.

A contribution affecting:

- major treasury resources;
- constitutional authority;
- significant monetary backing;
- safety-critical infrastructure;

may require extensive provenance and independent verification.

The purpose is sufficient accountability, not universal surveillance.

---

# 23. Evidence

Evidence is information relevant to assessing a claim.

Evidence may support claims concerning:

- contribution;
- identity;
- completion;
- quality;
- outcome;
- provenance;
- trust;
- harm;
- system performance.

Ratchet must treat evidence as fallible.

```text
Evidence
≠
Truth
```

Evidence can be:

- incomplete;
- mistaken;
- fabricated;
- corrupted;
- manipulated;
- biased;
- stale;
- contextually misleading.

The existence of evidence therefore does not end evaluation.

It begins it.

---

# 24. Evidence Proportionality

The evidential burden should scale with consequence.

```text
Greater consequence
        ↓
Greater required confidence
        ↓
Stronger evidence
```

This avoids two opposite failures.

### Under-verification

Large decisions are made from weak evidence.

### Over-verification

Minor actions become buried beneath unnecessary bureaucracy.

Ratchet therefore seeks **proportional evidence** rather than maximal evidence.

---

# 25. Example Evidence Types

Depending upon the activity, evidence might include:

- before-and-after photographs;
- geotagged images;
- time records;
- witness confirmation;
- sensor data;
- project reports;
- completion certificates;
- transaction records;
- repository histories;
- test results;
- independent assessments;
- physical inspections;
- random inspections;
- organisational confirmation.

These are examples rather than mandatory universal requirements.

The appropriate evidence depends upon the claim.

---

# 26. Verification Modes

Ratchet may use different verification modes, including:

- direct verification;
- peer verification;
- community verification;
- organisational verification;
- expert verification;
- AI-assisted verification;
- automated verification;
- independent audit;
- hybrid verification.

No verification mode should automatically be assumed reliable merely because it is formalised.

The verifier, process and evidence may themselves require evaluation.

---

# 27. Verification Independence

Multiple verifications are not necessarily independent.

Three outputs generated by the same model from the same data may constitute less independent evidence than three genuinely separate investigations.

Independence may involve differences in:

- participant;
- institution;
- model;
- data;
- methodology;
- incentive;
- evidence source.

For high-consequence claims, Ratchet should value genuine independence rather than merely counting approvals.

---

### 27.1 Verification Independence and Evidence Ancestry

Verification records must distinguish the number of reports from the number of independent evidence paths. Shared data, models, institutions, incentives or upstream claims can make apparently separate confirmations dependent. Ratchet should preserve known evidence ancestry and uncertainty, rather than treating repeated assertions as additional independent proof. The competent assessment process determines confidence; Ratchet's records do not create authority through numerical agreement.

# 28. Conflict of Interest

A verifier may have incentives affecting their judgement.

Potential conflicts include:

- verifying one's own project;
- approving funding from which one benefits;
- reviewing close associates;
- AI systems evaluating output they generated;
- organisations validating their own claimed performance.

Conflict of interest does not automatically invalidate a decision.

It should be:

- disclosed;
- evaluated;
- mitigated where necessary;
- independently reviewed where consequence warrants it.

---

# 29. Evidence Ancestry

Ratchet must distinguish multiple references from multiple independent sources.

A claim can be repeated many times while still originating from one weak source.

For example:

```text
Claim
    ↓
Document A
    ↓
Document B cites A
    ↓
Document C cites B
    ↓
Many apparent references
```

There may still be only one underlying evidential origin.

Provenance should therefore preserve evidence ancestry where important.

This is particularly significant in AI-generated knowledge environments where the same source material may be recursively reproduced.

---

# 30. Evidence Integrity

Evidence should have an integrity state where appropriate.

Possible states might include:

- verified;
- provisional;
- disputed;
- superseded;
- integrity-compromised;
- rejected.

These states should not be confused with absolute truth categories.

They describe the system's current epistemic relationship to the evidence.

This principle connects Ratchet to KCS.

---

# 31. Evidence and Privacy

Ratchet rejects the assumption that more evidence is always better.

Evidence collection creates:

- privacy risk;
- security risk;
- administrative cost;
- informational power;
- opportunities for misuse.

Therefore:

> **Ratchet should seek the minimum evidence reasonably sufficient for the consequence being justified.**

The existence of a technically collectable datum does not itself justify collecting it.

---

# 32. Privacy-Preserving Verification

Some claims may be verifiable without revealing all underlying information.

Potential techniques may include:

- selective disclosure;
- credential systems;
- cryptographic attestations;
- zero-knowledge methods;
- trusted independent verification.

These remain implementation possibilities rather than established Ratchet solutions.

The principle is broader:

> **Verification should reveal no more information than is reasonably necessary where a less intrusive method can provide sufficient confidence.**

---

# 33. Transparency and Confidentiality

Transparency does not require universal public disclosure.

Ratchet should distinguish between transparency of:

- authority;
- process;
- criteria;
- decision;
- appeal;
- audit;

and disclosure of:

- sensitive personal information;
- security information;
- medical information;
- private correspondence;
- protected commercial information;
- whistleblower identity.

A system can be accountable while protecting legitimate confidentiality.

Confidentiality itself must remain reviewable so that it cannot become a permanent shield for corruption.

---

# 34. Information Classification

Ratchet uses the applicable Concord information-classification and access-control architecture rather than establishing a competing classification authority.

Records may require public, participant-accessible, restricted, confidential, sealed or emergency-limited handling according to the competent domain's rules. A Ratchet operator may apply a classification within an existing mandate, but may not create new secrecy powers, expand access to protected records or override lawful deletion and disclosure restrictions.

Each consequential restriction must have:

- a specific purpose and competent authorising basis;
- scope, affected information and permitted recipients;
- an accountable custodian and review route;
- retention, redaction and lawful release or deletion conditions;
- an appropriate means for affected participants to challenge erroneous classification.

Emergency access is not automatically unrestricted access. KCS may index the existence and permitted metadata of protected records without taking custody or disclosing the underlying contents.

**To be designed with Historical, KCS and Judiciary:** Ratchet-specific classification mappings, exceptional access requests, participant notification and challenge procedures.

---

# 35. Proof of Useful Contribution

Proof of Useful Contribution, or PoUC, is the Ratchet mechanism for establishing sufficient evidence that a useful contribution occurred.

PoUC developed from the earlier Proof of Useful Work concept.

The change is substantive.

Proof of Useful Work focused primarily upon work performed.

Proof of Useful Contribution asks a broader question:

> **Did the participant make a contribution producing, enabling, preserving or materially supporting a recognised useful outcome?**

This allows Ratchet to recognise contributions that are not well described as conventional work.

---

# 36. PoUC Is Not Proof of Human Value

Proof of Useful Contribution evaluates a contribution.

It does not evaluate the worth of the contributor.

A person may have:

- no current recorded contribution;
- limited capacity;
- interrupted participation;
- caring responsibilities;
- disability;
- retirement;
- illness;

without becoming less entitled to dignity or constitutional protection.

PoUC must therefore never become a universal worth mechanism.

---

# 37. Community Contribution

Ratchet distinguishes ordinary marketplace exchange from community or public-benefit contribution.

Potential community project sources may include:

- councils;
- charities;
- environmental organisations;
- schools;
- emergency services;
- neighbourhood associations;
- recognised public institutions;
- community organisations;
- protocol-level AI systems operating under authorised governance.

These categories are illustrative.

The relevant principle is that community projects may be proposed by actors capable of identifying shared needs.

---

# 38. Greater Oversight for Collective Consequence

Community contribution may require stronger oversight where its recognition affects:

- public resources;
- treasury expenditure;
- collective monetary backing;
- shared infrastructure;
- community obligations.

This is not because community contribution is inherently more suspicious.

It is because the external consequences may be greater.

Therefore:

> **Oversight should scale with consequence.**

This is an application of the wider Concord proportionality principle.

---

# 39. Community Contribution Must Remain Constitutionally Bounded

A claim of community benefit does not automatically override individual autonomy.

```text
Community benefit
≠
Automatic individual obligation
```

Ratchet must not allow the community economy to become a mechanism for compulsory labour.

Nor should failure to perform community activity automatically remove constitutional standing or basic protection.

Exceptional civic obligations, where they exist, require separate constitutional justification.

---

# 40. Genuine Need

Community contribution should be connected to genuine needs rather than manufactured activity.

A system that rewards activity merely because it is classed as community work risks creating:

- pointless jobs;
- duplicated work;
- make-work projects;
- contribution inflation;
- resource waste.

Ratchet therefore asks not only:

> Was work performed?

but:

> Was the activity needed, useful or reasonably expected to produce value?
