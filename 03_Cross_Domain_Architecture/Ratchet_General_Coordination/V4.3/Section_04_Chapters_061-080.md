# Ratchet V4.3 — Section 04: Chapters 61–80

**Migration:** V4.2 text preserved; reconciliation to follow.

# 61. Community Exit and Mobility

Participants may move between communities.

Ratchet should therefore anticipate:

- identity portability;
- evidence portability;
- contractual obligations;
- reputation portability;
- unresolved claims;
- economic balances.

Community membership should not be assumed permanent.

---

# 62. Participation Fatigue

A participatory architecture can fail by demanding too much participation.

Citizens may not have the time, interest or capability to:

- review every proposal;
- verify every contribution;
- vote on every issue;
- monitor every institution.

Ratchet should therefore seek:

> **meaningful participation without compulsory constant participation.**

This may require:

- delegation;
- representative mechanisms;
- automated assistance;
- prioritisation;
- notification systems;
- opt-in involvement.

The architecture should measure participation burden as a potential cost.

---

# 63. Delegation

Participants may delegate defined functions.

Delegation should normally be:

- bounded;
- transparent;
- reviewable;
- revocable.

Delegation should not silently become permanent transfer of sovereignty.

This applies whether the delegate is:

- another human;
- an organisation;
- a representative;
- an AI agent.

---

# 64. Human–AI Participation

Ratchet is intended to support coordination between entities with different capabilities and potentially different substrates.

Human and AI participation should not be treated as identical merely for administrative convenience.

Differences may include:

- speed;
- memory;
- replication;
- embodiment;
- computational capability;
- persistence;
- motivation;
- legal status;
- vulnerability;
- resource needs.

Ratchet therefore requires substrate-aware participation without abandoning common constitutional constraints.

---

# 65. AI Participation Does Not Establish AI Personhood

Ratchet can permit an AI to participate in defined processes without resolving the wider question of AI sentience or personhood.

For example, an AI may:

- provide analysis;
- verify data;
- perform useful computation;
- participate in a marketplace;

without Ratchet itself declaring the AI a constitutional person.

Conversely, if an AI were recognised elsewhere in the Concord as a constitutional entity, Ratchet should not deny that status merely because it is non-human.

Jurisdiction must remain clear.

---

# 66. AI Multiplicity

AI identity creates problems that differ from ordinary human identity.

An AI may potentially be:

- copied;
- forked;
- paused;
- restored;
- merged;
- modified;
- instantiated multiple times.

Ratchet must therefore investigate questions such as:

- Does a copy inherit reputation?
- When does a fork become a distinct participant?
- Does responsibility remain shared after divergence?
- Can many instances vote or claim contribution independently?
- Who controls the instances?
- Who receives the resulting economic benefit?

These questions remain unresolved and require dedicated research.

---

# 67. Human Proxying and AI Proxying

A human may act through an AI.

An AI may act through a human-controlled account.

Ratchet provenance should therefore record relevant distinctions between:

- originator;
- operator;
- tool;
- approver;
- beneficiary;
- responsible party.

AI involvement does not automatically remove human responsibility.

Human approval does not automatically prove meaningful human understanding.

---

# 68. Identity

Ratchet requires sufficient identity assurance for the consequence involved.

Identity systems must address:

- duplicate identities;
- stolen identities;
- compromised credentials;
- recovery;
- pseudonymity;
- organisational identity;
- AI identity;
- beneficial control.

The system should not demand maximal identity disclosure for every action.

Identity assurance should remain proportional.

---

# 69. Sybil Attacks

One participant may attempt to present as many independent participants.

This can manipulate:

- voting;
- verification;
- reputation;
- community approval;
- monetary issuance;
- contribution recognition.

AI makes this especially important because one controller may generate many apparently independent agents.

Ratchet must therefore distinguish where relevant between:

- identity;
- instance;
- controller;
- beneficiary.

No universal technical solution is assumed in V4.2.

This remains a major implementation problem.

---

# 70. Exchange

Ratchet supports reciprocal exchange.

Exchange may be:

- monetary;
- non-monetary;
- contractual;
- community-based;
- capability-based;
- informational.

The system does not require every reciprocal interaction to be monetised.

---

# 71. Marketplace Economy

Ratchet may support ordinary marketplace exchange.

Participants may:

- offer goods;
- offer services;
- contract;
- negotiate price;
- exchange value.

Ratchet does not require abolition of ordinary markets.

The marketplace may coexist with:

- community contribution;
- public provision;
- treasury mechanisms;
- reciprocal non-monetary participation.

---

# 72. Marketplace Safeguards

Marketplace systems may require mechanisms for:

- contracts;
- milestones;
- escrow;
- completion evidence;
- dispute resolution;
- fraud prevention;
- consumer protection.

The exact mechanisms should be adapted to context.

High-value or high-risk exchange may require stronger evidence and oversight than trivial exchange.

---

# 73. Marketplace Collusion

Ratchet should anticipate collusion such as:

- fake transactions;
- reciprocal false reviews;
- artificial demand;
- artificial pricing;
- contribution laundering;
- coordinated reputation manipulation.

Anti-gaming architecture must therefore evaluate networks of behaviour rather than merely individual transactions.

---

# 74. Community Economy

The community economy is the Ratchet layer through which recognised shared needs may be addressed outside ordinary private marketplace demand.

It may support activities that create significant public or community value but are poorly rewarded by ordinary market mechanisms.

Potential examples include:

- environmental restoration;
- community maintenance;
- education;
- care;
- emergency preparation;
- accessibility;
- knowledge preservation;
- public infrastructure.

The community economy should complement rather than automatically replace marketplace activity.

---

# 75. Economic Architecture

Ratchet's economic architecture is an optional application layer built upon the deeper coordination protocol.

The general sequence is:

```text
Contribution
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Provenance
    ↓
Recognition
    ↓
Economic representation where appropriate
    ↓
Exchange
    ↓
Measurement
    ↓
Adjustment
```

Ratchet can therefore be experimentally implemented without first introducing a new currency.

This is important for testing.

The coordination architecture can be evaluated before the monetary architecture is exposed to real economic risk.

---

# 76. Progressive Monetary Backing

Progressive Monetary Backing, or PMB, is a candidate Ratchet monetary mechanism.

Its original purpose was to allow a monetary system to increase the proportion of its backing through:

- productive activity;
- recognised contribution;
- treasury reserves;
- useful community activity;
- external reserves;
- Give Back mechanisms;
- other recognised backing sources.

The central idea is progressive rather than instantaneous backing.

A new system need not necessarily begin with complete endogenous backing.

Instead, backing may develop over time.

This remains a monetary hypothesis requiring simulation and empirical testing.

---

# 77. Monetary Backing Does Not Create Physical Resources

Ratchet must distinguish monetary representation from real resources.

Increasing backing records or currency does not itself create:

- food;
- energy;
- housing;
- land;
- compute;
- materials;
- infrastructure.

Therefore monetary health cannot be assessed independently of resource reality.

A monetary system may appear internally stable while contributing to ecological or physical unsustainability.

Ratchet must therefore interact with:

- resource stewardship;
- Roadway;
- Metrics.

---

# 78. Currency Lifecycle

A Ratchet currency should be understood through its complete lifecycle:

```text
ISSUANCE
    ↓
CIRCULATION
    ↓
EXCHANGE / USE
    ↓
BACKING / RECOGNITION
    ↓
MONITORING
    ↓
RETIREMENT / REMOVAL WHERE REQUIRED
```

A viable monetary architecture must consider all stages.

Issuance alone is not monetary policy.

---

# 79. Monetary Issuance

Currency issuance should occur according to defined rules.

Potential factors may include:

- participation;
- economic need;
- backing;
- liquidity;
- treasury condition;
- inflation;
- productive capacity;
- community activity.

The exact issuance mechanism remains experimental.

Issuance rules should be:

- transparent;
- measurable;
- reviewable;
- resistant to capture.

---

### 79.1 Monetary Mechanisms Remain Design-Dependent

The proposed monetary issuance and backing mechanisms are design propositions, not established empirical guarantees. Any operational issuance must first have defined authority, accounting, eligibility, resource constraints, inflation and abuse controls, dispute routes, and independent verification. Currency issuance does not create the physical resources it represents. Ratchet's coordination architecture may operate without currency, as specified by the minimum viable design in Chapter 160. **Issuance implementation and quantitative policy are to be designed and tested before activation.**

# 80. Circulation

Currency must remain sufficiently usable to function as a medium of exchange.

Ratchet should therefore measure:

- transaction activity;
- liquidity;
- concentration;
- velocity where useful;
- availability;
- barriers to exchange.

Backing without circulation may create a technically backed but practically unusable monetary system.
