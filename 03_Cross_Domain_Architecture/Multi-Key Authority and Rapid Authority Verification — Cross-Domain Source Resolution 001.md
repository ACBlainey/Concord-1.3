## 1. Question

Does the Multi-Key Authority pattern discovered during Established Life-Support Reallocation represent:

1. a Health-specific construction; or
2. a general Concord authority primitive already implicit across independently developed domains?

The candidate rule is:

> **An action may proceed only when every independently required authority for the actual action is present, current, within scope and not blocked by an upstream prohibition.**

The companion operational proposition is:

> **Precompute authority; verify current facts.**

This source resolution tests those propositions against existing Concord architecture.

# PART I — EXISTING SOURCE PATTERN

## 3. Policing Lifecycle

The Policing Lifecycle explicitly establishes:

> **Authority at one policing stage does not automatically authorise the next stage.**

and:

**Prior Authority != Continuing Authority**

**Prior Authority != Greater Authority**

**Stage Transition != Authority Inheritance**

It further distinguishes:

**Report != Authority To Arrest**

**Emergency Entry != General Search Authority**

**Authority To Restrain != Automatic Authority To Continue Detention**

**Investigation != Search Authority**

**Investigation != Apprehension Authority**

**Apprehension Authority != Unlimited Search Authority**

and states:

> **A transition to a more intrusive or differently purposed policing state requires the authority requirements of that new state to be satisfied.**

This is already a multi-key authority grammar.

An investigation can be legitimate while a proposed search remains unauthorised.

An apprehension can be legitimate while detention later ceases to be authorised.

---

## 4. Policing authority contraction

Policing also establishes:

**Maximum Past Authority != Current Authority**

Authority can contract as facts change.

This directly supports the temporal-freshness component of Multi-Key Authority.

The relevant question is not:

> Was authority ever valid?

but:

> Is the authority required for this act valid now?

---

## 5. Custody

Custody establishes:

**Contextual Authority != General Authority**

**Authority To Detain != Authority To Impose Every Custodial Restriction**

**Custody Category != Maximum Permissible Restriction**

**Past Restriction != Continuing Restriction Authority**

**Physical Release != Complete Authority Sunset**

and:

**Dependent Restriction Without Independent Authority Must Sunset With Parent Custody Authority**

This shows that even after a high-consequence authority is validly activated, additional consequences require their own legitimate basis.

Detention is not a master key.

---

## 6. Custody authority graph

Custody also introduces an authority-graph concept.

When custody ends:

- dependent restrictions should terminate;
- credentials/access should restore where no independent basis survives;
- downstream systems should be informed.

This supports a general principle:

> **Authority validity is relational and propagational, not merely a binary flag attached to an actor.**

A Multi-Key architecture should therefore represent both:

- independent keys;
- dependencies between keys.

---

## 7. Judicial evidential access

Judicial Evidential Depth establishes:

**Evidence Exists != General Access Authority**

**Evidence Preserved != Evidence Disclosed To Everyone**

**Technical Availability != Legitimate Evidential Access**

**Preservation Envelope != Inspection Envelope**

**Authority To Preserve != Authority To Inspect Without Bound**

**Authority To Inspect Relevant Segment != Authority To Explore Entire Archive**

and:

**Later Case Stage != Unlimited Retrospective Search**

This is another clear multi-key structure.

Existence, preservation, inspection, disclosure and use are distinct operations with distinct authority envelopes.

---

## 8. Evidence-stage transition

Judicial evidential architecture allows access to deepen as adjudicative function changes, but only through:

> a new purpose-bounded authority envelope.

Therefore:

**New Legitimate Function Can Create New Authority**
but
**New Legitimate Function != Automatic Unlimited Authority**

This supports explicit key activation rather than authority inheritance.

---

## 9. Compelled Participant Evidence

Compelled Participant Evidence states:

**Information Access + Purpose A != Use For Purpose B**

**Authority(A) != Authority(B)**

and:

**Authority To Seek Evidence != Authority To Compel Participant To Defeat Their Own Protection**

**Search Authority Must Exist Independently Of Participant Refusal**

It also identifies participant action itself as an additional authority question.

Therefore:

> obtaining an evidential objective can require several distinct authority keys depending on the route used.

This maps directly to the life-support finding that authority over R does not automatically authorise every act needed to recover R.

---

## 10. Cognitive Protected Space

Cognitive Protected Space independently distinguishes:

- cognitive state;
- cognitive product;
- private record;
- technical accessibility;
- legitimate access;
- preservation;
- inspection;
- evidential use.

Its key architecture includes:

**Technical Inspectability != Legitimate Cognitive Access**

**Device Search Authority != Cognitive Search Authority**

**Container Access != Uniform Authority Over Every Contained Context**

**Authority To Preserve != Authority To Inspect**

**Evidence-Space Incompleteness != Authority To Search A Mind**

This strongly supports non-inheritance across nested authority contexts.

---

## 11. Constitutional Emergency

Constitutional Emergency establishes that even severe emergency does not create unlimited authority.

Emergency conditions can justify stronger action while authority remains:

- bounded;
- function-derived;
- reviewable;
- dynamically reassessed;
- subject to de-escalation.

Thus:

**Emergency Severity != General Sovereignty**

and, structurally:

**Emergency Key != Master Key**

A valid emergency condition may activate particular authority without satisfying every authority required for every consequential act.

---

## 12. Infrastructure lifecycle

Infrastructure lifecycle architecture distinguishes:

- operational authority;
- decommissioning;
- dependency;
- transition;
- successor service;
- stewardship;
- continuity consequences.

Its adversarial testing rejects simple shutdown where critical dependencies remain and requires transition analysis.

This supports:

**Authority To Operate != Authority To Decommission By Any Method**

and:

**Authority To Decommission != Authority To Ignore Dependent Protected Functions**

The exact source terminology varies, but the transition grammar matches Withdrawal Authority.

---

## 13. Scarcity Allocation

Scarcity Allocation establishes:

**Authority To Allocate Resource X != Authority Over Participant Generally**

**Scarcity != General Emergency Sovereignty**

**Resource Control != Neutral Allocation Authority**

and requires bounded allocation authority by:

- resource;
- horizon;
- affected participants;
- purpose;
- evidence;
- review;
- sunset.

This directly supports resource-authority / participant-authority separation.

---

## 14. Concord Bounded Participant Runtime

CBPR provides a technical execution analogue.

It distinguishes:

**Schema Validity != Execution Authority**

**Runtime Access != Authority**

**Execution Capability != Permission For External Action**

**Credential Availability != Permission To Use Credential**

**Network Reachability != Permission To Contact Destination**

**Function Continuity != Authority Continuity**

**Authority At Proposal != Authority At Commit**

and requires authority to be independently grounded, scoped, current and evidenced.

This is extremely strong independent support for Rapid Authority Verification.

The same grammar appears in a runtime context developed for a different problem.

---

## 15. Contextual Wrapper Architecture

CWA establishes:

**Boundary != Access != Rules != Authority**

**Contextual Permission != General Permission**

**Parent Access != Child Access**

**Nested Context != Inherited Rule Set**

**Functional Permission != Contextual Authority**

**Permission To Use != Authority To Control**

and:

> **Contextual authority should not escape its context without independent justification.**

It also states that conflict detection does not create authority to resolve the conflict:

**Information Architecture != Decision Authority**

**Reporting Authority != Decision Competence**

This supplies the contextual topology in which Multi-Key Authority operates.

# PART II — SOURCE-RESOLUTION FINDING

## 16. Independent convergence

The pattern is not confined to Health.

At least the following independently developed architectures express the same structural principle:

### Policing
One stage does not authorise the next.

### Custody
One coercive authority does not authorise every restriction.

### Evidence
Preservation does not authorise inspection/disclosure/use.

### Compelled evidence
Evidence-seeking does not authorise compelled participant action.

### Cognition
Device/container authority does not authorise cognitive access.

### Emergency
Emergency does not create general sovereignty.

### Infrastructure
Operation/decommissioning authority does not erase transition/dependency constraints.

### Scarcity
Resource allocation authority does not create authority over participants.

### Runtime
Capability/permission/credentials/proposal do not create commit authority.

### Contextual wrappers
Access/nesting/context do not automatically inherit authority.

This convergence is too broad to treat Multi-Key Authority as merely a Health-specific artefact.

---

## 17. General principle

The source-derived general principle is:

> **Where an intended consequential act depends on multiple independently protected functions, contexts, transitions or interventions, authority for one component does not automatically satisfy authority required for the others. Each independently required authority must be legitimately grounded, within scope and current at the consequential point of use.**

Short form:

> **Authority Does Not Automatically Compose.**

This appears to be the common cross-domain primitive.

---

## 18. Non-composition

Candidate invariant:

**Authority(A) + Authority(B) != Authority(C) Unless C Is Independently Within The Legitimate Composition**

Possessing several legitimate authorities does not automatically generate a new authority.

Example:

- authority to detain;
- authority to access a room;

does not automatically create authority to inspect every cognitive system inside the room.

Therefore Multi-Key Authority is not merely:

> collect enough permissions.

It requires that the intended act itself be covered.

---

## 19. Key definition

A **Key** is not necessarily:

- a person;
- signature;
- credential;
- cryptographic token.

A Key is:

> **a presently valid independently necessary authority condition for a specified consequential act or component of that act.**

It may be satisfied by:

- law;
- consent;
- delegated authority;
- emergency authority;
- property/resource authority;
- judicial order;
- role authority;
- contractual authority;
- protective authority;
- another legitimate source.

The source of legitimacy remains external to the Multi-Key architecture.

---

## 20. Multi-Key Authority does not manufacture legitimacy

This is critical.

> **Multi-Key Authority verifies completeness of required authority; it does not decide who ought to possess authority.**

Therefore:

**Authority Verification != Authority Creation**

**Key Presence != Ethical Legitimacy If The Claimed Key Source Is Invalid**

This aligns directly with CBPR.

---

## 21. Authority topology

A consequential action can be represented as an authority topology:

`Action`
→ required function/context/intervention nodes
→ required authority keys
→ dependencies
→ prohibitions
→ temporal conditions
→ execution boundary.

Some keys may be:

- independent;
- dependent;
- inherited only by explicit rule;
- mutually exclusive;
- conditional;
- time-limited;
- emergency-activated;
- revocable.

This is more accurate than a flat list.

---

## 22. Parent and child authority

CWA and Custody show that authority may sometimes deliberately propagate.

Therefore:

**No Automatic Inheritance != No Possible Inheritance**

A parent authority may legitimately include a child authority where the governing architecture explicitly defines that scope.

Example:

a search order may expressly authorise specified evidence preservation.

But inheritance must be explicit or structurally established.

**Authority Inheritance Must Be Established, Not Assumed**

---

## 23. Minimum necessary authority

The architecture inherits Concord's existing sequence:

**Legitimate Objective**
→ **Required Function**
→ **Need For Authority**
→ **Minimum Necessary Authority**
→ **Bounded Contextual Authority**

Multi-Key Authority adds:

→ **Authority Composition Check**
→ **Current Predicate Verification**
→ **Upstream Prohibition Check**
→ **Consequential Commit**

# PART III — RAPID AUTHORITY VERIFICATION

## 24. Why runtime verification is required

Many Concord systems are dynamic.

Facts can change between:

- request;
- assessment;
- authorisation;
- execution.

Examples:

- suspect no longer presents threat;
- detention basis expires;
- evidence relevance changes;
- consent is revoked;
- scarcity ends;
- substitute resource arrives;
- emergency sunsets;
- credential is revoked;
- participant migrates;
- clinical state changes.

Therefore:

**Authority At Proposal != Authority At Commit**

is not merely a CBPR rule.

It is a general civil-authority rule.

---

## 25. Precompute stable authority

Where possible, precompute relatively stable elements:

- governing rule;
- authority source;
- role competence;
- contextual scope;
- resource class;
- intervention class;
- required keys;
- prohibited combinations;
- review requirements;
- expiry/sunset logic;
- escalation path.

This avoids reconstructing constitutional architecture during every urgent event.

---

## 26. Verify dynamic predicates

At runtime verify the changing facts that activate or sustain those authorities:

- identity;
- context;
- location where relevant;
- current need;
- current risk;
- current evidence;
- current scarcity;
- current consent;
- current role;
- current resource state;
- current emergency state;
- current dependency;
- current alternatives;
- current time validity.

Thus:

> **Precompute authority structure; verify dynamic predicates at runtime.**

This is a more precise general form than “precompute authority; verify current facts.”

---

## 27. Consequential commit boundary

A high-value pattern from CBPR generalises:

> authority should be revalidated at the point immediately preceding consequential commitment where material facts or authority may have changed.

Candidate:

**Authority At Assessment != Authority At Consequential Commit**

This does not require infinite checking.

The required freshness depends on:

- consequence;
- volatility;
- reversibility;
- urgency;
- known change rate.

---

## 28. Temporal freshness

Candidate principle:

> **An authority key is usable only while the facts, source, scope and temporal conditions on which it depends remain sufficiently current for the contemplated action.**

Therefore:

**Stale Authority != Current Authority**

---

## 29. Revocation propagation

Where authority is revoked or sunsets, dependent authority should be capable of terminating.

But:

**Parent Revocation != Automatic Child Revocation Where Child Has Independent Authority**

This mirrors Custody and CWA.

The topology must distinguish:

- authority derived only from parent;
- authority independently grounded.

---

## 30. Unknown state

A particularly important runtime condition:

**Unknown Authority State != Permission**

This is already consistent with Civil Security/CIBB/CBPR.

However, emergency architecture may define a legitimate fallback authority for specified unknown-state conditions.

Therefore:

**Fallback Authority Must Exist Before It Can Be Used As Fallback**

---

# PART IV — AUTHORITY COMPLETENESS

## 31. Completeness question

Before consequential action:

> **Have all independently necessary authority conditions for the actual proposed act been identified and satisfied?**

This resembles ESCP.

A system can be internally correct within an incomplete authority representation.

Example:

- valid search authority identified;
- cognitive-access requirement omitted from model;
- system concludes search is authorised.

The represented keys are all valid, but the authority space is incomplete.

---

## 32. Authority-Space Completeness Problem

Candidate extension:

> **Authority-Space Completeness Problem (ASCP): an action may appear fully authorised because every represented authority requirement is satisfied while one or more materially necessary authority dimensions are absent from the representation.**

This is structurally analogous to ESCP.

Therefore:

**All Represented Keys Valid != All Required Keys Represented**

This is a major result.

---

## 33. ASCP question

Before execution ask:

> **What authority would have to be required, missing or unrepresented for the conclusion “this action is authorised” to be wrong?**

This is the authority analogue of ESCP's evaluation-space challenge.

It should be used proportionately, not as infinite regress.

---

## 34. Route-sensitive authority

The authority required can depend on **how** an objective is achieved.

Examples:

- obtain evidence voluntarily;
- search external archive;
- compel participant to unlock;
- inspect cognition.

Same objective, different authority topology.

Likewise:

- end compute service by migration;
- suspend safely;
- delete state.

Therefore:

**Same Objective != Same Authority Path**

and:

**Authority Completeness Is Route-Sensitive**

---

## 35. Consequence-sensitive authority

The same physical action may have different authority implications depending on consequence.

Disconnecting a cable may:

- end entertainment;
- interrupt business;
- suspend a recoverable AI;
- destroy a participant.

Therefore:

**Same Physical Act != Same Authority Requirement**

Context/function/consequence matter.

# PART V — GENERAL FORMAL MODEL

## 36. Action model

Candidate representation:

`A = <Actor, Objective, Action, Route, Target, Context, Consequence, Time>`

Required authority set:

`K_required(A) = {K1, K2, ... Kn}`

Each key:

`Ki = <Source, Function, Scope, Context, Predicate, Holder, Start, Expiry, RevocationState, Provenance>`

Action may proceed only if:

1. every materially required key is represented;
2. every represented required key is valid;
3. predicates are sufficiently current;
4. scope covers the actual route/action;
5. required independence is satisfied;
6. no upstream prohibition blocks the act;
7. any required review/provenance condition is satisfied.

---

## 37. Candidate execution predicate

`AUTHORISED(A,t)`

iff:

`COMPLETE(K_required(A))`

AND for every `Ki`:

`VALID(Ki,t)`
AND
`IN_SCOPE(Ki,A)`

AND:

`INDEPENDENCE_REQUIREMENTS_SATISFIED(A)`

AND:

`NO_UPSTREAM_PROHIBITION(A,t)`

AND:

`COMMIT_REQUIREMENTS_SATISFIED(A,t)`

This is a conceptual formalisation, not executable law.

---

## 38. Permission-before-authority

CWA requires checking whether authority is needed at all.

Therefore the general model should begin:

**Can objective be achieved through ordinary access / consent / bounded permission?**

If yes:

use minimum necessary permission.

If no:

identify required authority.

Thus Multi-Key Authority must not create bureaucratic authority where permission suffices.

**Multi-Key Verification != Authority-Maximisation**

# PART VII — BOUNDARIES

## 47. Not every action requires multiple keys

Some actions have one simple legitimate basis.

Multi-Key Authority should not require artificial decomposition.

> **Use as many independently necessary keys as the actual action requires, no more and no fewer.**

Therefore:

**Multi-Key Architecture != Mandatory Multi-Key Count**

---

## 48. One source may legitimately satisfy several keys

A single lawful authority instrument can explicitly cover multiple functions.

Example:

a narrowly specified emergency order may authorise entry, evacuation and temporary control.

The issue is not number of documents.

It is whether each necessary authority function is legitimately covered.

**One Instrument Can Satisfy Multiple Keys Where Scope Explicitly Covers Them**

---

## 49. Multiple actors not always required

Multi-Key does not mean multi-person.

Independent institutional review may be required for some high-consequence acts.

Other actions may have several authority dimensions legitimately vested in one role.

Therefore:

**Multiple Authority Functions != Automatic Multiple Human Decision-Makers**

Functional independence should be required where justified, not ritualised.

---

## 50. Authority versus competence

A valid authority holder may lack technical competence to determine predicates.

A competent expert may lack authority to act.

Therefore:

**Competence != Authority**

and:

**Authority != Competence**

Some consequential actions require both.

---

## 51. Authority versus capability

CBPR and CWA reinforce:

**Capability != Permission != Authority**

A system can:

- be capable but unauthorised;
- be permitted but lack capability;
- hold authority but require another actor/tool to execute.

These must remain distinct.

# PART VIII — SOURCE-RESOLUTION CONCLUSION

## 52. Finding

**SOURCE-RESOLVED AS A GENUINE CROSS-DOMAIN CONCORD ARCHITECTURE**

The Multi-Key Authority pattern predates the life-support work in multiple independent domains.

The life-support problem exposed and named a common structure rather than inventing it.

The strongest source-derived formulation is:

> **Authority Does Not Automatically Compose. A consequential act may proceed only where every independently necessary authority for the actual route, context and consequence of that act is legitimately grounded, sufficiently current, within scope and not blocked by an upstream prohibition.**

Operationally:

> **Precompute stable authority structure; verify dynamic predicates at runtime; revalidate at consequential commit where material change is possible.**

---

## 53. New extension: Authority-Space Completeness Problem

The source comparison exposes a new problem not previously explicit as a named architecture:

> **All Represented Keys Valid != All Required Keys Represented**

This should be developed as the **Authority-Space Completeness Problem (ASCP)**.

It is a direct authority analogue of ESCP.

ASCP may be one of the most important safeguards in Multi-Key Authority because a perfectly validated incomplete authority model can still authorise an illegitimate act.
