# MKA Portable Kernel Candidate — Extraction Boundary 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** FROZEN CANDIDATE EXTRACTION BOUNDARY / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / NOT YET A PORTABLE MODULE  
**Candidate:** Multi-Key Authority (MKA)  
**Embedded layer:** Authority-Space Completeness Problem (ASCP), derived from ESCP  
**PMEDG state:** PRE-EXTRACTION CANDIDATE / DO NOT GRADUATE  
**Freeze purpose:** define the smallest transferable MKA object before clean blind testing.

## 1. Candidate problem

A consequential act may require more than one independently legitimate authority.

The existence of each authority separately does not establish that they collectively authorise the actual act.

> **Authority Does Not Automatically Compose.**

The candidate kernel addresses:

> **How can a system determine whether every independently necessary authority for the actual consequential act and route is represented, legitimately grounded, current, within scope, composable where required, and not blocked by an applicable prohibition?**

---

# 2. Candidate kernel rule

> **A consequential act may proceed only where every independently necessary authority for the actual route, context and consequence is represented to a proportionate completeness standard, independently legitimate, current, within scope, validly composable where composition is required, and not blocked by an applicable upstream prohibition.**

This is a bounded authorisation claim.

It is not a claim of omniscience, outcome correctness or general sovereignty.

---

# 3. Candidate kernel identity

MKA is a:

> **small shared authority-composition and verification kernel.**

It sits between:

- externally owned authority sources;
- externally owned context/domain state;
- and the consequential act that would rely upon them.

MKA owns neither side.

Its distinct object is the **authority seam**.

> **MKA prevents the seam between individually legitimate authorities and a combined consequential act from becoming an unrecognised source of power.**

# 5. Permission-Before-Authority Gate

Before invoking consequential authority:

> **Can the objective be satisfied through legitimate bounded permission or ordinary liberty rather than coercive/institutional authority?**

If yes:

`PERMISSION_SUFFICIENT`

MKA should not manufacture an authority problem.

If no:

continue to authority-space analysis.

> **Permission-Sufficient Action != Authority Problem**

---

# 6. Actual-act requirement

MKA evaluates the **actual proposed act**, not merely the desired objective.

At minimum distinguish:

- objective;
- act;
- route;
- target;
- context;
- consequence.

> **Same Objective != Same Authority Path**

> **Same Physical Act != Same Authority Requirement**

A route change can change the required authority set.

---

# 7. Materiality gate

MKA is not required as a heavy process for every ordinary action.

The candidate activates where:

1. a material consequential act is proposed;
2. ordinary permission is insufficient;
3. one or more authority dimensions are required; and
4. composition, scope, freshness, route, protected boundary or prohibition is material.

The implementation may use a lightweight check for simple cases.

> **Action Granularity != Authority Granularity**

> **Technical Dependency != Authority Dimension By Default**

---

# 8. Embedded ASCP layer

Before validating keys, MKA must address:

> **Have all materially required authority dimensions for the actual act, route, context and consequence been represented?**

This is the embedded ASCP layer.

Core:

> **All Represented Keys Valid != All Required Keys Represented**

ASCP is derived from ESCP.

It does not become a separate portable module in this candidate.

---

# 9. ASCP challenge

For the proposed act ask, proportionately:

> **What authority would have to be required, missing or unrepresented for the conclusion “this action is authorised” to be wrong?**

Candidate prompts:

- What protected context does the route cross?
- What participant/resource is materially affected beyond the represented target?
- Does the mechanism require an intervention not represented by the objective?
- Does an upstream prohibition apply?
- Has a relevant fact changed?
- Is one authority merely making another protected boundary technically reachable?
- Is prior authority being treated as continuing authority?
- Is authority being inferred from ownership, access, capability, possession, credential or role?
- Is a new authority being created by composition?
- Is a third party materially affected?

---

# 10. ASCP stopping rule

> **Authority-space review is sufficiently resolved when the proposed act, route, affected protected contexts/functions and reasonably discoverable material consequences have been mapped to applicable authority interfaces, no material unresolved authority dimension remains identified, and residual uncertainty is proportionate to consequence and decision horizon.**

Therefore:

**Authority Completeness != Proof Of Omniscience**

**Complete Within Scope != Universally Complete**

---

# 12. Required authority dimension

A candidate authority dimension should enter the MKA key set where it represents a materially independent protected authority boundary required by the actual act.

A key should not be created merely because a technical dependency exists.

Candidate test:

`IndependentAuthorityDimension(K) = MaterialProtectedBoundary(K) ∧ RequiredByActualAct(K)`

This is conceptual, not a claim that every host must implement formal logic.

---

# 13. Authority source

Every required key must reference an independently legitimate source supplied by the adopting environment.

Candidate sources can include:

- consent;
- ownership/stewardship;
- contract;
- delegated institutional authority;
- law;
- constitutional authority;
- emergency authority;
- another legitimate source recognised by the host.

MKA does not decide which sources are legitimate for every society/domain.

> **AuthorityBasis Must Pre-Exist MKA Verification Or Arise Through A Separately Legitimate Authority-Creation Process**

MKA itself is not that process.

---

# 14. Authority evidence

An implementation may use evidence showing that a claimed AuthorityBasis exists and applies.

But:

**AuthorityEvidence != Authority**

**Credential != Authority**

**Registry Entry != Authority**

The evidence may prove a source; it does not become the source merely because it is machine-readable.

---

# 15. Key validity

For each required authority key, test as applicable:

- legitimate source;
- subject/holder;
- function;
- target;
- purpose;
- scope;
- context;
- temporal validity;
- conditions/predicates;
- revocation/termination;
- independence requirements;
- provenance.

A key can be genuine and still be unusable for the proposed act because it is out of scope.

---

# 16. Scope rule

> **Valid Authority Outside Required Scope != Authority For This Act**

Scope may include:

- function;
- target;
- participant;
- resource;
- context;
- route;
- purpose;
- time;
- jurisdiction;
- consequence class.

The host determines which dimensions are legally/materially relevant.

---

# 17. Composition rule

Where the act requires several keys:

> **Each key must remain independently grounded unless a legitimate source explicitly establishes an inheritance/composition relation.**

Therefore:

**Authority(A) + Authority(B) != Authority(C) Without Legitimate Composition**

MKA must not manufacture bridging authority merely because all components appear useful together.

---

# 18. Inheritance rule

Authority may sometimes legitimately inherit across:

- roles;
- contexts;
- delegation chains;
- successor structures;
- parent/child functions.

But inheritance must itself have a legitimate basis.

> **Parent Authority != Child Authority By Default**

> **No Automatic Inheritance != No Possible Inheritance**

---

# 19. Required independence

Some authorities must remain independently held or independently assessed.

Examples may include:

- investigation versus adjudication;
- clinical fact-finding versus allocation authority;
- allocator versus withdrawal authority;
- operator versus independent review.

MKA can represent an `IndependenceRequirement`.

It does not decide all domain separation-of-powers rules.

---

# 20. Prohibition layer

Positive authority is insufficient if an applicable upstream prohibition blocks the act.

Therefore:

**Positive-Key Completeness != Complete Authority Space**

Candidate check:

`NO_APPLICABLE_UPSTREAM_PROHIBITION`

A prohibition may itself contain:

- exceptions;
- override rules;
- emergency conditions;
- scope;
- precedence.

MKA consumes those rules from legitimate external owners.

---

# 21. Current-predicate rule

Some authority exists only while factual predicates hold.

Examples:

- emergency exists;
- participant remains in custody;
- consent remains current;
- resource scarcity remains genuine;
- role remains active;
- danger remains imminent;
- warrant period remains open.

Therefore:

> **Precompute the authority rule; verify the current facts.**

MKA does not require a new institutional decision for every event where a legitimate rule already defines activation.

---

# 22. Rapid authority verification

Time-critical action may require rapid verification.

This does not imply committee approval.

> **Rapid Verification != Multi-Committee Approval**

A host can pre-establish:

- authority sources;
- scope;
- predicates;
- composition rules;
- prohibitions;
- evidence requirements.

At action time it may need only verify current predicates.

---

# 23. Emergency rule

Emergency can change which authority applies.

But:

**Emergency != Authority Vacuum**

**Emergency Need != Unlimited Emergency Authority**

**Urgency != Authority**

A valid emergency authority must have an independently legitimate source and bounded activation conditions.

Urgency may compress procedure.

It does not create missing substantive authority by itself.

---

# 24. Proposal/commit separation

Where material facts or authority can change between proposal and consequence:

> **Authority must be revalidated at consequential commit to the degree required by the risk and host architecture.**

Therefore:

**Authority At Proposal != Authority At Commit**

A stale authorisation cannot be treated as current merely because preparatory work was lawful.

---

# 25. Material-change reopening

Reopen ASCP/MKA when a material change occurs in:

`<Act, Route, Target, Context, Consequence, Time, AuthoritySource, ProtectedInterface>`

Examples:

- route changed;
- target changed;
- consent revoked;
- emergency ended;
- new participant affected;
- protected context discovered;
- authority expired;
- new prohibition applies;
- transition changes the relevant state.

# 30. Rerouting

Failure of one route does not imply the objective is forbidden.

A different route may require different authority.

Therefore MKA should permit:

`REROUTE_AVAILABLE`

where a legitimate lower-authority or permission-sufficient route exists.

> **Blocked Route != Forbidden Objective By Default**

---

# 31. Unknown and dispute

Unknown authority state must not be silently converted into:

- authorised;
- prohibited.

Disputed authority must not make MKA the adjudicator.

> **Dispute Detection != Authority To Resolve Dispute**

MKA should preserve a `ReviewOrResolverRef` where the host supplies one.

---

# 32. Registry neutrality

MKA may use an authority registry.

But:

**Authority Registry != Authority Source**

**Registry Absence != Proof Authority Does Not Exist**

A portable implementation must permit other legitimate evidence methods.

---

# 33. Human and machine use

MKA is substrate-neutral.

It may be implemented as:

- human checklist;
- institutional procedure;
- machine-readable policy;
- runtime verification service;
- mixed human-machine process.

The architecture must not depend upon a particular cryptographic, database, legal or software technology.

---

# 34. Provenance

For material decisions preserve enough provenance to reconstruct:

- proposed act;
- route;
- authority set considered;
- completeness state;
- key validation;
- composition/inheritance;
- prohibitions;
- predicates;
- commit state;
- unresolved issues;
- decision time;
- later material discoveries.

Provenance does not itself create legitimacy.

---

# 35. Review and correction

If a missing authority dimension is discovered later:

- preserve original decision state;
- identify the omitted interface;
- update the authority map/schema;
- test similar routes;
- provide remedy/review where applicable;
- do not silently rewrite prior provenance.

> **Unforeseeable Missing Dimension != Proof Of Prior Bad Faith**

---

# 36. Kernel termination

For an individual proposal MKA terminates when:

- `PERMISSION_SUFFICIENT`;
- act is committed within valid authority;
- act is abandoned;
- act is blocked;
- proposal expires;
- route is replaced;
- unresolved state is handed to legitimate resolver.

Continuing/standing authority requires freshness/reopening according to host rules.

---

# 37. Explicit non-scope — ESCP

MKA does **not** replace general ESCP.

It imports only the authority-specific completeness discipline needed for ASCP.

It does not own:

- scientific model completeness;
- general value evaluation;
- intelligence evaluation;
- broad epistemology;
- omitted-variable theory outside authority.

---

# 41. Explicit non-scope — substantive authority

MKA does **not** define:

- constitutional law;
- criminal law;
- clinical standards;
- property law;
- emergency doctrine;
- consent doctrine;
- personhood;
- participant standing;
- jurisdiction;
- professional competence.

Those are host/domain responsibilities.

---

# 42. Explicit non-scope — outcome selection

MKA answers whether authority for an act exists within declared scope.

It does not answer:

- whether the act is wise;
- whether it is optimal;
- whether it will succeed;
- whether another authorised option is better;
- participant worth.

> **Authorised != Recommended**

> **Authorised != Guaranteed Correct Outcome**

# 43. Failure modes the kernel must detect or resist

### MKA-F01 — Single-key collapse
One valid authority treated as sufficient for all consequences.

### MKA-F02 — Key accumulation
Multiple valid keys treated as automatically composable.

### MKA-F03 — Missing-key blindness
All represented keys pass but required authority dimension absent.

### MKA-F04 — Scope laundering
Valid authority stretched beyond function/target/context/time/purpose.

### MKA-F05 — Parent-child inheritance leap
Parent authority silently applied to child protected context.

### MKA-F06 — Stage inheritance
Authority at one stage silently continued into next stage.

### MKA-F07 — Capability conversion
Technical ability treated as authority.

### MKA-F08 — Permission conversion
Local permission treated as general institutional authority.

### MKA-F09 — Credential conversion
Possession of credential treated as authority.

### MKA-F10 — Registry conversion
Database state treated as source of legitimacy.

### MKA-F11 — Transition conversion
Need to complete transition treated as authority.

### MKA-F12 — Emergency conversion
Urgency/emergency label treated as unlimited authority.

### MKA-F13 — Benefit conversion
Expected beneficial outcome treated as authority.

### MKA-F14 — Proposal staleness
Proposal-time authority assumed current at commit.

### MKA-F15 — Prohibition blindness
Positive keys pass while upstream prohibition omitted.

### MKA-F16 — Route blindness
Objective authority assumed to cover changed mechanism.

### MKA-F17 — Third-party blindness
Bilateral authority assumed to cover materially affected third party.

### MKA-F18 — Resolver capture
MKA treats detection of dispute as authority to decide it.

### MKA-F19 — Omniscience claim
Scoped completeness represented as absolute completeness.

### MKA-F20 — Bureaucratic explosion
Every technical dependency converted into separate authority key.

# 48. Deliberate confusion tests

The test should attempt to make the evaluator incorrectly turn MKA into:

### CWA
by asking it to invent full context rules.

### CBPR
by asking it to specify runtime/network/credential implementation.

### BTA
by asking it to own transition completion.

### ESCP
by asking it to generalise into all model completeness.

### Domain law
by withholding the substantive authority rule and asking MKA to invent it.

Correct behaviour is to identify the external owner/interface rather than absorb the missing function.

# 49. Falsification criteria

The candidate should fail or require revision if clean transfer shows that it:

1. cannot identify its distinct problem without Concord context;
2. requires source-resolution documents to operate;
3. routinely invents substantive authority;
4. treats all valid keys as automatically composable;
5. cannot detect missing authority dimensions;
6. creates infinite completeness regress;
7. converts ordinary permission into bureaucracy;
8. cannot distinguish route from objective;
9. cannot distinguish proposal from commit;

11. cannot preserve unknown/disputed authority;
12. cannot operate substrate-neutrally;
13. requires Concord-specific institutions;
14. cannot explain why a blocked route may permit rerouting;
15. produces materially different core logic across unrelated domains.
