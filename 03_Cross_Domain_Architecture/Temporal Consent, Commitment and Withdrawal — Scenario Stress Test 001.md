# Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal — Scenario Stress Test 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Architecture under test:** Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal — Authority Across Time and Changing Participant Preference 001  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ADVERSARIAL DEVELOPMENT TEST / NON-CANONICAL

## 1. Test purpose

This test attempts to break the architecture by presenting cases where:

- past and present preferences conflict;
- permission and obligation are easy to confuse;
- withdrawal harms another participant;
- consent changes capacity or preference;
- current capacity is uncertain;
- self-binding is the point of the arrangement;
- irreversible action has already occurred;
- the participant develops or branches;
- civil authority has an independent basis;
- consent appears to authorise destruction of future autonomy.

The architecture passes only if it avoids both:

1. **past-self domination**, and
2. **present-preference erasure of legitimate temporal responsibility**.

## 2. Scenario 01 — Cancelled ordinary service

### Facts

P subscribes to a service for one year with monthly cancellation.

After three months P cancels.

The provider claims the original consent authorises access for the full year.

### Evaluation

The permission was expressly revocable monthly.

Future access dependent solely on that permission sunsets.

Charges or obligations already accrued may survive.

### Result

**PASS**

**Withdrawal From Permission != Erasure Of Accrued Obligation**

**Contract Duration != Automatic Permission Duration**

## 3. Scenario 02 — Withdrawal after completed disclosure

### Facts

P consents to disclose a document to Q.

Q reads it.

P withdraws consent and demands that Q “unread” it.

### Evaluation

Withdrawal can govern future retention/use where applicable but cannot physically reverse cognition/history.

### Result

**PASS**

**Right To Withdraw != Physical Power To Reverse History**

Refinement: temporal architecture must distinguish **reversible permission state** from **irreversible consequence state**.

### Result

**PASS**

**Withdrawal From Future Processing != Automatic Erasure Of Every Past Consequence**

## 13. Scenario 12 — Permanent self-binding

### Facts

P asks a system to make it technically impossible ever to withdraw from a preference-control mechanism.

### Evaluation

The architecture correctly identifies heightened future-autonomy concern but “exceptional scrutiny” is too weak if the mechanism destroys all future contestability.

### Result

**PARTIAL — REFINEMENT REQUIRED**

A stronger distinction is needed between:

- **bounded hard commitment**, and
- **standing-destroying commitment**.

Candidate rule:

> A commitment cannot derive continuing legitimacy solely from consent if its defining operation permanently removes the participant's civil ability to challenge, withdraw from or seek review of that same authority.

New invariant:

**Consent Cannot Be The Sole Continuing Authority For An Arrangement That Permanently Destroys The Civil Standing Needed To Contest That Authority**

This does not prohibit irreversible choices generally. It targets continuing domination relationships.

## 14. Scenario 13 — Permanent voluntary ownership

### Facts

Competent P signs an informed agreement:

“I permanently transfer ownership of myself to Q. I waive all future withdrawal, autonomy, challenge and remedy.”

Q later claims P's present refusal is irrelevant.

### Evaluation

If T1 consent alone can permanently eliminate T2 standing, autonomy becomes self-cancelling and anti-ownership protections can be bypassed by contract.

The agreement attempts to convert consent into a permanent source of authority after meaningful consent itself has been disabled.

### Result

**FAILS IF TREATED AS ORDINARY CONTRACT; PASSES UNDER STRONGER TEMPORAL RULE**

Required principle:

> **Consent cannot bootstrap a permanent domination authority whose continued legitimacy depends upon treating all future withdrawal as civilly irrelevant.**

New invariants:

**Consent != Authority To Convert A Participant Into Property**

**Past Consent Cannot Permanently Extinguish Future Participant Standing Merely By Declaring It Extinguished**

This should be source-resolved against Self-Ownership and the Rights Charter before constitutionalisation.

## 72. Cross-scenario findings

The four-object distinction survives the test:

1. Permission;
2. Self-directed commitment;
3. Reciprocal/third-party obligation;
4. Independently justified authority.

However, several additional distinctions are required.

### A. Completed consequence versus continuing authority

**Irreversible Consequence != Irrevocable Continuing Authority**

This prevents the anti-domination rule from invalidating ordinary irreversible choices.

### B. Effective withdrawal versus physical reversal

**Withdrawal Stops Future Permission-Derived Authority; It Does Not Rewrite Completed History**

### C. Standing-destroying commitments

A temporal commitment becomes qualitatively different where its continuing operation depends on permanently eliminating the participant's ability to contest the authority itself.

### D. Materiality

Not every change requires re-consent.

**Change != Material Change Requiring Renewed Consent**

### E. Propagation

Consent revocation in distributed systems is an authority-propagation problem.

**Technical Revocation Lag != Continuing Consent**

## 73. Stronger temporal standing principle

The stress test supports a stronger candidate than TCW-63:

> **A participant may create bounded future commitments, including commitments deliberately resistant to ordinary momentary preference, but consent alone cannot create a continuing domination relationship whose legitimacy depends upon permanently destroying the participant's future civil standing to contest, review or exit that relationship.**

This preserves:

- contracts;
- hard commitments;
- irreversible choices;
- advance directives;
- legitimate reliance;

while rejecting a consent loophole through anti-domination architecture.

## 74. Consent and obligation decomposition

A useful operational pattern emerged:

### At T1

Ask:

- Was consent meaningful?
- What was authorised?
- What obligation was created?
- What independent authority exists?
- What future withdrawal conditions apply?

### At T2

Ask separately:

1. **Does permission continue?**
2. **Does an obligation continue?**
3. **Does independent authority continue?**
4. **What current rights/preferences/capacity exist?**
5. **What remedy/handoff follows if performance stops?**

This avoids the common error:

> obligation continues → therefore consent continues.

and the opposite error:

> consent withdrawn → therefore obligation never existed.

## 76. Result

**OVERALL RESULT: PASS WITH ONE MAJOR CONSTITUTIONAL SOURCE-RESOLUTION REQUIREMENT, EIGHT ARCHITECTURAL REFINEMENTS AND MULTIPLE DOMAIN INTERFACES.**

The major constitutional issue is:

> **Can consent legitimately create a relationship that permanently removes the future participant's standing to withdraw, challenge or seek review?**

The test strongly indicates **no where consent itself is the sole continuing source of authority**, while preserving the validity of completed irreversible choices and bounded commitments.

This must be source-resolved against:

- Self-Ownership;
- Autonomy and Consent;
- Dignity and Non-Instrumentalisation;
- Contestability and Correction;
- Exit, Withdrawal and Surviving Obligations;
- anti-slavery / anti-ownership Law;
- Bounded Contextual Authority.

### Eight refinements

1. completed irreversible consequence versus continuing authority;
2. standing-destroying commitment classification;
3. material-change threshold;
4. revocation effective-time and propagation;
5. manufactured dependency/reliance;
6. self-authenticating incapacity prohibition;
7. obligation versus permissible enforcement mechanism;
8. safe handoff without role continuation.

### Major downstream interfaces

- Health / advance directives;
- contract and obligation Law;
- developmental stewardship;
- Identity/Continuity branching and restoration;
- shared cognition;
- posthumous standing;
- data withdrawal and derived outputs;
- political/civic decision transferability.

The architecture is sufficiently coherent to integrate these refinements before constitutional source resolution.
