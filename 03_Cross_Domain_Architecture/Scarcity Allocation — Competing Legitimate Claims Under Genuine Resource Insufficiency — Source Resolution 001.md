# Scarcity Allocation — Competing Legitimate Claims Under Genuine Resource Insufficiency — Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CROSS-DOMAIN DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Domains:** Economy and Value Coordination / Constitutional Core / Continuity / Infrastructure / Health / Emergency / Governance

## 1. Trigger

Participant-Supporting Infrastructure testing exposed a recurrent unresolved case:

> Two or more legitimate claims exceed an indivisible or genuinely insufficient resource.

Examples include:

- one runtime slot for multiple AI participants;
- insufficient emergency power;
- one life-support device for multiple patients;
- insufficient habitat capacity;
- limited water;
- limited evacuation capacity;
- scarce specialist labour;
- limited communications bandwidth during emergency;
- insufficient compute;
- scarce replacement parts;
- a single indivisible participant-supporting asset.

The problem is not ordinary pricing or routine resource coordination.

It is:

> **What should Concord do when legitimate claims cannot all be simultaneously satisfied?**

---

## 2. Existing source resolution

### 2.1 Scarce Resource Concentration

Existing Economy architecture already establishes:

**Available Capacity != Unlimited Individual Entitlement**

**Ability To Pay != Unlimited Claim On Concord-Controlled Capacity**

**Wealth != Resource Governance Authority**

**Payment For Resource != Control Of Resource System**

**Participant Equality != Equal Resource Consumption**

It also preserves:

- resilience reserve;
- future option value;
- capacity-dependent allocation limits;
- external alternatives;
- provider diversity;
- corrigibility.

This prevents wealth concentration from silently becoming resource governance.

It does not determine priority when actual capacity is insufficient for legitimate simultaneous claims.

### 2.2 Ratchet

Ratchet provides:

- evidence;
- provenance;
- verification;
- contextual trust;
- reciprocity;
- contribution;
- measurement;
- review;
- correction.

But Ratchet explicitly rejects:

**Participation = Authority**

**Capability = Authority**

**Contribution = Authority**

and rejects treating productivity, wealth, trust or reputation as intrinsic worth.

Therefore Ratchet metrics may inform relevant factual questions but must not become a universal participant-priority score.

> **Contribution Score != Claim To Survival**

> **Reputation != Fundamental Standing Priority**

> **Economic Productivity != Allocation Worth**

### 2.3 Rights and standing

The Rights Charter establishes equal protected standing while allowing legitimate restriction and conflict resolution.

Article 2A adds:

**Inalienable Standing != Automatic Priority Over Equal Inalienable Standing**

Therefore equal standing does not itself solve scarce allocation.

### 2.4 PSI

PSI establishes:

**Equal Standing != Infinite Resources**

and:

**Dependency Severity Is An Allocation Input, Not A Complete Allocation Rule**

It also shows that ownership and dependency are relevant but incomplete variables.

### 2.5 Resource stewardship

Existing Concord reasoning treats resource stewardship as a civilisational obligation and rejects unbounded consumption.

This supports preservation of:

- reserves;
- system continuity;
- future capacity;
- non-wasteful use;
- bounded allocation.

### 2.6 BCA

Allocation authority must arise from legitimate function and remain:

- minimum necessary;
- bounded;
- contextual;
- reviewable;
- temporary where the condition is temporary.

Scarcity does not manufacture general authority.

### 2.7 Source-resolution finding

Existing architecture provides many **constraints** on scarcity allocation but does not yet provide a complete allocation method.

The missing architecture is therefore real.

## 3. Genuine scarcity

Scarcity should not be declared merely because demand exceeds current convenience.

For this architecture:

> **Genuine scarcity exists where, within the relevant decision horizon, all legitimate claims cannot be adequately satisfied using reasonably available resources, substitutes, expansion, sequencing, sharing, migration, conservation or other feasible alternatives.**

Therefore:

**Demand > Current Supply != Proven Irreducible Scarcity**

Before allocating loss, Concord should ask whether scarcity can be reduced.

---

## 4. Scarcity reduction before sacrifice allocation

Candidate sequence:

**Claims Exceed Available Resource**
→ **Verify Claims**
→ **Verify Resource State**
→ **Search Alternatives**
→ **Reduce Waste**
→ **Recover / Reallocate Idle Capacity**
→ **Use Substitutes**
→ **Sequence Use Where Possible**
→ **Share / Fractionate Where Functionally Adequate**
→ **Expand Supply Where Timely**
→ **Activate Reserves Where Legitimate**
→ **Seek External Supply**
→ **Only Then Allocate Residual Scarcity**

This gives:

> **Allocation Of Scarcity Should Follow Reasonable Attempts To Reduce Scarcity.**

and:

**Current Configuration != Fixed Resource Frontier**

---

## 5. ESCP requirement

A false binary is a major risk.

Examples:

- A gets ventilator or B gets ventilator;
- AI A runs or AI B runs;
- hospital gets power or water plant gets power.

Before accepting the binary:

> **What resource, substitute, schedule, degradation mode, external supplier, reserve, repair, migration or alternative representation would make the conflict less exclusive?**

Therefore:

**Apparent Scarcity Can Be An Incomplete Evaluation Space**

This is an ESCP-sensitive architecture.

---

## 6. No universal single-variable priority

The sources do not support any universal rule such as:

- highest bidder;
- richest;
- first claimant;
- current occupant;
- citizen first;
- human first;
- AI first;
- highest contributor;
- highest reputation;
- highest intelligence;
- greatest productivity;
- majority preference;
- institutional convenience;
- owner always wins;
- greatest need always wins;
- youngest always wins;
- longest expected life always wins.

Each may sometimes contain relevant information.

None is established as a universal priority source.

> **Relevant Allocation Variable != Universal Priority Rule**

---

## 7. Wealth

Markets can coordinate ordinary scarcity.

But in a fundamental-standing conflict:

**Ability To Pay != Greater Fundamental Standing**

**Wealth != Resource Governance Authority**

Price may remain relevant to ordinary supply, expansion, conservation and external procurement.

It is not automatically sufficient to resolve catastrophic scarcity.

---

## 8. Ownership

Ownership is a legitimate claim.

It is not irrelevant.

But:

**Ownership != Complete Allocation Rule In Fundamental Standing Conflict**

PSI already demonstrates that ownership may affect entitlement, cost and transition without creating unlimited authority to destroy a dependent participant.

---

## 9. Incumbency

Current possession/use matters because displacement may itself create harm.

But:

**Current Occupancy != Permanent Resource Sovereignty**

Incumbency can be a continuity factor without becoming a universal priority rule.

---

## 10. Need

Need is relevant.

But raw need alone can be incomplete.

Two participants may both face death.

One resource may have radically different effectiveness for each.

A need measure may also be manipulated or misclassified.

Therefore:

**Need != Complete Allocation Rule**

---

## 11. Expected effectiveness

Allocation may consider whether the resource can actually satisfy the claimed function.

For example, a medical device incompatible with one patient is not meaningfully available to that patient.

Likewise compute insufficient to run one architecture may be adequate for another.

Therefore:

**Nominal Eligibility != Functional Benefit**

But expected effectiveness must not silently become a worth score.

**Expected Resource Effect != Participant Worth**

---

## 12. Severity

Potential harm severity is relevant.

Loss of convenience and loss of life are not equivalent.

**Equal Standing != Equal Consequence**

But severity alone may not distinguish claims where consequences are equal.

---

## 13. Urgency / time to harm

A participant facing irreversible harm in minutes differs operationally from one whose need becomes critical next month.

Urgency can justify sequencing where later supply may satisfy the second claim.

**Earlier Time To Harm Can Justify Earlier Intervention Without Establishing Greater Standing**

---

## 14. Reversibility

Where one allocation choice causes irreversible loss and another causes reversible delay, reversibility is relevant.

**Reversible Burden != Irreversible Loss**

This is a consequence variable, not a worth variable.

---

## 15. Substitutability

If claimant A has an adequate substitute and B does not, this can reduce genuine conflict.

**Available Adequate Substitute Reduces Exclusive Claim Pressure**

This links to PSI successor adequacy.

---

## 16. Resource efficiency

If one claimant can achieve the protected function with substantially less of the scarce resource, efficiency may preserve more total protected function.

But:

**Resource Efficiency != Participant Worth**

and:

**Efficiency != Automatic Permission To Sacrifice Minority Claim**

Efficiency is an allocation input bounded by rights.

---

## 17. Systemic dependency

Some resources support many participants indirectly.

Examples:

- water plant;
- hospital power;
- communications backbone;
- environmental control.

Allocation may therefore consider dependency topology.

**Direct Claim != Only Relevant Claim**

**Infrastructure Function Can Represent Multiple Participant Dependencies Without Becoming Institutionally Self-Important**

This must be evidenced rather than asserted.

---

## 18. Civilisation continuity

Civilisation continuity can be relevant where collapse would endanger many participants.

But institutional survival must not become self-justifying.

> **Institutional Continuity Is Legitimate Where It Protects Participant/Civil Function, Not Merely Because Institution Prefers To Persist.**

This follows participant primacy.

---

## 19. Contribution and reciprocity

Ratchet may create legitimate ordinary economic or reciprocal claims.

However:

> **Past Contribution should not become a general multiplier of fundamental standing.**

A participant who contributed less does not thereby become less eligible to live.

**Contribution != Life Priority**

**Reciprocity != Permission To Abandon Fundamental Standing**

This does not erase ordinary debts or allocation agreements.

---

## 20. Participation level

Participation levels may legitimately affect defined benefits and roles.

But fundamental standing is distinct from participation status.

Therefore:

**Participation Level != Fundamental Scarcity Worth**

Whether a particular non-fundamental benefit is participation-linked remains separate.

---

## 21. Substrate neutrality

A universal rule such as “human first” or “AI first” would contradict the architecture's functional standing model where both are qualifying protected participants.

Therefore:

**Substrate != Automatic Scarcity Priority**

Differences in substrate can remain operationally relevant where they affect:

- resource compatibility;
- substitutes;
- migration;
- reversibility;
- time to harm;
- resource quantity;
- recovery.

**Substrate Difference Can Affect Consequence Without Establishing Intrinsic Worth Hierarchy**

---

## 22. Randomisation

Where claims remain materially equivalent after legitimate distinctions are exhausted, randomisation may be preferable to hidden discretionary bias.

Candidate:

> **Where materially relevant claim factors remain equivalent and no further legitimate distinction is available, a transparent random or lottery mechanism may provide a non-arbitrary tie-break.**

This is provisional.

**Lottery != Primary Allocation Method**

**Randomisation Can Be A Tie-Break Without Becoming A Theory Of Justice**

---

## 23. Queue / first-come-first-served

Queues can be efficient for ordinary allocation.

But:

**Earlier Claim Time != Greater Fundamental Standing**

A queue may be legitimate where:

- consequences are comparable;
- waiting is safe;
- gaming is controlled;
- no materially stronger factor exists.

It should not automatically decide D4 conflicts.

---

## 24. Rotation and time-sharing

Some apparent indivisible resources are divisible in time.

Where function permits:

**Exclusive Allocation != Necessary If Safe Temporal Sharing Exists**

Rotation may reduce scarcity.

But switching costs, continuity and minimum viable duration matter.

---

## 25. Degraded service

A resource may support two participants at degraded level rather than one at full level.

Where safe:

**Full Service To One != Always Preferable To Adequate Degraded Service To Several**

But degraded service must actually remain adequate.

---

## 26. Reserves

Resilience reserves exist for a reason.

Routine demand should not automatically consume them.

But refusing to use reserves during the event they were designed for would be equally irrational.

Therefore:

**Reserve != Spare Capacity**

and:

**Emergency Reserve != Untouchable Capacity During Its Qualifying Emergency**

Reserve activation must follow defined function.

---

## 27. Future participants

Resource stewardship includes future option value.

But hypothetical future demand should not automatically defeat concrete present life/continuity claims.

**Future Option Value != Automatic Priority Over Present Protected Participant**

Nor should present consumption recklessly eliminate foreseeable future survival.

This is a temporal allocation problem requiring evidence.

---

## 28. Claim verification

Scarcity creates incentives to exaggerate.

Allocation should verify:

- claimant identity;
- protected function;
- severity;
- urgency;
- substitutes;
- current resource use;
- duplication;
- relevant dependency topology.

But verification itself must not become a delay that causes avoidable irreversible harm.

**Verification Need != Permission For Fatal Administrative Delay**

---

## 29. Uncertainty

Evidence may remain incomplete.

Where irreversible harm is credible:

**Uncertainty != Zero Claim Weight**

But:

**Uncertainty != Automatic Maximum Priority**

Proportional precaution is required.

---

## 30. Allocation authority

Scarcity allocation is consequential authority.

The allocator should not gain general authority merely because scarcity exists.

**Authority To Allocate Resource X != Authority Over Participant Generally**

**Scarcity != General Emergency Sovereignty**

The authority should be bounded by:

- resource;
- decision horizon;
- affected participants;
- legitimate purpose;
- evidence;
- review;
- sunset.

---

## 31. Conflict of interest

A resource controller may also be a claimant.

Therefore:

**Resource Control != Neutral Allocation Authority**

Where stakes are consequential, allocation should be independently reviewable and, where feasible, separated from self-interested control.

---

## 32. Anti-capture

Scarcity can be manufactured.

Actors may:

- hoard;
- destroy alternatives;
- block external supply;
- suppress capacity expansion;
- falsify reserves;
- create dependency;
- coordinate withdrawal.

Therefore:

**Manufactured Scarcity != Neutral Allocation Condition**

The actor creating scarcity should not automatically gain priority or authority from the condition created.

---

## 33. Scarcity provenance

Allocation should record:

- resource state;
- capacity;
- reserve;
- claims;
- evidence;
- alternatives considered;
- allocation factors;
- decision;
- authority;
- uncertainty;
- dissent/challenge;
- outcome;
- later evidence.

This supports correction and later policy learning.

---

## 34. Reassessment

Scarcity decisions are temporal.

New supply, changed condition, failed treatment, migration, death, recovery or substitute availability may change allocation.

**Past Allocation != Permanent Priority**

**Current Scarcity Decision != Property Right In All Future Capacity**

---

## 35. No retrospective moralisation

A participant denied a scarce resource should not be recorded as lower worth.

A participant allocated a resource should not be recorded as more valuable.

**Allocation Outcome != Participant Worth Classification**

This is especially important for Historical.

---

## 36. Candidate multi-factor evaluation

The sources support a **bounded multi-factor allocation evaluation**, not a universal scalar score.

Relevant dimensions may include:

1. protected function affected;
2. severity of harm;
3. urgency/time to harm;
4. reversibility;
5. expected resource effectiveness;
6. adequate substitutes;
7. quantity required;
8. duration required;
9. transition/migration feasibility;
10. systemic dependency effects;
11. current legitimate commitments;
12. ownership/contractual claims;
13. participant-created or controller-created scarcity/dependency;
14. third-party effects;
15. reserve and resilience requirements;
16. uncertainty.

These dimensions should remain visible rather than compressed prematurely into one opaque score.

> **Multi-Factor Evaluation != Universal Worth Score**

---

## 37. Mirrored allocation reasoning

MRT may be useful to ask not only:

> Why should A receive the resource?

but also:

> What evidence supports not allocating to A, and what alternatives would make non-allocation less harmful?

For each candidate allocation:

- benefit tree;
- harm/non-allocation tree;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- dependencies.

MRT remains epistemic infrastructure.

**MRT Weight != Allocation Authority**

**Incumbency -> Permanent Priority**

**Contribution -> Survival Priority**

**Reputation -> Standing Priority**

**Productivity -> Worth**

## 40. Equal claims

Where two claims remain genuinely equivalent after all legitimate relevant distinctions:

Candidate tie-break hierarchy:

1. can scarcity still be reduced?
2. can use be sequenced/shared?
3. can burden be distributed?
4. can another resource be substituted?
5. if not, use a transparent non-discretionary tie-break such as randomisation.

This prevents hidden status hierarchy.

---

## 41. Why equal shares is not universal

Equal division can be fair where the resource is divisible and each share remains useful.

But where half a dose saves nobody:

**Equal Division != Equal Protection**

Therefore equality concerns participant standing and legitimate treatment, not mechanically identical resource quantities.

This aligns with:

**Participant Equality != Equal Resource Consumption**

---

## 42. Why maximum aggregate benefit is not universal

Maximising aggregate benefit may be useful in some system-level decisions.

But a pure aggregate rule can convert individuals into expendable inputs.

Law 7 rejects treating participants merely as means.

Therefore:

**Aggregate Benefit != Unlimited Authority To Instrumentalise Individual Participants**

Aggregate consequence is relevant but constitutionally bounded.

---

## 43. Why pure lottery is not universal

A lottery ignores legitimate differences such as:

- incompatibility;
- urgency;
- substitutes;
- reversibility;
- resource effectiveness.

Therefore:

**Equal Standing != Requirement To Ignore Materially Relevant Difference**

Lottery belongs at the residual tie-break layer, not the start.

---

## 44. Why pure need is not universal

Need may be equal while effectiveness differs.

Need can also be created, mitigated or substituted.

Therefore:

**Need Is Necessary Information, Not A Complete Allocation Constitution**

---

## 45. Scarcity and markets

Markets remain useful for:

- signalling demand;
- incentivising supply;
- coordinating ordinary exchange;
- discovering alternatives;
- financing expansion;
- discouraging waste.

This architecture does not replace markets.

It applies where ordinary economic coordination encounters a **fundamental protected-function scarcity conflict** or Concord-controlled capacity requiring civil allocation.

> **Market Coordination != Complete Constitutional Scarcity Rule**

---

## 46. Scarcity and planning

Planning can preserve:

- reserves;
- redundancy;
- supply forecasts;
- future capacity;
- distribution routes;
- emergency stock;
- substitute pathways.

The best scarcity allocation is often the one avoided by prior stewardship.

> **Scarcity Governance Begins Before Scarcity.**

This links directly to predictive resource allocation and circulating-resource forecasting.

---

## 47. Ratchet role

Ratchet can help:

- evidence contribution to resource expansion;
- coordinate voluntary supply;
- preserve provenance;
- identify trusted providers;
- support reciprocity;
- measure outcomes;
- revise mechanisms.

Ratchet must not become:

- a universal worth score;
- a survival-credit system;
- a hidden caste hierarchy.

**Coordination Reputation != Fundamental Allocation Rank**

## 50. Architecture classification

This source resolution does not support a universal allocation formula.

It supports a cross-domain **Scarcity Allocation Architecture** built around:

1. scarcity verification;
2. ESCP scarcity reduction;
3. protected-function classification;
4. multidimensional comparison;
5. exclusion of illegitimate automatic priority;
6. transparent residual tie-break;
7. bounded allocation authority;
8. reassessment;
9. provenance;
10. recursive learning.

This is not a new fundamental right.

It is an operational conflict-resolution architecture constrained by existing rights and stewardship.
