## 1. Survival finding

The V1.2 source developed a substantive architecture for **internal Peaceful Distance**: legitimate local or functional variation inside one civilisation without requiring either universal administrative uniformity or complete political separation.

V1.3 preserved most component architectures, but the integrated internal-jurisdiction result was not preserved as a clearly identifiable living object.

This document recovers that result.

## 2. Central proposition

> **Civilisational Coherence != Administrative Sameness**

A civilisation may retain common constitutional foundations and interoperable interfaces while permitting materially different local implementations where those differences are legitimately bounded.

> **Common Identity != Uniform Local Law**

> **Permissible Local Variation != Constitutional Exemption**

## 3. Candidate operating chain

**Persistent Legitimate Difference**  
→ **Uniformity Need Test**  
→ **Constitutional Compatibility Test**  
→ **Affected-Participant / Externality Test**  
→ **Spatial Or Functional Boundary**  
→ **Wrapper Definition**  
→ **Material-Difference Notice**  
→ **Consent / Standing / Non-Consenting Exposure Review**  
→ **Bounded Local Variation**  
→ **Interoperability / Externality Management**  
→ **Monitoring / Remedy / Review**  
→ **Continue / Modify / Terminate**

The architecture does not presume that every difference should be localised.

It asks whether forced uniformity is actually necessary.

## 4. Layered boundary

A bounded jurisdiction may distinguish:

1. **Constitutional invariants**
2. **Fundamental participant rights**
3. **Common civilisational interoperability requirements**
4. **Legitimately delegated local law or policy**
5. **Voluntary special conditions**
6. **Matters remaining with individual participants**

The existence of a local layer does not erase the higher constraints or lower participant rights.

## 5. Wrapper function

The contextual wrapper should make the local variation legible, including where relevant:

- where it applies;
- its legitimate owner/decision function;
- which rules differ;
- which rules remain common;
- affected participants;
- entry conditions;
- non-waivable rights;
- services/dependencies crossing the boundary;
- externalities;
- emergency interaction;
- exit routes;
- dispute/remedy routes;
- review and termination conditions.

> **Boundary != Necessarily Restriction**

A boundary can mark a change in legitimate conditions without prohibiting movement.

## 6. Entry and consent

Material differences that could reasonably alter participant choice should be discoverable before consequential entry.

**Approach / Proposed Entry**  
→ **Material Differences Identified**  
→ **Proportionate Notice**  
→ **Meaningful Understanding Opportunity**  
→ **Entry / Avoidance / Alternative**

But:

> **Entry != Unlimited Consent**

> **Notice != Meaningful Consent**

> **Voluntary Entry != Waiver Of Non-Waivable Rights**

Relevant dimensions include notice, understanding, meaningful alternatives, voluntariness, revocability/exit and constitutional limits.

## 7. Externalities

Localisation does not make exported consequences local.

> **Local Choice != Right To Export Unconsented Harm**

> **Voluntary Participation By Some != Consent By All Affected Parties**

Where effects cross the boundary:

**Local Variation**  
→ **Externality Identified**  
→ **Affected Standing Identified**  
→ **Causation / Materiality / Responsibility Assessment**  
→ **Mitigation / Compensation / Shared Procedure Where Legitimate**  
→ **Continuation, Modification Or Termination**

CBERRM supplies the cross-boundary standing/responsibility machinery; the existence of an externality does not itself create unlimited jurisdiction over the originating context.

> **Externality != Automatic Recentralisation Authority**

## 8. Meaningful exit

Local variation becomes less legitimately voluntary where participants cannot realistically avoid or leave it.

> **Formal Exit != Meaningful Exit**

Assessment may need to consider:
- residence;
- employment dependency;
- essential services;
- family/dependency relationships;
- mobility/capability;
- economic barriers;
- alternative access.

The existence of a theoretical exit route cannot manufacture consent.

## 9. Scale and subsidiarity

Possible bounded contexts can exist at different scales:
- property or facility;
- neighbourhood;
- municipality;
- cultural district;
- special regulatory area;
- digital context;
- experimental zone;
- region;
- other functionally bounded space.

But:

> **Spatial Boundary != Automatic Jurisdiction**

> **GTP Boundary != Automatic Political Jurisdiction**

Authority must still arise through legitimate function and bounded delegation.

The question is not which level is sovereign over everything, but which level can legitimately perform the particular function.

## 10. Anti-capture effect

Where every disagreement must produce one universal rule, participants have stronger incentives to capture central institutions.

Where legitimate differences can remain bounded:

> **Decentralising A Legitimately Localisable Disagreement May Reduce Incentives For Central Political Capture.**

This is a design hypothesis, not a universal decentralisation rule.

Central authority must not use vague externality claims to erase legitimate variation; local authority must not use locality to evade rights or export costs.

## 11. Experimentation

Bounded jurisdictions can sometimes support reversible policy experimentation where:
- constitutional safeguards remain;
- participation is legitimate;
- conditions are legible;
- outcomes and unintended effects are observed;
- externalities are controlled;
- revision/termination remains possible.

But:

> **Special Jurisdiction != Unconsented Experiment**

## 12. Failure modes

Any implementation should test at least:
- constitutional escape;
- rights laundering;
- coerced participation;
- meaningless exit;
- exported harms;
- jurisdiction shopping;
- criminal safe havens;
- race-to-bottom regulation;
- local elite capture;
- segregation/discrimination;
- informational opacity;
- boundary confusion;
- service fragmentation;
- emergency incompatibility;
- digital spillover;
- wealthy enclaves externalising costs;
- permanent local disadvantage;
- central misuse of externality claims to suppress legitimate local variation.

## 13. Relationship to existing V1.3 architecture

This recovery does **not** replace:
- CWA — contextual meaning and bounded rule composition;
- Fractal Permission Architecture — contextual permission/access;
- Peaceful Heterogeneity — general coexistence of legitimate difference;
- Spatial Planning — spatial compatibility and development;
- CBERRM — cross-boundary externality standing/responsibility;
- Governance — legitimate decision authority;
- Law — legal classification and enforceable rules.

It supplies the missing synthesis connecting them when legitimate difference is deliberately bounded **inside the same civilisation**.

## 14. Core recovered rules

> **Where Legitimate Difference Can Be Bounded, Interoperability May Be Preferable To Forced Uniformity.**

> **One Civilisation Can Contain Different Legitimate Local Implementations Without Making Every Local Context Sovereign.**

> **Local Variation Is Legitimate Only To The Extent That Constitutional Floors, Participant Rights, Meaningful Choice, External Effects And Current Authority Remain Properly Bounded.**
