# Decision Authorship and Contextual Participation — General Consequential Choice Architecture 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** CROSS-DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL
**Origin:** Decision Capacity and Apparent Subjective Reality; Authored Risk Acceptance; Contextual Wrapper Architecture

## 1. Purpose

The recurring cross-domain question is:

> **Can this participant sufficiently author this consequential choice, in this context, at this time?**

Where authorship is established, a legitimate system must be capable of respecting a choice or risk it would not itself choose. Where authorship is materially uncertain or impaired, support or independently bounded protective architecture may become relevant.

## 2. Standing, authorship and authority

Three questions remain separate:

1. **Standing** — is the entity a participant/rights-holder for the relevant purpose?
2. **Authorship** — can the participant sufficiently author this particular consequential choice?
3. **Authority** — what may another actor legitimately do in response?

> **Standing != Authorship != Authority**

Uncertain authorship does not erase standing. Established authorship does not create authority over another participant.

## 3. Decision-specific authorship

Authorship is decision-specific, contextual and temporal.

> **Global Competence Label != Decision-Specific Authorship**

A participant may author one choice while needing support for another because the material informational and causal structure differs.

## 4. Navigational competence

General consequential authorship asks whether the participant can sufficiently:
- identify the choice;
- represent material consequences;
- connect action or non-action to relevant consequences;
- represent material irreversibility;
- recognise meaningful alternatives;
- compare options according to their own values;
- understand material uncertainty;
- distinguish subjective experience from external consequence where necessary;
- establish a sufficiently stable choice;
- act with sufficient voluntariness.

> **Authorship != Expert-Level Knowledge**

The participant needs sufficient decision-relevant navigation, not professional mastery.

## 5. Support

Accessible explanation, translation, assistive technology, additional time, trusted support, visualisation or simulation may improve decision navigation.

> **Supported Authorship Is Still Authorship**

> **Support != Substitution**

## 6. Subjective reality and disagreement

Concord need not prove a participant's metaphysical worldview. A subjective belief becomes capacity-relevant where it materially prevents navigation of concrete decision consequences.

> **Reality Content != Reality-Navigation Capacity**

> **Disfavoured Outcome != Failed Authorship**

> **Conclusion Cannot Be Its Own Capacity Evidence**

Danger, eccentricity, financial cost, political unpopularity or disagreement with professional advice cannot by themselves establish failed authorship.

## 7. Contextual participation

Consequential choices commonly concern entry into, continuation within, modification of or exit from a contextual wrapper.

**Decision Authorship
-> Participant Choice
-> Wrapper Entry / Continuation / Exit
-> Contextual Permissions / Risks / Responsibilities
-> Independently Bounded Authority Of Other Actors**

> **Authorship Determines Legitimate Participation; Wrapper Determines Contextual Meaning; Authority Remains Independently Bounded**

## 8. Material change and withdrawal

Authorship attaches to a sufficiently understood state, not every imaginable future state.

> **Authorship Of State A != Automatic Authorship Of Materially Different State B**

Where withdrawal is legitimate:

> **Authorship Of Entry != Irrevocable Authorship Of Continuation**

Material change can therefore require proportionate revalidation of authorship, wrapper state and authority.

## 9. Authored risk

Some choices author exposure to risk rather than a harmful result.

> **Objective != Accepted Risk != Result**

A participant may rationally choose an objective while accepting severe residual risk.

## 10. Refusal and third parties

Refusal is itself a consequential choice.

> **Refusal != Incapacity**

Self-authorship does not author another participant's exposure or actions.

> **Self-Authorship != Third-Party Authorship**

## 11. Prior commitment

A participant may author bounded future obligations, but prior consent does not become unlimited future sovereignty.

> **Prior Authorship Can Create Bounded Obligation Without Creating General Future Ownership**

## 12. Provisional authorship states

**A0 — sufficiently established authorship:** participant authors the decision.

**A1 — supported authorship:** participant authors the decision with legitimate support.

**A2 — materially uncertain authorship:** clarification, support and proportionate reassessment are required.

**A3 — materially impaired decision-specific authorship:** participant cannot sufficiently navigate one or more material dimensions despite proportionate support.

**A4 — no present decision authorship:** substitute/protective mechanisms may be relevant where independently authorised.

These states describe the decision, not participant worth or standing.

> **Authorship State != Authority State**

A3/A4 does not automatically authorise every protective act. A0/A1 does not automatically authorise every requested act by another participant.

## 13. Cross-domain application

The architecture applies to:
- medical consent and refusal;
- employment and occupational risk;
- research participation;
- commerce and contracts;
- governance choices;
- digital/AI operation and self-modification;
- sport and hazardous activity;
- withdrawal from participation;
- other consequential choices.

Domain-specific competence, authority, duty and externality rules remain independently necessary.

## 14. General principle

> **A Legitimate System Must Be Capable Of Respecting An Authored Choice, Risk Or Outcome It Would Not Itself Choose.**

If autonomy exists only while the participant selects institutionally preferred outcomes, the system has permissioned preference rather than participant sovereignty.

## 16. Result

Decision authorship is a general bridge between participant autonomy and contextual architecture.

It does not replace domain expertise, consent, Contextual Wrapper Architecture, Bounded Authorisation, BTA, emergency/protective authority, contract or Law.

It supplies the participant-side question those systems require:

> **Is this consequential choice sufficiently the participant's own authored choice?**

Once resolved, the wrapper determines what that choice means in context and the authority architecture determines what anyone else may legitimately do.
