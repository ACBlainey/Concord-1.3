Architectures examined:

1. Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem (ESCP)
2. Contextual Wrapper Architecture (CWA)
3. Multi-Key Authority (MKA)
4. Authority-Space Completeness Problem (ASCP)

- state owned;
- authority owned;
- inputs;

- trigger;

The objective is architectural economy.

# PART I — EVALUATION-SPACE COMPLETENESS PROBLEM

## 2. ESCP problem owned

ESCP owns the general epistemic problem:

> **Accuracy within a represented evaluation space does not establish that the evaluation space contains all dimensions relevant to the wider conclusion.**

Core distinction:

**Accuracy Within Evaluation Space != Completeness Of Evaluation Space**

ESCP is domain-neutral and authority-neutral.

---

## 3. ESCP state owned

ESCP owns epistemic descriptions such as:

- represented dimensions;
- known missing dimensions;
- potentially unrepresented dimensions;
- evaluation-space scope;
- completeness uncertainty;
- completeness leap;
- recursive epistemic closure risk.

It does not own civil authority state.

---

## 4. ESCP authority owned

**None.**

ESCP does not grant, revoke, validate or compose civil authority.

It can show that an authority evaluation may be epistemically incomplete.

It cannot make the action authorised.

---

## 5. ESCP inputs

- evaluator;
- evaluation architecture;
- accessible dimensions;
- intended conclusion;
- decision-relevant dimensions where discoverable;
- evidence concerning omitted dimensions.

---

## 6. ESCP outputs

- local-accuracy/completeness distinction;
- completeness challenge;
- known/unknown gap state;
- scope qualification;
- recommendation for dimensional expansion, heterogeneity, reversibility or further testing.

---

## 7. ESCP trigger

Use when a conclusion is broader than the directly represented measurements/dimensions, especially where:

- stakes are high;
- decision is irreversible;
- model claims exhaustiveness;
- unrepresented variables are plausible;
- homogeneous evaluators may share the same blind spot.

---

## 8. ESCP termination

ESCP does not prove absolute completeness.

Operational review terminates proportionately when the evaluation space is sufficiently justified for the declared conclusion and stakes.

---

## 9. ESCP must never infer

ESCP MUST NOT infer:

- authority from epistemic confidence;
- authority from completeness;
- permission from model accuracy;
- legal legitimacy;
- contextual precedence;
- transition completion;
- runtime execution permission.

---

## 10. Removal consequence

If ESCP is removed:

MKA/ASCP may correctly validate a represented authority schema while lacking the general epistemic discipline needed to recognise that the schema itself may omit relevant dimensions.

**ESCP is not removable.**

---

# PART II — CONTEXTUAL WRAPPER ARCHITECTURE

## 11. CWA problem owned

CWA owns the problem of representing contextual variation in:

- boundaries;
- access;
- permissions;
- rules;
- protected spaces;
- authority;
- responsibility;
- nesting;
- overlap;
- precedence-routing;
- termination/residue.

Core:

**Boundary != Access != Rules != Authority**

---

## 12. CWA state owned

CWA owns contextual state such as:

- active context;
- surrounding/default context;
- participant relationship;
- boundary activation;
- permissions;
- restrictions;
- authority references;
- nested/overlapping contexts;
- termination;
- residual capability/access;
- unresolved precedence.

---

## 13. CWA authority owned

CWA may represent authority legitimately supplied by the wider system.

It does not manufacture substantive authority.

Where conflict requires resolution:

**Information Architecture != Decision Authority**

**Reporting Authority != Decision Competence**

CWA routes to legitimate external resolution.

---

## 14. CWA inputs

- place/function/context;
- participants;
- permissions;
- protected constraints;
- authority references;
- context relationships;
- emergency state;
- externality/jurisdiction state;
- observed execution.

---

## 15. CWA outputs

- contextual wrapper;
- access/permission/rule representation;
- authority topology;
- nested/overlap relations;
- precedence-resolution route;
- termination/residue state;
- discrepancy state.

---

## 16. CWA trigger

Use where material rules, permissions, protections or authority vary by context or where contexts nest/overlap.

---

## 17. CWA termination

Context ends when its legitimate function/boundary ends.

Function-derived permissions should normally terminate with function completion.

Residual effects are separately assessed.

---

## 18. CWA must never infer

CWA MUST NOT infer:

- child authority from parent access;
- universal precedence from nesting;
- authority from technical access;
- legitimacy from observed execution;
- resolution authority from conflict detection;
- general sovereignty from emergency context.

---

## 19. Removal consequence

If CWA is removed:

MKA loses a principal method for discovering the contexts and protected boundaries that determine which authority dimensions are material.

MKA cannot replace the full contextual representation.

**CWA is not removable.**

# PART III — MULTI-KEY AUTHORITY

## 20. MKA problem owned

MKA owns the cross-domain problem:

> **How can a consequential act requiring several independently protected authority functions be committed without allowing individually valid authorities, permissions, capabilities, contexts or transitions to silently manufacture the authority required for the combined act?**

Core:

**Authority Does Not Automatically Compose**

and:

> **A consequential act may proceed only where every independently necessary authority for the actual route, context and consequence is legitimately grounded, sufficiently current, within scope and not blocked by an upstream prohibition.**

This is the distinct MKA object.

---

## 21. MKA state owned

MKA owns only the minimum authority-composition state required for a proposed consequential act:

- ActionRef;
- RouteRef;
- required authority references;
- authority source references;
- scope;
- composition/inheritance relation;
- current predicate references;
- prohibition state;
- completeness state reference;
- commit state;
- freshness;
- provenance.

MKA should not absorb full domain state.

---

## 22. MKA authority owned

**None as an original source of legitimacy.**

MKA verifies whether externally legitimate authorities:

- exist;
- cover required functions;
- remain current;
- are within scope;
- compose legitimately;
- survive prohibitions;
- remain valid at consequential commit.

Therefore:

**Authority Verification != Authority Creation**

MKA is an authority **composition and verification kernel**, not an authority sovereign.

---

## 23. MKA inputs

- proposed act;
- actual route;
- material consequence;
- protected-context references from CWA or domain architecture;
- required authority dimensions from ASCP/domain architecture;
- AuthorityBasis/AuthorityEvidence;
- composition/inheritance rules;
- current factual predicates;
- prohibitions/precedence rules;
- time/freshness;
- required independence;
- commit conditions.

---

## 24. MKA outputs

Possible outputs:

- AUTHORISED-WITHIN-DECLARED-SCOPE;
- NOT-AUTHORISED-MISSING-KEY;
- NOT-AUTHORISED-INVALID-KEY;
- NOT-AUTHORISED-OUT-OF-SCOPE;
- NOT-AUTHORISED-UPSTREAM-PROHIBITION;
- UNKNOWN-AUTHORITY-STATE;
- DISPUTED-AUTHORITY;
- STALE/REVALIDATION-REQUIRED;
- PERMISSION-SUFFICIENT / MKA-NOT-REQUIRED;
- REROUTE-POSSIBLE;
- COMMIT-VALID / COMMIT-BLOCKED.

These are candidate states, not canonical law.

---

## 25. MKA trigger

MKA should activate where:

1. a material consequential act is proposed; and
2. ordinary permission alone is insufficient; and
3. one or more authority dimensions are required; and
4. composition, scope, freshness, route or protected-boundary interaction is material.

Simple cases may use a minimal MKA check.

---

## 26. MKA termination

For an individual act:

- act is validly committed;
- act is abandoned;
- act is rerouted into a permission-sufficient path;
- authority fails and action is blocked;
- unresolved state is transferred to legitimate resolver;
- proposal expires.

For continuing authority:

revalidation occurs according to material change/freshness rules.

---

## 27. MKA must never infer

MKA MUST NOT infer:

- legitimacy merely from key presence;
- new authority from combining valid authorities;
- authority from capability;
- authority from permission alone where public/coercive authority is required;
- authority from credentials;
- authority from registry state alone;
- authority from context membership;
- authority from transition need;
- authority from urgency unless legitimate emergency rule exists;
- authority from technical reachability;
- authority from beneficial outcome;
- participant worth from authority state;
- factual predicate truth merely from authority availability.

---

## 28. Removal consequence

If MKA is removed:

CWA can describe the contexts.

ESCP can warn about incomplete evaluation.

CBPR can guard runtime commits.

BTA can coordinate transitions.

But no general architecture asks:

> **Do the independently necessary authorities for this actual cross-context route legitimately compose into authority to commit this act?**

Domains would continue reinventing:

- stage-transition authority;
- search versus cognitive access;
- allocation versus withdrawal;
- operation versus decommission;
- emergency versus general authority;
- preservation versus inspection.

**MKA is not removable without loss.**

# PART IV — AUTHORITY-SPACE COMPLETENESS PROBLEM

## 29. ASCP problem owned

ASCP owns the specialised completeness question:

> **Have all materially required authority dimensions for the proposed act, route, context and consequence been represented before MKA validates them?**

Core:

**All Represented Keys Valid != All Required Keys Represented**

---

## 30. ASCP state owned

ASCP owns a scoped completeness assessment:

- declared act/route scope;
- represented authority dimensions;
- known missing dimensions;
- unknown material dimension state;
- disputed dimension state;
- affected protected interfaces;
- residual uncertainty;
- freshness/review triggers.

---

## 31. ASCP authority owned

**None.**

ASCP neither creates nor validates the substantive legitimacy of authority.

It evaluates the adequacy of the represented authority space.

---

## 32. ASCP inputs

- ESCP method;
- proposed act/route;
- CWA/domain protected interfaces;
- MKA candidate key set;
- affected participants/resources;
- consequence;
- known prohibitions;
- materiality;
- novelty;
- decision horizon.

---

## 33. ASCP outputs

- COMPLETE-WITHIN-DECLARED-SCOPE;
- INCOMPLETE-KNOWN-GAP;
- UNKNOWN-MATERIAL-DIMENSION;
- DISPUTED-AUTHORITY-DIMENSION;
- NOT-APPLICABLE/NO-AUTHORITY-REQUIRED;
- REVIEW-DUE/STALE;
- discovered candidate authority dimension.

---

## 34. ASCP trigger

Use before or during MKA where:

- multiple authority dimensions may exist;
- route crosses protected interfaces;
- high consequence;
- novelty;
- nested context;
- transition;
- third-party effect;
- authority schema may be incomplete.

---

## 35. ASCP termination

ASCP is sufficiently resolved when:

> the proposed act, route, affected protected contexts/functions and reasonably discoverable material consequences have been mapped to applicable authority interfaces, no material unresolved authority dimension remains identified, and residual uncertainty is proportionate to consequence and decision horizon.

**Authority Completeness != Proof Of Omniscience**

---

## 36. ASCP must never infer

ASCP MUST NOT infer:

- substantive legitimacy;
- factual predicate truth;
- outcome correctness;
- absolute completeness;
- authority merely because no additional key was discovered;
- prohibition merely because novelty exists.

---

## 37. Removal consequence

If ASCP is removed while MKA remains:

MKA can perfectly validate an incomplete key set and produce false completeness.

If ESCP remains but ASCP is removed:

the general epistemic warning survives, but the operational mapping from missing dimension to authority-route/context/commit is lost.

Therefore the **function** is necessary.

However, ASCP does not presently justify an independent portable module.

**ASCP is necessary as an MKA layer derived from ESCP, not as a separate peer module.**

Core:

**CAP != PERM != AUTH**

---

# PART VI — BOUNDED TRANSITION ARCHITECTURE

## 47. BTA problem owned

BTA owns:

> **How can independently owned systems coordinate one consequential transition when different state dimensions change asynchronously without allowing completion, authority, permission, responsibility, validation or recovery in one dimension to be silently inferred in another?**

Core:

**State != Transition**

**Completion In One Dimension != Completion Of The Bounded Transition**

**Integration Must Not Become Sovereignty**

---

## 48. BTA state owned

BTA owns transition-coordination state:

- TransitionID;
- scope;
- transition class;
- participating owner references;
- prior-valid-state references;
- transition basis;
- phase;
- owner transition states;
- pending state;
- protected unresolved state;
- terminated-authority references;
- next-state references;
- reversibility;
- dispute/uncertainty;
- recovery;
- provenance.

Substantive state remains externally owned.

---

## 49. BTA authority owned

**None.**

BTA preserves external authority references.

It does not manufacture, terminate or validate substantive authority.

**Transition Need Does Not Create Authority**

---

## 50. BTA inputs

- owner states;
- transition request;
- scope;
- basis references;
- completion criteria;
- pending/unresolved state;
- authority/permission references;
- dependency/external consequence references;
- recovery state.

---

## 51. BTA outputs

- coherent transition record;
- current integrated transition state;
- pending/unresolved references;
- non-propagation state;
- completion state;
- residual/recovery state;
- provenance.

---

## 52. BTA trigger

Use for material consequential transitions spanning independently owned state systems.

Do not use for universal event logging.

---

## 53. BTA termination

Transition completes only when declared completion criteria supplied by legitimate owners are satisfied.

Failed/partial transitions preserve residual state.

---

## 54. BTA must never infer

BTA MUST NOT infer:

- substantive owner state;
- authority from transition need;
- authority/consent/rights/identity/liability from object continuity;
- completion from one local completion;
- restoration from rollback alone;
- validation completeness;
- transition-wide legitimacy from one owner state.

---

## 55. Removal consequence

If BTA is removed:

MKA may establish authority to perform each action, but independently owned systems can still disagree about:

- whether transition began;
- what crossed;
- what remains pending;
- whether completion occurred;
- what residual state survives;
- whether rollback restored prior state.

MKA does not own transition coherence.

**BTA is not removable.**

# PART VII — COMPOSITION MATRIX

## 56. Primary ownership matrix

| Architecture | Primary problem | Owns substantive authority? | Primary state |
|---|---|---:|---|
| ESCP | evaluation-space completeness | No | epistemic evaluation-space state |
| CWA | contextual boundaries/rules/permissions/authority topology | No, represents external authority | contextual state |
| ASCP | authority-space completeness | No | scoped authority-completeness state |
| MKA | authority composition/current validity for consequential act | No, verifies external authority | act-specific authority-composition state |
| CBPR | bounded participant runtime execution | No, consumes external authority | runtime state |
| BTA | multi-owner consequential transition coherence | No, references external authority | transition state |

No two architectures own the same primary state.

---

# 57. Canonical composition path

A high-consequence cross-domain action can use:

**Objective**
→ **CWA: identify active/protected contexts and permission possibilities**
→ **Permission-Before-Authority Gate**
→ **Route identified**
→ **ESCP discipline: do not assume represented evaluation space is complete**
→ **ASCP: test whether materially required authority dimensions are represented**
→ **Relevant domain/BCA sources: establish legitimate AuthorityBasis for each required function**
→ **MKA: validate scope, composition, inheritance, freshness, prohibitions and current predicates**
→ **CBPR where automated/runtime execution is used: enforce runtime capability/permission/authority separation and commit check**
→ **BTA where action causes a consequential multi-owner transition: coordinate transition state**
→ **Domain systems: own substantive outcomes**
→ **Review / remedy / learning**
→ **ESCP/ASCP reopen if new dimensions are discovered**

Not every action requires every architecture.

---

# 58. Lightweight composition path

Ordinary low-risk action:

**Context**
→ **CWA/ordinary social rule**
→ **Permission sufficient**
→ **Action**

MKA/ASCP/CBPR/BTA need not be invoked as heavy procedures.

---

# 59. Human institutional path

Example: police search of digital system.

**CWA/domain context**
→ search context identified
→ **ASCP** identifies device-search + possible cognitive-access dimensions
→ Law supplies legitimate authority sources
→ **MKA** verifies each required authority and composition
→ officer/tool executes
→ **BTA** only if a material multi-owner state transition requires coordination.

CBPR is not required merely because MKA is used.

---

# 60. Automated path

Example: AI agent performs authorised external transaction.

**CWA** identifies relevant service/context boundaries
→ **ASCP** checks authority dimensions
→ **MKA** validates authority composition/current state
→ **CBPR** enforces runtime grant/credential/network/commit
→ **BTA** if transaction initiates consequential multi-system transition.

---

# 61. Transition path

Example: participant-supporting infrastructure migration.

**CWA** maps protected contexts
→ **ASCP** maps authority dimensions
→ **MKA** verifies migration/withdrawal/resource/intervention authorities
→ **BTA** coordinates handover and partial states
→ **CBPR** only where participant-associated runtime executes part of transition.

# PART VIII — NON-DUPLICATION TESTS

## 62. Can ESCP absorb ASCP?

Partly.

ESCP already supplies the general logical problem.

But ESCP does not define:

- authority keys;
- route-sensitive authority;
- scope;
- composition;
- inheritance;
- prohibitions;
- commit consequences.

### Finding

ASCP should remain **derived from ESCP but operationally embedded in MKA**.

Do not create a separate portable ASCP module at present.

---

# 63. Can CWA absorb MKA?

No.

CWA represents authority topology and contextual conflict but explicitly does not claim universal composition/adjudication authority.

MKA must work across contexts and domains after CWA has exposed relevant boundaries.

### Finding

**Distinct.**

---

# 64. Can CBPR absorb MKA?

No.

CBPR contains a strong runtime implementation of MKA-like principles but is scoped to bounded participant-associated computation/execution.

MKA applies to:

- police;
- courts;
- clinicians;
- property owners;
- infrastructure operators;
- emergency responders;
- allocators;
- institutions.

### Finding

**CBPR is an implementation host for MKA principles, not the general owner.**

---

# 65. Can BTA absorb MKA?

No.

BTA explicitly refuses ownership of substantive authority.

It can preserve AuthorityRefs but cannot determine whether the authority set for a transition action is complete/current/composable.

### Finding

**Distinct and complementary.**

---

# 66. Can MKA absorb CWA?

No.

MKA cannot independently discover/represent the full contextual topology, nested spaces, permission variation, boundary activation or context residue.

### Finding

**No.**

---

# 67. Can MKA absorb CBPR?

No.

MKA does not own:

- compute;
- resource budgets;
- storage mounts;
- tool permissions;
- network controls;
- credentials custody;
- activation;
- checkpoint/recovery;
- runtime lifecycle.

### Finding

**No.**

---

# 68. Can MKA absorb BTA?

No.

MKA does not own multi-owner transition identity, partial crossing, pending state, completion, rollback/recovery or transition provenance.

### Finding

**No.**

---

# 69. Can MKA absorb ESCP?

No.

ASCP is only one specialised ESCP application.

ESCP remains much broader across science, medicine, intelligence evaluation, governance, economics, engineering and other evaluation problems.

### Finding

**No.**

# PART IX — MODULE-BOUNDARY DECISION

## 70. Is MKA independently necessary?

**YES.**

Removal produces a specific gap:

> no general cross-domain owner remains for determining whether independently legitimate authority components actually cover and legitimately compose into the authority required for the proposed consequential act.

This gap is visible in independently developed Concord systems.

---

## 71. Should MKA become a large omnibus module?

**NO.**

A large MKA module would duplicate:

- CWA context representation;
- ESCP completeness theory;
- CBPR runtime controls;
- BTA transition state;
- domain authority law.

MKA's value lies in being small.

---

## 72. Preferred architecture

MKA should be developed toward a:

> **Small Shared Authority Composition Kernel**

with ASCP embedded as its ESCP-derived completeness layer.

Candidate kernel responsibilities:

1. Permission-Before-Authority Gate.
2. Identify/receive actual act and route.
3. Receive protected context/interface references.
4. ASCP completeness check.
5. Receive externally grounded AuthorityBasis references.
6. Verify required authority coverage.
7. Verify scope.
8. Verify explicit composition/inheritance.
9. Verify required independence.
10. Verify dynamic predicates/freshness.
11. Check upstream prohibitions.
12. Revalidate at consequential commit where required.
13. Emit bounded authorisation/hold/block/unknown/dispute state.
14. Preserve provenance.
15. Reopen on material route/context/consequence/authority change.

Everything else remains with its existing owner.

# 75. Authority ownership invariant

The audit exposes a crucial design invariant:

> **MKA must never become the owner of the authority it verifies.**

Otherwise the verifier could manufacture the state it is supposed to test.

Therefore:

**Authority Verifier != Authority Source**

This should become foundational.

---

# 76. Completeness ownership invariant

Likewise:

> **ASCP may challenge completeness but cannot certify absolute completeness.**

Therefore:

**Completeness Checker != Omniscience Source**

---

# 77. Transition ownership invariant

> **BTA may coordinate authority references through a transition but does not validate or create them.**

**Transition Coordinator != Authority Source**

---

# 78. Runtime ownership invariant

> **CBPR may enforce an authority grant at runtime but does not create its civil legitimacy.**

**Runtime Enforcer != Authority Source**

---

# 79. Context ownership invariant

> **CWA may expose that an authority conflict exists but does not gain authority to resolve it merely by representing it.**

**Context Mapper != Conflict Sovereign**

---

# 80. Epistemic ownership invariant

> **ESCP may reveal that a conclusion overreaches its evaluation space but does not determine the substantive answer merely by exposing incompleteness.**

**Completeness Challenge != Substantive Decision Authority**

Reject.

Reject.

# PART XII — AUDIT RESULT

## 91. Overall result

**PASS — DISTINCT ARCHITECTURAL ROLE CONFIRMED / MODULE BOUNDARY NARROWED**

The six architectures are not redundant.

Their relationship is:

### ESCP
General epistemic completeness problem.

### CWA
Context and permission/authority topology.

### ASCP
ESCP-derived authority-space completeness layer.

### MKA
Authority composition, coverage, current validity and commit gate.

### CBPR
Participant-runtime enforcement of bounded execution.

### BTA
Multi-owner consequential transition coherence.
