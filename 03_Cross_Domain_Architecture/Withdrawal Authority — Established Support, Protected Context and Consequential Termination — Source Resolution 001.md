# Withdrawal Authority — Established Support, Protected Context and Consequential Termination — Source Resolution 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CONSTITUTIONAL / CROSS-DOMAIN DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE RESOLUTION / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Domains:** Rights / Law / Health / Continuity / Infrastructure / Economy / Civil Security / Emergency  
**Triggered By:** Fundamental Aggregation Problem — Adversarial Stress Test 001

## 1. Question

Scarcity and aggregation testing exposed a boundary that cannot be treated as ordinary allocation:

> **What additional authority is required to terminate, withdraw or remove an already-established support relation, and when does that support relation itself legitimately end?**

Examples include:

- withdrawing established life support;
- terminating a participant-supporting compute host;
- disconnecting an essential utility;
- ending an established medical treatment;
- removing a participant from an established protected home/context;
- terminating continuity infrastructure;
- withdrawing an established communication or mobility support;
- decommissioning infrastructure upon which a participant presently depends;
- ending a service after its original contractual or consent basis changes.

The problem is not:

> Does an incumbent participant own the resource forever?

Nor is it:

> May the controller terminate whenever its original title or contract permits?

The problem is the **authority and transition grammar of consequential withdrawal**.

---

## 2. Source resolution

### 2.1 Temporal Consent, Commitment and Withdrawal

Existing architecture establishes:

**Past Authority != Permanent Authority**

**Past Consent != Permanent Universal Authority**

**Withdrawal != Requirement For Unsafe Instantaneous Termination**

**Duty To Handoff != Duty To Remain Indefinitely**

**Role Exit Duty != Permanent Forced Service**

and:

> continuing force derives from the kind of relationship created, legitimate reliance, continuing function and applicable rights—not merely chronological priority.

This means neither original consent nor original provider commitment is automatically timeless.

### 2.2 Participant-Supporting Infrastructure

PSI establishes:

**Ownership Of Participant-Supporting Infrastructure != Ownership Of Participant**

**Participant Standing != Automatic Ownership Of Supporting Infrastructure**

**Greater Participant Dependency -> Stronger Transition Duty Where Reasonably Possible**

**Dependency Consequence Changes The Required Manner Of Withdrawal Before It Automatically Changes Ownership**

**Safe Transition Duty != Permanent Service Duty**

**Temporary Continuity != Permanent Resource Entitlement**

**Participant Refusal != Automatic Extension Of Provider Duty**

**Service Continuation != State Preservation**

**Inability To Continue Service != Automatic Authority To Destroy Preservable Participant State**

The PSI objective is:

> **not indefinite provision, but safe separation where separation is possible.**

### 2.3 Bounded Transition Architecture

BTA treats consequential change as a transition, not a label.

A termination event can affect:

- authority;
- state;
- dependencies;
- identity/continuity;
- downstream systems;
- obligations;
- records;
- safe handoff.

Therefore:

**Termination Decision != Completed Termination**

A valid transition may require intermediate states.

### 2.4 Infrastructure lifecycle

Existing infrastructure architecture establishes:

**Infrastructure Function != Current Infrastructure Asset**

and:

> **Asset Retirement Must Not Accidentally Retire an Unmapped Dependent Function.**

It requires dependency identification, successor readiness, transition and verification before withdrawal where applicable.

### 2.5 Custody as protected context

Custody provides a useful inverse analogy.

It establishes:

**Lawful Custody At T1 != Lawful Custody At T2**

**Past Restriction != Continuing Restriction Authority**

and that authority sunset must propagate operationally.

The same temporal logic applies in reverse:

> a support relation legitimate at T1 is not automatically required forever, but ending it must propagate through the dependency graph rather than merely changing a legal label.

### 2.6 Fundamental Aggregation

Aggregation testing establishes:

**Incumbency != Worth**

but:

**Displacement Can Be A Distinct Consequential Act**

**Current Protected Context != Mere Administrative Incumbency**

**Greater Beneficiary Count != Automatic Displacement Authority**

Thus established support changes the act being evaluated without increasing the participant's intrinsic worth.

## 3. Core distinction

At least four questions must remain separate:

1. **May the support continue?**
2. **Must the support continue?**
3. **May the controller withdraw the support now?**
4. **What transition duties exist if withdrawal is legitimate?**

These are not equivalent.

Therefore:

**Authority To Continue != Duty To Continue**

**No Duty To Continue Forever != Authority For Immediate Withdrawal**

**Authority To Withdraw != Authority To Withdraw By Any Method**

---

## 4. Established Support Relation

An **Established Support Relation (ESR)** exists where:

1. a resource/service/context is presently performing a material participant-supporting function;
2. the participant has actually come to rely upon that function;
3. withdrawal would create a consequential state change;
4. the relation is not merely hypothetical or prospective.

Examples can include:

- active ventilation;
- current dialysis;
- active cognitive hosting;
- established habitation;
- current mobility support;
- operating utility service;
- active continuity storage;
- current prosthetic integration;
- active communications necessary for basic agency.

This is a functional classification.

**Contract Exists != Established Support Relation**

and:

**No Formal Contract != No Established Support Relation**

---

## 5. Incumbency

Incumbency is not a worth multiplier.

The participant does not become intrinsically more valuable because support began earlier.

But an established relation may create:

- reliance;
- protected context;
- transition cost;
- withdrawal harm;
- identity/continuity risk;
- additional controller duties;
- state dependencies.

Therefore:

**Incumbency != Priority By Worth**

but:

**Established Reliance Can Change The Consequence Of Withdrawal**

---

## 6. Withdrawal is an act

Withdrawal should not be modelled merely as:

> stop doing something.

Where support is established, withdrawal can actively change a participant's state.

Therefore:

**Withdrawal Of Established Support != Mere Absence Of Future Benefit**

This does not mean withdrawal is prohibited.

It means its consequences and authority must be evaluated as a transition.

---

## 7. Continuing authority and withdrawal authority

A support provider/controller may have authority to operate a service.

That does not automatically answer termination.

Likewise a participant's prior consent to termination terms does not automatically settle every later critical dependency.

Candidate rule:

> **Continuing support and withdrawing support are distinct consequential functions and may require different authority justifications.**

Therefore:

**Operational Authority != Unlimited Termination Authority**

---

## 8. Sources of legitimate withdrawal authority

Possible sources may include:

- completion of the support's defined function;
- valid participant withdrawal;
- expiry of a bounded agreement;
- lawful provider exit;
- resource exhaustion;
- technical impossibility;
- safety failure;
- treatment no longer providing the supported function;
- replacement by adequate successor;
- independently justified reallocation;
- lawful property/service termination;
- emergency necessity;
- participant death/end of relevant standing where established;
- adjudicated authority.

No single source is universally sufficient.

Its effect depends on dependency and transition consequence.

---

## 9. Expiry

A contract/service can expire.

But:

**Contract Expiry != Physical Transition Completion**

Expiry may terminate ordinary entitlement while still leaving a bounded safe-transition duty.

Conversely:

**Transition Duty != Contract Renewal**

The provider need not be treated as having entered a new indefinite contract merely because safe termination takes time.

---

## 10. Participant withdrawal

A participant may withdraw from a voluntary support relation.

But abrupt termination may itself harm the participant or others.

Temporal Consent already establishes:

**Withdrawal != Requirement For Unsafe Instantaneous Termination**

Therefore participant withdrawal may trigger:

- safe shutdown;
- handoff;
- export;
- preservation;
- successor transition.

The continuation derives from termination safety, not renewed broad permission.

---

## 11. Provider withdrawal

A provider may also have legitimate exit interests.

PSI establishes:

**Participant Protection != Ownership Of Provider Labour Or Resources**

Therefore:

**Provider Exit Can Be Legitimate**

but:

**Provider Exit != Authority For Avoidably Destructive Exit**

---

## 12. Safe separation

The preferred termination object is:

> **Safe Separation**

Safe separation means, as reasonably possible:

- participant no longer depends on provider/controller;
- provider/controller no longer bears continuing service duty;
- protected state is preserved where required;
- successor function is established or safe degraded state reached;
- credentials/data/state/property are transferred appropriately;
- residual obligations/costs are separated for later resolution;
- authority terminates cleanly.

Therefore:

**Successful Withdrawal = Separation Of Dependency Without Unnecessary Destruction**

---

## 13. Transition states

A withdrawal transition may require states such as:

**Active Support**
→ **Withdrawal Proposed**
→ **Dependency Review**
→ **Termination Authority Review**
→ **Successor / Alternative Search**
→ **Transition Preparation**
→ **Degraded / Bridging Support**
→ **State Preservation / Handoff**
→ **Successor Verification**
→ **Support Withdrawal**
→ **Residual Review**
→ **Relation Closed**

Not every case needs every state.

But high-consequence cases should not jump from Active Support directly to Relation Closed merely because a legal termination event occurred.

---

## 14. Consequence-sensitive withdrawal

The stronger the foreseeable withdrawal harm, the stronger the case for:

- notice;
- review;
- successor search;
- preservation;
- transition assistance;
- temporary bridge;
- independent authority;
- verification;
- remedy.

Therefore:

**Greater Withdrawal Consequence -> Stronger Withdrawal Process Where Reasonably Possible**

This does not automatically create a stronger permanent entitlement.

---

## 15. Withdrawal and scarcity

Suppose resource R currently supports A and could instead support B+C+D+E+F.

Aggregation establishes that the five comparable harms can be consequence-relevant.

But A's established relation means reallocation includes an additional act:

> terminate A's established support.

Therefore the decision contains two layers:

1. **allocation consequence comparison**;
2. **withdrawal-authority analysis**.

**Better Aggregate Allocation != Automatic Withdrawal Authority**

---

## 16. Withdrawal does not make participant the resource

If A's support is withdrawn solely to reallocate an external resource, A is not automatically being used as the resource.

However, concealed instrumentalisation remains possible.

If A must be destroyed specifically to release inseparable embodiment/components/state for others:

**Formal Withdrawal Can Conceal Participant Instrumentalisation**

Mechanism and separability matter.

---

## 17. Treatment effectiveness

An established treatment/support relation may cease to perform its function.

Examples:

- ventilator cannot produce meaningful physiological benefit;
- treatment is ineffective;
- compute allocation no longer supports a viable participant process;
- infrastructure cannot perform required service.

Therefore:

**Past Functional Support != Present Functional Support**

If the function has genuinely ended, continued occupancy of the resource may no longer represent the same protected support claim.

But Health/Identity/domain evidence must establish this.

---

## 18. Futility must not become convenience

A controller must not label support “futile” merely because:

- another claimant is preferred;
- participant is expensive;
- participant is unpopular;
- participant is disabled;
- participant is low contribution;
- participant is a different substrate;
- withdrawal is administratively convenient.

Therefore:

**Provider Preference != Functional Futility**

**Higher Alternative Demand != Proof Existing Support Is Futile**

---

## 19. Adequate successor

Where an adequate successor exists:

**Available Alternative != Adequate Successor**

Adequacy must be functional and participant-specific.

A successor should be evaluated for relevant:

- life/health function;
- cognition;
- continuity;
- accessibility;
- privacy;
- security;
- identity;
- agency;
- timing.

A participant cannot manufacture indefinite service by rejecting a genuinely adequate transition without legitimate reason.

**Participant Refusal != Automatic Extension Of Provider Duty**

---

## 20. Refusal of transition

A participant may have legitimate reasons to reject a proposed successor.

Examples:

- identity discontinuity;
- unsafe migration;
- unacceptable cognitive exposure;
- materially lower protected function;
- incompatible embodiment;
- loss of essential accessibility.

Therefore:

**Refusal != Proof Of Unreasonableness**

But once adequacy is independently established and reasonable transition opportunity provided:

**Unreasonable Refusal != Permanent Provider Captivity**

---

## 21. Preservation versus operation

Withdrawal may require preserving participant state without continuing full operation.

Therefore:

**Authority/Duty To Preserve != Authority/Duty To Continue Full Service**

Examples:

- safe AI suspension;
- encrypted state preservation;
- medical stabilisation;
- protected data export;
- temporary storage pending migration.

CPS still applies:

**Preservation != Inspection**

---

## 22. Technical impossibility

A provider cannot be required to perform the impossible.

**Duty To Transition != Duty To Perform The Impossible**

But:

**Claimed Impossibility != Established Impossibility**

and:

**Deliberately Destroyed Transition Capacity != Neutral Impossibility**

Feasibility should be evidenced proportionately to consequence.

---

## 23. Resource exhaustion

Where the resource literally ceases to exist or becomes unusable, withdrawal may be unavoidable.

The architecture then shifts from preventing withdrawal to:

- mitigation;
- preservation;
- alternatives;
- emergency support;
- provenance;
- remedy;
- learning.

**Unavoidable Withdrawal != No Transition Duty At All**

The duty is bounded by what remains feasible.

---

## 24. Provider failure

Provider insolvency, death, shutdown or technical collapse may end ordinary service capacity.

Existing PSI establishes:

**Provider Failure Should Not Automatically Become Participant Failure Where Reasonable Continuity Measures Were Available**

This supports advance succession, escrow, portability and reserve architecture.

It does not create impossible post-failure duties.

---

## 25. Safety termination

A support system may itself become dangerous.

Examples:

- infected compute host;
- unsafe medical device;
- contaminated utility;
- structurally unsafe habitat.

Immediate restriction or shutdown may be justified.

But:

**Safety Termination != Punitive Destruction**

Where feasible:

- preserve state;
- isolate;
- quarantine;
- migrate;
- degrade safely;
- provide alternate route.

---

## 26. Emergency withdrawal

Emergency may compress process.

It does not create unlimited authority.

Candidate:

**Urgency Can Contract Withdrawal Procedure Without Erasing Withdrawal Justification**

The minimum necessary emergency action should be followed by review and restoration/transition where possible.

---

## 27. Ownership

Ownership remains relevant.

But:

**Ownership != Instantaneous Destructive Termination Authority In Every Established Support Context**

Likewise:

**Established Support != Transfer Of Ownership To Participant**

This is the central property/standing balance.

---

## 28. Payment and debt

Nonpayment can create legitimate service termination rights.

But where abrupt termination creates catastrophic participant harm:

**Debt != Automatic Authority For Avoidable Catastrophic Withdrawal**

This does not erase the debt.

PSI's Transition Debt concept may preserve unresolved cost while urgent transition occurs.

**Safe Transition != Debt Cancellation**

---

## 29. Participant misconduct

Misconduct may justify:

- restriction;
- security isolation;
- liability;
- lawful termination;
- alternate service conditions.

It does not automatically erase standing.

**Participant Misconduct != Automatic Forfeiture Of Safe Withdrawal Process**

Where immediate danger exists, emergency restriction may be necessary.

---

## 30. Provider misconduct

A provider must not manufacture the termination crisis.

Examples:

- disable export;
- destroy backups;
- conceal expiry;
- block successor access;
- create lock-in;
- deliberately allow critical transition capacity to lapse.

Therefore:

**Manufactured Withdrawal Crisis != Neutral Termination Condition**

---

## 31. Notice

Notice can be part of safe withdrawal.

Its required duration depends on:

- consequence;
- alternatives;
- migration time;
- predictability;
- urgency;
- participant capacity;
- third-party effects.

Therefore:

**Notice Period != Universal Fixed Duration**

and:

**Formal Notice != Safe Transition By Itself**

---

## 32. Review

High-consequence withdrawal should be reviewable where time permits.

Review questions include:

- what is the support function?
- is dependency established?
- what is the withdrawal authority source?
- is support still effective?
- what happens if support ends?
- what alternatives exist?
- is successor adequate?
- is urgency genuine?
- is scarcity genuine?
- is the provider/controller conflicted?
- is the participant being instrumentalised?
- can state be preserved?
- can separation be achieved with less harm?

---

## 33. Independent review

Where the provider benefits materially from withdrawal and the participant faces fundamental irreversible harm:

**Provider Interest != Neutral Withdrawal Adjudication**

Independent review becomes increasingly important as consequence rises, subject to urgency.

---

## 34. Withdrawal and participant wishes

A participant may request withdrawal even where withdrawal risks death or destruction.

Inalienable Standing establishes:

**Non-Property Status != Mandatory Self-Preservation**

Therefore participant self-directed withdrawal cannot simply be prohibited because others prefer preservation.

But capacity, consent, third-party obligations and exact Health/Law rules remain relevant.

---

## 35. Advance refusal

A participant may have refused certain support in advance.

Temporal Consent establishes that advance preferences can matter without being context-free permanent commands.

Therefore:

**Advance Refusal != Automatically Irrelevant**

and:

**Advance Refusal != Context-Free Automatic Termination Command**

Health-specific development is required.

---

## 36. Support imposed without consent

Emergency support may begin without prior consent under independent protective authority.

Once the emergency basis changes:

**Emergency Initiation Authority != Permanent Continuation Authority**

and participant preference must regain weight.

---

## 37. Developing participants

Support for developing participants may involve guardian/steward authority.

As capacity changes:

**Developmental Support Authority Must Track Developmental State**

Withdrawal or continuation cannot rely indefinitely on an obsolete guardian authority.

---

## 38. AI / digital participants

Withdrawal may mean:

- compute reduction;
- suspension;
- migration;
- shutdown;
- storage;
- network isolation;
- deletion;
- hardware removal.

These are not interchangeable.

**Compute Withdrawal != Automatically Participant Destruction**

and:

**Shutdown != Automatically Safe Suspension**

Identity/Continuity must determine consequence.

---

## 39. Biological participants

Withdrawal may mean:

- stopping treatment;
- device removal;
- service discharge;
- medication discontinuation;
- utility/habitat withdrawal.

Health must determine clinical function and safe transition.

The cross-domain architecture should not decide medical futility.

---

## 40. Hybrid participants

Hybrid support may cross:

- biological;
- computational;
- prosthetic;
- informational;
- network;
- environmental infrastructure.

Withdrawal should follow function across boundaries rather than assume one substrate controls the analysis.

---

## 41. Third parties

Support withdrawal can affect:

- dependants;
- co-users;
- family;
- employees;
- other hosted participants;
- public systems.

Therefore:

**Bilateral Contract View != Complete Withdrawal Consequence Space**

ESCP/dependency mapping may be required.

---

## 42. Shared resource

A resource may support multiple participants.

Withdrawal from one may free capacity for others.

But shared systems create technical coupling.

**Individual Withdrawal != Permission To Destabilise Shared Support**

Safe separation must include co-user consequences.

---

## 43. Withdrawal authority is bounded

Candidate authority object:

`WithdrawalAuthority = <SupportRelationRef, ControllerRef, ParticipantRef, AuthoritySource, WithdrawalObject, Purpose, ConsequenceClass, DependencyState, EffectiveTime, TransitionRequirements, PreservationRequirements, ReviewState, SunsetCondition, Provenance>`

This prevents “termination authority” becoming an undefined general power.

---

## 44. Withdrawal burden of proof

No single universal burden is established.

But as foreseeable irreversible harm rises, the system should demand stronger evidence for:

- authority;
- factual basis;
- successor adequacy;
- claimed impossibility;
- claimed futility;
- claimed emergency.

Candidate:

> **Greater Irreversibility -> Stronger Evidential Justification For Consequential Withdrawal**

This is not a criminal proof standard.

---

## 45. Reallocation

Where withdrawal is proposed to reallocate scarce resource:

**Reallocation Need != Withdrawal Authority**

The process should establish:

1. genuine scarcity;
2. legitimate aggregation/allocation result;
3. independent withdrawal authority;
4. transition method;
5. preservation/remedy where applicable.

This prevents a scarcity decision from silently performing the work of a rights decision.

---

## 46. Reallocation after support ends independently

If A's support relation legitimately ends for an independent reason—e.g. support no longer functions—and resource becomes available, allocation to B is a separate subsequent decision.

**Support Ended -> Resource Available != B Caused A's Termination**

Provenance should preserve the distinction.

---

## 47. Withdrawal and direct harm

Some withdrawals may be so physically integrated that withdrawal itself constitutes direct bodily/cognitive intervention.

Example:

- removing an implanted device;
- deleting inseparable cognitive state;
- physically disconnecting life support.

The architecture must not evade direct-harm law by calling every act “service termination.”

**Termination Label != Elimination Of Direct-Intervention Analysis**

---

## 48. Safe withdrawal does not imply harmless withdrawal

Even best available transition may cause loss.

Therefore:

**Safe Withdrawal != Zero Harm**

“Safe” means minimum reasonably achievable avoidable harm within legitimate authority and feasible alternatives.

---

## 49. Remedy

Where legitimate withdrawal imposes residual civil burden, remedy may still be appropriate.

**Lawful Withdrawal != No Harm**

**Remedy != Finding Withdrawal Was Unlawful**

This parallels custody.

Possible remedy includes:

- restoration;
- replacement;
- migration support;
- compensation;
- debt adjustment;
- continuity support;
- correction;
- provenance.

Exact entitlement is domain-specific.

---

## 50. Withdrawal does not purchase authority

**Compensability Of Withdrawal Harm != Permission To Impose Withdrawal Harm**

Future compensation cannot lower the threshold for termination.

---

## 51. Historical record

Historical should distinguish:

- support existed;
- dependency existed;
- termination authority;
- withdrawal proposed;
- transition performed;
- resource reallocated;
- harm occurred;
- remedy;
- later review.

It should not record the participant as lower worth because support ended.

**Withdrawal Outcome != Participant Worth Classification**

---

## 52. Anti-gaming — participant

A participant should not be able to manufacture permanent provider captivity through:

- refusing adequate transition;
- intentionally destroying alternatives;
- strategic dependency escalation;
- preventing export;
- manipulating status.

Therefore:

**Manufactured Non-Separability By Participant != Automatic Permanent Provider Duty**

Standing remains.

---

## 53. Anti-gaming — provider

A provider should not be able to manufacture easy termination through:

- destroying transition routes;
- withholding information;
- degrading service deliberately;
- declaring false impossibility;
- creating unsafe dependency then invoking it.

**Manufactured Termination Necessity By Provider != Neutral Withdrawal Basis**

---

## 54. Minimum viable continuation

Where immediate termination is not legitimate but full service cannot continue, a **Minimum Viable Continuation** state may bridge transition.

This can mean:

- reduced compute;
- safe storage;
- minimum power;
- medical stabilisation;
- minimal communications;
- temporary shelter;
- limited mobility support.

Candidate:

**Transition Continuation Should Be Function-Bounded, Not Full-Service By Default**

---

## 55. Withdrawal completion

Withdrawal is complete only when:

- provider's relevant authority/duty has ended;
- participant is no longer illegitimately dependent on the provider;
- required state/property/data has been transferred/preserved/disposed lawfully;
- downstream restrictions/permissions have sunset;
- residual claims are separately recorded;
- provenance is complete.

**Service Stopped != Withdrawal Transition Completed**

---

## 56. Compact architecture

**Established Support**
→ **Withdrawal Trigger**
→ **Identify Withdrawal Authority Source**
→ **Map Current Dependency**
→ **Classify Consequence / Irreversibility**
→ **Check Current Support Effectiveness**
→ **Check Scarcity/Reallocation If Relevant**
→ **ESCP Search For Alternatives**
→ **Determine Transition Requirements**
→ **Independent Review Where Required/Feasible**
→ **Minimum Viable Continuation / Preservation Where Needed**
→ **Successor / Safe State Verification**
→ **Withdraw**
→ **Propagate Authority Sunset**
→ **Residual Remedy / Cost / Provenance**
→ **Relation Closed**

## 58. Architecture finding

The source material supports a distinct cross-domain architecture:

> **Withdrawal Authority is the bounded authority to transition an established support relation toward legitimate termination while preserving participant standing, protected context and minimum necessary continuity to the extent reasonably possible.**

It is not:

- permanent service entitlement;
- provider ownership;
- participant ownership of provider;
- automatic incumbent priority;
- automatic reallocation authority;
- a medical futility doctrine;
- a debt-erasure rule;
- a universal emergency power.

The central relation is:

> **A support relation may legitimately end without treating either party as the property of the other.**

---

## 59. Strongest derived rule

> **Where support is already performing a protected participant function, the existence of a legitimate reason to end that support does not by itself determine the permissible manner or timing of withdrawal. Withdrawal should be treated as a consequential state transition: the authority to terminate, the participant's dependency, the availability of safe separation, and the minimum necessary transition duties must be evaluated independently.**

This is consistent with Temporal Consent, PSI, BTA, infrastructure lifecycle, custody temporal authority and Fundamental Aggregation.

---

6. insolvency/bankruptcy;
7. provider labour needed for transition;
8. identity-preserving digital suspension/migration;
9. uncertain participant standing;
