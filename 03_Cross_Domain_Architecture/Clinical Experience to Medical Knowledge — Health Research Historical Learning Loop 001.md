# Clinical Experience to Medical Knowledge — Health Research Historical Learning Loop 001

**Project:** The Concord
**Date:** 4 October 2026
**Status:** CROSS-DOMAIN ARCHITECTURE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL / SPECIALIST VALIDATION REQUIRED
**Domains:** Health / Research / Historical / Education
**Dependencies:** Health outcome monitoring / Medical Knowledge Stewardship / Treatment Protocol Governance / KCS / CIBB / PLE / ESCP

## 1. Purpose

Clinical practice continuously produces information that may improve medical knowledge.

Useful signals can arise from:
- individual cases;
- diagnostic successes and failures;
- treatment outcomes;
- adverse events;
- unexpected recovery;
- unexpected non-response;
- recurrence;
- subgroup differences;
- practitioner observation;
- repeated practical experience;
- direct professional feedback;
- participant feedback;
- device/procedure performance;
- patterns across multiple cases.

This information must be able to feed back into civilisation's medical knowledge without allowing anecdote, professional status or raw operational data to promote itself directly into established medical truth.

> **Clinical Experience Is A Knowledge Source, Not Automatic Medical Knowledge Authority**

## 2. Domain loop

The primary loop is:

**Participant care / clinical case**
→ Health case and outcome evidence
→ observation / anomaly / professional or participant feedback
→ bounded learning signal
→ Research intake
→ hypothesis / aggregation / comparison / investigation
→ evidence evaluation
→ validation
→ Historical medical knowledge update where warranted
→ KCS dependency propagation
→ Health protocol/diagnostic review
→ Education curriculum/qualification dependency review
→ changed practice
→ further outcomes.

This creates a recursive learning system while preserving domain ownership.

## 3. Health owns the clinical evidence context

Health preserves the participant-specific clinical record and the operational meaning of:
- what was observed;
- what was diagnosed;
- what intervention occurred;
- what protocol/version applied;
- what outcome followed;
- what uncertainty remained;
- what the participant and professionals reported.

Health does not automatically generalise an individual result into population knowledge.

> **Case Evidence != General Medical Knowledge**

## 4. Professional experience as an input

Medical professionals accumulate practical observations that may not initially appear in formal research.

They need a discoverable route to report:
- recurring anomalies;
- suspected side effects;
- diagnostic patterns;
- treatment failures;
- unexpectedly effective approaches;
- procedural problems;
- protocol limitations;
- missing distinctions;
- new candidate explanations;
- equipment/device issues;
- population/subgroup differences;
- suspected interactions;
- observations that current medical knowledge does not adequately explain.

Professional status makes the observation potentially valuable; it does not make the conclusion true.

> **Professional Observation != Validated Medical Conclusion**

> **Repeated Professional Experience != Automatic Generalisation**

The report should preserve the distinction between observation, interpretation and proposed explanation.

## 5. Participant feedback

Participants are also direct observers of their own Health state.

Useful feedback may include:
- symptoms not represented in the protocol;
- side effects;
- functional outcomes;
- treatment burden;
- delayed effects;
- recurrence;
- quality-of-life changes;
- discrepancies between measured and experienced outcome.

> **Participant Report != Mere Noise**

and:

> **Participant Report != Automatic Diagnosis**

The participant's evidence enters the evaluation according to what it can legitimately establish.

## 6. Outcome-derived learning

Clinical outcomes should be capable of producing learning signals.

Examples:
- expected response did not occur;
- expected adverse effect did occur;
- unexpected adverse effect occurred;
- outcome differs by subgroup;
- diagnostic prediction repeatedly fails;
- protocol succeeds only under a narrower condition;
- recurrence reveals incomplete causal model.

But:

> **Treatment Response != Unique Proof Of Diagnosis**

> **Successful Outcome != Proof Of General Treatment Superiority**

> **Failed Outcome != Automatic Protocol Refutation**

Outcomes are evidence to investigate, not self-interpreting conclusions.

## 7. Learning signal

A learning signal is a bounded claim that something may warrant Research attention.

It may contain:
- source type;
- clinical context;
- observation;
- outcome;
- expected outcome where applicable;
- discrepancy;
- professional/participant interpretation clearly marked as interpretation;
- protocol/diagnostic version;
- uncertainty;
- possible dependencies;
- urgency/safety relevance.

> **Learning Signal != Research Conclusion**

> **Learning Signal != Authority To Change Care**

## 8. Direct professional feedback route

Health should provide a low-friction route for practitioners to submit learning signals during or after clinical work.

The route should not require the practitioner to write a formal research paper before a potentially important observation can enter Research.

Possible inputs:
- structured case anomaly report;
- protocol feedback;
- adverse-event report;
- diagnostic mismatch report;
- treatment-response observation;
- free-form professional note;
- candidate research question.

Research can then determine whether the signal warrants aggregation, investigation or no further action.

> **Ease Of Reporting Should Be High; Evidential Promotion Threshold Should Remain Independent**

## 9. Automated signal generation

Health systems may detect patterns automatically, such as:
- repeated treatment failure;
- unusual adverse-event frequency;
- unexpected diagnostic reversals;
- recurrent escalation;
- systematic difference between predicted and actual outcomes.

Automated detection may create a Research candidate signal.

> **Pattern Detection != Causal Explanation**

> **Automated Signal != Automatic Medical Knowledge Change**

## 10. Privacy-preserving research intake

The learning loop should not require routine transfer of complete identifiable Health records into Research.

Depending on the question, Research may receive:
- de-identified bounded evidence where genuinely sufficient;
- aggregate statistics;
- minimum-disclosure results;
- locally evaluated cohort queries;
- explicitly consented richer case material;
- protected exceptional access under legitimate authority.

PLE may allow Research questions to move to protected Health data rather than centralising source records.

> **Need To Learn From Cases != Need To Centralise Every Case**

> **Research Need != General Health-Record Access**

Genomic information retains its stronger non-anonymisation assumptions.

## 11. Case reports and rare events

A single case may be important even when it cannot establish general prevalence or causality.

Rare adverse events, novel pathology or unusual treatment responses may legitimately begin with one observation.

Research should preserve the signal without overstating what it establishes.

> **Single Case May Justify Investigation Without Establishing General Truth**

## 12. Aggregation and recurrence

Research can determine whether similar learning signals exist across:
- participants;
- practitioners;
- facilities;
- time;
- protocols;
- treatments;
- diagnostic categories;
- relevant subgroups.

Repeated signals increase the case for investigation but do not automatically solve confounding or causal ambiguity.

> **Repeated Observation != Automatic Causation**

## 13. Research transformation

Research converts operational signals into epistemic work.

Possible actions:
- formulate competing hypotheses;
- use mirrored Reality Trees;
- seek disconfirming evidence;
- compare cohorts;
- design observational study;
- design experiment/trial where appropriate;
- investigate mechanism;
- test alternative explanations;
- identify missing evaluation dimensions through ESCP.

Research owns this transformation from candidate signal to evaluated knowledge claim.

## 14. Validation and Historical promotion

Only after appropriate validation should a result alter the validated medical knowledge state held by Historical.

Possible outcomes:
- no supported change;
- unresolved;
- candidate finding retained in Research;
- safety concern requiring bounded precaution;
- validated narrow finding;
- validated broader medical knowledge change;
- previous knowledge corrected/superseded.

> **Research Finding != Historical Promotion**

> **Publication != Historical Promotion**

Historical preserves both the validated current state and the provenance of change.

## 15. Safety signals before full validation

Some observations may indicate serious harm before causal certainty is available.

The system may route urgent safety review or precautionary protocol action without pretending that the final medical explanation is already established.

> **Precautionary Action != Epistemic Certainty**

> **Urgent Review != Automatic Permanent Knowledge Change**

This preserves both participant safety and epistemic honesty.

## 16. Return path into Health

When Historical's validated medical knowledge changes, KCS identifies affected Health dependencies.

Possible affected objects:
- diagnostic trees;
- treatment protocols;
- contraindications;
- interactions;
- screening;
- monitoring;
- participant information;
- genomic queries;
- competence requirements.

Health then reviews and changes only what the new knowledge materially affects.

## 17. Return path into Education

Validated medical knowledge change may also affect:
- curricula;
- assessment standards;
- CFCC knowledge dependencies;
- qualification components;
- function-critical patches;
- requalification/reassessment requirements.

Education owns the educational response.

> **Medical Knowledge Change != Automatic Whole-Qualification Retraining**

## 18. Feedback to the reporter

Where practical and legitimate, a professional or participant who submits a meaningful learning signal should be able to see its status through an appropriate feedback route.

Possible states:
- RECEIVED;
- TRIAGED;
- DUPLICATE/LINKED;
- RESEARCH REVIEW;
- INVESTIGATION OPEN;
- EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT;
- VALIDATION PENDING;
- VALIDATED CHANGE;
- NO CHANGE SUPPORTED;
- CLOSED;
- SAFETY ESCALATION.

This supports trust and encourages useful reporting without promising that every observation becomes accepted knowledge.

## 19. Credit and provenance

Where a professional, participant or other reporter materially contributes to a discovery, provenance should preserve that contribution where appropriate and consented.

Credit does not create ownership of medical truth or authority over later clinical use.

> **Discovery Credit != Knowledge Sovereignty**

## 20. Bias and reporting distortion

The learning system must account for:
- only unusual cases being reported;
- professional prestige;
- institutional incentives;
- publication bias;
- manufacturer interests;
- participant selection;
- missing outcomes;
- duplicated cases;
- correlated reports;
- changing diagnostic criteria.

ESCP remains relevant because a large quantity of accurately recorded cases can still be evaluated inside an incomplete representational space.

## 21. Negative knowledge

Learning should include evidence that something does not work as expected.

Negative, null and failed outcomes can prevent repeated waste and harm.

> **No Benefit Detected Can Be Valuable Knowledge**

Such evidence must still be interpreted according to study quality and context.

## 22. Separation from discipline

A clinical learning report should not automatically become a misconduct report.

Fear that every anomaly report will trigger punishment would suppress useful learning.

Where evidence independently indicates negligence or misconduct, the appropriate lawful route may also be triggered.

> **Learning Signal != Misconduct Finding**

> **Safety Learning != Immunity From Accountability**

Both routes can coexist without being collapsed.

## 23. Compact architecture

**Case / Outcome / Professional Observation / Participant Feedback**
→ **Health evidence**
→ **bounded learning signal**
→ **Research**
→ **hypothesis + comparison + investigation**
→ **validation**
→ **Historical validated medical knowledge**
→ **KCS**
→ **Health protocols/diagnostics + Education curricula/qualifications**
→ **clinical practice**
→ **new outcomes**.

## 25. Remaining development

This architecture does not yet define:
- exact clinical learning-signal schema;
- Research triage thresholds;
- formal medical knowledge validation authority/process;
- exact aggregation/privacy technology;
- specialty-specific reporting;
- pharmacovigilance implementation;
- mandatory reporting thresholds for serious safety events;
- cross-jurisdiction research/record rules.

It establishes the civil learning loop and domain ownership so those mechanisms can be developed without collapsing Health, Research, Historical or Education into one another.
