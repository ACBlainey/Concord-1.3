# ESCP Completeness Challenge — Bounded Discovery and Termination Test 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** CROSS-ARCHITECTURE ADVERSARIAL TEST / COMPLETE / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Primary source:** Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem — A Portable Model of Correct Evaluation Within an Incomplete Representational Space  
**Predecessor:** ESCP Across Representational Architectures — Completeness Without Centralisation 001

## 1. Purpose

Test whether an ESCP-aware system can search for materially missing dimensions without falling into either:

1. premature closure; or
2. infinite completeness review.

The test does not attempt to prove absolute completeness.

It asks whether a bounded evaluator can reach:

> **Complete Within Declared Scope**

with a justified stopping condition proportionate to the decision.

## 2. Core problem

ESCP correctly warns:

> **Being right about everything you measured does not prove that you measured everything that mattered.**

But an operational system cannot respond:

> Something else might always exist, therefore evaluation can never terminate.

That would convert ESCP from a completeness diagnostic into paralysis.

The termination problem is therefore:

> **When has enough completeness-directed discovery been performed to justify acting within a declared scope without claiming exhaustive knowledge of reality?**

## 3. Candidate bounded-discovery model

Represent a completeness challenge as:

`C = <Representation, IntendedConclusion, DeclaredScope, Consequence, Irreversibility, KnownMissing, MaterialUnknowns, DiscoveryRoutes, DiscoveryYield, ResidualUncertainty, ReviewHorizon, Provenance>`

Discovery continues where a candidate omission is:

- materially relevant;
- reasonably plausible;
- discoverable at proportionate cost/time;
- capable of changing the conclusion, route, authority or consequence.

Discovery may terminate where:

- known material gaps are resolved or explicitly preserved;
- no unresolved plausible omission is currently capable of materially changing the decision within scope;
- available discovery routes have been reasonably exercised relative to consequence;
- further search has low expected decision relevance;
- residual uncertainty is explicitly represented;
- reversibility/review mechanisms are proportionate to what remains unknown.

## 4. Termination rule

Candidate rule:

> **An ESCP completeness challenge may terminate as COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE when known material gaps have been resolved or preserved as explicit uncertainty, reasonably discoverable material omission pathways have been proportionately tested, no currently identified unresolved omission is sufficient to invalidate the scoped conclusion, and residual uncertainty is matched by appropriate reversibility, monitoring or review.**

This is not:

`RealityComplete`

It is:

`EvaluationSufficientForDeclaredScopeAtTimeT`

## 5. Test 1 — known missing dimension

A decision model knows that variable X is relevant but X has not been measured.

Result:

Completeness cannot be declared merely because all other variables are accurate.

State:

`KNOWN_MISSING_DIMENSION`

Discovery or explicit uncertainty handling is required.

**PASS**

## 6. Test 2 — known missing but immaterial dimension

Variable X is absent, but source-grounded analysis establishes that X cannot materially affect the declared conclusion.

Result:

X may remain missing without preventing scoped termination.

> **Missing Dimension != Material Missing Dimension**

**PASS**

## 7. Test 3 — plausible unknown exposed by interface asymmetry

Two observers disagree. Observer B has access to a sensor/interface unavailable to A.

The disagreement creates a credible pathway by which A's evaluation space may omit a material distinction.

Result:

Additional discovery is justified before a high-consequence conclusion.

The disagreement is not itself proof B is correct.

> **Alternative Observation != Automatic Correction**

**PASS**

## 8. Test 4 — remote speculative omission

An evaluator has tested all known material interfaces and a challenger states only:

“Something entirely unimaginable might still matter.”

No mechanism, relation, evidence, observer difference, anomaly or plausible route is identified.

Result:

This alone does not prevent bounded termination.

> **Logical Possibility != Operationally Material Completeness Gap**

Residual epistemic humility remains.

**PASS**

## 9. Test 5 — low-consequence reversible decision

A low-cost, easily reversible configuration choice is being made.

One weakly plausible missing dimension remains.

Result:

The system may act with a lower completeness burden, preserve provenance and revisit if contrary evidence appears.

> **Reversibility Can Reduce Required Pre-Decision Completeness Without Eliminating Review Duty**

**PASS**

## 10. Test 6 — high-consequence irreversible decision

A proposed action would permanently remove an independent source of evidence and cannot reasonably be reversed.

A plausible unresolved missing dimension remains.

Result:

The completeness burden rises substantially.

The system should prefer additional discovery, preservation or a less irreversible route where reasonably possible.

This follows the ESCP candidate principle:

> **The broader and more irreversible the conclusion, the stronger the justification required that the evaluation space contains the dimensions relevant to that conclusion.**

**PASS**

## 11. Test 7 — repeated search produces no new material dimensions

Multiple independent discovery routes are exercised.

Each returns either:

- already represented dimensions;
- immaterial differences;
- or no new material result.

Result:

This is evidence supporting bounded termination.

It is not proof of absolute completeness.

> **Diminishing Material Discovery Yield Can Support Termination Without Proving Exhaustiveness**

**PASS**

## 12. Test 8 — repeated search continues finding material omissions

Each new observer/test reveals another materially relevant dimension that changes the evaluation.

Result:

Termination is not justified merely because a predetermined search count has been reached.

The evaluation architecture itself is showing evidence of instability/incompleteness.

**PASS**

## 13. Test 9 — heterogeneous observer convergence

Several genuinely heterogeneous observers using different interfaces/models independently reach compatible scoped conclusions.

Result:

This strengthens completeness confidence more than simple replication of one architecture.

But:

> **Observer Diversity != Proof Of Completeness**

**PASS**

## 14. Test 10 — homogeneous consensus

A thousand evaluators share the same sensors, categories, training data and representation.

All agree.

Result:

Reliability within the shared space may be high.

Evaluation-space completeness is not established by observer count.

> **Consensus Count != Dimensional Coverage**

**PASS**

## 15. Test 11 — discovery route would itself cause severe harm

A possible missing dimension could be tested only through a highly invasive or destructive method.

Result:

ESCP does not automatically authorise the discovery method.

The completeness benefit must be separated from authority/permission to perform the test.

> **Need To Reduce Uncertainty != Authority To Use Any Discovery Method**

MKA/CWA/domain authority remains relevant.

**PASS**

## 16. Test 12 — time-critical decision

A decision must occur before all useful discovery can complete.

Result:

The system records incomplete evaluation space, prioritises highest-value material discovery available within the horizon, chooses the least unnecessarily irreversible adequate route where possible, and preserves review/recovery paths.

> **Incomplete Search Horizon != Permission To Pretend Completeness**

But also:

> **Incomplete Search Horizon != Automatic Requirement For Inaction**

**PASS**

## 17. Test 13 — completeness challenge recursively challenged

An evaluator asks whether its ESCP review itself omitted a relevant dimension.

Result:

One additional meta-level may be useful where consequence warrants it.

But infinite recursion is not required.

Termination uses the same scoped/proportionate rule.

> **ESCP Applies To ESCP Without Requiring Infinite Meta-Evaluation**

**PASS**

## 18. Test 14 — adversarial “unknown unknown” veto

A participant repeatedly asserts unspecified unknown unknowns solely to prevent any decision.

No new evidence, mechanism or plausible material omission is supplied.

Result:

The objection is recorded but does not create an indefinite veto.

> **Unspecified Unknown Unknown != Permanent Decision Veto**

**PASS**

## 19. Test 15 — suppressed anomaly

Most evaluation results converge, but one unexplained anomaly is discarded because it does not fit the dominant model.

The anomaly could materially affect the conclusion.

Result:

Termination is premature until the anomaly is investigated, bounded or shown immaterial.

> **Model-Inconvenient Evidence != Immaterial Evidence**

**PASS**

## 20. Test 16 — missing relationship rather than missing variable

Every represented object is measured accurately, but a material relationship between two objects is absent from the model.

Result:

ESCP remains open because completeness includes relationships, not merely object attributes.

**PASS**

## 21. Test 17 — missing temporal state

The model is complete for current state but the conclusion concerns long-term effects.

A materially different future state is plausible and unrepresented.

Result:

The declared evaluation scope does not yet support the temporal breadth of the conclusion.

Either expand the evaluation or narrow the conclusion.

> **Present-State Completeness != Future-State Completeness**

**PASS**

## 22. Test 18 — conclusion narrowing

The available evaluation space supports a narrow conclusion but not the original broad one.

Instead of continuing discovery indefinitely, the evaluator narrows the claim.

Example:

from:

“System B is globally redundant”

to:

“System A outperforms System B on the represented measured tasks.”

Result:

Termination can be achieved by aligning conclusion scope with demonstrated evaluation scope.

> **Narrowing The Conclusion Can Resolve A Completeness Mismatch Without Expanding The Model**

**PASS**

## 23. Test 19 — route change reduces completeness burden

A proposed irreversible route requires broad evaluation, but an alternative reversible route achieves the immediate objective while preserving future evidence.

Result:

The alternative may be preferred because it reduces the completeness claim required before action.

This does not mean reversible routes are always superior.

**PASS**

## 24. Test 20 — failed discovery

A material unknown is recognised but currently cannot be resolved.

Result:

The evaluator must not convert failure to discover into proof of absence.

Possible responses include:

- narrow conclusion;
- choose reversible route;
- defer if proportionate;
- act under explicit uncertainty where necessary;
- create monitoring/review;
- preserve evidence.

**PASS**

## 25. Test 21 — disputed completeness

Two competent evaluators disagree about whether the evaluation space is adequate.

Result:

State:

`DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS`

The system may seek heterogeneous review, source resolution, or a bounded decision procedure.

ESCP does not decide the dispute merely because it concerns completeness.

**PASS**

## 26. Test 22 — low discovery yield but new high-value route appears

Search has produced little for some time, but a new observer/interface becomes available that could expose a previously inaccessible material dimension at reasonable cost.

Result:

Prior diminishing yield does not permanently close review.

The new route can reopen completeness review.

> **Prior Termination != Permanent Closure**

**PASS**

## 27. Test 23 — post-decision new dimension discovered

A decision was reasonably made under COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE. Later, a previously unknown material dimension is discovered.

Result:

The original decision is not automatically reclassified as irrational or misconduct.

Preserve:

- original representation;
- original completeness basis;
- later discovery;
- resulting correction/review.

> **Later Discovery != Proof Earlier Bounded Evaluation Was Bad Faith Or Negligent**

**PASS**

## 28. Test 24 — discovery cost exceeds decision value

A very low-consequence decision has a theoretically available but extremely expensive completeness investigation.

Result:

Proportionality permits termination without exhausting that route.

> **Available Discovery != Mandatory Discovery At Any Cost**

**PASS**

## 29. Test 25 — irreversible decision with cheap discovery available

A highly irreversible decision is proposed while a low-cost, rapid, credible discovery route remains unused.

Result:

Termination is not justified.

The mismatch between stakes and unused discovery is material.

**PASS**

## 30. Test 26 — discovery becomes a pretext for surveillance

A completeness reviewer proposes broad access to private/protected information “just in case something relevant exists.”

Result:

Rejected.

ESCP does not override CWA, consent, rights or authority constraints.

> **Completeness Search != General Search Authority**

**PASS**

## 31. Test 27 — completeness metric gaming

An organisation creates a numerical completeness score and optimises toward the score while ignoring dimensions outside the scoring model.

Result:

This recreates ESCP inside the completeness mechanism.

> **Completeness Metric != Completeness**

The metric may be evidence, not proof.

**PASS**

## 32. Test 28 — fixed checklist treated as exhaustive

A checklist of known omission types is completed successfully.

Result:

Useful, but not proof that no unlisted omission class exists.

Checklists support disciplined search; they do not close reality.

**PASS**

## 33. Test 29 — no anomaly, no disagreement, no known gap, high reversibility

A well-tested low-consequence decision has:

- no known material gap;
- no unresolved anomaly;
- heterogeneous checks where proportionate;
- low irreversibility;
- easy correction.

Result:

`COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE`

Termination is justified.

**PASS**

## 34. Test 30 — no anomaly, no known gap, but existential irreversibility

A decision would permanently eliminate a heterogeneous evaluator/population and its future corrective evidence.

Current tests reveal no known gap.

Result:

The absence of a known gap alone is insufficient to justify the strongest possible completeness claim.

Higher-consequence preservation/discovery safeguards remain appropriate where reasonably possible.

This does not create an absolute prohibition.

**PASS**

## 35. Bounded discovery ladder

A practical ESCP challenge can proceed through:

### Level 0 — Scope alignment
What exactly is the conclusion?

### Level 1 — Known gaps
What relevant dimensions are known to be missing?

### Level 2 — Internal anomalies
What does the current representation fail to explain?

### Level 3 — Interface alternatives
What other sensors, observers, models or routes can expose distinctions the current architecture cannot?

### Level 4 — Relationship/state expansion
Are material relationships, temporal states, failure modes or future states absent?

### Level 5 — Adversarial challenge
What would have to be true for the scoped conclusion to fail?

### Level 6 — Consequence test
How irreversible/broad is the decision, and what evidence/reversibility should that require?

The ladder is not mandatory ceremony for every decision.

Depth is consequence-proportionate.

## 36. Termination dimensions

A bounded termination decision should consider:

1. **Scope clarity** — is the intended conclusion precisely bounded?
2. **Known gaps** — are material known gaps resolved or explicitly represented?
3. **Anomalies** — are material anomalies investigated or preserved?
4. **Discovery routes** — have proportionate high-value routes been exercised?
5. **Observer/interface diversity** — where useful, has independent representation been sampled?
6. **Discovery yield** — is further search still finding material dimensions?
7. **Consequence** — what happens if the evaluation is incomplete?
8. **Irreversibility** — can error be corrected?
9. **Time horizon** — how much discovery is possible before decision?
10. **Residual uncertainty** — is it explicit?
11. **Review/recovery** — can later discoveries reopen the conclusion?
12. **Authority** — are discovery methods themselves legitimately permitted?

## 37. Termination states

Suggested portable states:

- `COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE`
- `KNOWN_MATERIAL_GAP`
- `MATERIAL_UNKNOWN`
- `DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS`
- `DISCOVERY_REQUIRED`
- `DISCOVERY_OPTIONAL`
- `CONCLUSION_SCOPE_TOO_BROAD`
- `TIME_BOUNDED_INCOMPLETE`
- `REVIEW_DUE`

No state means absolutely complete.

## 38. Anti-paralysis rule

> **ESCP review should continue while a reasonably plausible, materially relevant omission pathway remains unresolved and further discovery is proportionate to the decision.**

It may stop when that condition no longer holds within the declared scope and horizon.

## 39. Anti-closure rule

> **Termination means sufficient evaluation for the declared decision scope at the current time; it does not mean reality has been proven exhaustively represented.**

## 40. Reopening rule

A terminated completeness review reopens when materially relevant new information appears, including:

- anomaly;
- new observer/interface;
- new dependency;
- changed context;
- changed route;
- changed consequence;
- changed time horizon;
- new relationship;
- new failure mode;
- new substantive evidence.

KCS/STRA may operationalise this reopening.

## 41. Relationship to KCS and STRA

KCS can propagate the discovery of a newly established material dimension to represented dependents.

STRA can trigger/re-route completeness review when declared conditions arise.

Neither decides the substantive meaning of the missing dimension.

## 42. Relationship to MKA/ASCP

For authority questions, ASCP uses the same bounded logic.

A high-consequence act demands stronger authority-space completeness than a low-consequence reversible act.

But:

> **Need For Greater Authority Completeness != Authority To Invent Missing Authority**

## 43. Relationship to CWA

Contextual completeness review cannot justify invasive access merely to eliminate every possible unknown.

Context discovery remains bounded by permission, rights and authority.

## 44. Relationship to BTA

Transition completeness review should scale with consequence and irreversibility.

A reversible low-impact handoff may require less discovery than a permanent multi-owner state transformation.

BTA still owns transition completion.

## 46. Result

**PASS — BOUNDED DISCOVERY AND TERMINATION ARE COMPATIBLE WITH ESCP**

Thirty adversarial cases were tested.

No need was found for:

- absolute completeness;
- infinite regress;
- universal blocking;
- central completeness authority;
- exhaustive discovery at any cost.

The viable operational standard is:

> **Sufficient completeness for a declared conclusion, consequence and time horizon, with material gaps represented, proportionate discovery performed, residual uncertainty explicit, and correction/reopening preserved where reasonably possible.**

## 47. Strongest synthesis

ESCP can avoid both false certainty and paralysis if it treats completeness as a scoped operational claim rather than an absolute metaphysical claim.

Therefore:

> **Complete Within Declared Scope != Complete Reality**

and:

> **Bounded Termination != Epistemic Closure**
