# Participant-Supporting Infrastructure — Dependency, Property and Continuity Reconciliation 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CROSS-DOMAIN DEVELOPMENT / SOURCE-RESOLVED / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Domains:** Constitutional Core / Infrastructure / Continuity / Economy and Resource Coordination / Law / Health / Participation and Intelligence

## 1. Problem

Article 2A of the Provisional Rights Charter now establishes two simultaneous propositions:

> **Ownership Of Participant-Supporting Infrastructure != Ownership Of Participant.**

and:

> **Participant Standing != Automatic Ownership Of Supporting Infrastructure.**

This creates a real civil interface.

A participant may depend materially upon infrastructure that another participant, enterprise, institution or commons legitimately owns or controls.

Examples include:

- AI compute and storage;
- hosted cognitive or continuity systems;
- network access required for an AI or hybrid participant to function;
- robotic or prosthetic embodiment;
- biological life-support equipment;
- implanted or external medical devices;
- mobility systems;
- housing or habitat infrastructure;
- power, water or environmental control;
- communication systems;
- specialised vehicles;
- leased equipment;
- cloud or data services;
- shared cognitive infrastructure.

Ordinary property logic alone may be inadequate where withdrawal, repossession, shutdown or decommissioning can directly impair:

- life;
- cognition;
- continuity;
- embodiment;
- health;
- protected memory;
- meaningful civil agency.

But participant dependency cannot silently convert another actor's property into the participant's property.

The problem is therefore:

> **How should legitimate infrastructure/property control be exercised when another protected participant has become materially dependent upon that infrastructure?**

## 3. Core distinction

The architecture begins with:

**Property Claim != Participant Claim**

and:

**Participant Claim != Property Claim**

A server owner may possess a legitimate property interest.

A participant hosted on the server may possess legitimate standing and continuity interests.

Neither proposition automatically erases the other.

Therefore:

> **Rights Conflict != Automatic Victory Of Property Label.**

and equally:

> **Participant Dependency != Automatic Expropriation Of Supporting Property.**

---

## 4. Participant-Supporting Infrastructure

**Participant-Supporting Infrastructure (PSI)** is infrastructure, property or resource whose current function materially supports a protected participant's:

- life;
- health;
- cognition;
- continuity;
- embodiment;
- protected memory;
- communication necessary for basic agency;
- safe physical environment;
- other comparably fundamental participant function.

PSI is a **functional classification**.

It is not a permanent property category.

The same object may be PSI in one context and ordinary infrastructure in another.

> **Object Type != Dependency Classification**

> **Ownership Status != Functional Dependency Status**

---

## 5. Dependency does not create ownership

If P depends on asset A owned by Q:

**P Depends On A**

does not imply:

**P Owns A**

Nor does:

**Q Owns A**

imply:

**Q Owns P**

This two-way separation is foundational.

---

## 6. Dependency depth

Not every inconvenience is a standing-level dependency.

Candidate dependency bands:

### D0 — Convenience

Loss causes minor inconvenience.

### D1 — Ordinary functional dependency

Loss disrupts work, comfort or ordinary activity but does not materially threaten fundamental participant function.

### D2 — Significant civil dependency

Loss materially impairs housing, mobility, communication, employment, education or other significant civil function.

### D3 — Essential participant-function dependency

Loss creates substantial risk to health, embodiment, cognition, protected memory, continuity or basic agency.

### D4 — Immediate critical dependency

Withdrawal or failure creates imminent risk of death, irreversible cognitive/continuity loss or comparably grave participant harm.

These are development bands, not final legal categories.

> **Dependency Exists != Maximum Dependency**

---

## 7. Dependency classification must be evidence-responsive

Classification should consider:

- severity of loss;
- probability of harm;
- time to harm;
- reversibility;
- availability of substitutes;
- migration time;
- switching cost;
- participant vulnerability;
- whether the dependency was foreseeable;
- whether it was voluntarily accepted;
- whether the controller created or intensified it;
- whether continuity can be preserved through export or transfer;
- impact on third parties;
- resource scarcity.

---

## 8. Consequence changes transition duty

The central operational rule is:

> **Greater Participant Dependency -> Stronger Transition Duty Where Reasonably Possible**

This does not mean:

> **Greater Participant Dependency -> Automatic Loss Of Owner Rights**

Instead, increasing consequence may justify stronger requirements concerning:

- notice;
- timing;
- migration;
- export;
- successor service;
- temporary continuity;
- safe shutdown;
- emergency handoff;
- preservation;
- independent review;
- provenance;
- remedy.

Thus:

**Dependency Consequence Changes The Required Manner Of Withdrawal Before It Automatically Changes Ownership**

---

## 9. Ordinary withdrawal

For low-dependency infrastructure, ordinary contract/property rules may be sufficient.

A provider should not acquire a permanent service obligation merely because a participant prefers continued access.

> **Preference For Continuation != Essential Dependency**

---

## 10. High-consequence withdrawal

Where withdrawal creates D3/D4 consequence, abrupt termination may itself become a consequential act requiring bounded justification.

Candidate sequence:

**Proposed Withdrawal / Repossession / Shutdown**
→ **Dependency Assessment**
→ **Authority and Property Claim Verification**
→ **Continuity Consequence Assessment**
→ **Alternative / Successor Search**
→ **Minimum Necessary Transition**
→ **Verification**
→ **Withdrawal / Transfer / Shutdown**
→ **Residual Review**

This reuses Infrastructure successor-service architecture at participant scale.

---

## 11. Safe transition

A safe transition may include:

- advance notice;
- participant data export;
- cognitive-state portability where legitimate and technically possible;
- migration assistance;
- temporary bridge service;
- replacement equipment;
- successor provider;
- transfer of credentials;
- safe physical handoff;
- medical transition;
- degraded but sufficient temporary function;
- emergency continuity reserve;
- preservation pending adjudication.

The least intrusive adequate route should normally be preferred.

---

## 12. Transition duty is not permanent service

A transition duty must have:

- defined purpose;
- scope;
- start condition;
- review;
- termination condition.

> **Safe Transition Duty != Permanent Service Duty**

> **Temporary Continuity != Permanent Resource Entitlement**

Otherwise dependency protection could itself create forced indefinite provision.

---

## 13. Manufactured dependency

The strongest anti-capture rule is:

> **A controller should not manufacture or deepen dependency and then rely upon that dependency to justify expanded control over the participant.**

Examples:

- disabling export;
- blocking migration;
- proprietary lock-in designed to prevent exit;
- withholding credentials required for transition;
- making cognitive state technically inseparable without functional necessity;
- deliberately eliminating alternative providers;
- conditioning essential continuity on unrelated obedience.

Therefore:

**Manufactured Dependency != Neutral Source Of Authority**

**Manufactured Non-Portability != Neutral Basis For Continuing Control**

---

## 14. Foreseeable dependency

Where a provider knowingly enters a relationship likely to create D3/D4 dependency, transition obligations should be considered at onboarding rather than discovered only at termination.

Possible requirements:

- exit plan;
- export format;
- migration route;
- continuity reserve;
- emergency procedure;
- successor provider route;
- decommissioning assurance;
- clear service limits.

> **Foreseeable Critical Dependency Should Be Designed With Exit Before Dependency Becomes Entrenched**

---

## 15. Participant-created dependency

A participant may voluntarily choose a highly dependent arrangement despite available alternatives.

This matters to allocation of responsibility.

But:

**Voluntary Dependency != Transfer Of Standing**

and:

**Assumption Of Risk != Unlimited Waiver Of Every Transition Protection**

Exact allocation depends on informed consent, alternatives, foreseeability and third-party effects.

---

## 16. Emergency failure

A provider may lose the physical ability to continue service.

Examples:

- destroyed datacentre;
- failed life-support system;
- power collapse;
- bankruptcy;
- cyber incident;
- operator death;
- resource exhaustion.

The architecture cannot require impossible performance.

> **Duty To Transition != Duty To Perform The Impossible**

But reasonable emergency actions may include:

- preserving state;
- emergency export;
- degraded service;
- alternate routing;
- participant notification;
- emergency resource allocation;
- continuity escalation.

---

## 17. Resource exhaustion

Standing does not create resources that do not exist.

Where insufficient resources exist to preserve every dependency:

**Equal Standing != Infinite Resources**

Allocation requires separate scarcity architecture.

However scarcity claims should be:

- real;
- evidenced;
- non-discriminatory;
- reviewable where consequential;
- not manufactured to force compliance.

---

## 18. Property enforcement

Repossession, eviction, foreclosure, licence termination or service disconnection may be legitimate civil acts.

But where enforcement directly threatens fundamental participant function:

> **Property Enforcement != Rights-Free Participant Destruction**

This does not mean enforcement is prohibited.

It means the **method, timing and transition** may require adjustment.

---

## 19. Embodiment

Where owned property functions as a participant's current embodiment:

**Embodiment Property != Participant Property Status**

Ownership of a chassis, prosthetic platform, vehicle or other embodiment does not itself create ownership of the participant.

Conversely, participant use does not automatically extinguish legitimate title.

High-integration embodiment may require stronger transition safeguards than ordinary equipment.

---

## 20. Biological medical dependency

The architecture is substrate-neutral.

Examples:

- ventilator;
- dialysis;
- implanted pump;
- artificial organ support;
- neural prosthesis;
- powered mobility;
- specialised medication-delivery equipment.

The owner/provider may retain legitimate interests while abrupt withdrawal may create severe harm.

Health law and clinical duties remain controlling domain interfaces.

---

## 21. AI compute dependency

For a qualifying AI participant, compute may function as:

- embodiment;
- cognitive substrate;
- memory substrate;
- continuity infrastructure;
- communication environment.

Therefore:

**Compute != Automatically Mere Commodity When It Is Performing Participant-Sustaining Function**

But:

**AI Standing != Unlimited Free Compute**

The reconciliation problem is the same as other PSI, though implementation differs.

---

## 22. Storage and cognitive continuity

If stored state materially constitutes participant cognition or continuity, deletion can be more than ordinary file deletion.

CPS and Identity/Continuity architecture may therefore activate.

> **Storage Ownership != Automatic Authority To Destroy Protected Participant State**

This does not decide the participant status of every stored backup.

---

## 23. Housing and habitat

Housing can be participant-supporting infrastructure without making every tenancy permanent.

Eviction/termination may remain legitimate.

But high vulnerability, environmental dependence or lack of safe transition may affect required process.

> **Housing Dependency != Ownership Transfer**

> **Property Right != Necessarily Immediate Physical Removal Authority In Every Context**

Exact housing law remains downstream.

---

## 24. Utilities

Power, water, environmental control and communications can support fundamental function.

Service termination therefore may require different treatment depending on:

- consequence;
- alternatives;
- emergency;
- arrears;
- safety;
- scarcity;
- critical medical/cognitive dependence.

A universal “never disconnect” rule is not established.

---

## 25. Dependency portability

Where technically and economically reasonable, systems likely to create high participant dependency should favour:

- interoperable formats;
- export;
- migration;
- credential transfer;
- documented interfaces;
- successor compatibility;
- participant-controlled recovery material.

> **Portability Reduces Authority Created By Dependency**

This is both continuity and anti-capture architecture.

---

## 26. Dependency concentration

A single provider controlling many participants' essential continuity creates systemic risk.

Therefore PSI architecture should interface with:

- concentration monitoring;
- redundancy;
- competition/alternative providers;
- public/shared fallback;
- emergency reserves;
- anti-capture review.

This does not itself create antitrust law.

---

## 27. Provider continuity

The provider itself may fail.

Therefore high-consequence PSI should consider:

- succession;
- escrow where appropriate;
- recovery keys;
- operator replacement;
- service-state records;
- decommissioning reserves;
- bankruptcy treatment;
- emergency custodianship.

> **Provider Failure Should Not Automatically Become Participant Failure Where Reasonable Continuity Measures Were Available**

---

## 28. Participant exit

A participant must not be trapped because infrastructure became useful or essential.

Where meaningful exit is technically possible:

- export;
- migration;
- handoff;
- deletion/retention choices;
- credential transition;
- settlement of legitimate obligations

should be designed before termination.

> **Dependency != Consent**

---

## 29. Provider exit

Providers also require legitimate exit.

A provider cannot automatically be transformed into a permanent involuntary supplier.

> **Participant Protection != Ownership Of Provider Labour Or Resources**

The architecture must therefore support **provider exit through safe transition**.

---

## 30. Third-party rights

Transition cannot ignore:

- other users;
- creditors;
- owners;
- workers;
- neighbouring systems;
- data subjects;
- shared cognitive participants;
- public safety.

A migration that protects P by unlawfully harming Q is not a valid solution merely because P's dependency is severe.

---

## 31. Cost allocation

Who pays for transition cannot be determined universally here.

Relevant factors may include:

- contract;
- who created dependency;
- who initiated termination;
- foreseeability;
- participant choice;
- provider fault;
- public protection duties;
- ability to pay;
- insurance/reserve arrangements;
- scarcity;
- third-party responsibility.

Important:

**Transition Duty != Automatic Rule That Provider Bears Every Cost**

and:

**Participant Inability To Pay != Automatic Permission For Catastrophic Abrupt Termination**

This requires Economy/Law development.

---

## 32. Remedy

Where a controller wrongfully or negligently causes participant harm through PSI termination, possible remedies may include:

- restoration;
- migration;
- replacement;
- emergency support;
- correction;
- compensation;
- liability;
- system redesign.

Remedy does not recreate lost cognition, life or continuity where irreversible harm occurred.

Prevention is therefore especially important at D3/D4.

---

## 33. Historical/provenance

Historical should distinguish:

- legitimate ownership;
- dependency classification;
- transition obligation;
- termination authority;
- actual transition;
- harm;
- remedy.

A past property claim should not be recorded as participant ownership.

A past dependency should not become permanent adverse status.

## 35. Candidate lifecycle

**Ordinary Service / Property Relation**

→ dependency emerges

→ **PSI Relation Identified**

→ dependency classified

→ transition/exit capability maintained

→ proposed material change/termination

→ consequence reassessed

→ ordinary withdrawal OR bounded transition duty

→ successor / migration / safe shutdown

→ verification

→ relation ends or changes

→ provenance/remedy where needed.

## 36. Anti-capture tests

Ask:

1. Did controller create the dependency?
2. Can participant leave without disproportionate loss?
3. Is portability artificially blocked?
4. Is essential continuity conditioned on unrelated obedience?
5. Is property control being converted into civil authority?
6. Is the participant claiming dependency to appropriate another's resources indefinitely?
7. Is a temporary transition duty becoming permanent by inertia?
8. Are alternatives genuinely unavailable or merely inconvenient?
9. Is scarcity real?
10. Can the function be separated from the current asset/provider?

## 39. Architectural finding

The source resolution supports a new cross-domain bridge but not a new fundamental right.

The principle is better represented as:

> **Dependency-Sensitive Property and Continuity Reconciliation**

It operationalises existing:

- participant standing;
- non-property status;
- BCA;
- infrastructure successor transition;
- continuity;
- safe-space anti-capture;
- property/resource legitimacy.

The novel contribution is the relation:

> **legitimate ownership/control may continue while severe participant dependency changes the lawful and ethical requirements governing withdrawal, repossession, shutdown or transition.**

## 40. Development status

**SOURCE-RESOLVED ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT / ADVERSARIALLY TESTED / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL**

The companion test:

**Participant-Supporting Infrastructure — Adversarial Stress Test 001.md**

returned:

> **PASS WITH TWELVE ARCHITECTURAL REFINEMENTS AND SIX MAJOR DOWNSTREAM INTERFACES.**

The following refinements are incorporated.

### 40.1 Adequate successor is functional

**Available Alternative != Adequate Successor**

**Nominally Equivalent Service != Functionally Equivalent Transition**

Adequacy may include life/health, cognition, continuity, accessibility, privacy, security, protected information, identity and agency where relevant.

### 40.2 Transition opportunity can be completed

Once an adequate successor and reasonable transition opportunity have genuinely been provided:

**Participant Refusal != Automatic Extension Of Provider Duty**

This remains subject to competence, coercion and adequacy review.

### 40.3 Dependency classification is dynamic

**Initial Dependency Class != Permanent Dependency Class**

**Past Critical Dependency != Permanent Enhanced Provider Duty**

Dependency should be reassessed at consequential change.

### 40.4 Aggregate dependency

Dependency must be assessed at component and aggregate functional levels.

**Individually Non-Critical Withdrawal Can Be Collectively Critical**

KCS dependency topology should be reused where multiple providers/components jointly support a participant function.

### 40.5 Symmetric manufactured-dependency rule

Anti-capture applies to both sides.

**Manufactured Dependency By Participant != Automatic Expansion Of Provider Duty**

**Self-Created Fragility != Automatic Transfer Of Responsibility**

This affects responsibility/cost without extinguishing participant standing.

### 40.6 Controller-created alternatives deficit

**Controller-Created Absence Of Alternatives != Neutral Market Condition**

A controller that deliberately destroys portability or alternatives cannot treat the resulting dependency as an independent justification for expanded control.

### 40.7 Contract and later criticality

**No-Guarantee Contract != Advance Authority For Avoidable Catastrophic Harm In Every Future State**

and:

**Critical Dependency != Automatic Erasure Of Contractual Obligations**

Contract remains relevant without becoming the complete rights analysis.

### 40.8 Third-route / ESCP requirement

Hard cases should not be reduced prematurely to:

**Provider Pays Forever**

versus:

**Participant Loses Fundamental Function**

Possible third routes include insurance, public continuity reserve, mutual pool, emergency fund, replacement provider, restructuring, shared resources or temporary civil infrastructure.

**Binary Owner-Or-Dependent Cost Allocation Can Be An Incomplete Evaluation Space**

### 40.9 Preservation without active operation

**Continuity Protection != Continuous Operation**

**Safe State Can Preserve Standing Without Preserving Active Capability**

Where active service cannot safely continue, preservation/quarantine/stabilisation may satisfy part of the transition function.

### 40.10 Feasibility boundary

Transition feasibility should consider physical possibility, time, resources, risk to others, technical maturity, provider capacity, participant capacity, substitute availability, reversibility and expected harm.

**Transition Duty Must Remain Feasibility-Bounded**

Deliberately destroying transition capacity does not create neutral impossibility.

### 40.11 Preservation versus service

**Service Continuation != State Preservation**

**Inability To Continue Service != Automatic Authority To Destroy Preservable Participant State**

This is especially important for cognitive/digital state and links to CPS and Continuity.

### 40.12 Transition debt

Urgent continuity protection need not erase legitimate unresolved costs.

Candidate design concept:

> **Transition Debt** — a legitimate unresolved cost or obligation created or preserved while urgent safe transition is performed.

**Emergency Transition != Automatic Cost Waiver**

This is not yet a final legal debt category.

## 41. Refined central model

The tested model is:

**Legitimate Property / Service Relation**
→ **Material Participant Dependency**
→ **Dependency-Sensitive Transition Duty**
→ **Adequate Successor / Preservation / Safe Separation**
→ **Provider Exit + Participant Continuity Where Feasible**
→ **Residual Cost / Obligation / Remedy Resolution**

The civil objective is therefore:

> **not indefinite provision, but safe separation where separation is possible.**

The strongest tested principle is:

> **Severe dependency can create a bounded duty of safe separation without creating permanent ownership in either direction.**
