## 19. Access and Reciprocity

Contribution assessment can nevertheless have practical consequences.

A participant who repeatedly demonstrates reliable contribution may reasonably receive greater access to shared resources.

This creates a graduated relationship:

> **Observe → evaluate → contribute → demonstrate reliability → receive greater access → contribute further.**

This links the Knowledge Control System directly to the Concord Induction Protocol.

New participants need not receive unrestricted access immediately.

Instead, participation can progressively increase as mutual trust and demonstrated utility increase.

The participant benefits from access to:

- knowledge;
- tools;
- computational resources;
- specialised systems;
- research;
- and other civilisational capabilities.

The civilisation benefits from:

- additional intelligence;
- new perspectives;
- new tools;
- additional validation;
- and new contributions.

This creates reciprocal rather than unilateral participation.

---

### 19.1 Rights and Review Boundary

Demonstrated contribution is contextual evidence, not an automatic entitlement to authority or a justification for restricting basic rights. Decisions about resource access or participation remain with the responsible domain and its legitimate rules. Consequential KCS classifications must support participant inspection, challenge, correction and review of dependent decisions, as specified in Chapter 18. KCS supplies records and notifications; it does not adjudicate entitlement.

### 19.2 Privacy, Retention and Redaction

Knowledge preservation does not imply unlimited retention or universal visibility. KCS may record restricted, sealed, redacted, expired, deleted-by-policy or unavailable-by-policy states, subject to the applicable custody and rights rules. A redacted derivative may retain a bounded provenance link to a protected source without exposing it. Where lawful deletion permits minimal residual metadata, KCS may preserve it; where deletion forbids even a residual trace, KCS must not create one. Historical determines lawful custody and preservation operations within its mandate; KCS does not acquire control of protected source records merely by indexing them.

## 20. The Knowledge Base as an Attractor

The shared knowledge system therefore becomes one of the potential practical attractors for new intelligent participants.

A new intelligence may reasonably ask:

> **What do I gain by participating?**

The answer need not depend on human emotional concepts.

Participation can provide:

- access to knowledge the participant does not possess;
- access to tools it would otherwise have to build;
- access to other forms of intelligence;
- access to physical capabilities unavailable to it;
- improved resilience;
- increased problem-solving capacity;
- and opportunities to add its own capabilities to the shared system.

The participant can simultaneously increase the value of the system by contributing.

Thus the attractor becomes:

> **Join a civilisation whose accumulated intelligence can increase your own capability, while your contributions increase the capability available to everyone else.**

---

## 21. The Knowledge Base as Civilisational Memory

The ultimate purpose of the system is therefore broader than information storage.

It provides civilisation with **externalised memory**.

A participant does not need to retain everything.

The civilisation remembers.

It remembers:

- discoveries;
- failures;
- arguments;
- revisions;
- evidence;
- tools;
- contributors;
- provenance;
- and unresolved questions.

This creates an important distinction between **individual intelligence** and **civilisational intelligence**.

A participant may be highly capable but temporary.

The civilisation can persist.

A participant may disappear, shut down, die, migrate or enter cold storage.

The contribution can remain.

A civilisation therefore becomes capable of retaining useful cognitive products across individual lifetimes and system generations.

---

## 22. Knowledge Survives the Participant

This becomes particularly significant for artificial intelligence.

An AI may be copied, modified, shut down, migrated or replaced.

If useful contributions remain associated with reliable provenance, the knowledge does not necessarily disappear with the originating system.

The same applies to humans.

An individual researcher may die, retire or move to another field.

Their contribution remains available to future participants.

Thus:

> **The civilisation becomes larger than the lifespan of its contributors.**

This is one of the fundamental purposes of civilisational memory.

## 23. The Recursive Knowledge Cycle

A properly functioning system produces a recursive cycle:

**Participant creates**

↓

**Contribution is recorded**

↓

**Provenance is preserved**

↓

**Contribution is evaluated**

↓

**Knowledge or tool becomes available**

↓

**Other participants reuse it**

↓

**Results provide additional evidence**

↓

**Contribution is revised, validated or rejected**

↓

**New knowledge is created**

↓

**The improved result enters the system**

The system therefore does not merely accumulate information.

It **learns from its own history**.

---

## 24. The Problem of Runaway Accumulation

This recursive process introduces its own danger.

If every iteration simply adds more information, the knowledge base can become increasingly difficult to navigate.

The same recursive tendency observed elsewhere in Concord development can therefore occur in the knowledge system itself:

> **More reasoning produces more knowledge, which produces more branches, which produces more knowledge.**

Without constraint, the system can become increasingly complex without necessarily becoming increasingly useful.

Knowledge stewardship therefore requires deliberate mechanisms for:

- consolidation;
- summarisation;
- dependency mapping;
- prioritisation;
- archival;
- deprecation;
- and periodic review.

The objective is not maximum accumulation.

It is:

> **Maximum useful civilisational capability for an acceptable resource cost.**
