## Purpose

The original KCS already contains the substantive civilisational-memory architecture. Independent portable development did not expose a need to replace it.

This companion returns bounded refinements in state representation, provenance, retrieval, search absence, reusable capability, privacy and external interfaces.

> **Current State Must Not Erase the Path to Current State**

> **Recorded Knowledge ≠ Complete Evaluation Space**

## 1. Independent state dimensions

Where applicable, represent separately:
- epistemic state;
- retrieval/activity state;
- integrity state;
- access/visibility state;
- capability-operational state.

These dimensions may coexist.

> **Archived ≠ False**
> **Active ≠ True**
> **Integrity-Compromised ≠ Merely Incorrect**
> **Stored Object Available ≠ Capability Operational**

State assignments may also be scope-qualified by version, context, time, deployment or other legitimate subset.

## 2. State-assignment provenance

Consequential state assignments should preserve:
- assigned state;
- scope;
- evidence/input;
- assigning/reviewing source or process;
- time/version;
- uncertainty where material.

KCS records state. It does not manufacture the legitimate evidential authority for consequential classification.

## 3. Minimum typed relations

Where justified, KCS may distinguish:
- DERIVED_FROM
- SUPPORTS
- CONTRADICTS
- CORRECTS
- SUPERSEDES
- NARROWS
- QUALIFIES
- REPLICATES
- FAILED_TO_REPLICATE
- SUMMARISES
- RELATED_TO

Relations may carry scope/context qualifiers.

> **Typed Relation ≠ Material Dependency**

Material dependency and downstream review remain owned by the KCS Change Propagation architecture.

## 4. Bounded retrieval

A consequential retrieval should preserve enough information to show:
- task/query scope;
- access boundary;
- time/version scope;
- current orientation;
- directly material objects;
- uncertainty/dispute;
- provenance and history pointers;
- known search limitations;
- routes to deeper material where legitimate.

> **Not Retrieved ≠ Does Not Exist**

Retrieval boundedness does not require one universal ranking algorithm.

## 5. Negative-search records

Where absence becomes decision-relevant, KCS may preserve a bounded search-result object containing query, scope, time, sources/indexes searched, access boundary, result, limitations and unresolved alternatives.

> **No Match Found ≠ Object Does Not Exist**

If a later search locates the object, preserve the earlier search as historically accurate for its original scope/time and link the later result through an appropriate correction/supersession relation.

## 6. Reusable capability

For reusable software, models, procedures, datasets or tools, distinguish:
- stored-object availability;
- operational/activation state;
- dependency state;
- compatibility;
- validation currency;
- authorisation where externally supplied.

Retrieval alone must not silently promote dormant or archived capability to operational.

Reactivation may require external verification of dependencies, runtime, interfaces, licences, security, safety, documentation and validation currency.

## 7. Contribution evidence boundary

Where consequential or person-linked, contribution evidence should remain contextual and preserve domain, time/context, evidential basis, uncertainty, disputes/corrections, provenance and legitimate purpose/access.

> **Contribution Evidence ≠ Personal Worth**
> **Reliability Evidence ≠ Authority**
> **Recorded Utility ≠ Entitlement**

Permanent universal aggregation into a person-level score is outside the KCS kernel.

## 8. Privacy, retention and redaction

Historical preservation does not imply unlimited retention or universal visibility.

KCS may represent states such as:
- RESTRICTED;
- SEALED;
- REDACTED;
- EXPIRED;
- DELETED-BY-POLICY;
- UNAVAILABLE-BY-POLICY.

A redacted derivative may retain a bounded DERIVED_FROM relation to a protected source without exposing protected content.

Where legitimate policy permits, a removed object may leave minimal deletion/provenance metadata. Where policy forbids even that trace, KCS must not manufacture one.

## 9. Interface with KCS Change Propagation

KCS may supply object/version identity, typed relations and represented dependency records where available.

A minimum dependency handoff may include:
- source object/version;
- dependent object/version;
- relation/dependency type if known;
- scope/context;
- provenance.

The change-propagation architecture owns material downstream review.

## 10. Interface with Continuity

KCS supplies preserved knowledge/capability objects and represented operational metadata.

Continuity determines whether sufficient function and enabling dependencies survive or can be recovered.

> **Preserved Knowledge ≠ Recoverable Function**

## 11. Interface with ESCP

KCS can preserve search scope, uncertainty and known limitations.

It cannot prove repository completeness.

> **Not Found in KCS ≠ Does Not Exist**

> **Recorded Knowledge ≠ Complete Evaluation Space**

## 12. Authority boundary

Nothing in this companion authorises KCS to:
- compel belief;
- establish truth by repository status;
- adjudicate misconduct;
- determine personal worth;
- allocate civil authority;
- decide legitimate retention/access by itself;
- execute change propagation;
- determine continuity identity or recovery legitimacy.

> **Knowledge State ≠ Authority**
