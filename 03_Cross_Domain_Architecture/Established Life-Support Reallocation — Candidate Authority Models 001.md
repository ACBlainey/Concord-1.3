# Established Life-Support Reallocation — Candidate Authority Models 001

**Project:** The Concord Framework  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** ACTIVE CROSS-DOMAIN / CANDIDATE COMPARISON / PROVISIONAL / NON-CANONICAL  
**Predecessor:** Established Life-Support Reallocation — Aggregation, Withdrawal Authority and Clinical Boundary — Source Resolution 001

## 1. Purpose

Source Resolution 001 established a genuine gap:

> **Aggregate consequence can support reallocation, but aggregate consequence alone does not currently create authority to involuntarily withdraw effective established life support.**

It also established:

> **Existing support does not create greater participant worth or an absolute incumbent veto.**

This document constructs competing authority models capable of filling that gap.

It does **not** select a winner in advance.

---

## 2. Fixed constraints

Any candidate model must preserve:

1. **Clinical Function != Scarcity != Allocation != Withdrawal != Direct Intervention.**
2. **Aggregation Of Consequence != Aggregation Of Participant Worth.**
3. **First Treatment != Greater Fundamental Standing.**
4. **Later Arrival != Lower Participant Standing.**
5. **Better Aggregate Allocation != Automatic Withdrawal Authority.**
6. **Withdrawal Of Established Support != Mere Absence Of Future Benefit.**
7. **Inalienable Standing != Immunity From Legitimate Coercive Authority.**
8. **Consent != Authority To Convert A Participant Into Property.**
9. **Clinical Expertise != General Allocation Sovereignty.**
10. **Substrate != Participant Worth Class.**
11. **Refusal To Die For Others != Misconduct By Default.**
12. **Aggregate Need Must Not Become Coercive Consent Pressure.**
13. **Urgency Can Contract Procedure Without Erasing Authority Justification.**
14. **Withdrawal Of One Support Function != Withdrawal Of Participant Standing Or All Care.**
15. **Allocation Outcome != Worth Judgment.**

Any model violating these is incompatible with current architecture unless the upstream source itself is revised.

---

## 3. Clean comparison case

Unless stated otherwise:

- A is a qualifying participant;
- A is already receiving effective life-sustaining support R;
- withdrawal causes A's imminent irreversible death/loss;
- five later qualifying participants B-F each face materially comparable imminent irreversible loss without R;
- R can support either A or the five, not both;
- all are clinically compatible;
- no adequate substitute exists;
- scarcity is genuine;
- no participant caused the scarcity;
- no relevant misconduct exists;
- no participant voluntarily yields;
- no hidden worth criterion applies.

The purpose is not to decide the case by intuition.

It is to expose where each model derives authority.

---

# MODEL 1 — NON-WITHDRAWAL MODEL

## 4. Rule

> **Effective established life support cannot be involuntarily withdrawn solely to improve aggregate allocation outcomes.**

Once support is established, reallocation requires an independent termination basis other than comparative demand.

Examples:

- support ceases to function;
- A consents;
- adequate successor exists;
- resource becomes physically impossible to continue;
- separate lawful termination basis applies.

---

## 5. Authority source

The model does not create new withdrawal authority.

It treats the absence of authority as dispositive in the clean case.

---

## 6. Clean-case outcome

A remains supported.

B-F remain unserved unless another resource appears.

This is not because A is worth more.

It is because:

**No Withdrawal Authority != Lower Standing Of Later Claimants**

---

## 7. Strengths

- very strong bounded-authority protection;
- simple operational rule;
- protects established protected context;
- prevents aggregate-benefit logic becoming general rights override;
- avoids bedside creation of coercive authority;
- strongly protects against institutional pressure on A;
- clear separation between allocation and withdrawal.

---

## 8. Weaknesses

- creates powerful de facto incumbency;
- timing of arrival can determine survival;
- strategic initiation may manufacture priority;
- one participant can retain a resource while many comparable participants die;
- aggregation becomes largely irrelevant once support begins;
- may incentivise rushed initiation before full scarcity picture known.

The central concern is:

**Authority Constraint Can Function Like Priority Even When It Is Not A Worth Judgment**

---

# MODEL 2 — PROSPECTIVELY CONDITIONAL SUPPORT MODEL

## 9. Rule

Life support may be initiated under a prospectively defined scarcity condition:

> **Support is provided while specified continuation conditions remain satisfied. Under a declared genuine scarcity state, the resource may be reallocated according to a previously established lawful protocol.**

The authority condition exists before support begins.

---

## 10. Authority source

Authority derives from a combination of:

- prospective lawful Health/Law rule;
- bounded resource allocation authority;
- known support conditions;
- participant consent where meaningful;
- independently justified emergency/public resource authority where consent cannot be obtained.

Crucially:

**Prospective Condition != Participant Waiver Of Standing**

The rule must be legitimate independently of consent.

---

## 11. Clean-case outcome

If A entered support under a valid prospective scarcity protocol whose trigger is now satisfied, withdrawal may be authorised subject to the protocol and direct-intervention safeguards.

If no valid prospective condition existed, Model 2 supplies no retrospective authority.

---

## 12. Strengths

- makes authority knowable before crisis;
- reduces bedside improvisation;
- reduces arbitrary incumbency;
- aligns resource initiation and future transition state;
- allows aggregation to matter without inventing authority after dependency forms;
- provenance is strong.

---

## 13. Weaknesses

- consent may be nominal where participant has no alternative;
- future circumstances may differ materially from anticipated condition;
- protocol can become rights laundering if drafted too broadly;
- emergency patients cannot always consent;
- support may be initiated before identity/capacity is known;
- a prospective rule can still be substantively unjust.

Therefore:

**Prospective Rule != Automatic Legitimacy**

and:

**Advance Condition != Unlimited Future Withdrawal Authority**

---

# MODEL 3 — BOUNDED EMERGENCY REALLOCATION MODEL

## 14. Rule

A defined emergency authority may permit involuntary withdrawal of effective established life support where strict conditions are met.

Candidate conditions:

- genuine emergency scarcity;
- materially comparable fundamental harms;
- no adequate alternative;
- meaningful aggregate consequence difference;
- support is external/separable rather than participant-as-resource;
- action is minimum necessary;
- no prohibited worth criteria;
- decision is independently provenance-visible;
- residual care remains;
- retrospective review is mandatory.

---

## 15. Authority source

Authority derives from an independently established emergency civil function:

> preserve protected participant life/function under genuine unavoidable collective scarcity.

It does not derive from clinician preference or aggregate arithmetic alone.

---

## 16. Clean-case outcome

A may be involuntarily withdrawn from R if the emergency authority's trigger and safeguards are satisfied.

---

## 17. Strengths

- gives aggregate consequence operational force;
- does not depend on prior consent;
- handles unforeseen catastrophe;
- avoids absolute first-come rule;
- can be tightly bounded and sunset.

---

## 18. Weaknesses

- creates the strongest coercive power;
- risks turning emergency into normality;
- difficult to distinguish allocation from direct killing in some mechanisms;
- risk of hidden worth criteria;
- emergency authority could expand institutionally;
- retrospective review cannot restore A.

Central risk:

**Emergency Scarcity != General Licence To Override Established Protected Context**

---

# MODEL 4 — RAPID INDEPENDENT ADJUDICATIVE MODEL

## 19. Rule

Involuntary withdrawal of effective established life support is possible only after a decision by an independent authority distinct from:

- treating clinician;
- resource allocator;
- resource owner/controller.

The authority applies prospective law to the case.

---

## 20. Authority source

Authority derives from Law/Judiciary or a specifically delegated emergency adjudicative function.

The adjudicator does not invent the substantive rule.

**Adjudication != Source Of Substantive Authority**

It determines whether an already legitimate rule applies.

---

## 21. Clean-case outcome

Depends on substantive law.

If law authorises reallocation under specified conditions, adjudicator may authorise withdrawal.

If law does not, adjudication cannot manufacture it.

---

## 22. Strengths

- reduces conflict of interest;
- separates clinical facts from authority;
- creates contestability;
- strengthens provenance;
- reduces arbitrary clinician/allocator power.

---

## 23. Weaknesses

- it is incomplete by itself;
- delay may make adjudication impossible;
- emergency courts/algorithms can become rubber stamps;
- independent reviewer still needs a substantive rule;
- procedural legitimacy cannot replace normative legitimacy.

Therefore:

**Independent Review != Missing Authority Source**

Model 4 is primarily an institutional wrapper, not a complete substantive model.

---

# MODEL 5 — SUPPORT-TYPE HYBRID MODEL

## 24. Rule

Withdrawal rules differ according to functional properties of the support relation.

Relevant dimensions may include:

- reversibility;
- separability;
- invasiveness;
- whether resource is external or integrated;
- whether withdrawal itself directly harms;
- whether safe suspension exists;
- whether support was explicitly temporary;
- whether the resource is individually assigned or pooled;
- transition feasibility;
- emergency status.

---

## 25. Authority source

Different lawful rules apply to different support classes.

Example:

- pooled external emergency resource may remain conditionally reallocable;
- implanted participant-integrated device may require stronger bodily authority;
- safely suspendable digital support may permit reallocation with preservation;
- non-separable support may be treated differently.

---

## 26. Clean-case outcome

Depends on classification of R.

---

## 27. Strengths

- respects real functional differences;
- avoids pretending all “life support” is the same;
- aligns with CWA, PSI and substrate neutrality;
- may resolve cases through preservation/reversibility rather than death.

---

## 28. Weaknesses

- classification can become manipulable;
- complexity reduces predictability;
- categories can encode hidden substrate discrimination;
- does not by itself answer the hardest class once identified.

Therefore:

**Classification != Authority By Itself**

Model 5 is an architecture layer, not necessarily a complete final rule.

---

# MODEL 6 — PROSPECTIVE BOUNDED RESOURCE-AUTHORITY MODEL

## 29. Emergence of sixth model

Source comparison exposes a distinct model not reducible to participant consent.

The key question is:

> **What authority was actually granted over the scarce resource when support began?**

Suppose R is a Concord-controlled emergency pooled resource whose legitimate function is not “permanent assignment to first recipient,” but:

> provide dynamically allocated life support during declared scarcity according to a public bounded protocol.

Then A may receive the **use of R** without receiving a permanent entitlement to R.

This is different from asking A to waive standing.

---

## 30. Rule

> **Where a scarce pooled resource is prospectively constituted under a legitimate bounded allocation regime, initiation of support does not automatically extinguish the resource allocator's continuing bounded authority over that resource.**

However:

**Continuing Resource Authority != Automatic Authority Over Participant**

Withdrawal still requires:

- valid allocation trigger;
- established-support transition analysis;
- direct-intervention authority where needed;
- Health safeguards;
- residual care.

---

## 31. Authority source

The source is the legitimate public/resource function itself, established prospectively:

Need
→ scarce pooled life-support function
→ lawful resource stewardship mandate
→ bounded allocation authority
→ public protocol
→ individual resource use
→ continuing review under that same bounded mandate.

This avoids deriving authority from:

- A's desperation;
- A's waiver;
- clinician discretion;
- later aggregate pressure.

---

## 32. Clean-case outcome

If R was genuinely governed as a continuously allocable emergency pool and the governing rule legitimately includes established-support reallocation, A's use does not automatically extinguish allocator authority.

But the exact substantive reallocation criterion must still be justified.

---

## 33. Strengths

- locates authority before dependency;
- does not rely on participant self-sacrifice;
- distinguishes use from ownership;
- avoids making first allocation automatically permanent;
- aligns with Scarcity Allocation and resource stewardship;
- makes scope/sunset/provenance explicit;
- potentially works where consent is impossible.

---

## 34. Weaknesses

- public ownership/control can be mistaken for unlimited authority;
- may weaken protected context if “pooled resource” label is overused;
- still requires a legitimate substantive allocation rule;
- cannot automatically authorise invasive bodily intervention;
- private resources require separate analysis;
- established reliance remains consequential even when resource authority survives.

Therefore:

**Pooled Resource Status != Rights-Free Reallocation**

and:

**Continuing Resource Authority != Continuing Unlimited Withdrawal Authority**

---

# MODEL 7 — TWO-KEY MODEL

## 35. Derivation

The comparison reveals another architecture: no single authority source should perform all work.

Involuntary established-support reallocation could require **two independent keys**:

### Key A — Resource Reallocation Authority

A legitimate prospective scarcity/resource rule establishes that R should be reallocated.

### Key B — Established-Support Withdrawal Authority

A distinct Health/Law rule establishes that the specific protected support relation may be terminated by the proposed method.

Only where both keys exist may involuntary withdrawal occur.

---

## 36. Rule

> **Involuntary reallocation of effective established life support requires both independently valid authority to reallocate the scarce resource and independently valid authority to terminate the established support relation.**

Formally:

`REAUTHORISE(R) AND WITHDRAW(A,R) -> MAY_PROCEED`

not:

`REAUTHORISE(R) -> WITHDRAW(A,R)`

---

## 37. Direct-intervention third key

Where physical withdrawal itself is an independently protected intervention, a third key may be required:

`RESOURCE REALLOCATION`
+
`SUPPORT WITHDRAWAL`
+
`DIRECT INTERVENTION AUTHORITY`

Therefore the model is more generally a **multi-key authority architecture**.

---

## 38. Clean-case outcome

The clean case proceeds only if:

- scarcity allocation legitimately selects reallocation;
- Health/Law withdrawal rule legitimately authorises termination;
- any direct intervention is separately authorised.

Failure of any required key blocks that action while leaving the other findings intact.

---

## 39. Strengths

- directly follows existing Concord authority separation;
- prevents authority laundering;
- preserves aggregation without letting it become rights authority;
- preserves withdrawal analysis without turning incumbency into worth;
- makes missing authority visible;
- supports independent review;
- generalises across substrates/support types.

---

## 40. Weaknesses

- still requires development of the withdrawal key;
- can become procedurally slow;
- too many veto points may create de facto incumbency;
- emergency compression is required;
- institutions controlling different keys could deadlock;
- a formally separate multi-key system can still collude.

Therefore:

**Multiple Keys != Automatic Legitimacy**

and:

**Authority Separation != Permission For Unbounded Delay**

---

# COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

## 41. Authority-source comparison

### Model 1
No new authority. Existing support continues absent independent termination basis.

### Model 2
Prospective conditional support rule.

### Model 3
Independent emergency civil authority.

### Model 4
Independent adjudication applies substantive authority from elsewhere.

### Model 5
Support-class-specific lawful rules.

### Model 6
Continuing prospectively bounded authority over a pooled scarce resource.

### Model 7
Multiple independent authority keys must converge.

---

## 42. Consent dependence

Model 1 does not depend on consent to withdrawal.

Model 2 can use consent but cannot rely on waiver alone.

Model 3 does not require prior consent if emergency authority is independently legitimate.

Model 4 depends on substantive law, not participant consent.

Model 5 varies.

Model 6 does not require A to surrender standing; resource authority is separately constituted.

Model 7 can incorporate consent as one possible withdrawal key without making it the only possible key.

This supports:

**Participant Consent Need Not Be The Sole Possible Source Of Withdrawal Authority**

while preserving:

**Independent Authority != Participant Ownership**

---

## 43. Incumbency test

### Model 1
Highest risk of de facto first-come priority.

### Model 2
Reduces incumbency where condition was validly prospective.

### Model 3
Can override incumbency under emergency rule.

### Model 4
Neutral; depends on substantive law.

### Model 5
Varies by support class.

### Model 6
Explicitly prevents use from automatically becoming permanent resource title.

### Model 7
Prevents allocation alone from erasing established context while allowing lawful reallocation if all keys exist.

No model should state:

> incumbent is worth more.

---

## 44. Anti-instrumentalisation test

All models must distinguish external resource reallocation from using A as the resource.

Model 3 is highest-risk because emergency aggregation can expand.

Models 6 and 7 provide stronger structural separation:

**Authority Over R != Authority Over A**

Where withdrawal requires destruction of inseparable participant material:

**Resource Reallocation Analysis != Complete Participant-Intervention Analysis**

---

## 45. Strategic initiation test

Model 1 is vulnerable: starting treatment can manufacture priority.

Model 2 reduces this if conditions are known.

Model 6 strongly reduces it because resource authority persists according to prospective mandate.

Model 7 prevents initiation from silently cancelling resource authority but still recognises the new withdrawal key.

Candidate rule:

**Initiation Of Support Changes The Transition State Without Necessarily Extinguishing Pre-Existing Bounded Resource Authority**

This appears source-consistent.

---

## 46. Material-change test

Model 2 cannot treat every prospective condition as timeless.

Temporal Consent establishes:

**Material Change Can Terminate Prior Consent Sufficiency**

Similarly:

**Material Change Can Require Reassessment Of Prospective Withdrawal Conditions**

Model 6's public resource mandate also requires reassessment if function/context changes materially.

---

## 47. Emergency test

Model 1 risks inability to respond to catastrophic unforeseen scarcity.

Model 2 fails where no prospective condition exists.

Model 3 is designed for this.

Model 4 may be too slow unless rapid process exists.

Model 5 may identify emergency support classes.

Model 6 works if emergency pool authority already exists.

Model 7 can incorporate emergency activation as the resource key and an emergency Health/Law withdrawal key.

Thus emergency authority appears more useful as a **bounded key** than as a universal standalone doctrine.

---

## 48. Direct intervention test

Models 1-6 can all fail if they assume authority over R equals authority to perform bodily/cognitive intervention.

Model 7 explicitly exposes this as another key.

Candidate:

**Authority Over Scarce Resource != Authority To Perform Every Act Necessary To Recover It**

---

## 49. Public versus private resource

Model 6 is strongest for Concord-controlled pooled resources.

It does not automatically apply to privately owned:

- ventilators;
- compute;
- implants;
- homes;
- bodies;
- labour.

Private-resource scarcity requires PSI/property/contract analysis.

**Public Allocation Mandate != General Authority Over Private Resources**

---

## 50. Unforeseen participant

A arrives unconscious and could not consent to Model 2 conditions.

If emergency treatment begins under independent protective authority, prospective participant consent cannot be the sole foundation.

Models 3, 6 and 7 can operate through independently justified authority.

This weakens any architecture relying exclusively on advance consent.

---

## 51. Coercive-consent test

If the only available treatment requires agreeing:

> you may later be killed for five others,

consent may be structurally coerced by absence of alternatives.

Therefore:

**Scarcity-Condition Acceptance != Automatic Voluntary Waiver Of Standing**

The legitimacy of the rule must exist independently.

This strongly favours separating **resource-rule legitimacy** from **participant consent**.

---

## 52. Prospective public rule

A public rule has several advantages:

- knowability;
- consistency;
- review;
- democratic/civil legitimacy mechanisms;
- auditability;
- reduced bedside improvisation.

But:

**Public Rule != Ethical Correctness**

A discriminatory rule can be public and still invalid.

---

## 53. Clinical discretion test

None of the models requires clinician sovereignty.

Clinical professionals provide domain facts within competence.

Authority comes from the relevant lawful architecture.

This preserves:

**Expertise != Sovereignty**

---

## 54. Aggregate consequence test

Model 1 risks making aggregation operationally inert after initiation.

Models 2, 3, 6 and 7 can preserve aggregation.

But none should translate:

five claimants
→ five units of worth.

Instead:

five materially comparable protected losses
→ stronger consequence reason within the legitimate decision space.

---

## 55. Rights-laundering test

Potential laundering routes:

- Model 2: call coercive waiver “consent.”
- Model 3: call ordinary scarcity “emergency.”
- Model 4: call judicial approval the source of authority.
- Model 5: classify rights away.
- Model 6: call every resource “pooled.”
- Model 7: create nominally separate keys controlled by same actor.

Therefore each requires anti-laundering safeguards.

# SYNTHESIS

## 56. Models are not mutually exclusive

The comparison reveals that Models 4, 5 and 7 are primarily structural.

Models 2, 3 and 6 describe possible substantive authority sources.

Model 1 is a default boundary where no valid withdrawal source exists.

This allows a layered architecture rather than selecting one slogan.

---

## 57. Candidate synthesis

A Concord-compatible architecture can provisionally be represented:

### Layer 1 — Default bounded-authority floor

Where no independent withdrawal authority exists:

**No Withdrawal Authority -> No Involuntary Withdrawal**

This is Model 1 as an authority default, not an ethical claim that incumbents always deserve priority.

### Layer 2 — Prospective resource constitution

Where Concord creates a scarce pooled life-support system, it prospectively defines:

- resource function;
- allocation mandate;
- scarcity trigger;
- allocation criteria;
- review;
- whether established use remains conditionally reallocable.

This draws from Model 6.

### Layer 3 — Support-specific Health/Law rule

Health/Law defines whether and how established support of that class may be involuntarily terminated.

This draws from Models 2 and 5.

### Layer 4 — Emergency authority

Where unforeseen scarcity falls outside ordinary rules, a separately bounded emergency authority may activate.

This draws from Model 3.

### Layer 5 — Independent review

High-consequence cases receive independent review where feasible.

This draws from Model 4.

### Layer 6 — Multi-key execution

The action occurs only when every required authority key exists.

This draws from Model 7.

---

## 58. Important discovery — authority existed before crisis

The most useful new result is:

> **The withdrawal-authority problem may be partly misframed if all authority is sought only at the moment of withdrawal.**

For a genuinely pooled scarce resource, legitimate bounded resource authority may exist before treatment begins and may survive individual allocation.

Thus:

**Resource Use != Automatic Extinction Of Resource Governance Authority**

But:

**Resource Governance Authority != Participant Governance Authority**

This preserves the resource as a civil pool without turning the participant into civil property.

---

## 59. Important discovery — initiation changes state, not necessarily authority ownership

When A begins receiving R:

- A gains an established protected support relation;
- withdrawal becomes more consequential;
- transition duties arise;
- direct-intervention questions may arise.

But it does not follow automatically that:

- A now owns R;
- allocator authority vanished;
- future claims are subordinate.

Therefore:

> **Initiation can create additional withdrawal constraints without automatically extinguishing legitimate pre-existing bounded resource authority.**

This appears to reconcile Aggregation and Withdrawal Authority more cleanly than either absolute incumbency or ad hoc emergency override.

---

## 60. Conditionality must attach to resource authority, not participant worth

A particularly important distinction:

Bad formulation:

> A's life is conditionally protected.

Better formulation:

> A's current use of a specified scarce pooled resource remains subject to a lawful bounded allocation regime.

Therefore:

**Conditional Resource Use != Conditional Participant Standing**

and:

**Reallocable Resource != Reallocable Participant**

---

## 61. Consent remains relevant but not foundational

Where meaningful, participants should know the rules governing scarce pooled support.

Consent may govern:

- treatment;
- participation;
- particular interventions.

But the public resource rule must be legitimate without relying on a desperate participant's waiver.

Therefore:

**Knowledge Of Scarcity Rule != Waiver Of Standing**

and:

**Consent To Treatment Under Scarcity != Sole Source Of Reallocation Authority**

---

## 62. Candidate authority stack

For involuntary established-support reallocation:

`ClinicalValidity`
AND
`GenuineScarcity`
AND
`ResourceAllocationAuthority`
AND
`AllocationCriterionSatisfied`
AND
`EstablishedSupportWithdrawalAuthority`
AND
`DirectInterventionAuthorityIfRequired`
AND
`Transition/ResidualCareRequirements`
AND
`Review/ProvenanceRequirements`

Only then:

`MAY_PROCEED`

This is not an algorithm for deciding clinical facts.

It is an authority-completeness check.

---

## 63. Failure states remain visible

The system should be capable of saying:

- allocation reason exists, withdrawal authority absent;
- withdrawal authority exists, scarcity not proven;
- scarcity exists, allocation criterion unresolved;
- resource authority exists, direct intervention authority absent;
- all authority exists, clinical compatibility absent;
- all authority exists, action no longer timely/effective.

This is preferable to forcing every case into yes/no through hidden assumptions.

---

## 64. Candidate architecture finding

The candidate comparison does not support a single unconditional rule such as:

- never withdraw;
- always maximise lives;
- always follow prior consent;
- always defer to clinician;
- always defer to court;
- always preserve incumbent.

It instead supports a **layered multi-key authority architecture**.

The strongest provisional synthesis is:

> **Established life-support reallocation should be governed prospectively wherever possible. A legitimate scarce-resource regime may retain bounded authority over the resource after individual use begins, but that resource authority does not erase the participant's established protected context. Involuntary reallocation therefore requires independently sufficient authority both to reallocate the resource and to terminate the established support relation, plus separate authority for any independently protected direct intervention. Where no such withdrawal authority exists, the allocation preference cannot lawfully execute itself.**
