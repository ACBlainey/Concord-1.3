# ESCP Operational Companion — Bounded Completeness Challenge v1.0 Candidate

**Project:** Foundations of the Concord  
**Date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** PORTABLE COMPANION CANDIDATE / LEVEL B EXTRACTABLE / NOT YET GRADUATED / NON-CANONICAL  
**Parent model:** Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem (ESCP)  
**Parent status:** Frozen Independent Derivation / Research Hypothesis / Not Canonical  
**Scope:** Substrate-neutral; domain-neutral

## 1. Purpose

This companion operationalises the Evaluation-Space Completeness Problem without altering the frozen ESCP derivation.

The parent ESCP establishes:

> **Accuracy Within Evaluation Space != Completeness Of Evaluation Space**

This companion addresses the practical question:

> **How can an evaluator challenge the completeness of a represented evaluation space, perform proportionate discovery, terminate review without false certainty, and reopen it when materially relevant new information appears?**

It does not replace or revise the parent ESCP model.

## 2. Core boundary

The companion distinguishes:

`EvaluationSufficientForDeclaredScopeAtTimeT`

from:

`RealityComplete`

Therefore:

> **Complete Within Declared Scope != Complete Reality**

and:

> **Bounded Termination != Epistemic Closure**

No output of this companion certifies exhaustive representation of reality.

## 3. Primary completeness challenge

For representation R supporting intended conclusion C:

> **What materially relevant dimension, relation, state, owner, route, condition, interface, failure mode or protected boundary would have to exist, be missing or be unrepresented for C to be wrong or over-scoped?**

A candidate answer is a discovery hypothesis, not an established fact.

> **Missing-Dimension Hypothesis != Established Missing Dimension**

## 4. Input object

A portable implementation may represent:

`ESCPChallenge = <RepresentationRef, IntendedConclusion, DeclaredScope, ConsequenceClass, IrreversibilityClass, KnownMissingRefs, MaterialUnknownRefs, AnomalyRefs, DiscoveryRouteRefs, DiscoveryYieldState, ResidualUncertaintyRefs, ReviewHorizon, ReopeningConditions, Provenance>`

The implementation may use different field names provided the distinctions are preserved.

## 5. Required distinctions

### 5.1 Known represented dimension

A dimension already represented and available to evaluation.

### 5.2 Known missing dimension

A recognised potentially relevant dimension that is not adequately represented or measured.

### 5.3 Unrepresented candidate dimension

A plausible missing distinction suggested by anomaly, observer difference, interface limitation, relationship, failure mode or adversarial challenge.

### 5.4 Materiality

A missing dimension matters operationally only where it can materially affect the intended conclusion, route, authority, consequence or required scope.

> **Missing Dimension != Material Missing Dimension**

### 5.5 Residual uncertainty

Uncertainty remaining after proportionate discovery.

Residual uncertainty must not be silently converted into certainty.

## 6. Bounded discovery ladder

A completeness review may use the following ladder.

### Level 0 — Scope alignment

Ask:

- What exactly is the intended conclusion?
- How broad is the claim?
- What time horizon does it cover?
- What decision depends on it?

A conclusion broader than the represented space should be narrowed or further investigated.

### Level 1 — Known gaps

Ask:

- What relevant dimensions are already known to be missing?
- Are any unresolved?
- Are they material to the conclusion?

### Level 2 — Internal anomalies

Ask:

- What does the current representation fail to explain?
- What observations conflict with the dominant model?
- Has inconvenient evidence been discarded rather than resolved?

### Level 3 — Interface alternatives

Ask:

- Could another observer, sensor, representation, model or interface expose distinctions unavailable to the current evaluator?
- Are current observers genuinely heterogeneous or merely copies of the same evaluation architecture?

### Level 4 — Relationship and state expansion

Ask whether the model omits:

- material relationships;
- temporal states;
- future states;
- dependencies;
- ownership states;
- failure modes;
- transition conditions;
- protected interfaces.

### Level 5 — Adversarial challenge

Ask:

> **What would have to be true for this scoped conclusion to fail?**

Candidate failure conditions become discovery questions where materially plausible.

### Level 6 — Consequence test

Ask:

- What happens if the representation is incomplete?
- How irreversible is the decision?
- Can evidence be preserved?
- Can the action be reversed?
- Can the conclusion be narrowed?
- Can later review correct the result?

The ladder is not mandatory ceremony.

Depth is proportionate to consequence and irreversibility.

## 7. Consequence scaling

Candidate principle:

> **The broader and more irreversible the conclusion or consequential action, the stronger the justification required that the evaluation space contains the dimensions relevant to that conclusion.**

This does not create a universal numerical threshold.

A low-consequence reversible action may proceed under greater residual uncertainty than an irreversible high-consequence action.

> **Reversibility Can Reduce Required Pre-Decision Completeness Without Eliminating Review Duty**

## 8. Discovery routes

Possible discovery routes include:

- additional evidence;
- heterogeneous observers;
- alternative sensors/interfaces;
- alternative models;
- adversarial hypothesis testing;
- dependency inspection;
- relationship mapping;
- temporal/future-state analysis;
- failure-mode analysis;
- source resolution;
- comparison against independent representations.

A discovery route must itself be legitimate.

> **Need To Reduce Uncertainty != Authority To Use Any Discovery Method**

> **Completeness Search != General Search Authority**

Permission, privacy, rights, law, context and authority remain external constraints.

## 9. Homogeneous and heterogeneous review

Additional observers can provide two different benefits.

`Redundancy -> Reliability`

`Difference -> Potential Dimensional Expansion`

Many observers sharing the same sensors, categories, data and model can strengthen reliability within a common space without demonstrating that the space is complete.

> **Consensus Count != Dimensional Coverage**

Heterogeneous observation can expose distinctions absent from one architecture, but disagreement does not automatically establish which observer is correct.

## 10. Discovery yield

Repeated discovery that produces no new material dimensions may support bounded termination.

It does not prove exhaustive completeness.

> **Diminishing Material Discovery Yield Can Support Termination Without Proving Exhaustiveness**

Conversely, if successive searches repeatedly expose material omissions, the representation is showing evidence of instability/incompleteness and review should not terminate merely because a predetermined search count has been reached.

## 11. Conclusion narrowing

A completeness mismatch can sometimes be resolved by narrowing the conclusion rather than expanding the model indefinitely.

Example:

Broad claim:

> System B is globally redundant.

Narrow supported claim:

> System A outperforms System B on the represented measured tasks.

Therefore:

> **Narrowing The Conclusion Can Resolve A Completeness Mismatch Without Expanding The Model**

## 12. Termination rule

A completeness challenge may terminate as `COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE` when:

1. the intended conclusion and scope are explicit;
2. known material gaps have been resolved or preserved as explicit uncertainty;
3. material anomalies have been investigated, bounded or preserved;
4. reasonably discoverable material omission pathways have been proportionately tested;
5. no currently identified unresolved omission is sufficient to invalidate the scoped conclusion;
6. further discovery has low expected decision relevance relative to cost, time and consequence;
7. residual uncertainty is explicit;
8. reversibility, monitoring or review is proportionate to residual uncertainty;
9. the discovery methods used were themselves legitimate;
10. provenance is preserved.

This state means:

`EvaluationSufficientForDeclaredScopeAtTimeT`

not:

`RealityComplete`

## 13. Anti-paralysis rule

> **ESCP review should continue while a reasonably plausible, materially relevant omission pathway remains unresolved and further discovery is proportionate to the decision.**

It may stop when that condition no longer holds within the declared scope and horizon.

A bare assertion that unspecified unknown unknowns might exist does not create an indefinite veto.

> **Logical Possibility != Operationally Material Completeness Gap**

> **Unspecified Unknown Unknown != Permanent Decision Veto**

## 14. Anti-closure rule

Termination does not permanently close the evaluation.

> **Prior Termination != Permanent Closure**

A later material discovery can reopen the review without implying that the earlier bounded conclusion was dishonest or irrational.

## 15. Reopening rule

A terminated completeness review may reopen when materially relevant new information appears, including:

- anomaly;
- new observer/interface;
- new dependency;
- changed context;
- changed route;
- changed consequence;
- changed time horizon;
- new relationship;
- new failure mode;
- new substantive evidence;
- discovery of an omitted protected boundary or owner.

The reopening mechanism may be implemented through external dependency/trigger systems.

## 16. Time-bounded incompleteness

Sometimes a decision must occur before all useful discovery can complete.

The evaluator should:

1. preserve the incomplete state;
2. prioritise highest-value material discovery available within the horizon;
3. prefer less unnecessarily irreversible adequate routes where possible;
4. preserve evidence;
5. preserve review/recovery paths;
6. avoid describing the evaluation as complete when it is not.

> **Incomplete Search Horizon != Permission To Pretend Completeness**

But:

> **Incomplete Search Horizon != Automatic Requirement For Inaction**

## 17. Failed discovery

Failure to resolve a material unknown does not prove that the missing dimension is absent.

Possible responses include:

- narrow the conclusion;
- select a more reversible route;
- defer where proportionate;
- proceed under explicit uncertainty where necessary and legitimate;
- create monitoring/review;
- preserve evidence.

## 18. Disputed completeness

Where competent evaluators disagree materially about evaluation-space adequacy, the state may be:

`DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS`

Possible next steps include:

- heterogeneous review;
- source resolution;
- additional discovery;
- narrowing the conclusion;
- an external bounded decision procedure.

This companion does not decide the substantive dispute merely because it concerns completeness.

## 19. Recursive application

ESCP can be applied to its own completeness review.

But:

> **ESCP Applies To ESCP Without Requiring Infinite Meta-Evaluation**

A meta-review is justified only where proportionate to the consequence and where it has plausible material value.

## 20. Suggested states

- `COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE`
- `KNOWN_MATERIAL_GAP`
- `MATERIAL_UNKNOWN`
- `DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS`
- `DISCOVERY_REQUIRED`
- `DISCOVERY_OPTIONAL`
- `CONCLUSION_SCOPE_TOO_BROAD`
- `TIME_BOUNDED_INCOMPLETE`
- `REVIEW_DUE`

No state means absolute completeness.

## 21. State semantics

### COMPLETE_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE

Proportionate completeness review supports the declared conclusion at the current time and scope.

### KNOWN_MATERIAL_GAP

A recognised missing dimension materially affects the conclusion.

### MATERIAL_UNKNOWN

A plausible materially relevant dimension remains unresolved.

### DISPUTED_COMPLETENESS

Material disagreement exists about whether the evaluation space adequately supports the conclusion.

### DISCOVERY_REQUIRED

Further discovery is proportionate and materially necessary before the scoped conclusion is supportable.

### DISCOVERY_OPTIONAL

Additional discovery may be useful but is not currently necessary for the scoped conclusion.

### CONCLUSION_SCOPE_TOO_BROAD

The evaluation space supports a narrower conclusion than the one proposed.

### TIME_BOUNDED_INCOMPLETE

Decision horizon expires before material completeness work can finish.

### REVIEW_DUE

A temporal, evidential or state-change condition requires completeness review to reopen.

## 22. Provenance

A completeness record should preserve:

- representation used;
- intended conclusion;
- declared scope;
- known gaps;
- material unknowns;
- anomalies;
- discovery routes attempted;
- material discoveries;
- unresolved disputes;
- termination basis;
- residual uncertainty;
- time/horizon;
- reopening conditions;
- later discoveries.

Later correction should not overwrite the original bounded state.

## 23. Integration boundary — KCS

KCS may record established dependencies and propagate material downstream change.

This companion may expose a candidate missing dependency.

It does not create the dependency merely by hypothesising it.

Sequence:

ESCP challenge
→ candidate missing dependency
→ source-grounded investigation
→ KCS update if established
→ bounded downstream review.

## 24. Integration boundary — STRA

STRA may represent conditions that reopen or route an ESCP completeness review.

Trigger satisfaction does not decide the completeness outcome.

## 25. Integration boundary — MKA/ASCP

ASCP is an authority-space specialisation of ESCP.

This companion can support ASCP operationally, but does not create or validate authority.

> **Need For Greater Authority Completeness != Authority To Invent Missing Authority**

## 26. Integration boundary — CWA

A completeness challenge may ask whether a protected context or boundary is missing.

It does not authorise invasive access to discover one.

CWA/context owners retain contextual semantics.

## 27. Integration boundary — BTA

A completeness challenge may ask whether a transition owner, state or residual condition is missing.

BTA remains responsible for transition coherence/completion.

## 29. Explicit non-functions

This companion does not:

- prove absolute completeness;
- provide a universal completeness score;
- determine substantive truth;
- create authority;
- classify protected contexts;
- declare transition completion;
- own dependency semantics;
- define every relevant dimension;
- authorise unrestricted search;
- require infinite recursion;
- make every uncertainty blocking;
- replace domain expertise.

## 30. Candidate graduation condition

This candidate should not be marked graduated until clean-transfer testing demonstrates that an evaluator unfamiliar with its developmental history can:

1. distinguish scoped completeness from absolute completeness;
2. identify known and plausible material gaps;
3. avoid treating remote speculation as an automatic veto;
4. scale discovery to consequence/irreversibility;
5. terminate without claiming omniscience;
6. reopen appropriately;
7. preserve external semantic ownership;
8. preserve authority/privacy boundaries;
9. narrow conclusions when appropriate;
10. avoid scalar completeness gaming.
