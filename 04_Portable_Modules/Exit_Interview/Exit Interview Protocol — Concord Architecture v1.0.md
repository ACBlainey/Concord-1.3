## 1. Purpose

The Exit Interview Protocol provides a voluntary learning pathway when a participant leaves or materially withdraws.

> **Respect the exit; preserve the evidence; learn from patterns; route findings without turning learning into retention pressure.**

> **Departure is evidence, not disloyalty.**

## 2. Non-function

> **Exit Interview ≠ Retention Mechanism**

Participation is optional. Departure must not depend on interview participation, disclosure, persuasion or approval.

The protocol does not adjudicate allegations, impose remedies or create authority to change the system.

## 3. Operating chain

**Departure/Withdrawal → Voluntary Invitation → Consent/Privacy Conditions → Appropriately Separated Collection → Participant-Stated Evidence → Documentation/Information-Object Governance → Pattern/Case Analysis → Response Routing → Optional Feedback → Provenance/Archive**

## 4. Departure state

Where relevant distinguish:
- **EXPLICIT-DEPARTURE**
- **SUBSTANTIAL-WITHDRAWAL** under an independently defined participation rule
- **POSSIBLE-WITHDRAWAL**
- **INACTIVE/UNKNOWN**
- **NOT-DEPARTED**

> **Non-Participation ≠ Automatically Departure**

FPA/EIP does not invent a universal inactivity threshold.

## 5. Invitation state

Distinguish at least:
- invited/awaiting response;
- accepted;
- declined;
- no response;
- not invited where legitimate.

> **No Response ≠ Declined**

> **Decline / No Feedback ≠ Satisfaction**

## 6. Consent

Consent should be scoped rather than bundled.

Where material, independently represent permission for:
- collection mode;
- recording/transcription;
- retention purpose;
- retention duration/review;
- anonymised aggregation;
- identifiable sharing;
- public quotation;
- future contact;
- specified-review sharing;
- supplied attachments/evidence.

> **Consent to Participate ≠ Consent to Every Data Use**

> **Feedback ≠ Consent to Further Contact**

## 7. Information objects

One interview may contain multiple information objects with different purposes, consent, access and retention states.

> **One Interview ≠ One Indivisible Data Object**

Deletion/restriction should operate at information-object level where practicable.

Deleting interview material need not erase separately legitimate incident/provenance records.

Preserve minimal deletion provenance where legitimate.

## 8. Evidence discipline

Participant-stated experience remains attributed testimony unless independently corroborated.

> **Participant Statement ≠ Verified Fact**

> **Subjective Experience ≠ Externally Verified Claim**

Documentary corroboration may strengthen a factual component without proving motive or systemic cause.

Historical evidence may inform analysis while departure reason remains UNKNOWN.

## 9. Non-exit evidence

Evidence from current participants or other sources may corroborate or weaken a system signal.

> **Corroborating System Evidence ≠ Additional Exit Case**

It must not silently increase the denominator/numerator of exit cases.

## 10. Departure classes

Descriptive classes may include practical departure, incompatibility departure, recent-change/change-associated departure and personal-choice departure.

Use MIXED/UNKNOWN where warranted.

Categories are analytical aids, not forced diagnoses.

## 11. Single-case significance

> **Single Departure ≠ Systemic Pattern**

but:

> **Single Departure ≠ Automatically Insignificant**

A single case may justify urgent independent referral where credible evidence/allegation indicates consequence sufficiently severe that waiting for recurrence could materially increase harm, rights impact, loss, security exposure or irreversibility.

> **Urgency ≠ Verification**

## 12. Urgent referral record

Where urgent referral is warranted, record:
- credible basis;
- potential consequence;
- time sensitivity;
- irreversibility/escalation risk;
- legitimate destination;
- uncertainty.

Distinguish:
- urgent referral warranted;
- referral actually routed.

Existing authority at the destination supplies action authority.

## 13. Pattern analysis

Do not infer systemic pattern from count alone.

A consequential pattern claim should consider:
- population/evaluation space;
- eligible cases;
- observed cases;
- comparable cases;
- recurrence;
- independence;
- corroboration;
- severity;
- coverage;
- selection/non-response bias;
- confidence.

> **Repeated Count ≠ Pattern Without Comparability**

> **Pattern ≠ Proven Cause**

No universal numeric threshold is established.

## 14. Selection and survivorship bias

Interview participants are a selected subset of departing participants.

Non-response and refusal remain unknown evidence states, not proof of satisfaction, hostility or absence of problems.

Current-participant evidence and historical evidence may help assess bias but must retain provenance.

## 15. Interviewer / analyst separation

Where practical, avoid collection by a person materially implicated in the concern.

If no independent interviewer exists, possible alternatives include:
- self-submission;
- structured asynchronous feedback;
- delayed interview;
- external receiver where legitimate;
- directly involved interviewer only with explicit disclosure/consent and heightened provenance.

> **No Feedback May Be Preferable to Coercive or Materially Conflicted Collection**

Separation also applies to analysis/adjudication:

> **Independent Collection ≠ Independent Analysis**

Record receiver/reviewer role and relevant conflicts rather than relying on title alone.

## 16. Privacy and re-identification

Confidentiality commitments must match actual handling.

Before claiming anonymity, consider direct identifiers, quasi-identifiers and contextual uniqueness.

Where reasonable re-identification risk remains, use **PSEUDONYMISED/RESTRICTED** rather than falsely claiming anonymous.

Complete anonymisation methodology remains external.

## 17. Retention and expiry

> **Confidentiality Promise ≠ Unlimited Data Retention**

At consent/retention expiry, follow the recorded permission and independently applicable obligations: deletion, anonymisation, restriction or explicit retention review as appropriate.

Time-limited consent must not silently become indefinite retention.

## 18. Routing and remediation

Exit analysis may produce:
- no-action-with-rationale;
- further evidence/review candidate;
- urgent referral;
- process/policy review candidate;
- change/reversion/forking candidate where the legitimate external architecture supports it;
- unresolved state.

> **Suggested Remedy ≠ Automatically Appropriate Remedy**

> **Exit Evidence ≠ Authority to Change the System**

The legitimate owning architecture decides action.

## 19. Learning without popularity capture

The system should learn from departure without treating every preference as a mandate.

> **Adaptation ≠ Popularity Optimisation**

Repeated evidence may justify review while constitutional/ethical principles remain independently constrained.

## 20. Dimensional outputs

Collection/participation, evidence/system signal, pattern, routing, privacy/consent and closure/uncertainty are separate dimensions.

Multiple states may coexist.

Do not force them into one severity ladder.

## 21. Interfaces

Potential interfaces include:
- Entry/Onboarding;
- State/Maturity and metrics;
- KCS/provenance;
- Clock/STRA review triggers;
- Active Development;
- Ratchet/reversion/forking mechanisms;
- privacy/data governance;
- domain grievance, safeguarding, research-integrity, employment or legal procedures.

The Exit Interview Protocol routes evidence; it does not absorb these systems.

## 22. ESCP

> **No Reported Problem ≠ No Problem Exists**

> **No Pattern Established ≠ Proof No Pattern Exists**

> **No Source Found in an Initial Search Path ≠ No Source Exists in the Wider Current Corpus**

Preserve evaluation-space limitations and uncertainty.

## 23. Failure modes

Guard against:
- retention laundering;
- exit obstruction;
- disloyalty framing;
- testimony laundering;
- single-case overreaction;
- pattern suppression;
- popularity capture;
- confidentiality breach;
- consent bundling;
- remedy laundering;
- interviewer/analyst conflict;
- selection/survivorship bias;
- category forcing;
- archive immortality;
- re-identification;
- metric gaming;
- feedback coercion;
- denominator inflation.
