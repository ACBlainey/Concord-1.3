# Civil Attention Module v1.0 — Plain-Language Guide

## What is it?

This module gives people one understandable way to tell a complex organisation or civilisation about something that may need attention.

You should not need to know which department, system or authority owns the problem before you report it.

The basic principle is:

**Tell the system once; the system works out who needs to deal with it.**

## What can be submitted?

The core supports three main kinds of input:

- **Suggestion** — an idea for improving something.
- **Problem Report** — something may be wrong, harmful, broken or failing.
- **Petition** — several participants want a concern or requested change to receive attention.

Submitting something does not automatically prove it is true or require the requested outcome.

## What happens next?

The original submission is preserved. The system then works out what the underlying issue is, what information exists, who should examine it, and whether more than one specialist area needs to be involved.

If the submission was sent to the wrong place, the participant should not have to start again.

If one report contains several different problems, they can be separated for processing without changing the original report.

If many reports concern the same problem, they can be grouped without erasing where the information came from.

## Does popularity decide what happens?

No.

A thousand people repeating the same claim does not automatically make it true, and one person's report is not automatically unimportant.

Volume can legitimately cause something to receive attention or re-evaluation, but evidence, consequences and legitimate priority assessment remain separate questions.

## Who makes the actual decision?

The specialist function that legitimately owns that kind of decision.

The Civil Attention system routes and tracks work. It does not take over the authority of engineers, judges, researchers, safety specialists, administrators or other legitimate domain owners.

**Central visibility does not mean central control.**

## What if several areas are involved?

The same Issue can be sent to several responsible functions. Each gets its own linked Domain Action Object, or DAO.

Their work remains connected to the original Issue, and each must return enough information for the overall system to know what happened.

Finishing one part does not automatically mean the whole problem is resolved.

## What if nobody accepts responsibility?

The Issue does not disappear.

A Responsibility Resolution Object, or RRO, can record who was asked, why responsibility is disputed and who is responsible for resolving the ownership question.

Resolving who owns the work is separate from resolving the problem itself.

## What if the work is blocked?

Waiting should be explicit.

The system should record what it is waiting for, who owns that dependency, what condition would allow work to continue and when the situation should be reviewed.

Simply changing a date or saying “still waiting” should not count as meaningful progress.

## What if another organisation must act first?

The external dependency can be recorded, but the local organisation does not lose responsibility for tracking its own unresolved Issue.

**External dependency does not mean local responsibility disappears.**

## What about private or sensitive information?

Sensitive evidence does not have to be copied into the central system.

The specialist domain can keep protected material while returning enough bounded information to show that the Issue is being handled.

## What if the system gets something wrong?

Classification and processing are reviewable. New evidence, missed information, stale processing, conflicts of interest, failed implementation or changed circumstances can justify challenge or reopening.

A participant can trigger review without gaining authority to dictate the result.

## What are Domain Companions?

The core method is deliberately general. Different domains need extra rules.

For example, a Judiciary companion may specify judicial evidence and confidentiality rules, while an engineering companion may specify inspection and safety procedures.

Those companions are added to the core. They do not rewrite it.

**Core Method + Domain Companion + Host Services = Domain Implementation**

If a domain discovers something that appears to require changing the common method, it must be sent back for controlled review rather than silently changing the local copy.

## What has been tested?

The module passed two blind-transfer programmes containing 32 cases in total: a multi-site facilities organisation and a distributed open-source software ecosystem.

Both required substantial domain-specific rules, but neither required changing the portable core.

This is evidence that the architecture transfers across those tested domains. It is not a claim that every real-world implementation, legal system or security environment has already been validated.
