## 1. Purpose

Functionally Bounded Summits are a general-purpose negotiation protocol for situations in which participants have a wider contested, low-trust or complex relationship but face a practical problem that may be addressed at a narrower functional resolution.

The protocol is designed to prevent a bounded problem from automatically becoming a negotiation over the entire relationship.

Its compact rule is:

> **Bound the problem. Bound the negotiation. Bound the failure. Expand only when the function genuinely requires it.**

The protocol does not require reconciliation, common ideology, general trust or agreement on every surrounding dispute.

It attempts to make limited cooperation possible without pretending wider disagreement has disappeared.

---

## 2. Core problem

A practical problem in Domain A may trigger a meeting.

Without scope discipline, the interaction can expand:

**Problem A**  
→ **Wider Relationship Discussion**  
→ **Unrelated Grievance B**  
→ **Demand C**  
→ **Threat D**  
→ **Retaliation E**  
→ **Damage to otherwise functioning Domains F, G and H**

The protocol calls this pattern **negotiation contagion**.

The alternative is:

**Problem A**  
→ **Functionally Bounded Negotiation**  
→ **Agreement / Modification / Trial / Rejection / No Agreement**  
→ **Result remains bounded unless a genuine dependency requires wider action**

The protocol cannot guarantee that participants will behave this way. It supplies a structure intended to make scope, expansion and violations explicit.

---

## 3. Applicability

Use the protocol where:

1. two or more participants face a shared, interacting or cross-boundary problem;
2. the participants may disagree substantially outside that problem;
3. the problem appears capable of meaningful functional separation;
4. relevant participants can be identified;
5. an initial negotiating scope can be proposed;
6. participants are willing to use a bounded process.

Potential contexts include organisations, institutions, communities, federated systems, technical consortia, multi-agent systems, jurisdictions and civilisations.

These are candidate applications, not validated use domains.

---

## 4. Non-applicability

Do not force functional boundedness where the supposed domain cannot meaningfully be separated from necessary surrounding conditions.

A narrow scope is invalid if it hides a material dependency.

Therefore:

> **Functional Boundedness ≠ Functional Isolation.**

If solving A genuinely requires B, the process must be able to recognise B and deliberately expand.

The protocol should also not be used to make an illegitimate objective acceptable merely by narrowing the forum around it.

---

## 5. Core protocol

### Step 1 — Define the problem

State the practical problem without assuming a preferred solution.

Record:

- what is happening;
- who or what is materially affected;
- why interaction is required;
- what remains uncertain.

Avoid beginning with a mechanism disguised as the problem.

**“We need an additional crossing”** may be a proposed mechanism.

**“Current food-transit capacity is insufficient”** is a functional problem.

---

### Step 2 — Identify the required function

Ask:

> **What function must become possible, change or be restored for this problem to be addressed?**

Examples:

- exchange specified information;
- restore one transport connection;
- coordinate a shared technical standard;
- establish an emergency communication channel;
- allocate access to a resource;
- perform a joint inspection;
- agree a temporary operating procedure.

The function defines the first candidate boundary.

---

### Step 3 — Identify minimum necessary participants

Include participants materially required to:

- authorise the function;
- supply necessary information;
- implement the result;
- bear material consequences;
- or represent an indispensable affected interest where the surrounding system requires such representation.

Do not enlarge the forum merely because additional actors have interests in the wider relationship.

Do not exclude actors whose participation is genuinely required merely to preserve a smaller meeting.

A person, institution or system may be necessary to establish facts without becoming a negotiating participant or acquiring decision authority.

> **Necessary Evidence Source ≠ Necessary Negotiating Participant.**

Where possible, distinguish the roles of evidence source, affected party, negotiating participant, implementation actor and decision authority.

---

### Step 4 — Define relevant evidence

Specify the evidence needed to understand or operate the function.

Separate:

- known information;
- disputed information;
- missing information;
- information requiring verification;
- and information that is outside the current functional question.

A bounded forum must not use scope limitation to exclude evidence genuinely necessary to the function.

---

### Step 5 — Propose the minimum negotiating scope

Define:

**Problem**  
→ **Required Function**  
→ **Required Participants**  
→ **Required Evidence**  
→ **Minimum Negotiating Scope**

The scope should be sufficient to address the function but should not automatically absorb unrelated disputes.

This is **Minimum Necessary Negotiation**.

---

### Step 6 — Establish the interaction grammar

Before substantive negotiation, record a proportionate grammar.

Where relevant, include:

- remit;
- participants and roles;
- agenda;
- evidence procedure;
- decision procedure;
- recordkeeping;
- time horizon;
- confidentiality conditions;
- excluded matters;
- scope-expansion procedure;
- pause conditions;
- withdrawal conditions;
- interpretation of outcomes;
- non-inference conditions.

Not every use requires an elaborate formal process.

The grammar should be no more complex than necessary for the consequence and risk of the interaction.

---

### Step 7 — Negotiate within scope

Participants examine proposals relevant to the function.

Permitted outcomes may include:

- Accept;
- Reject;
- Modify;
- Temporary Trial;
- Conditional Acceptance;
- Request Functionally Relevant Evidence;
- No Agreement.

A participant may identify a new dependency.

That does not automatically expand the forum.

It initiates the scope-review procedure.

---

### Step 8 — Test proposed scope expansion

For every proposed additional issue, ask:

1. What dependency connects this issue to the current function?
2. Is the dependency material?
3. Can the function be addressed without resolving it?
4. What minimum additional scope is required?
5. Are additional participants or evidence required?
6. What remains excluded after expansion?

If the dependency is genuine:

**Dependency Identified**  
→ **Dependency Demonstrated**  
→ **Scope Expansion Explicitly Agreed/Authorised Under Applicable Rules**  
→ **Additional Participants/Evidence Added Where Necessary**  
→ **New Boundary Recorded**

Thus:

> **Scope should expand deliberately, not contagiously.**

---

### Step 9 — Record the outcome at its actual resolution

A successful result should say what was agreed and no more.

Agreement on A means agreement on A unless participants explicitly establish broader implications.

Likewise, no agreement on A means no agreement on A unless a real dependency produces wider consequences.

Therefore:

> **Functional Success ≠ General Agreement.**

and:

> **Functional Failure ≠ General Relationship Failure.**

---

### Step 10 — Contain failure where practicable

Where negotiation fails, preserve unrelated functions unless:

- the failed function creates a genuine dependency;
- an external rule legitimately requires a wider consequence;
- or participants explicitly make a wider decision through an appropriate process.

The default objective is:

> **Failure should, where practicable, remain at the functional resolution at which it occurred.**

This is **Functional Failure Containment**.

It is a presumption against unnecessary contamination, not a guarantee that all domains are independent.

---

### Step 11 — Separate scope proposals, violations and outcomes

An out-of-scope proposal is not automatically a protocol violation. It may be a legitimate attempt to identify a previously unrecognised dependency.

Use the sequence:

**Proposed Linkage**  
→ **Dependency Test**  
→ **Scope Ruling**  
→ **Preserve Unresolved Matter Where Appropriate**  
→ **Return to Function**

Therefore:

> **Out-of-Scope Proposal ≠ Protocol Violation by Default.**

If, after the scope ruling, a participant persists in prohibited linkage, bypasses the agreed grammar, uses prohibited cross-domain coercion, or otherwise violates the process, record that separately from the substantive negotiation outcome.

Do not silently redefine the protocol around the violation.

At the same time:

> **Repeated Participant Violation ≠ Architecturally Irrelevant.**

Repeated violations may reveal bad incentives, unrealistic rules, missing dependencies, ambiguity, power asymmetry or an inadequate architecture.

---

### Step 12 — Preserve provenance and review conditions

The record should identify:

- why the forum was convened;
- authorised scope;
- participants and roles;
- evidence considered;
- dependencies identified;
- scope changes;
- proposals considered;
- outcome;
- unresolved matters;
- violations;
- non-inference conditions;
- review or expiry conditions;
- and matters remaining outside the result.

If the problem recurs, retrieve the previous record and review changed conditions rather than assuming the old answer still applies.

## 6. Non-inference

A bounded interaction should not acquire implications merely because participants attended it.

Examples:

**Participation ≠ General Recognition**

**Technical Agreement ≠ Political Agreement**

**Functional Cooperation ≠ Alliance**

**Acceptance of One Procedure ≠ Acceptance of Another Participant's Wider Claims**

**Use of a Protocol ≠ Submission to the Protocol Designer**

Any broader inference must have an independent basis.

---

## 7. Bounded authority

The forum's authority, if any, derives from the function and the external system that legitimately authorises the participants.

The protocol itself does not create authority.

Therefore:

> **Forum Existence ≠ Standing Authority.**

> **Successful Negotiation ≠ Permanent Jurisdiction.**

> **Protocol Design ≠ Authority Over Protocol Users.**

If a temporary remit ends, any authority created specifically for that remit should ordinarily end unless independently renewed or justified.

A bounded summit may also end in legitimate non-agreement while an external participant subsequently acts under authority that existed independently of the summit.

> **Negotiated Agreement ≠ Independent Exercise of Pre-Existing Authority.**

The forum neither creates nor invalidates that external authority merely because negotiation did not produce agreement.

---

## 8. Third-party facilitation

A facilitator, mediator, technical operator or recordkeeper may assist.

Their presence does not create general authority.

The role should specify:

- authorised function;
- information access;
- permitted interventions;
- accountability;
- review;
- termination.

A protocol provider need not operate the negotiation.

---

## 9. Functional trust

The protocol does not require a single global trust score.

Participants may have:

- high trust in one emergency channel;
- moderate trust in one technical process;
- low trust in commercial commitments;
- and severe distrust elsewhere.

Preserve this resolution.

> **Low General Trust ≠ Zero Functional Trust.**

A reliable narrow interface may exist inside an otherwise hostile relationship.

---

## 10. Failure classification

Do not classify every unsuccessful outcome as the same kind of failure.

### F1 — Architecture Failure
The protocol design is inadequate for the problem.

### F2 — Implementation Failure
The protocol could have worked as specified but was implemented incorrectly.

### F3 — Participant Protocol Violation
A participant acts outside the agreed grammar.

### F4 — Adversarial Exploitation
A participant formally complies while the observable structure of the interaction is used to manipulate the process contrary to its function.

Classification should distinguish observable structure from unsupported claims about motive.

> **Exploitative Structure ≠ Proven Malicious Intent.**

### F5 — Environmental Failure
External conditions make the mechanism insufficient or obsolete.

### N1 — Legitimate Non-Agreement
The protocol operated as intended but participants did not find mutually acceptable terms.

N1 is not automatically evidence of architecture failure.

---

## 11. Failure-mode checklist

Before or after use, examine at least:

1. artificially narrow agenda;
2. exclusion of relevant evidence;
3. powerful participant controlling scope;
4. endless expansion requests;
5. hidden issue linkage;
6. cross-domain coercion;
7. side-channel threats;
8. performative or theatrical use;
9. strategic delay;
10. false urgency;
11. asymmetrical dependence;
12. leverage over essential or humanitarian needs;
13. selective protocol enforcement;
14. participation being misrepresented as wider recognition;
15. technically compliant bad faith;
16. deliberate breakdown to justify wider retaliation;
17. functional silos hiding systemic dependencies;
18. repeated failure without architectural review;
19. successful forums accumulating unintended authority;
20. procedure becoming too complex for timely action.

---

## 12. Emergency variant

Where delay itself creates serious risk, a predefined bounded channel may use:

**Emergency Identified**  
→ **Predefined Functional Channel Activated**  
→ **Minimum Participants**  
→ **Known Grammar**  
→ **Rapid Evidence Exchange**  
→ **Temporary Action**  
→ **Post-Event Review**

Emergency compression does not erase provenance, scope or later review.

---

## 13. Repeated use

Repeated successful bounded interactions may justify proposing a standing interface.

The transition should be explicit:

**Repeated Problem**  
→ **Repeated Bounded Forum**  
→ **Stable Pattern Identified**  
→ **Standing Protocol Proposed**  
→ **Independent Review/Authorisation**  
→ **Standing Interface if Legitimately Established**

Repeated use alone does not create jurisdiction.

---

## 14. Minimum implementation record

A lightweight implementation can use:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Problem | What practical problem requires interaction? |
| Function | What must become possible/change? |
| Participants | Who is materially required? |
| Evidence | Known, disputed and missing information |
| Scope | What may this forum address? |
| Exclusions | What remains outside scope? |
| Dependencies | What could legitimately widen scope? |
| Expansion | What was added, why and by what process? |
| Proposals | What options were considered? |
| Outcome | Accept / Reject / Modify / Trial / Conditional / Evidence Request / No Agreement |
| Violations | Any departures from agreed grammar |
| Non-inference | What must not be inferred from participation/outcome? |
| Review | Expiry, reassessment or reopening conditions |

---

## 15. Success questions

Evaluate use of the protocol by asking:

- Was the problem defined at an appropriate resolution?
- Were necessary participants present?
- Was relevant evidence available or its absence made explicit?
- Was unrelated issue linkage limited?
- Were genuine dependencies recognised?
- Was scope expansion explicit?
- Was the functional problem resolved?
- If unresolved, was unnecessary wider damage avoided?
- Were protocol violations distinguishable from negotiation outcomes?
- Did participation or agreement acquire unintended implications?
- Was the outcome recorded accurately?
- Did the forum accumulate authority beyond its function?
- Was the process proportionate to urgency and consequence?

Agreement rate alone is not a sufficient evaluation metric.

## 16. Relationship to wider systems

Functionally Bounded Summits can operate alongside external:

- governance;
- law;
- contracts;
- mediation;
- technical standards;
- recordkeeping;
- dispute resolution;
- authority structures;
- emergency systems.

Those systems determine substantive legitimacy where relevant.

This module determines a procedural question:

> **How can the negotiation remain at the resolution of the problem while still recognising dependencies that reality makes inseparable?**

---

## 17. Evidential boundary

This specification is extracted from developed Concord architecture.

It has not yet demonstrated that Functionally Bounded Summits:

- increase agreement rates;
- reduce escalation in practice;
- outperform general negotiation;
- overcome severe power asymmetry;
- prevent coercion;
- work across all cultures or institutions;
- or transfer reliably across all contexts outside the source architecture.

FBS-BTT-001 provides bounded transfer evidence: a fresh AI instance applied v0.1 coherently to a supplied non-Concord, non-state organisational dispute. The test supported four minor clarifications now incorporated in v0.2. It does not establish human usability or real-world effectiveness.

The module completed source-resolution audit, standalone extraction, FBS-BTT-001 blind transfer testing, test-supported revision and portable-package graduation review before this release.

The development evidence supports specification-level standalone portability for the tested AI transfer context. It does not establish human usability or real-world effectiveness.

> **Portable graduation ≠ empirical validation.**
